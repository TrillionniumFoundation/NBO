\subsection{Decomposition of the observed primary computation}
\label{dev:clockprofile125}
The following accounting uses every stored clock in the complete 232-case census for each enhancement service. No service was re-timed and no new economic observation was made. The query timer includes the original continuous-risk query, original-gradient recovery and bracket update. The remaining time within a service includes setup, reconstruction of paid-support cases, global lower-bound and candidate calculations, policy selection and local bookkeeping; it cannot be assigned exclusively to algebra. The last overhead column includes cohort work and the equal allocation of the observed parent residual. All four components sum exactly to the complete primary clock before decimal display rounding.

\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\hline
Service & Prefix & \shortstack{Query and\\recovery} & \shortstack{Remaining\\service} & \shortstack{Cohort and\\parent} & Total \\
\hline
Warm adaptive & 5.631801 & 3.195700 & 5.469696 & 0.734253 & 15.031450 \\
Cold adaptive & 5.462398 & 6.021707 & 19.733199 & 0.759275 & 31.976579 \\
Warm fixed 512 & 5.745743 & 5.348735 & 1.563042 & 0.718276 & 13.375796 \\
\hline\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These are descriptive components of one execution on the shared host. Warm adaptive acquisition reduces the observed query-and-recovery component while increasing the remaining service component relative to fixed precision. A resource claim based only on terminal contexts would miss that cost. A future implemented precision or stopping rule must account for the complete marginal cost of extending and processing the support record. The accounting is not a new runtime experiment, does not supply a confidence interval, and does not change the original adoption analysis. The exact component sums, source hash and reporting-only execution receipt are retained in \texttt{SAVED\_CLOCK\_PROFILE125.json}.
