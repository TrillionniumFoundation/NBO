\subsection{Policy identity and conditional adoption}
\label{sec:policyidentity125}

Separate value intervals need not exploit an exact identity between two
implemented policies. The following observation gives a stronger adoption
test when that identity is available. It applies to any two representations.
The application below is a post-execution analysis of saved R125 records,
with no new economic evaluation, timing run or experimental reclassification.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional cost dominance of an identical policy]
\label{prop:policyidentity125}
Fix a queried state, economic information, original transition law and
finite-horizon recursive objective that is well-defined and finite for
both policies. Suppose services $n$ and $c$ return
the same realized first action and use the same implemented continuation
policy on all future histories reachable from that state. The identity
includes observation conventions, feasible actions, numerical rounding,
and any policy randomization. For randomized policies, identity means
the same conditional decision rules under a common adapted randomization
and economic filtration.
They then have identical original-policy objectives, $J_n=J_c$.

Let $B_n,B_c$ be their declared acquisition charges, $N>0$ the common
amortization volume, and
$t_i\in[\underline t_i,\overline t_i]$ their complete per-decision
service times. Suppose the recorded quantities establish
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identitycostgap125}
 \varepsilon_N:=B_n/N+\underline t_n
                  -(B_c/N+\overline t_c)>0.
\end{equation}
For every pair of times in these ranges and every $\lambda>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identitydominance125}
 [J_n+\lambda(B_n/N+t_n)]-[J_c+\lambda(B_c/N+t_c)]
                         \ge\lambda\varepsilon_N>0.
\end{equation}
Thus service $n$ cannot minimize the priced objective over any comparator
set containing $c$ at a strictly positive price. At $\lambda=0$ it ties
$c$; no optimality relative to a different policy is implied. The result
is conditional on the stated cost ranges and accounting rule, and holds
only for the specified queried state and its reachable continuation histories.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use the same future innovations and, where needed, the same coupled policy
randomization for both services. Their initial states, economic information
and realized first actions coincide. The common transition law therefore
gives the same next state. The identical implemented continuation rule,
including its observations and rounding, then gives the same next action.
Induction gives the same states, actions and realized costs at all future
dates on the reachable histories. Their terminal costs coincide. Backward
evaluation applies the same conditional risk or recursive-preference
operator to identical continuation quantities at each date; it consequently
gives the same value at each preceding history, including $J_n=J_c$ at
the queried state. This argument does not replace the original recursive
objective by an expectation or require a different information filtration.

For any admitted times,
\[
 B_n/N+t_n-(B_c/N+t_c)
 \ge B_n/N+\underline t_n-(B_c/N+\overline t_c)
 =\varepsilon_N.
\]
Subtract the two priced objectives, cancel their equal original values,
and multiply this inequality by $\lambda>0$ to obtain
\eqref{eq:identitydominance125}. At zero price the entire computational
term vanishes, so this pair ties. A policy outside the identical-policy
pair can still have a lower original value. No independence of the two
service times is needed: the inequality holds for every joint realization
inside the stated ranges.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What an interval-only outer region omits.}
If the earlier comparison retains only separate value boxes, its
optimistic comparison with $c$ uses
\[
 \underline J_n+\lambda(B_n/N+\underline t_n)
 \le \overline J_c+\lambda(B_c/N+\overline t_c).
\]
When \eqref{eq:identitycostgap125} holds, this permits
$\lambda\le(\overline J_c-\underline J_n)/\varepsilon_N$.
The right side may be positive even though the true difference
$J_n-J_c$ is exactly zero. The separate boxes are a valid relaxation,
but they do not encode the policy identity. Retaining them as the original
reported outer region is consistent with deriving the stronger exclusion
from the additional equality. Neither a narrower fitted residual nor a
newly evaluated economic outcome is involved.

\paragraph{Saved first-state evidence and its limits.}
At the first declared state $(1/4,1/4)$, all 116 learned or online
services within each risk return the same action bits,
\texttt{0x1.3fffffe000000p-3}. They use the same terminal controller
with downward 40-bit state observations. The source-bound diagnostic
verifies the state, risk, action and continuation identities. For each
of the 128 neural-service/amortization cases, it checks all 100
conventional services with that same policy and retains an exact
rational witness of \eqref{eq:identitycostgap125}. The uniform-action
DP control returns a different action and is not used in this identity
comparison. Conditional on the recorded acquisition allocations and
descriptive timing ranges, every strictly positive computation price is
therefore excluded by a conventional identical-policy comparator. Zero
price supplies a tie with that comparator, not a strict neural improvement
or a conclusion about the different-policy grid control.

The recorded least-upper-cost blockers are online services in 64 cases
and degree-two polynomial services in 64 cases. This is competition
between complete services with their own charged acquisition routes;
the blockers need not share features, training losses, information budgets
or service modes with the neural service. It is not a matched-feature
representation result or an architectural complexity separation. Equality
of the original economic filtration and realized complete policy does not
assert equality of the computations used to obtain that policy.

All 128 originally reported optimistic regions remain unchanged as
nonempty interval-only outer sets, including their positive-price
endpoints. All 128 originally reported sufficient regions remain empty.
The new exclusion is a separately identified post-execution consequence
of policy identity and saved cost evidence. The three observed timing
ranges have no confidence-level or future-runtime interpretation; the
conclusion does not extend to other states, workloads, hardware, future
controllers or neural architectures. Its exact blocker witnesses and
source hashes are retained in
\texttt{ADOPTION\_SUPPORT\_DIAGNOSTIC125.json}.
