## What R45 adds

The constructive theorem gives an explicit own-future ReLU network, a feasible actor, signed residuals and an all-adapted-policy loss account from primitive Lipschitz moduli. A deterministic termination corollary allocates state, action and numerical accuracy from an economic tolerance. The network's Lipschitz bound is independent of label error; exact one-dimensional compilation preserves the defining network and permits analytic integration under the original continuous innovation law. This is not a convergence theorem for the earlier Adam/L-BFGS optimizers and not a claim that classical Lipschitz extension is new.

The predeclared matched catalogue contains 24 services, 144 rungs and 48 distinct method/economy/resolution rows. Both generators construct their own future labels. Three isolated repetitions have byte-identical policy/certificate checkpoints and separately recorded clocks. All failed rungs and complete prefix work are retained.

At targets 0.5 and 0.25, both methods certify 12/12 services. At 0.125, the neural backend certifies 9/12 and the spline 12/12. The three neural failures repeat one economy, with a final gap approximately 0.12503. The spline is earlier in all 33 common successful method/target/repetition timing comparisons. These are local construction-to-certificate measurements, not direct policy-cost comparisons or independent random economic trials. The sufficient-budget theorem and finite-ladder failures are stated separately.

## Scientific scope

This revision proves and executes a deterministic constructive backend while preserving the original NBO program. It does not establish neural superiority, resolve the existing coupled direct-cost ranking, execute a genuinely high-dimensional nonlinear comparison, demonstrate a mixed-precision work benefit, or supply a calibrated economic application unavailable to conventional methods. The response explicitly distinguishes completed work from these remaining substantive review questions.
