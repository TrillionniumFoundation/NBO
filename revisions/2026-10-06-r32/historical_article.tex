\title{Neural Bellman Operators}
\runtitle{Neural Bellman Operators}
\begin{aug}
\author[id=au1,addressref={add1}]{\fnms{Qian}~\snm{QI}\ead[label=e1]{qiqian@pku.edu.cn}}
\address[id=add1]{Peking University}
\end{aug}
\begin{abstract}

This paper develops Neural Bellman Operators for policy evaluation and feasible
improvement in controlled economies. Centered continuation errors and economic
transport bounds determine when an evaluated future can be reused or must be
refreshed. A composition theorem links current-decision certificates to policy
accuracy under explicit domain and continuation conditions, and an allocation
result distributes computation across dates. Capital experiments compare
complete neural, conventional-surrogate, and simulation procedures at a common
welfare tolerance, with all fitting, queries, failed checks, and verification
charged. A frozen future-change design executes 168 services over 840 outcomes.
Checking and refreshing NBO certifies every future-specific scalar decision
problem and establishes signed withdrawal responses to future technology and
valuation; unchanged reuse leaves 48 outcomes uncertified. Conventional methods
remain competitive, and adaptive neural reuse is not uniformly cheaper than
refitting. The general controlled-economy model, recursive utility, endogenous
preferences, temporal selves, and games retain their complete economic accounts.
Local finite-law evidence and full continuous-time policy guarantees are
connected by explicit assumptions rather than conflated.
\end{abstract}
