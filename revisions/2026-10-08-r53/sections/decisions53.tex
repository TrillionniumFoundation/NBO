\section{Cost inference and economic decisions}\label{sec:decisions53}
Conditional on the construction information, let $Y_i^j$ be the independent-bin path score of a frozen policy or a centered contrast. Numerical evaluation gives $L_i^j\le Y_i^j\le U_i^j$, with both endpoints in a known support $[A_j,B_j]$. The simulation bins are uniformly drawn and enclose every continuous realization within each bin. Hence expectations of the endpoint variables enclose the original continuous-law cost. A deterministic seed makes the calculation reproducible; the coverage theorem is conditional on the stated independent-bin sampling model, not a theorem that pseudorandom bits are physically independent.

For $n>1$, let $\overline L_j^-$ and $\overline U_j^+$ be outward sample means and $s_{Lj,+}^2,s_{Uj,+}^2$ valid upper sample variances. For a family of at most $m$ records and $\ell\ge\log(4m/\alpha)$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radius53}
 r(s^2)=\sqrt{\frac{2s^2\ell}{n}}+
              \frac{7(B_j-A_j)\ell}{3(n-1)}.
\end{equation}
Applying the empirical-Bernstein bound of \citet{maurer2009} to the lower and upper endpoint variables and taking a union bound gives simultaneous intervals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inference53}
 \mathbb EY^j\in
 [\overline L_j^- -r(s_{Lj,+}^2),\;\overline U_j^+ +r(s_{Uj,+}^2)]
 \cap[A_j,B_j]
\end{equation}
with probability at least $1-\alpha$. Selection among the already covered policies or cost thresholds preserves this event. For the primary study all horizon records fit in the common limit $m=128$, with $\alpha=0.01$ and $\ell=13$; the extension has a separate family with the same constants. Their union has coverage at least $0.98$. Identity comparisons are exact zeros and do not create new draws.

For the two-dimensional economy a pathwise cost bound is
\[
 B(T)=\frac{99+4p}{64}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\beta^t+
                   \frac{37}{16}\beta^T.
\]
Absolute costs use $[0,B(T)]$. Both cross-generator and own-incumbent path gains use $[-B(T),B(T)]$. The latter support cannot be replaced by $[0,B(T)]$ merely because Theorem~\ref{thm:directed53} establishes expected improvement. The release independently checks the exact dyadic first and second moments of every stored endpoint vector, so coverage does not depend on reproducing a particular host's floating-point summation order.

A controller replacement with fee $f\ge0$ is identified as profitable when the lower gain endpoint exceeds $f$ and identified as unprofitable when the upper endpoint is below $f$. The new witness policies therefore identify nonempty fee intervals supporting replacement of their own incumbents. This conclusion does not choose a generous fee after seeing the data: the entire nonnegative fee line is evaluated on the same event. Without an externally justified fee or benefit scale, the paper does not claim a calibrated adoption recommendation.

The original finite-catalogue information-price result also remains available. For covered implementation costs $J_b\in[l_b,u_b]$ and known information charges $q_b(\lambda)$, select $\widehat b\in\arg\min_b\{u_b+q_b(\lambda)\}$. On the coverage event,
\[
 J_{\widehat b}+q_{\widehat b}(\lambda)-\min_b\{J_b+q_b(\lambda)\}
 \le\min_b\{u_b+q_b(\lambda)\}-\min_b\{l_b+q_b(\lambda)\}.
\]
The proof is immediate by bounding the selected cost above and every comparator below. It applies simultaneously to every charge in the declared family; it is an optimum over the evaluated catalogue, not all possible information technologies. The full original information study and separated resource-allocation theorem remain in the preserved exposition.

For conventional FVI, transporting its nearest-node action from the reported state to the true state and then applying cell-wide capacity repair adds the inherited allowance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fvisensor53}
 (L_t^{\mathrm{gr}}+L_t^f+2D_tK_t^A)r(b)+D_t\Delta_t.
\end{equation}
Here $L_t^f$ is the actual interpolated critic modulus. The graph term bounds transport of the feasible node pair, the critic term bounds $f_t(\widehat x)-f_t(x)$, and robust repair contributes $D_t(2K_t^Ar(b)+\Delta_t)$. No continuity of the actor is used. The witness case has $L_t^f=L_t^{\mathrm{gr}}$ and recovers the construction allowance. The direct full-cell certificates in the new study already include these effects and do not add this bound a second time.
