\section{Unrestricted support certificates and the global cost gap}\label{app:global}
This appendix supplements Section~\ref{art-sec:global} of the article. The original upper witness and all new support objects use the same rational representation: affine formulas on open cells and independently stored values at their knots. Jumps induced by a compiled installed rule are allowed; measure-zero values are not discarded from any restart guarantee.

\subsection{Verification instructions and proof dependencies}
A support certificate stores the exact model, its multiplier, $U$, the selector $\gamma$, the defect bounds $d_t$, the installed policy, its support-optimal deployment, $W$, and that deployment's operating and implementation values. The checker first verifies the model and horizon, legal point and interval actions, and the terminal conditions. It checks $T^aU_{t+1}\le U_t$ for every action and the displayed defect inequality for $\gamma$. The propagated errors and $L=U-b$ are then reconstructed by the checker. No file purporting to contain $V$ is read.

For every support object, the checker verifies that every action-continuation alternative in the support Bellman equation lies below $W_t$, and that the stored deployment attains equality. It also checks the own-policy Bellman equalities for $J,C$ and the identity $W=\lambda J-C$. These checks imply unrestricted support optimality by backward induction. They do not require a maximizing-envelope computation. The same check proves that no adaptive or randomized action choice can exceed $W$: conditional expectation and a convex combination preserve the upper comparison.

An assembled certificate stores a feasible deployment, its $J,C$, its feasibility upper witness, the lower-cost function $B$, and a selector identifying the attaining lower-bound term on every open cell and knot. It binds the complete ordered multiplier portfolio by SHA-256 hashes of canonical uncompressed JSON. Every support certificate must first pass its own check; hashes alone do not establish mathematical validity. The assembled checker verifies the lower envelope by comparing each candidate term to $B$ and checking equality with the selected term. It verifies $B\le C$, every restart's operating tolerance, and the exact integrated gap. A separate check compares the selected $J$ to the installed own-policy value to establish operating preservation when it holds.

The implementation of the lower selector accepts integer indices for lower-bound terms, not action labels. Its feasible policy selector accepts only the three legal maintenance actions. Confusing these objects could produce a silently spliced policy, so their validations are distinct. The terminal cost is zero, the terminal operating value is $g$, and the terminal support value is $\lambda g$.

\subsection{Feasibility, finite selection, and the scope of the lower bound}
The sufficient-multiplier proposition guarantees that the declared price 4096 has a feasible support selector for every installed rule in the declared model and horizon set. This is an ex ante statement from the bounded implementation cost and witness defect, not an assertion inferred from the 42 observed passes. The independent audit verifies the rational sufficient bound for each horizon. Every actual serialized candidate is nevertheless tested directly at all dates and states.

The selected primal policy minimizes integrated implementation cost only among the finite set of individually verified complete policies, including the class baseline. This selection is not an optimization over all feasible policies. In contrast, every lower-bound term applies to every feasible policy, so the assembled cost interval bounds the unrestricted constrained optimum. The two scopes must not be exchanged. In particular, the computation does not assume that a support-optimal policy exists for every constrained-optimal operating budget, or that a single common multiplier is a complete dual for the simultaneous continuum of restart constraints.

The pointwise maximum of lower bounds remains a lower bound even when no single policy attains it everywhere. Integration is valid because every admissible policy dominates that pointwise function. Equality of the integrated bounds proves optimality for the stated initial distribution; without a pointwise zero gap it does not by itself prove that the deployment minimizes cost at each individual restart. The supplied all-restart cost-gap envelope is an additional reported quantity, not a consequence of a small integrated gap.

All implementation costs are nonnegative. At zero cost, the integrated gap vanishes for the declared spline cases. Their stronger every-restart zero-cost result follows directly because the retained incumbent has identically zero intervention, not merely zero cost almost everywhere. Isolated action differences in other deployments remain part of every feasibility check even when they do not affect a uniform-distribution integral.

\subsection{Costs, unfavorable cases, and preservation of attempts}
The eight multiplier problems are all solved and charged, including every candidate that fails an operating tolerance. The common witness is produced from primitives; no precomputed optimal-value file is supplied. The classical class candidate is rebuilt and checked on the same machine using the complete-cost constructor. Every proposed support policy is independently evaluated rather than assigned a cost from its optimization label. The final byte-level audit is an additional cost, reported separately from the checks within construction. Exact operating dynamic programming is run only as a recorded comparator and diagnostic.

The replication stores all 168 support outcomes and all 42 final intervals, not only the neural improvements. Timing records distinguish fitting of the previously installed proposals from revising those fixed proposals now. They do not add incompatible machine timings to obtain a purported speedup. The frozen R32 records and staged R33 source archive remain losslessly preserved. R34 execution records are new and separately identified; no unavailable prior run is reconstructed or asserted as evidence. Compressed encodings and their checksums remain explicitly distinguished from canonical uncompressed certificate bytes.

The checker rejects eight malformed support certificates and eight malformed assembled certificates, including changed endpoint values, missing dates and intervals, false costs or lower bounds, illegal actions, bad multiplier signs, incorrect terminal conditions, and altered support hashes. Four two-period rational restart states are independently compared with all 27 contingent two-step action trees. The final read-only audit disables the optimizing envelope routine, so a hidden repeat of the constructor's optimization is an error rather than a fallback.
