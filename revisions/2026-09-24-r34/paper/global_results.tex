\subsection{Economic gain and the remaining global gap}

All 18 installed-neural configurations receive a strictly less costly certified deployment than the local-class minimum, at unchanged operating tolerance and with separately verified operating preservation. The gain is not attributed to superior neural approximation: all twelve spline configurations have zero-cost feasible incumbents, as already identified by the earlier zero-intervention test. Rather, the positive-cost comparisons demonstrate economically relevant conservatism of the local class. A policy outside that class can distribute its operating losses over time while meeting the original all-restart constraint.

For example, at $T=12$, $\varepsilon=.05$, and installed seed 31003, the new policy's normalized intervention cost is approximately $.674484$, compared with $1.404696$ for the class minimum. Its operating-regret certificate remains below $.05$ and it preserves the installed return. The global lower cost bound is approximately $.041222$, leaving a certified upper bound of $.633262$ on the policy's excess cost over the unrestricted constrained optimum. The approximately $.730212$ reduction relative to the local class is therefore established, whereas global optimality of this positive-cost deployment is not. Reporting both comparisons prevents a strong improvement over a conservative rule from being mistaken for a sharp global solution.

The lower bound is zero and the upper bound is zero in the twelve spline cases; these are genuine global optima, including relative to randomized or history-dependent feasible policies. For the other cases the complete lower/upper intervals, rather than a binary ``success'' label, appear in the supplement. Adding multipliers can tighten the lower envelope but cannot worsen it; the finite portfolio supplies no guarantee that this process closes every gap.

The displayed per-configuration costs include all eight support solves and their checks, even when a candidate is infeasible, and a fresh class-control construction. There is no measured neural-specific acceleration or high-dimensional claim.

The experimental price grid consists of computational multipliers used to produce support policies and lower bounds. These are not calibrated monetary shadow prices, nor are they the illustrative external revision-price parameter used in the inherited welfare sensitivity exercise. No universal success count at an arbitrary external price is substituted for the full cost bounds or the retained sensitivity frontier. The normalized unit remains one implementation intervention weighted by $1+x$ and discounted under the deployed policy's own occupancy.
