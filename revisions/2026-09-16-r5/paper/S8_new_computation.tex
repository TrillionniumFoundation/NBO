The quadratic comparator minimizes squared fitting error over
\[
 q(x)+b+\ell^\mathsf Tz+z^\mathsf THz,\qquad z=x-\mathbf1,\quad H\succeq0.
\]
The regression is convex in $(b,\ell,H)$. Its gradient is computed from the full quadratic design matrix. Projected accelerated gradient leaves $(b,\ell)$ unrestricted and projects the symmetric matrix onto the positive-semidefinite cone by clipping negative eigenvalues. A small ridge term stabilizes the design. The reported projected-gradient norm is below the declared $10^{-8}$ stopping threshold in the executed fits. At deployment the analytic gradient is $\nabla q(x)+\ell+2Hz$, and its Hessian bound is the largest eigenvalue of $\nabla^2q+2H$. The common investment projection and full scenario-tree cost calculation are unchanged. This comparison tests a genuinely coupled convex quadratic, rather than a separable or unoptimized baseline.

The fresh-state workload charges only for the computations used by each competing method. The reusable policy's cost is critic training plus zero-start online improvement and full policy evaluation. The specialized solver's cost is a complete nonanticipative reference optimization at tolerance $10^{-3}$. A reference solved more tightly to gap below $10^{-7}$ supplies the accuracy upper bounds for both; it is not charged asymmetrically as part of one solver's online cost. Hardware, dependencies, seeds, and individual times accompany the outputs. A three-period, rank-two-shock experiment cannot establish dimension-independent performance, even when its measured query reuse is useful.

\subsection{Persistent Capacity and All-Date Dynamic Deviations}
Let $b_i=1-sK_i$, so that next-period capacity probability is $sK_i+b_i a_i$. Write $W_i(k'_1,k'_2)$ for player $i$'s continuation value after averaging over next demand. For player one the best response is
\begin{equation}
 a_1=\clip\left(
 \frac{\delta b_1}{\kappa}
 \left\{W_1(1,0)-W_1(0,0)+(sK_2+b_2a_2)\Delta W_1\right\},0,1\right),
 \label{eq:game_persistent_br}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta W_1=W_1(1,1)-W_1(1,0)-W_1(0,1)+W_1(0,0)$. The corresponding formula holds for player two. The action criterion is strictly concave because the continuation is affine in the player's own probability and the action cost has curvature $\kappa>0$. Enumerating whether each clipped best response is at zero, interior, or one gives nine active-set combinations. Feasible candidates are checked against both original best-response equations.

The product of the two affine slopes bounds the Lipschitz constant of the composed best response; clipping is nonexpansive. Its recorded maximum below one therefore provides a unique one-period fixed point for every continuation game. Backward induction supplies a finite-horizon Markov-perfect profile. This argument does not assume current-capacity independence and does not rely on convergence from a single initialization.

For independent dynamic verification, let $J_{i,n}$ be the value of the frozen profile and define the full unilateral best-response value
\[
 B_{i,n}(s)=\max_{a_i}\{r_i(s,a_i,p_{-i,n}(s))+
 \delta\E[B_{i,n+1}(s')\mid s,a_i,p_{-i,n}(s)]\}.
\]
At \emph{every} backward date, the code stores $B_{i,n}(s)-J_{i,n}(s)$ for both players and all eight states. It reports the largest positive gain, the signed maximum and minimum, and the attaining date, player, capacities, and demand. The historical code had placed the comparison after the date loop, which verified only date zero. The corrected scope is executed for both the renewal regression and the persistent economy.

As a negative control, the final-period investment of player one at capacities $(1,1)$ and low demand is replaced by one. The independent routine recomputes the frozen-profile value and all best responses. It detects a gain exceeding $0.1$, at a recorded late-date node. This test would not be passed by a routine that merely reports convergence of the equilibrium solver. The reset case $s=0$, four dates, reproduces its early closed-form actions and terminal investment to numerical precision. Both examples remain exact finite-state references, not neural-equilibrium training results.
