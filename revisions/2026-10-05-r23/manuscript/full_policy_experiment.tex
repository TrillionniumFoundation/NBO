\section{Learning and Certifying Complete Capital Policies}\label{sec:r23experiment}
The experiment instantiates every term in the discrete-time policy account.
It compares a trained neural continuation with matched conventional
quadratics and a structure-exploiting dynamic program. The economic target
is a loss no greater than $10^{-4}$, uniformly for initial states with
$\|x_0\|_2^2\leq d$, against all adapted finite-cost policies over 24 dates.
The Gaussian state law is not truncated, and the action at each date ranges
over the full vector space $\R^d$.

\subsection{Primitives and prospective design}
For $d\in\{10,50\}$, let $S$ be the cyclic coordinate-shift matrix and let
$\mathbf1$ be the vector of ones. The anchor economy has
\begin{align*}
 A&=.92I+.025S+.015\mathbf1\mathbf1'/d,&B&=.20I+.005S,\\
 Q&=\operatorname{diag}\{(1+j/d)/d\}_{j=0}^{d-1},&
 R&=\operatorname{diag}\{(.40+.10j/d)/d\}_{j=0}^{d-1},\\
 Q_T&=Q,&\Sigma&=.05^2(I+.25\mathbf1\mathbf1'/d),\qquad\beta=.96.
\end{align*}
The technology regime adds $.02I$ to $A_t$ from date four onward.
The valuation regime multiplies $Q_t$ from date four onward and $Q_T$ by
$1.25$. Each regime is solved as a complete new dynamic problem; only
initializations may be carried between regimes. Current primitives remain
unchanged. Parameters are declared laboratory inputs, not estimates.

Before primary execution, the design fixed sixteen seeds, the three regimes,
the five procedures, and check budgets of 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 policy-improvement
passes. Each neural pass has 32 hidden-weight gradient updates. The
registered randomizer is uniform on the sixteen listed seeds and is
exhaustively enumerated. Its exact success probability and expected capped
evaluation, gradient-update, solve and check counts are the method-level
estimands. No success probability for an unspecified population of training
runs or economic requests is inferred. Repeated deterministic comparator
outputs are not independent scientific replications.

The stopping rule uses the direct residual certificate in
Corollary~\ref{cor:r23capital}, with the additional action-error envelope
for $\epsilon_{\rm exec}=10^{-12}$. It stops at the first successful check.
The source hashes and complete design were committed before execution;
the executable sources matched those hashes before any primary outcome.
Every attempted certificate, candidate matrix, neural factor, model array,
and failed check is retained. The trained-factor analysis in
Proposition~\ref{prop:r23factor} is a separate deterministic analysis of the
returned policies, not a post-selected modification of the stopping rule.

\subsection{Candidate generators and their information}
NBO evaluates the current linear policy, trains the square-activation scalar
critic $\|W_tx\|^2/d+c_t$, and improves the actor using its fitted
continuation. All entries of $W_t$ are trainable. The exact own-policy
population derivative-regression target is available in this structured
economy. It is not a sample label from an optimal or Riccati policy.
The conventional quadratic procedure receives the identical target and may
use its exact population minimizer. Both representations have cold and
warm variants. Warm variants carry the previous regime's candidate and,
for NBO, its hidden weights; cold variants restart. A separately timed
Riccati comparator computes the complete structural solution. Its candidate
is checked by the same outward verifier, which never calls Riccati.

The comparison does not claim a neural approximation advantage from a
change of function class: the neural factor and conventional quadratics
represent the same quadratic continuations. Nor does access to an analytic
population target establish simulation efficiency for a general NBO
implementation. The original simulation, radial-basis, direct-actor and HJB
comparisons remain in the prior evidence and retain their different targets.

\subsection{Full-policy accuracy and charged work}
All 480 complete services achieve the registered policy threshold. This
includes all 192 neural services. The 1,088 attempted checks include 608
unsuccessful checks, none of which is discarded from cost or evidence.
A failed check is an unsuccessful sufficient certificate, not proof of
excessive actual loss. Tables~\ref{tab:r23frontier} and
\ref{tab:r23streams} report both candidate accuracy and method work.

The reported service clock begins before model and candidate initialization
and includes own-policy evaluation, every fit and actor update, failed and
successful verification, compressed candidate writes, and synchronized
attempt records. Final service-ledger writes and loop overhead are separately
visible in the whole-execution account. Shared interpreter and environment
startup and publication work are not silently allocated to a favored
method. Clocks are realized measurements, not population expectations or
high-precision estimates of small timing differences. Stored parameter
bytes and the process-wide memory high-water mark are retained; the latter
is not mislabeled as a per-method memory measurement.

\subsection{Decisions under reoptimized futures}
At $x_0=-\mathbf1$, the economic contrast is the change in mean current
investment when future technology or stock valuation changes. The policy
bound controls the error in the initial action through strong convexity;
Section~\ref{sec:r23proofs} proves the resulting optimum-response enclosure.
These are responses under reoptimized futures, unlike the earlier scalar
exercise conditional on specified future policies. The comparison with
Riccati remains explicit: a sign obtained from an NBO certificate is not
thereby a sign that requires a neural representation.
