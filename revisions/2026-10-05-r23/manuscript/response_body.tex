\section*{Blocking concerns}
\subsection*{B1. Work to accuracy and adaptive reuse}
We retain the original finding that adaptive neural checking and refresh
costs more than fresh neural fitting in every changing-future cell. We have
not renamed that result as amortization success. The new experiment freezes
cold and warm policy improvement for both neural and conventional quadratic
representations. Table~\ref{tab:r23streams} reports complete three-regime
streams at a full-policy target; Table~\ref{tab:r23frontier} includes the
strong Riccati comparator. Warm continuation learning can reduce the work
relative to cold learning without establishing superiority to structure-
exploiting dynamic programming. The new evidence therefore addresses the
refresh design and policy target, but does not mark neural cost dominance
as established merely because all neural candidates certify.

\subsection*{B2. Accuracy versus economic necessity}
We agree that the earlier signs do not establish that a neural continuation
is necessary. The revised introduction, experiment and conclusion separate
verifier, candidate and candidate-generator results. The new investment
contrast is computed under fully reoptimized futures, with an action-radius
argument derived from full-policy loss. The same argument and response table
include Riccati and every conventional procedure. We retain the sharper
historical enumeration result; neither its target nor its cost is suppressed.

\subsection*{B3. Completing the dynamic policy certificate}
Theorem~\ref{thm:r23coercive} supplies a cost-weighted alternative to an
absolute sup-norm advantage bound on unbounded domains. In the executed
capital economy, the own-policy value is evaluated by a backward matrix
recursion. Completing the square encloses every vector-action deviation at
every state. Positive stage costs establish the coercive comparison against
all adapted finite-cost policies. Corollary~\ref{cor:r23capital} gives the
uniform initial-ball loss bound; Proposition~\ref{prop:r23implementation}
adds the implementation envelope. There is no class relaxation and no model
transfer in this declared discrete-time problem. These are not zeros
assigned to a diffusion approximation. The evidence now instantiates a
complete policy certificate rather than a mean scalar catalogue premise.

\subsection*{B4. Decision dimension, horizon and shock support}
The new design has 24 decision dates, correlated continuous Gaussian
innovations, unbounded state domains, and ten or fifty independently
adjustable controls. Every future policy is reoptimized within the declared
policy tolerance. This removes the finite-support and uniform-current-scalar
restrictions from the new full-policy test. The quadratic structure remains
explicit and its efficient structural solver is retained. We do not claim
that action dimension alone establishes a neural advantage or defeats every
structure-exploiting competitor.

\subsection*{B5. A statement for trained neural parameters}
Proposition~\ref{prop:r23factor} now concerns the actual trainable hidden
factor, not a fixed dictionary. It bounds the continuation error by the
own-policy gradient divided by a verified singular-value lower bound and
then bounds the actor's full-policy residual. All hidden weights are trained.
The post-execution diagnostic re-evaluates the returned policy rather than
reusing a last-iteration loss against an obsolete policy. Failed rank or
threshold diagnostics are preserved. The proposition is an a posteriori
implication for this architecture; it does not assume convergence of an
arbitrary nonconvex training procedure or assert an advantage over an
equivalent quadratic representation.

\subsection*{B6. Method-level reliability}
The registered method randomizer is uniform over sixteen listed seeds and
is exhaustively enumerated. The method estimands are its exact probability
of meeting the all-state policy target and its expected capped evaluation,
gradient-update, solve and check counts. Failures are not conditioned away.
Realized clocks remain descriptive. This is a genuine, precisely scoped
method-level statement for the declared finite randomizer, not a population
inference from sixteen observations under an unspecified randomization law.
A claim about an unrestricted population of future training runs remains
outside this design.

\subsection*{B7. The other economic applications}
The original recursive-utility, preference, temporal-self and game models
are preserved in full. Their admissible utility domains, information,
transversality and deviation requirements are not replaced by the quadratic
example. The new application instantiates the common evaluation--improvement
architecture and a complete dynamic certificate in one controlled economy.
It does not establish the same numerical frontier or equilibrium constants
for every application. The main text states this distinction explicitly;
we do not report the general-application numerical objection as resolved by
one model's policy theorem.

\subsection*{B8. Cumulative scope and the reader's argument}
The main article now brings the continuation-to-policy argument and the
complete-policy experiment together. Previously main-text nonlinear
numerical sections are reproduced, without substantive deletion, in the
Prior Numerical Evidence companion. The original technical supplement and
Economic Applications companion remain. A preservation map checks the old
source objects and labels against the pinned review baseline. This improves
the route through the argument without pretending that moving historical
material reduces its total scientific volume. No adverse cell is removed
to make the new narrative appear stronger.

\subsection*{B9. Economic interpretation}
The new tracking economy has fully specified costs, transitions, innovation
covariance and future changes. Its investment response uses reoptimized
future policies, rather than a specified exogenous future plan. It remains
a controlled laboratory, not an empirically calibrated industry or an
estimate of an observed reform. Both the model's structural solution and
neural response intervals are reported. We separate this completed dynamic
counterfactual from a claim of empirical novelty or neural necessity.

\section*{Major comments}
\subsection*{M1. Keep a coherent paper}
We retain the paper's original topic, Neural Bellman Operators. The new
results concern a learned policy-specific scalar continuation, the actor
it generates, and the economic quality and work of the resulting policy.
The verifier is used to assess that method, not to replace its subject.
The same introduction and conclusion now distinguish mathematical
certification from numerical advantages. The report's alternative title is
not adopted, and the original economic content is not arbitrarily removed.

\subsection*{M2. Every term in the policy account}
The all-state local advantage is $x'D_t'H_t^{-1}D_tx$, evaluated under the
implemented ideal policy. Coverage is the entire state space, established
algebraically rather than sampled. Discounting enters the exact evaluation
recursion and the telescoping argument. The comparison is directly with all
adapted finite-cost policies, so no policy-class approximation is introduced.
The model is the declared discrete-time economy, so no discretization or
value-transfer step is introduced. The norm-bounded action-execution class
has the explicit allowance in Proposition~\ref{prop:r23implementation}.
The numerical verifier encloses all these quantities, and the stopping
target includes the execution allowance. None is inferred from a fitting
loss or assigned by visual agreement of trajectories.

\subsection*{M3. A stronger dynamic design}
The finite innovation catalogue is replaced by a continuous Gaussian law;
the horizon and action dimension are increased and the future policies are
reoptimized. Exact Gaussian moments make the verifier independent of shock
enumeration. They also make a powerful structural comparator available,
which we retain. We do not manufacture a computational barrier by omitting
that comparator. Consequently this design completes the policy-accuracy
stress test, but is not presented as proof that simulation and conventional
methods are infeasible whenever NBO succeeds.

\subsection*{M4. Prospective estimands and chronology}
The complete randomizer, stopping budgets, models, procedures and source
hashes were fixed before primary outcomes. The execution checked the hashes
before fitting. All dimension--seed--method--regime combinations are
accounted for, including the dependence of warm regimes within a stream.
The publication replay verifies every stored candidate hash and every
stopping decision without rerunning fits or replacing measured clocks.
The gradient/rank diagnostic and response bands are identified as
deterministic analyses of those frozen objects, not new independent fits.

\subsection*{M5. Symmetric warm-start policies}
The new warm procedure carries its complete prior policy and, for NBO, its
trained hidden factor. It performs the prespecified evaluation/improvement
stages and checks the true full-policy target. The conventional quadratic
warm procedure receives the same reuse permission and information. Cold
procedures restart. All unsuccessful checks count in the recorded bill.
This is a prospectively specified comparison of different refresh choices;
it does not retroactively optimize the earlier adaptive rule or omit its
unfavorable cost record.

\subsection*{M6. Contribution-isolating work}
The new tables separate evaluation passes, gradient updates, vector-action
linear solves, checks, and realized service clocks. Stored parameter bytes
and the process-wide memory high-water mark are retained with their proper
scope. The clocks include initialization, all attempted fitting and
verification, and durable candidate and attempt writes; service-ledger and
other loop overhead are separately reported. Shared startup and publication
work are not silently charged to one procedure. The new analytic-target
experiment is not mislabeled a simulation-transition benchmark. The original
SAA, direct-actor, radial-basis and HJB studies remain available with their
own targets. A new matched full-policy comparison against all those
procedures, and per-method peak-memory profiling, are not claimed here.

\subsection*{M7. Candidate and construction are different claims}
The abstract, introduction, full-policy section, result interpretation and
conclusion now make this distinction explicitly. The direct residual
corollary is a verifier result. A successful stopping record is a candidate
result. The finite-randomizer reliability and work tables are method
results for the declared procedures. The trainable-factor proposition adds
an architecture-specific sufficient account, but not a theorem of universal
neural superiority. Each reported empirical advantage is restricted to its
actual comparator and target.

\subsection*{M8. Future-policy interpretation}
The earlier nonlinear future-change study is still described as a current
decision conditional on specified future policies. In the new quadratic
study, all dates' actions are reoptimized in every regime and certified
against the full adapted class. The initial-action radius follows from
full-policy loss relative to the optimal future. The two economic
interpretations are not conflated.

\subsection*{M9. Organization and preservation}
The current main article has one central continuation-to-policy chain,
followed by the original NBO construction and the new full-policy design.
The prior detailed nonlinear comparisons have an explicit companion and
cross-references rather than being interleaved as a revision genealogy.
All inherited scientific components and labels remain accessible, with a
machine-checked preservation map. This is a substantive reorganization,
not a claim that the entire retained research archive has become short.

\subsection*{M10. Evidence standards}
The source freeze, immutable candidate arrays, failed checks, finite versus
full-policy distinctions, and exact identity records are retained. The
publication contains native Econometrica-class sources, generated tables,
replay and unit-test scripts, compilation diagnostics and a source manifest.
The original 1,088 attempted candidates remain on a separate evidence
branch as well as in the integrated revision ancestry. Nothing in this
response relies on renaming an unresolved comparison as a positive result.

\section*{Summary}
The revision supplies an executed full-policy application and a proof linking
an actually trained neural factor to that policy target. It strengthens the
original NBO paper without changing its subject or discarding its history.
It also keeps the central numerical distinction visible: a successful
neural policy and a complete economic certificate do not by themselves
establish a cost advantage over the strongest alternatives.
