\begin{abstract}
This paper develops Neural Bellman Operators for constructive policy evaluation and feasible improvement. A witness-preserving continuation connects approximation resources to the original economic optimum. Qualification-optimal fixed-feature readouts have exact primal--dual certificates, and verified action comparisons permit trainable proposals without assuming successful unrestricted optimization. We connect the attained controllers to original-law costs through fitted-continuation identities and a finite acquired-policy displacement bound. The latter accommodates discontinuous actors without an unknown policy-value oracle. Predictable certificates support optional stopping; a separate, prospectively fixed experiment evaluates four constructed controllers in the nonlinear investment economy. Matched ReLU controllers have strictly lower expected costs than the declared Bernstein comparators at both resolutions. Seven of eight paired intervals meet the specified precision target. Complete numerical reconstruction and explicit qualification charges support a controller-adoption decision, while retained scalar, vector and reuse evidence identifies the scope of the comparison.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction65}
A numerical continuation matters because it helps produce an economic decision. Its fitting error, the loss of the implemented policy and the work required to establish that loss are different objects. Improving a numerical bound may leave a policy unchanged; improving a policy may leave a conservative bound unchanged. A computational method should connect its approximation choices to a feasible decision under the original economic law, and should account for the resources used to construct and qualify that decision.

We develop Neural Bellman Operators (NBOs) along this chain. The constructive implementation evaluates each continuation against its own fitted future and retains feasible action witnesses. Its affine--ReLU realization and primitive error budget do not require unrestricted nonconvex training to succeed. A complementary recursive implementation queries the original Bellman problem where its action search needs information. A trained readout can propose an action, but verified lower and upper bounds determine whether the returned policy meets the original-optimum contract. A proposed neural action, a constructed continuation and an original-value witness have distinct roles.

The nonlinear investment economy makes these distinctions concrete. State-dependent capacity, bilinear transitions and continuous innovations enter both the Bellman calculation and implementation. Primitive curvature controls the transition's convexity defect and supplies regularity of the true value. A continuous-action certificate can therefore avoid a stored state lattice. A strong-convexity upper chord can certify a rounded proposal before another endpoint query; protected conventional refinement remains available otherwise. The fixed-feature readout and its attained optimization gap make that proposal problem constructive. Neither a fitted continuation nor an arbitrary proposed action is assumed convex.

The continuation itself requires a second qualification. A frozen fitted NBO continuation supplies original-law cost observations whose range is controlled by residual oscillation and the implemented greedy gaps. State-independent shifts cancel, and a terminal correction preserves the identity when the fitted terminal value is inexact. For shallow affine--ReLU continuations, conditional expectations under the original one- and two-innovation laws are finite positive-part moment calculations. A state-and-action cover qualifies their residuals without querying the optimal Bellman tree. This is a proved connection between approximation, integration and a policy's original-optimum account, not a substitution of sampled training loss for a uniform guarantee.

\paragraph{An attained fit and its actual policy cost.}
The residual-cover account determines a finite fitting objective. Conditional on the actual continuation returned at the next date, a linear program minimizes this objective over a fixed feature class. Rational readouts and probability multipliers certify the achieved primal--dual gap. Backward construction therefore yields an attained continuation and an implementable policy, with the fitting, covering, integration and verification work made explicit. A matched conventional basis receives the same construction rule and coefficient budget; its feature moduli and spanning space remain its own.

The new result connects these attained controllers to an identified economic comparison. A finite acquired-policy displacement modulus, computed from the complete action maps and primitive transition and cost moduli, bounds the difference of two coupled path costs. Closed-cell comparisons accommodate actor discontinuities and the actual boundary convention. The calculation uses neither an unknown policy value nor realized evaluation outcomes. Once the finite actors have been returned, direct original-law simulation requires no optimal-value descendants. The cost of obtaining those actors and the tensor cover used in their construction remain separate, paid operations.

In a prospectively fixed comparison, the two-date ReLU controllers have lower expected cost than the declared Bernstein controllers at both acquisition resolutions. The difference is identified by direct continuous-law paired intervals, not by a better readout objective. All four fitted controllers also improve on zero investment. Seven of eight paired intervals meet the prespecified width target $1/256$; the remaining interval identifies improvement but misses that precision target. The design was not extended after this outcome. Refinement within either basis yields smaller gains lying inside the equivalence band, illustrating that a strict ranking and economic equivalence at a declared scale can coexist.

Every new policy-path enclosure is reconstructed through a separate arbitrary-precision integer evaluator. The production calculation uses outward binary64 arithmetic; the independent calculation uses an expanded transition, separate acquired-action ranges and fresh cumulative endpoints. Statistical means, variances and confidence radii are calculated from the independently reconstructed endpoints. The entire continuous bin is enclosed in both routes. A clean repetition reproduces the complete arrays and exact intervals without creating another independent dataset.

The empirical ranking is conditional on the declared fixed-feature constructions. It does not identify an intrinsic advantage of every neural encoding. The earlier recursive-policy experiments identify equivalence at their stated margin, not strict neural superiority, and their complete cold-start records do not support a uniform neural work advantage. Those policies differ from the newly evaluated table controllers. Their adverse observations remain part of the evidence rather than being replaced by a favorable comparison of different objects.

\paragraph{Qualification, reuse and the economic decision.}
A numerical comparison also has a stopping and accounting contract. A fixed-sample interval does not justify inspection until a favorable sign appears. Predictable original-law certificates support optional stopping while distinguishing a fixed complete controller's cost from the conditional average of changing controllers. A failed numerical episode consumes its previously qualified support and attempted work, instead of disappearing after its outcome is observed. The new fixed-sample experiment uses a separate declared family and is not retrospectively assigned an anytime interpretation.

Computational reuse addresses a different cost. An original-Bellman lower bound and feasible upper witness can be transported to a nearby state after paying for state displacement, capacity repair and action rounding. An inconclusive reuse check invokes the original recursive verifier. The policy guarantee survives history-dependent selection among valid records, but does not imply equality with the fresh-search policy. A finite-cover theorem states the tighter-fill and retention premises needed to bound expensive fills. The executed bounded cache is assessed using its actual hits, misses, invalidations and descendant work, not premises it does not automatically satisfy.

The economic endpoint is a controller-adoption decision. A negative incremental-cost interval supports a positive range of replacement and qualification charges, amortized over an explicitly declared number of deployments. Processor seconds require an explicit conversion into cost units; qualification rows do not determine the deployment count. The new identified gains give a nonempty adoption region in the normalized investment model. They are not calibrated installation prices, an estimated welfare effect or a cost certificate for a different cached controller. The original information-acquisition and economic-choice results remain available under their stated hypotheses.

\paragraph{Relation to existing methods.}
Lipschitz extension and distance transforms underlie the constructive envelope \citep{mcshane1934,felzenszwalb2012}. Approximate and safe policy iteration already have error and performance analyses \citep{munos2003,pirotta2013,scherrer2015}; \citet{yang2020convex} develops convex dynamic-programming approximation. Learned warm starts and optimization-based control have their own numerical contracts \citep{sakaue2023warm,sambharya2023,agrawal2020control}. We connect those choices to an acquired feasible policy, an attained qualification objective, a valid comparison and the cost of returning the controller. The common verifier is not described as an exclusively neural idea.

Value-function control variates and telescoping identities are established evaluation tools \citep{munos2006variance,jiang2016}. Here residual qualification supplies both an original-optimum account and a valid range for original-law observations. The acquired-policy displacement construction supplies an alternative range from complete finite actors, including their discontinuities. The empirical-Bernstein inequality is due to \citet{maurer2009}; our contribution specifies the numerical endpoints, independently checked supports and implemented-policy target to which it is applied. Time-uniform concentration through nonnegative supermartingales is established \citep{howard2020time}, as are off-policy confidence sequences \citep{karampatziakis2021}. We use those principles without claiming them as new.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion65}
Neural Bellman Operators connect the construction of a continuation to a feasible decision under the original economic law. A witness-preserving implementation has an explicit own-future error account. Verified action comparisons admit trained proposals without allowing fitting error to alter the original-optimum certificate. Qualification-optimal continuation readouts give an attained numerical objective and an exact optimization certificate rather than an assumption that unrestricted training succeeds.

The new direct qualification result completes a further step in that chain. A finite displacement calculation bounds coupled path costs even when acquired policies are discontinuous. Independently reconstructed original-law observations then identify lower costs for the declared ReLU continuations relative to their matched Bernstein controls. The positive adoption region follows from those actual-cost intervals and explicitly charged qualification, not from the residual bound alone. One declared precision target is missed and is retained as such.

Construction, qualification and deployment remain distinct resource accounts. Nonrecursive evaluation of a returned actor does not make its construction free. A verified reuse hit removes descendants, but a first fill or later miss retains the original horizon cost. Changing a complete controller or its memory/reset contract changes the cost target. The retained vector, reuse, equivalence and cold-start studies therefore continue to delimit the demonstrated scaling and comparative claims. The paper's constructive policy-evaluation and feasible-improvement objective is unchanged; the present results add an attained-fit--implemented-policy--identified-cost connection with complete mathematical and numerical premises.
