## M9 / B9. An identified controller-adoption decision

The new actual-cost comparison makes the policy-quality term informative. For the sixteen-cell ReLU controller, the simultaneous lower gain over zero investment exceeds $0.164484$ in normalized loss units. Its lower gain over the matched Bernstein controller exceeds $0.074459$, with these displayed bounds rounded downward.

With intended deployment count $D$, value per unit of normalized loss $\lambda$, priced one-off construction, qualification and installation cost $K_k$, and incremental per-deployment cost $d_k$, the net-benefit interval is

$$[D(\lambda l_k-d_k)-K_k,\ D(\lambda u_k-d_k)-K_k].$$

A positive lower endpoint supports adoption. A negative upper endpoint rules it out; otherwise the purchase comparison is unresolved. The same simultaneous event supports the whole parameter region, without selecting a favorable fee as the only reported result. The lower bounds give a nonempty region for beneficial adoption and show precisely how additional computation must be paid for.

The economic units and prices remain explicit external inputs. No market installation charge, monetary conversion or calibrated welfare magnitude has been estimated. The positive result is an identified policy-cost difference and the resulting priced adoption region in the original model, not an invented empirical calibration. Construction, verification and deployment are distinguished rather than treating a processor second as a monetary unit.
