## Frozen design

Use the retained R67 kernel66.py protected Bellman oracle, with its unchanged R64/R63 and constructive economic dependencies. Fit on 128 independently generated states in dimension 2 and 128 in dimension 8, using PCG64 seed 202610138901 and states from Generator.random on [0,1)^d. All have two remaining dates. Feasible action labels are generated by protected adaptive search at tolerance 1/256 and divided by the original feasible cap. No dimension-4 training state is used. These are seeded numerical workloads, not evidence of randomized deployment in an observed economy.

The common feature vector has 14 coordinates: eight zero-padded state coordinates, the overall mean, even-coordinate mean, odd-coordinate mean, mean square, mean adjacent-coordinate product and d/8. Compare one shared width-32 ReLU MLP with one shared complete quadratic polynomial on precisely these features. The ReLU uses MLPRegressor, lbfgs, alpha=1e-5, max_iter=1000, max_fun=15000, tol=1e-8, random_state=89. The polynomial uses ridge coefficient 1e-6, with an unpenalized intercept. All fitting warnings and exceptions remain recorded; an invalid predictor is retained and uses a documented midpoint proposal, never an omitted arm. There is one attempted fit per family and no hyperparameter selection.

Before generating calibration states, save complete training states, labels, coefficients and a model hash. The five services are adaptive, bisection, analytic one-date proposal, shared ReLU and shared polynomial, in that tie-breaking order. Each is evaluated with the same protected oracle at tolerance 1/64. An optional proposal supplies only a query/action candidate: the original-law bracket is the sole acceptance authority.

Calibration uses 64 fresh states in each of dimensions 2 and 8, seed 202610138902. Choose the service minimizing the sum of q_queries over the paired calibration catalogue. q_queries already includes terminal_queries: its disjoint decomposition is nonterminal=q_queries-terminal_queries plus terminal_queries. No terminal query is counted twice in this selector. Freeze the selected service and calibration hash before generating evaluation states.

Evaluation uses 128 fresh states in each of dimensions 2, 4 and 8, seed 202610138903. Execute every service on exactly the same states, with three fresh-process repetitions per dimension and service. Cycle service order across repetitions. A process includes interpreter startup, loading, computation, serialization and shutdown. Preserve all repetitions, machine identity, package versions, peak process RSS, and per-workload oracle counters, screen records, protected refinements, zero-progress probes, feasibility repairs, invalid predictions and exceptions. A failed service is charged infinity for empirical selection rather than omitted. ## Resource and inference conventions

Report disjoint oracle work next to model arithmetic, payload bytes and complete process time. The model arithmetic account is the explicitly declared dense arithmetic implementation of the saved coefficients; it is not a hardware instruction counter. Interval-operation counts and energy are not measured by these counters and must not be filled with zeros. Peak RSS is process capacity, not additive computation. A cross-machine replay uses the very same saved coefficients and states, does not refit, and does not increase the independent workload count. Compare hashes and retain any machine disagreements.

There are 256 training states, 128 calibration states and 384 evaluation states. Three repetitions and two machines do not multiply these numbers. Report paired finite-catalogue work differences and all recorded timing ranges. Do not turn three timing repetitions into an unsupported asymptotic confidence claim. Any probabilistic population statement relies on the separately proved sampling assumptions, not merely on pseudorandom seeds. This study tests query proposals and certificate work, not complete-policy economic superiority; that is the separate inherited R85 original-law evaluation with its own margin and simultaneous error allocation.
