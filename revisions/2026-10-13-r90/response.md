The restrictions of the first-date actionwise theorem remain explicit at the start of the recursive extension. That theorem fixes its finite partition, finite menu and subsequent continuation. Section `pattern89`, now an active main section, disaggregates complete contingent patterns before relaxing occupations. Each pattern prescribes actions even at unvisited cells. Its projection lies in the earlier valid conflict-flow relaxation, so the new bounds cannot be wider. With all attainable patterns and qualification witnesses, exact finite-state inputs give actual class extrema. For continuous laws, the stated approximation error separately charges advantage and transition enclosures. The proof does not assume that a coarsened continuous-state process is Markov.

R90 adds `transport90` for a limitation of that convergence account: nearby deterministic successors can have total-variation distance one. If the stored successor continuation is K-Lipschitz and the transition approximation has Wasserstein distance at most e, its one-step action-value discrepancy is at most beta K e plus the separately certified arithmetic allowance. Backward comparison of both the implemented complete policy and the original optimum with the same continuation gives the full policy bound. The result holds at every original initial state, for every qualified common parameter, with actions and induced distributions changing at every date. It requires neither a fixed future controller nor continuity of the greedy policy.

A quantized successor supplies a coupling certificate even for mutually singular kernels. The added transition charge is twice the sum of the discounted continuation moduli times the quantization distances. A stationary discounted corollary is also proved. These are constructive additions to the full-scope program; finite-horizon or stationary existence and regularity assumptions are stated, not inferred from a simulation.

The expectation proof is not applied without modification to nonlinear preferences or games. Cash-invariant monotone evaluations use a separately established numerical discrepancy and the signed recursion. General Lipschitz aggregators use their own absolute-error moduli. Games use fixed-opponent best-response comparisons; temporal selves remain distinct decision makers. An approximate unilateral incentive certificate is not an equilibrium-existence theorem.
