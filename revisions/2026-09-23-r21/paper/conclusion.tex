\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:r21conclusion}
The new stochastic policies establish three different facts with different tests: strict expert-payoff improvement, uniform initial-to-final mixture improvement, and useful optimality bounds on a continuum of initial states and adjustment prices. The Jensen-gap argument is needed for the second fact; it cannot be replaced by four successful expert updates. The martingale-price decoder is needed for admissibility; it cannot be replaced by a sampled wealth trajectory. The unrestricted dual is needed for optimality; it cannot be replaced by policy comparisons alone.

These additions advance the original neural computation program without discarding its difficult evidence. They concern newly trained, genuinely stochastic neural policy generators, not a renamed deterministic library. They also retain the strong direct stochastic comparator, which supplies similarly sharp local accuracy without hidden layers and without a demonstrated neural speed disadvantage being concealed. The older complete-domain feedback certificates, continuation-trace obstruction, and high-dimensional correction results keep their stated scopes.

The all-domain $.01$ feedback objective is unchanged. Extending the present domain requires an admissible policy system and a sharp comparison object covering additional initial states and starting times, together with a matched global classical frontier. The present manuscript supplies complete, reproducible regional theorems and calculations for that program; it does not replace the global objective with an assertion that it has already been attained.
