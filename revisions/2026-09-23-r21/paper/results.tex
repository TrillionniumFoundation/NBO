The confirmatory design fixes seeds 20100--20104, the four vertices of $K$, Adam step size $.005$, and retained budgets $0,100,400,1000$. The exploratory seed 20000 is excluded. \subsection{The neural and classical cost frontier}
The conventional stochastic comparator directly optimizes sixteen triples of coefficients in \eqref{eq:r21cons}, with no hidden neural layers. It uses L-BFGS-B, the same conditional-price decoder, the same four initial states, and precisely the same payoff and dual checks. The retained budgets are $0,10,50,200$, with convergence recorded when earlier. This is a matched \emph{local} continuous-time accuracy comparison, not a surrogate comparison on a different discrete economy.

The direct stochastic method reaches $.005$ at ten retained iterations for each expert and has final $K$-uniform bound $\RXXIClassical$. At the first $.005$ hit, neural seed 20100 uses $1.107$ seconds for proposal generation and $15.036$ seconds for the accumulated binary interval checks, or $16.143$ seconds in total after outward rounding. The direct method uses $.153$ and $15.360$ seconds, respectively, or $15.512$ seconds. All five seed-specific ledgers, target hits and misses, and final wall times appear in the supplement. Different optimizer iterations are not equated: the comparison is made at the common checked tolerance.

These are recorded costs conditional on access to the common optimal upper. A cold-start ledger additionally charges the full $415.789$ seconds of the inherited twenty-node dual-library construction, or explicitly declares its reuse. The final twenty-expert MPFR audit and R21 replays are additional publication-validation costs, not silently free checks. Recorded neural peak process memory is retained. The R20 direct-stochastic run did not record peak RSS; its 192 scalar coefficients must not be substituted for process memory. Consequently, the data support a local accuracy frontier and an explicit cost ledger, but neither a complete global MC/SL frontier nor a neural memory or speed advantage.

\subsection{A policy-sensitive stochastic improvement}
Three separately executed deterministic SLSQP transcriptions, with 16, 32, and 64 time slabs, receive their own supporting affine payoff upper bounds on $K$. These are upper bounds for the named policies, not for the optimal value. The neural mixtures' lower bounds exceed each such affine upper by at least $\RXXIDeterministicGain$ throughout $K$. This comparison orders actual stopped payoffs without changing the evaluator to favor the neural policy. It establishes an improvement over these three deterministic policies; the sharper and faster direct stochastic comparator remains visible and precludes a blanket neural-superiority claim.

\subsection{Budget-feasible welfare compensation for the learned policies}
For every final neural expert, retain the learned slopes and preference drifts and raise only its consumption intercept by a common budget offset. Finance the resulting consumption stream from initial wealth $x_i+.002$, and verify the exact residual reserve again using \eqref{eq:r21reserve}. The twenty compensated-policy lower bounds exceed the original, uncompensated optimal-value upper bounds by at least $\RXXIWealthGain$ after continuum transfer. Thus each learned mixture, provided with an additional $.002$ of initial wealth, achieves at least $V_2^*(u,x)$ for every $(u,x)\in K$. The sufficient compensation is $.16$ percent at reference wealth $1.25$, and less than $.162$ percent throughout $K$. It is neither the minimum compensating variation nor an external unfinanced transfer. Unlike the historical $.0078$ result for a deterministic library, this statement concerns the new learned stochastic policies.

\subsection{Relation to the historical whole-domain experiments}
The R18 complete-domain feedback results remain 7.278--7.344 against the unchanged $.01$ target. Their final policy-side residual component is zero for all ten accessibility-aware seeds; the remaining bound is the positive comparison component. The all-seed actor--critic factorial, signed decompositions and trace-floor trajectories are retained below and in the supplement. A smaller historical bound cannot identify actor progress.

The new evaluator does not apply another interval wrapper to those failed actors or critics. It evaluates newly trained stochastic consumption policies and uses a separate localized unrestricted dual upper. Its success on $K$ is therefore compatible with the historical fixed-critic floor above 6.27. It does not prove that the historical critic has learned the correct continuation trace, or supply a sharp supersolution on the whole cylinder. The retained one-sided continuation targets provide constructive information for that separate global problem.

The nonlinear high-dimensional control material is likewise preserved with its actual attribution. The initialization-independent strongly-convex theorem is not evidence that training is necessary. The subsequent learned-gradient theorem and 32-query matched-tolerance experiment do identify a limited reduction in online gradient work, including 31 of 32 paired queries at dimension 128 and tolerance $.01$. They do not establish a same-hardware amortized speedup, performance for all cube states from those 32 queries, or a broad stochastic high-dimensional advantage.
