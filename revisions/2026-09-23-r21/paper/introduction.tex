\section{Introduction}\label{sec:r21intro}
A useful numerical solution of a dynamic economic model must improve decisions, not merely the function used to evaluate them. This distinction is particularly important when a neural policy and a value witness are fitted jointly. A smaller upper bound on policy loss need not mean that the policy has improved: the same policy may receive a better witness. We therefore separate the generation of a policy, the verification of its own payoff, and comparison with the unrestricted optimum.

This paper develops Neural Bellman Operators with that separation. The original consumption--portfolio economy permits costly adjustment of preferences, correlated preference and financial shocks, and stopping at the boundary of the economic state space. Its primitives, admissible controls, stopping rule, and accuracy objective are unchanged. The new solver generates genuinely stochastic consumption and a nonzero portfolio. Neural coefficients determine an adapted consumption rule; an explicit contingent-claim price finances the rule and determines its portfolio. Independently enclosed stopped payoffs, rather than a changing critic, decide whether a proposed policy is accepted.

There are three substantive additions. First, we give a continuous-time self-financing and admissibility theorem for the executed neural policies. The decoder is not a discretized wealth recursion: its conditional-price identity holds between decision dates and for every realization of the traded factor. Second, we prove a continuum transfer theorem for four expert policies. Concavity of running utility alone is not enough to order two mixtures, so we also bound the old mixture's Jensen gain. This supplies an independently verified improvement in actual policy value at every initial state in a rectangle. Third, we combine policy-specific lower bounds with the previously verified unrestricted dual upper bound. This proves useful neural accuracy without requiring the inaccurate historical feedback critic to serve as the evaluator.

Five confirmatory seeds, four experts per seed, and four retained work levels were fixed before the R20 execution. All sixty noninitial expert proposals pass a policy-specific strict acceptance test before further training. The present revision supplies their missing manuscript, the continuum arguments, a price-sensitivity theorem, and independent re-execution of the frozen-policy checks. It does not relabel those R20 optimization runs as new random R21 experiments. At time zero and adjustment price $k=2$, every final neural mixture has regret below $.002909$ uniformly on
\[
 K=[1.98,2.02]\times[1.24,1.26].
\]
The final policies improve actual payoff over their corresponding initial mixtures by at least $\RXXIGain$ throughout $K$. Consequently, true regret is reduced by more than $85.8$ percent at every point of this rectangle. A further, explicitly post-training calculation keeps the policies fixed and proves a regret bound of $\RXXIPrice$ throughout $K\times[1.5,2.5]$.

The comparison is not made easy for the neural method. A direct, non-neural stochastic-control transcription is evaluated with the same four-state checker, unrestricted optimal upper bound, and continuum theorem. It reaches the same $.005$ tolerance and is faster in the recorded generation-plus-verification ledger. The five neural policies nevertheless have payoff lower bounds strictly above the policy-specific upper bounds of each of three named deterministic time-control transcriptions, uniformly on $K$. This identifies an economically consequential stochastic policy improvement without asserting superiority over all conventional solvers. A budget-financed wealth increment of $.002$ suffices for each learned mixture to attain at least the original optimum's upper bound, at the uncompensated initial wealth, throughout $K$.

The domain of each assertion matters. A theorem uniform on $K$ at time zero is not a theorem for all initial states of $D$ and all starting times. The outstanding all-domain feedback target remains $.01$. Neither the localized dual comparison nor the new pricing decoder is presented as a globally sharp continuation supersolution. The earlier continuation-trace results, complete-domain failures, signed residual decompositions, actor--critic interventions, and nonlinear high-dimensional experiments remain in the paper and supplement. They are indispensable controls on the interpretation of the new results, rather than observations to remove after a successful localized experiment.
