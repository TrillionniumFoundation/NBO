\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction65}
A learned continuation is economically useful when it changes what can be computed and which decisions can be justified under the original law. Neural Bellman Operators (NBOs) connect a constructed continuation, its feasible action witnesses and the complete policy actually implemented. The central comparison is
\[
 \underline V\leq V^*\leq J^{\widehat\pi}\leq\overline J.
\]
The lower inequality covers every original feasible action; the upper inequality includes observation, numerical action realization and the controller's subsequent choices. A guarantee for a fitted surrogate is not a substitute for this comparison.

Our first contribution is constructive. Each dated NBO is formed against its own already attained future, with explicit primitive state and action covers, integration allowances and feasible action owners. The affine--ReLU realization does not assume successful unrestricted nonconvex training. Original-owner compilation preserves the continuation and its feasible witness while changing evaluation work. Joint-domain and common-readout extensions retain the coupling between decisions at different acquired states. Their hypotheses and full statements remain available in the accompanying material.

Our second contribution is an original-law qualification account for complete policies. Under recursive risk, centering the original conditional dual at the stored continuation retains the penalty for changing the maximizing risk density. State-dependent subsolutions and supersolutions then compare the implemented policy with the original optimum. The continuous investment construction derives the required curvature, value supports and all-action inequalities from the original technology. Feasible risk witnesses minorize the lower action problem, and full-law envelopes dominate the policy value. These inequalities are valid for any supplied continuation satisfying their assumptions. Neural construction, representation quality, certificate validity and computational gain are therefore separate questions.

Our third contribution concerns economically useful precision. The preceding investment experiment established equivalence at a broad normalized tolerance; its intervals did not resolve small policy differences. We derive a convex-order AVaR enclosure with width proportional to the inverse square of innovation resolution, without requiring a differentiable value function or a fixed risk maximizer. Coupled with a complete admission-and-fallback policy, this gives a deterministic support for paired policy differences. The numerical and statistical budgets can consequently be chosen together before evaluation. The new margin is one percent of the largest first-date investment charge, eighty times smaller than the preceding margin, and the prospective simultaneous half-width budget is less than half that margin.

We test this account on a new independently generated initial-state stream. ReLU, projected-quadratic and adaptive spline continuations have the same six-dimensional design rank, labels and 128-byte executable payload. Their feature searches and all attempted fits are charged. The conventional online risk-DP comparator receives the same admission rule. Each fitted service retains rejected proposals and pays for its fallback; it is not relabeled as the unchanged R95 actor. The protocol and then the actual fitted models and calibration selector are committed before the confirmation stream is opened. All pairwise results, including unfavorable or unresolved comparisons, remain in the experiment.

The initial-state estimand is explicitly the mean under a uniform 20-bit dyadic state distribution. The state domain for construction and the subsequent innovation law remain continuous. Uniform residual qualification and pointwise first-date policy admission have different quantifiers. Repeated timing processes are not new economic observations. A separately coded rational implementation reconstructs stored proposals and original-law bounds on a random stratified audit, while independent exact arithmetic checks simultaneous inference on every paired row. Neither this audit nor the fixed-rank comparison is presented as a full independent learning-stack or maximal-vector replication.

The final link is a purchase decision. We separate one-time construction and audit charges from deployment work, and derive the region of workload counts and externally supplied computation prices for which a qualified service improves total cost. A policy-quality requirement remains an additional inequality. Fresh-process timings include startup, loading, checking, evaluation, fallback, output and shutdown, and their observed ranges support conditional sensitivity calculations rather than unmeasured guarantees on future machines.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Lipschitz extension and distance transforms provide established construction tools \citep{mcshane1934,felzenszwalb2012}. Approximate policy iteration, safe improvement and convex dynamic programming have established comparison results \citep{munos2003,pirotta2013,scherrer2015,yang2020convex}. The NBO construction retains the actual own-future approximation and feasible action owner throughout its primitive accuracy account. A native and a neural encoding of the same function receive the same economic comparison.

Risk-averse Bellman equations and feature-based risk learning are established research directions \citep{ruszczynski2010risk,ruszczynski2014erratum,ruszczynskiyang2025}. We do not claim elementary dual centering or the AVaR threshold identity as new. The new precision result combines convex quadrature order \citep{alomari2016hermite} with that identity \citep{RockafellarUryasev2000}, and connects its executable interval to complete-policy admission and prespecified resolving power. The concentration step is Hoeffding's inequality \citep{hoeffding1963}, not a new concentration theorem. The comparison table in Section~\ref{sec:comparison95} locates the retained qualification results relative to their established mathematical tools.

\paragraph{Organization.}
The article presents own-future construction, primitive accuracy, complete-policy and recursive-risk qualification, continuous investment construction, decision-scale precision and the new frozen comparison. The Technical Supplement provides the complete proofs. The Numerical and Development Appendix retains the previous studies, joint-domain and deployment results, singular-transition analysis and adverse vector-scaling evidence as complete sections. Original diffusions, endogenous preferences, temporal selves and games retain their companion statements and assumptions. Historical content is preserved, not used as evidence that a different new empirical question has been answered.
