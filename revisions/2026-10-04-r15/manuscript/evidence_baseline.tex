\section{Computational Design and the Existing Comparison}\label{sec:r15design}
The numerical question is whether the learned evaluation block improves the economic accuracy or complete work of the NBO map. All comparisons identify the economic model, feasible actions, evaluation target, selected policy, final payoff law, and work boundaries. A policy-specific confidence interval conditions on its fitted objects. A statement about a training method additionally specifies the distribution of training randomness and its sampling uncertainty. An interval containing zero establishes neither superiority nor practical equivalence.

\subsection{The executed capital comparison}
The source-bound reference study contains ninety primary fitted policies in dimensions ten, twenty, and fifty. Its primary fitting design uses an announced twenty-second budget, completes the current iteration, and selects the checkpoint using validation data. Each final primary row uses 8,192 independent paths and 1,024 time cells. The comparison concerns a fixed nine-profile population and a separate origin calculation. The simultaneous inference construction covers the declared finite family of selected policies and profiles.

Every fitted policy in the reported primary schedule comparisons has a positive simultaneous lower gain endpoint. For the prespecified population, all thirty NBO policies have this property. These are improvements over the analytical schedule; the direct method comparisons have a different conclusion. Table~\ref{tab:r15referenceevidence} retains the findings that motivate the current evaluation experiment.

The negative sample means do not prove that Raw dominates NBO, because the simultaneous direct intervals remain inconclusive. The mechanism comparisons nevertheless provide no positive evidence for the fitted critic in that design. They cannot be converted into a favorable mechanism result by observing that the common verifier accepts NBO policies. Likewise, the fixed-work experiment matches simulator visits and checkpoints but has different optimizer update counts; it does not establish equal total computation.

\subsection{Accuracy objects and economic scale}
The original finite-population NBO regret upper bounds are approximately 0.02097, 0.02847 and 0.03039 in dimensions ten, twenty and fifty. The corresponding mean schedule improvements are approximately 0.00147, 0.00135 and 0.00161. The regret bounds combine the analytical anchor gap with the measured improvement. They are reported as absolute upper bounds, without interpreting them as tight high-dimensional HJB errors.

The independent scalar computation uses a monotone implicit HJB scheme and full-box action maximization on four original grids. At initial log capital zero, the finest domain-six NBO loss is about $3.44\times10^{-6}$, compared with $1.16\times10^{-4}$ for direct policy optimization. At initial state one, the corresponding losses are about $1.05\times10^{-4}$ and $5.02\times10^{-5}$. The ordering is therefore state dependent. These are finite-grid losses of Markov feedbacks in the scalar model; the widening experiment changes both domain and spatial spacing. The residual, refinement and five-state accounts remain in Appendix~\ref{app:r15diagnostics}.

The balance-sheet profiles and the resource fee in \eqref{eq:r12fee} are transparent model experiments. They are not an empirical calibration. The fee transformation reports what administration cost a verified utility gain can support under the specified log-felicity resource account. It does not provide an additional independent source of evidence for the critic.

\subsection{Work and comparison semantics}\label{sec:r15work}
The original time-budget and fixed-work records retain fitting clocks, simulator visits, optimizer updates, peak process memory, selected checkpoints and verification components. Their verification clock begins after weight loading and deterministic-constant construction. Their prefix frontier is retrospective. Those quantities remain useful descriptions of the original computation, with those boundaries.

The current method-level comparison specifies its training-randomness estimand, economic target, actual stopping rule, complete cost boundary, and final inferential object before its confirmatory evaluation. It must charge failed checks and retain unattained targets. A direct HJB or policy-iteration comparator isolates the role of a separately learned actor and continuation object under a shared model and verifier. A classical low-dimensional method serves as a policy generator as well as an independent reference. The following study gives the concrete protocol and its executed results.
