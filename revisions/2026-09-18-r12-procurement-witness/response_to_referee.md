**Response target:** `reviews/2026-09-18-econometrica-r11-service-certificate-audit/referee_report.md`, review commit `442b009379402df7da354eacb4ff8f82d44cdce7`, assessing scientific revision `3ab1ee131ee246fe1353ad3115451d0b72b60272`.

**New revision:** R12, *Neural Bellman Operators*. The report is an owner-commissioned advisory assessment, not an Econometrica editorial decision. This response does not purport to predict acceptance or replace further independent review.

## Overview

We accept the distinction between a genuine conditional result and a completed economic choice. We have not relabeled the previously priced implementation frontier as unrestricted procurement. The new main paper starts with an explicit purchaser choosing the initial mandate, fee capacity, compulsory term and participation grant jointly, with subsequent operation and surrender chosen by the agent. It then supplies a value-to-service theorem robust to ties, a complete finite-menu comparison, an opportunity-exposure argument, and a canonical-input/witness-reconstruction chain. The prior dynamic transport, joint regional, implementation, operator and extension results are retained in the supplement and complete compendium.

The new economic result does not force a financial reversal where procurement does not exhibit one. Rather, the purchaser selects the positive mandate in both regimes, while adjustment changes the chosen compulsory term. This substantive distinction preserves the agent reversal as a correctly conditional economic result and resolves the purchaser's previously omitted service margin by optimizing it.

## E1: allocation selection, delivered service, and participation

**Comment.** The prior proposition prices implementation of a previously selected allocation. Even a two-mandate purchaser requires service and participation comparisons, not only the agent's value difference.

**Revision.** Main Sections 2–4 and Supplement S.15 specify the timing, public information and contractible menu. Each regime has 96 offers: two first-interval mandates, six standardized capacities (`0, .2, .4, .6, .8, 1`), and eight compulsory terms. The purchaser also has a no-offer option. The permissions are public fixed risk standards, not instruments silently optimized or ignored. Capacity cost is borne by the agent in a separate numeraire, term cost by the purchaser, and fees accrue to an external sector. Grants do not change managed wealth. The positive-part participation formula is retained.

The service price `b` is distinct from the agent's operating benefit `d`. The new service proposition derives bounds for **every** `eta`-optimal response from certified values at `d-h`, `d`, and `d+h`. Thus a policy selected by a numerical tie-breaking rule is not used as the procurement certificate. The menu-selection theorem compares the candidate's least favorable surplus against every rival's most favorable feasible bound and the outside option. The chosen grant is executable with numerical/response slack; it is not falsely called the exact minimum grant.

At the central economy, the selected discrete contracts are `(positive, E=.6, m=3)` with adjustment and `(positive, E=.6, m=4)` without adjustment. The full comparison is uniform over `b in [.998,1.02]` and `kappa in [.0198,.0202]` by affine vertex comparisons of already uniform response bounds. The machine-readable record contains every offer, not only favorable candidates. Price variation also identifies changes in the unadjusted chosen term and capacity, including active-surrender alternatives and no procurement at low service prices.

This is finite-menu procurement, not unrestricted mechanism design or a certified approximation to every continuous capacity/permission contract. That is an explicit economic environment with verifiable instruments, not the earlier unspecified allocation margin. We retain the continuously priced conditional implementation frontier as a different result.

## M1: a class-specific mechanism, distinct from direction

**Comment.** Downward-drift elimination does not order the classes' future adjustment exposure. The previous decomposition also used a benefit outside the new joint box.

**Revision.** Main Section 5 proves an exact decomposition retaining the financial-replacement term. It separates relative current-drift gains, a signed difference of class kernels applied to future adjustment opportunities, and replacement of the financial controls. Nonnegative option value does not sign the signed expectation. A finite opportunity-tail identity gives an interpretable sufficient ordering, including the mass term/cemetery treatment; no unverified wealth monotonicity is imposed.

The new computed decomposition includes the **actual** benefit midpoint `.42425`, both the active-surrender contract and a noncancellable one, and two additional law/benefit points. The signed continuation contribution is independently reconstructed, bounded with continuation uncertainty, and decomposed into wealth-bin contributions. Pointwise component accounting is explicitly distinguished from a uniform mechanism on the five-dimensional region. The procurement price comparisons further map how surrender and service change the institution the purchaser actually chooses.

The valid R11 dynamic transport argument remains intact and is independently reconstructed on the new target. The alternative initial state where downward adjustment is valuable and the adverse permission experiments remain fully visible in the compendium. We do not convert a local support-tube theorem into global monotonicity.
