\runtitle{Common Policies and Global Certification}
\begin{aug}\author[id=au1,addressref={add1}]{\fnms{Qian}~\snm{QI}\ead[label=e1]{qiqian@pku.edu.cn}}\address[id=add1]{\orgname{Peking University}}\end{aug}
\begin{abstract}
We study minimum-cost revision of an installed policy subject to operating guarantees at every state and date, using one common randomized Markov continuation. Backward repair links approximate feasibility to deployment cost. Bellman support envelopes remain valid at operating ties, and a multi-action search theorem separates policy resolution from arithmetic precision. For general Borel dynamics, a repair-density theorem gives an upper approximation without smoothing controlled atoms. Frozen finite experiments compare two complete search constructions with SCIP. Their best independently certified intersection meets an absolute $10^{-3}$ target in five of sixteen cases; SCIP reports eight numerical target hits, with its lower bounds not independently certified. Earlier root tests met no targets in eight cases; two returned uninformative fallback intervals. Forty-two further root computations expose allowance regimes and numerical floors. The original thirty positive-cost continuous-state randomized intervals remain open. Exact aggregation, observable fibers, and strict randomized savings retain their separate assumptions and economic content.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro45}
An organization revising an installed decision rule faces two different questions. How much operating performance can it sacrifice after a particular state and date? How much does implementing the revised rule cost under the states that the rule itself generates? A revision that looks inexpensive locally can change future occupancy and become expensive globally. A collection of inexpensive restart-specific plans need not be one implementable Markov rule. We study the minimum implementation cost subject to an operating guarantee after every restart, with a single continuation at any shared state and date.

The choice variable is a randomized Markov policy. Randomization is drawn anew conditional on the current state and date; it does not introduce a hidden initial coin that selects a persistent continuation. Implementation costs attach to realized departures from the installed rule. The objective averages these costs under a specified initial law, while the operating inequalities hold at every state, including states with zero initial mass. Neither a deterministic restriction nor independent optimization after each restart preserves this economic problem.

The central contribution is an end-to-end certification argument for this common-policy object. Backward repair converts approximate operating feasibility into exact admissibility with an explicit cost modulus. A covering search over shared action probabilities supplies global lower bounds. Bellman support envelopes and simplex-product identities strengthen those bounds without dividing by an operating-action gap. A finite stopping theorem tracks the diameter of each policy box and the error in directed arithmetic separately. Consequently, exact ties do not invalidate certification or finite-model completeness. The theorem does not imply a favorable number of search nodes.

This separation is consequential near indifference. A strictly positive operating-action gap can make nonreference actions rare and yield a quadratic small-allowance bound for a root relaxation. But that is a fixed-model result. A switching surface can drive the minimum gap to zero as a state representation is refined. We retain the strict-gap theorem and its full constants, and provide a gap-free stopping modulus as the alternative guarantee. Fixed precision is also not unlimited precision: the frozen implementation uses denominator $2^{44}$ for node bounds and $2^{40}$ for deployed probabilities, while a theorem covering every positive requested accuracy must allow the rounding error to decrease.

For continuous states, two distinctions remain essential. Valid Bellman support functions can accommodate action-dependent atomic transitions without treating nearby atoms as close in total variation. In addition, a new repair-density argument approximates every feasible Borel common policy in implementation value using repaired simple proposals. It applies to controlled kernels, not only autonomous observable types. For the original affine maintenance model it identifies a constructive, atom-preserving upper-approximation route. This result is one-sided: it does not prove that a finite set of support prices supplies convergent lower endpoints. The paired convergence results for exact aggregation, reset approximation, and observable fibers therefore retain their explicit structural hypotheses.

The numerical evaluation now includes actual global trees rather than only root-node ablations. Twelve designed environments from maintenance, inventory, and queueing families and four near-tie variants were fixed with source and primitive hashes before execution. Both custom methods retain a covering tree; SCIP receives the same common-policy constraints and strengthening information. At the registered absolute width $10^{-3}$, the Bellman-envelope formulation closes four cases, the aggregate-product formulation closes five, and their valid intersection with SCIP's independently evaluated candidate policies closes five. SCIP reports eight numerical target hits. Its native lower bounds are not passed off as independent rational certificates. The stronger custom formulation has narrower final intervals in eleven cases, but loses on each of the three largest environments. These outcomes measure a computational tradeoff, not uniform superiority.

The original continuous-state problem remains part of the scientific target. Its thirty positive-cost randomized intervals are still open, even though twenty-seven feasible lotteries cost less than a lower bound valid for every deterministic feasible rule. The nonlinear two-state experiment likewise supplies informative but wide intervals. Those facts remain visible in the article. All earlier proofs, counterexamples, sensitivity exercises, failed calculations, and stopped-control material are preserved in the research archive, with the previous complete article and supplement reproduced in a historical volume. The current argument does not depend on reading that development history.

\subsection{Relation to the literature}
Constrained Markov decision processes supply the natural framework \citep{Altman1999}. Occupation measures are particularly useful for expected constraints under a designated initial law. Here a separate occupancy measure for every restart would additionally have to agree on conditional action probabilities at shared state--date pairs. Omitting that compatibility would change the feasible set. Our direct formulation uses one probability variable at each such pair. This is a formulation requirement, not a claim that occupation measures cannot express the problem.

Approximation theory for constrained control studies finite representations, policy recovery, and value convergence \citep{DufourPrietoRumeau2013,Saldi2018,SaldiLinderYuksel2018}. The particular bridge here is exact backward repair under all-restart inequalities. The repair-density theorem uses a finite family of action kernels to dominate every possible occupancy distribution. Approximation occurs in that measure, while repair restores inequalities at every point, not merely almost everywhere. Its upper approximation must not be confused with a paired global convergence theorem for arbitrary controlled continuous dynamics.

The optimization ingredients are established tools. Product envelopes originate in \citet{McCormick1976}; the identity that products sum to their common continuation variable follows from the probability simplex. Best-bound spatial search, dynamic programming, and weak duality are not new. The additional result is a policy-compatible stopping modulus that charges feasibility restoration and arithmetic error. We compare with SCIP rather than excluding a strong general-purpose solver \citep{HojnyEtAl2025}. Numerical LP proposals use HiGHS; their associated simplex methods are discussed by \citet{HuangfuHall2018}. Independent residual corrections follow the distinction between computation and verification emphasized by \citet{Rump2010}.

Bellman support functions are related to information-relaxation and dual bounds \citep{BrownSmithSun2010,BrownSmith2014}. No finite price portfolio is asserted to be a complete dual. Whole-region inequalities, as in semi-infinite optimization \citep{HettichKortanek1993}, are required for a continuous-state certificate. A certificate on sampled restarts is not substituted for the original universal constraint.

The economic motivation is organizational adjustment under a performance requirement, rather than learning an unknown operating model. Safe policy improvement and constrained learning solve distinct information problems \citep{Laroche2019,DingEtAl2020,NiKamgarpour2025}. Numerical economics also emphasizes model structure and state-space approximation \citep{Judd1998,BrummScheidegger2017}. In contrast to estimated replacement models such as \citet{Rust1987}, our environments are designed known-model exercises. They do not identify empirical implementation costs or monetary welfare. The institutional meaning of a lottery and its admissible administration charge are stated explicitly below.

\section{The economic object}\label{sec:model45}
Let $X$ be a standard Borel space, $A=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ a finite action set, and $t=0,\ldots,T-1$. The known Borel kernels are $P_t^a$. Borel operating rewards, implementation costs, and terminal payoff satisfy $|r_t|\le R$, $0\le k_t\le K$, and $|g|\le G$. Throughout the central results $0<\beta<1$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Write $\Ssum h=\sum_{s=0}^{h-1}\beta^s$, with $\Ssum0=0$.

The operating value and the values of a common policy $p$ obey
\begin{align}
 V_T&=g,& V_t(x)&=\max_a\{r_t(x,a)+\beta P_t^aV_{t+1}(x)\},\label{eq:V45}\\
 J_T^p&=g,& J_t^p&=\sum_a p_t(a\mid x)\{r_t(x,a)+\beta P_t^aJ_{t+1}^p(x)\},\label{eq:J45}\\
 C_T^p&=0,& C_t^p&=\sum_a p_t(a\mid x)\{k_t(x,a)+\beta P_t^aC_{t+1}^p(x)\}.\label{eq:C45}
\end{align}
The target is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target45}
 \KR(\nu,\varepsilon)=\inf_{p\in\cF(\varepsilon)}\int C_0^p\,d\nu,
 \qquad \cF(\varepsilon)=\{p:J_t^p(x)\ge V_t(x)-\varepsilon\ \hbox{for every }(t,x)\}.
\end{equation}
All policies in this definition are Borel randomized Markov kernels. The finite action set gives Borel maximizing selectors by choosing the first maximizer. An operating-optimal policy is feasible, so the set is nonempty. We do not require an optimizer to exist in an infinite-dimensional Borel policy space; infimum arguments use approximate minimizers.

A representative implementation specification is $k_t(x,a)=w_t(x)\ind\{a\ne a_t^0(x)\}$ for an installed rule $a^0$. The relevant cost is departure from this installed rule, not a switch from the last physical action. A physical switching friction would require that previous action in the state. A fixed cost of administering a randomized rule is also a distinct primitive.

\subsection{Disadvantage, savings, and common continuation}
Choose any bounded Borel potential $H_t$ with $H_T=0$. Define
\begin{align}
 d_t(x,a)&=V_t(x)-r_t(x,a)-\beta P_t^aV_{t+1}(x),\label{eq:d45}\\
 h_t(x,a)&=H_t(x)-k_t(x,a)-\beta P_t^aH_{t+1}(x),\label{eq:h45}\\
 D_t^p&=V_t-J_t^p, & S_t^p&=H_t-C_t^p.\nonumber
\end{align}
Then $d_t\ge0$, $D_T^p=S_T^p=0$, and
\begin{align}
 D_t^p&=\sum_a p_t(a\mid x)\{d_t(x,a)+\beta P_t^aD_{t+1}^p(x)\},\label{eq:D45}\\
 S_t^p&=\sum_a p_t(a\mid x)\{h_t(x,a)+\beta P_t^aS_{t+1}^p(x)\}.\label{eq:S45}
\end{align}
These are identities for the same policy. In the executed finite formulation $H$ is the implementation value of a first-index operating-optimal reference action $a^*$. Consequently $d_t(x,a^*)=h_t(x,a^*)=0$. Eliminating the reference probability leaves bilinear terms between nonreference action probabilities and continuation values. The analytic support results below do not require this particular reference choice, and therefore do not rely on its stability when operating actions tie.
