\section{Controlled atomic states and approximation}\label{sec:atomic45}
A continuously indexed state is economically relevant when it affects operating rewards, implementation costs, or action-dependent transitions. It cannot be dismissed as a nuisance coordinate merely because a separate aggregation example has that property. The following results retain Borel common policies and allow controlled transitions. They distinguish an approximation of an upper policy from a certificate covering the unrestricted optimum.

\subsection{Repair density for unrestricted Borel policies}\label{sec:density45}
Define finite, not necessarily probability, measures
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dominating45}
 \mu_0=\nu,\qquad \mu_{t+1}(B)=\sum_{a\in A}\int P_t^a(x,B)\,\mu_t(dx).
\end{equation}
Their masses are $m^t$. Every state distribution induced from $\nu$ by any admissible or inadmissible Markov policy is dominated by $\mu_t$. Let $\Pi_N$ be increasing finite Borel partitions generating the Borel sigma field of $X$. Such partitions exist on a standard Borel space. Let $\mathcal Q$ be the countable class of all rational-probability policies that are constant on some $\Pi_N$ at each date.

\begin{theorem}[Repair-complete upper approximation]\label{thm:density45}
Under the bounded Borel assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:model45},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density45}
 \inf_{q\in\mathcal Q}\int C_0^{\mathcal R_\varepsilon q}\,d\nu
       =\KR(\nu,\varepsilon).
\end{equation}
If $(q_j)_{j\ge1}$ enumerates $\mathcal Q$, the exact feasible upper values
$U_N=\min_{j\le N}\int C_0^{\mathcal R_\varepsilon q_j}\,d\nu$ decrease to the unrestricted Borel-policy infimum. No exogeneity, recoverable type label, positive action gap, continuity of the policy, or smoothing of an atomic kernel is assumed. This is an upper-approximation theorem; it supplies neither a finite $N$ error rate nor a matching global lower sequence.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For any feasible $p$, conditional expectations of its probabilities on $\Pi_N$ converge in $L_1(\mu_t)$ to $p_t$. Rational simplex approximations preserve that convergence. Write the resulting simple proposal as $q^N$. A backward projection argument shows that the operating value of $\mathcal R_\varepsilon q^N$ approaches $J^p$ in $L_1(\mu_t)$: its scalar value is the maximum of the proposal value against the repaired continuation and $V_t-\varepsilon$, and $J_t^p$ is above that threshold. The repair probability is bounded by the value discrepancy divided by $(1-\beta)\varepsilon$. Therefore repaired action distributions approach $p$ in the same measures. A performance-difference identity and occupancy domination then imply convergence of initial implementation costs. Appendix~\ref{app:density45} supplies the complete quantitative induction, including measure-zero states. Every repaired policy is globally feasible, so the reverse inequality in \eqref{eq:density45} is immediate. Approximate feasible minimizers finish the argument.
\end{proof}
The result does not restrict the target policy class to a sieve. Only proposals are simple; their repairs need not be. All-state feasibility is restored by pointwise Bellman construction, including states excluded from every $\mu_t$-integral. Dominating measures can be expensive: their total masses grow as $m^t$. The theorem therefore removes an expressivity obstruction without claiming a practical dimension-free rate.

For the original maintenance primitives in Section~\ref{sec:old45}, rational interval partitions give a concrete representational consequence. With rational piecewise-affine rewards and finitely many affine successor maps, $V$ is piecewise affine on a finite partition at every finite horizon. If $q$ is rational piecewise constant, the repaired operating recursion is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection45}
 J_t^{\mathcal R_\varepsilon q}=
 \max\left\{\sum_a q_t(a\mid x)\big(r_t+\beta P_t^aJ_{t+1}^{\mathcal R_\varepsilon q}\big),\ V_t-\varepsilon\right\}.
\end{equation}
It remains piecewise affine. First-index maximizing action selection and the repair fraction are piecewise rational, and the fraction's denominator on its active region is at least $(1-\beta)\varepsilon$. For installed rules with finitely certified regions and piecewise-affine costs, implementation values are piecewise rational. This representation condition must be checked for a compiled incumbent; it is not inferred for an arbitrary floating-point neural network. Exact breakpoint refinement and convergent outward integration under the uniform initial law can consequently certify each upper value. Jumps and isolated breakpoints are assigned explicitly; an integral ignoring a point is not an excuse to omit its operating constraint. This establishes a constructive upper route for the original controlled atoms. No new optimization of the thirty original positive-cost rows is reported in this revision.

\subsection{Support witnesses that preserve moving atoms}\label{sec:atomicwitness45}
Theorem~\ref{thm:support45} gives global lower bounds even when transitions interact across continuous labels. Suppose certified functions $\underline V_t\le V_t$ and $\overline Z_{t,\lambda}\ge Z_{t,\lambda}$ are available. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witnesslower45}
 \int\max\{0,\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
       [\lambda(\underline V_0-\varepsilon)-\overline Z_{0,\lambda}]\}\,d\nu
 \le\KR(\nu,\varepsilon)
\end{equation}
for finite nonnegative prices. A terminal inequality and a Bellman supersolution for $Z$ establish its upper witness by induction. In a finitely atomic model the operator is evaluated as the original finite weighted sum at its exact successor maps. No weak-to-total-variation substitution is used.

These bounds also have a gap-free primitive stability statement. Suppose corresponding models satisfy $\|V_t-\widetilde V_t\|_\infty\le a_t$ and $\|Z_{t,\lambda}-\widetilde Z_{t,\lambda}\|_\infty\le z_{t,\lambda}$. Then the support lower functions differ by at most $\lambda a_t+z_{t,\lambda}$ at the same allowance. The bound does not involve a reference selector. For example, with uniform reward error $e_r$, cost error $e_k$, terminal error $e_g$, and transition total variation at most $\alpha_t$, valid backward errors are
\begin{align}
 a_T&=e_g,&a_t&=e_r+\beta a_{t+1}+2\beta\alpha_t M^V_{t+1},\label{eq:stab45}\\
 z_{T,\lambda}&=\lambda e_g,&z_{t,\lambda}&=\lambda e_r+e_k+\beta z_{t+1,\lambda}
                     +2\beta\alpha_t M^Z_{t+1,\lambda},\nonumber
\end{align}
where $M^V,M^Z$ bound absolute values in the comparison model. The factor two follows from the total-variation convention. More generally the last terms may be replaced by verified expectation errors on the particular comparison functions. For controlled affine atoms and Lipschitz comparison functions, those errors can be bounded by weighted displacements of the atoms times the Lipschitz constant. That is a test-function error estimate, not a bound on total variation between distinct Dirac masses. Discontinuities require one-sided cell bounds or explicit breakpoint treatment.

An approximate operating solution must also be charged on the upper side. The retained witness-driven repair theorem uses globally valid operating enclosures and their Bellman residuals, rather than treating an approximate $V$ as exact. The frozen finite experiments use exact rational operating dynamic programming, so they do not test a difficult operating-oracle construction. The new witness formulas state what an application must verify; they are not reported as a completed high-dimensional oracle experiment.

\subsection{Where paired continuum convergence is available}\label{sec:structural45}
The earlier exact aggregation and reset-transfer results are preserved in full. Exact exogenous aggregation requires both pointwise aggregate transition identities and a compatible family of within-cell measures. These hypotheses can hold for atomic transitions. The resulting cost problem is an ordinary finite linear program, and the continuous nuisance coordinates of its service examples do not affect rewards, implementation costs, or regime transitions. This is an exactness test, not evidence of solving an economically coupled high-dimensional continuous model.

Observable fibers give a different paired route. Suppose the state is $(i,z)$ and the next state is $(j,\Phi_t(z))$ with action-dependent regime probability $P_t^a(i,j;z)$. Each $\Phi_t$ is an autonomous Borel bijection with Borel inverse. With $F_t=\Phi_{t-1}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_0$, the label $\zeta=F_t^{-1}(z)$ is observable. If the initial law is $\nu(di,d\zeta)=q_i(\zeta)\xi(d\zeta)$ for any Borel probability vector $q(\zeta)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fiber45}
 \KR(\nu,\varepsilon)=\int v(\zeta,q(\zeta),\varepsilon)\,\xi(d\zeta),
\end{equation}
where $v$ is the finite common-policy optimum in that fiber. Independence of initial regime and label is unnecessary. Appendix~\ref{app:fiber45} gives the full Borel finite-selection construction and the error for approximating the conditional initial law.

Refining a fiber partition alone cannot eliminate residual finite optimization gaps. Both the primitive variation and the finite global intervals must be refined. Nor can \eqref{eq:fiber45} apply to the original condition process merely because its shocks are discrete: actions move its continuous condition and do not preserve an autonomous observable label. The repair-density result and the atomic support witnesses address that class directly, but a convergent pairing of their two sides is not established here.
