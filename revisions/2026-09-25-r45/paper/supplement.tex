\section{Reading conventions and provenance}
This supplement accompanies revision R45. All numerical optimization outcomes below are the preserved R43 development experiments or the R44 frozen global experiment. R45 contributes their integrated analysis, independent rechecking, new analytic results, and exact tests of the stopping contract. It does not rebrand an already observed experiment as a new prospective sample.

B denotes Bellman supports with disaggregated simplex-consistent products. A denotes the aggregate-product relaxation. A certified intersection takes the larger of the two independently checked lower bounds and the smallest independently checked feasible upper cost, also allowing the rationally repaired SCIP proposal. SCIP's native lower bound is not used in that intersection. Absolute widths are compared with the registered target $10^{-3}$. Rounded table endpoints are descriptive; the exact fractions in the machine-readable results and proof objects determine validity and target status.

The global cases are twelve designed environments (four each in maintenance, inventory, and queueing) plus four perturbations of one near-tie construction. Forty-two additional scaling outputs are three model families, seven allowances, and two root formulations. They are not forty-two independent environments. The historical controlled suite consists of eight cases and two root formulations. Its fiber calculation is one parametric family with seventeen endpoints.


\subsection{Designed family primitives}
Inventory has state $i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$, action $a\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}$, and demand shock $z\in\{0,1,2\}$. Set $o=\min\{a,n-1-i\}$, $b=i+o$, $s=\min\{b,z\}$, and $i'=b-s$. The stage operating payoff is $2s/n-3o/(4n)-i'/(5n)-(z-b)_+/(2n)$. The installed action is $\min\{m-1,\max\{0,n/2-i\}\}$ for the executed even state counts, the departure charge is $(1+i/n)\ind\{a\ne a^0(i)\}$, and terminal reward is $i/(4n)$.

Queueing uses $i'=\min\{n-1,(i-a)_++z\}$ and stage operating payoff $-i/n-a^2/(3m^2)-(i-a+z-(n-1))_+/n$. Its installed action is one below $n/2$ and $\min\{2,m-1\}$ otherwise; departure costs are again $1+i/n$, and terminal reward is $-i/(2n)$. In both families the initial regime is uniform. Shock probabilities are rational $(w_0,w_1,8-w_0-w_1)/8$ with $w_0\in\{1,2\}$ and $w_1\in\{3,4\}$ chosen by the frozen seed. Maintenance uses the preserved controlled rational transition constructor and departure charges from R43; every resulting primitive is serialized in the frozen model files. All three families are known-model designs, not estimated data.

\section{Original randomized savings and continuum evidence}
The following signed savings are $s=L_D-U_R$, where $L_D$ bounds every feasible deterministic policy and $U_R$ is a feasible randomized cost. A positive value proves a policy-class separation; a negative value does not prove that deterministic policies are better. The percentages are computed from the preserved six-decimal display inputs: $100s/(U_R+s)$ and $100s/U_R$. They are approximate display summaries, not new exact certificates. All forty-two rows are retained, including twelve zero-cost rows and three negative signed comparisons. Exactly twenty-seven rows have positive signed savings.

\section{Verification, reproducibility, and analytic contracts}
The present recheck covers all 32 complete custom trees, all 16 repaired SCIP candidate policies, and all 42 scaling root proofs. All 90 passed. Eleven frozen scientific-source identities and sixteen primitive identities were also matched. The local interpreter is Python 3.13.5; these rechecks do not remeasure the original solver benchmark on Python 3.13.15. The global replay report can resume previously completed checks, so its coordinator elapsed time must not be interpreted as a fresh single-run timing of every object.
