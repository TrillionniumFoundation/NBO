\section{Stopping accessibility and the neural approximation space}\label{sec:accessibility}
A stopping contract need not be the limit of continuation value at every geometrical boundary point. This distinction matters for a degenerate controlled diffusion: an investor can eliminate portfolio risk and move away from a wealth boundary. It therefore cannot be decided by assigning a larger penalty to every boundary observation in a neural loss.

\begin{proposition}[Trace consistency in the unchanged economy]\label{prop:traceconsistency}
For the economy in Section~\ref{sec:economy}, at $k=2$,
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{x\uparrow2}V_2(0,2,x)-g(0,2,2)
 \geq 6.45-.1\log2-25.1e^{-8}>6.37.\label{eq:tracejump}
\end{equation}
Consequently, a sequence of witnesses continuous on the closed cylinder, each equal to $g$ on its entire stopping boundary, cannot have its uniform positive Bellman residual converge to zero.
\end{proposition}
The proposition concerns the consistency of an approximation space, not the existence of a solution to the economic decision problem. The original utility, adapted controls, horizon, and liquidation fee are unchanged. Its implication is constructive: enforce the settlement where the proposed policy can stop, and use the correct one-sided comparison where it cannot. In particular, imposing the upper-wealth settlement as a hard equality would exclude the relevant continuation limit. The proof uses the elementary feasible policy $(c,\theta,p)=(.8,0,0)$ and does not use an estimated optimal value.

\subsection{A signed, facewise comparison}
Partition the stopping boundary into measurable faces $F_i$, assigning intersections to one face. Let
\[
 \omega_i^*(t,z)\geq\sup_a\E_{t,z}[e^{-\rho(\tau-t)}1_{F_i}(\tau,Z_\tau)],
 \qquad
 \omega_i^\pi(t,z)\geq\E_{t,z}^{\pi}[e^{-\rho(\tau-t)}1_{F_i}(\tau,Z_\tau)].
\]
These are upper bounds on discounted hitting probabilities, not probabilities estimated by simulation. A nonnegative smooth function whose discounted generator is nonpositive and whose stopping trace dominates the face indicator supplies such a bound by optional stopping. A separate invariance argument can give the exact value zero for an inaccessible face.

\begin{theorem}[Accessibility-weighted neural verification]\label{thm:accessibility}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:verification}, suppose complete enclosures establish
\[
 \sup_a\cR_a v\leq e_+(s),\qquad-\cR_\pi v\leq e_-(s),\qquad e_+,e_-\geq0,
\]
and $(g-v)_+\leq\beta_i^+$, $(v-g)_+\leq\beta_i^-$ on $F_i$. For an interior initial state,
\begin{align}
 0\leq V(t,z)-J(t,z;\pi)
 &\leq\int_t^T e^{-\rho(s-t)}\{e_+(s)+e_-(s)\}\,ds\notag\\
 &\quad+\sum_i\beta_i^+\omega_i^*(t,z)+\sum_i\beta_i^-\omega_i^\pi(t,z).
 \label{eq:accessbound}
\end{align}
The upper comparison retains the entire original action set. Inaccessibility for the candidate policy does not remove an action from that comparison.
\end{theorem}
This theorem retains the stopped verification identity underlying Theorem~\ref{thm:bridge}. The new distinction is which sign of a trace discrepancy must be paid, and under whose stopping distribution it is paid. Replacing both signs by a common supremum discards precisely the boundary information identified by Proposition~\ref{prop:traceconsistency}. The inequality is still an a posteriori certificate; convergence of a candidate-generation procedure is a separate statement.

\subsection{An executable accessibility-preserving architecture}
Let $N_c,N_\theta,N_p,N_v$ be smooth neural outputs. We use
\begin{align}
 c&=.425+.375\tanh N_c,&\theta&=.2\tanh N_\theta,\notag\\
 p&=\frac{2-x}{1.5}\{.15+.65\tanh N_p\},\label{eq:invariantactor}\\
 v&=G+(1-t)\{-8+B(u,x)\operatorname{softplus}(N_v)\},\label{eq:accessiblecritic}\\
 B(u,x)&=(1-e^{-12(u-1.2)})(1-e^{-12(2.8-u)})(1-e^{-4(x-.5)}).\notag
\end{align}
Here $\operatorname{softplus}(y)=\log(1+e^y)$. Consumption and preference controls obey the original bounds. The portfolio head also obeys the original bounds throughout the state domain. Its boundary factor is part of the policy, not a replacement of the economy's portfolio opportunity.

\begin{proposition}[Exact elimination of the trace budget]\label{prop:architecture}
For an interior initial state, the policy in \eqref{eq:invariantactor} cannot hit $x=2$ before another stopping event. The witness in \eqref{eq:accessiblecritic} equals $g$ on the two preference faces, the lower wealth face, and the terminal face, and is at least $g$ on the upper wealth face. Hence every trace contribution in \eqref{eq:accessbound} is zero.
\end{proposition}
The statement remains true for bounded admissible perturbations of the action heads, clipped to their stated intervals \emph{before} multiplication of the portfolio head by $(2-x)/1.5$. An unrestricted additive error in the final portfolio output is different: it can destroy inaccessibility and is not covered by this claim. The replication records this implementation contract explicitly. The certificate describes a real mathematical feedback with stored dyadic weights; it is not a claim about an undocumented floating-point activation routine or an SDE simulator.

The architecture does not by itself make the residual small. Full-domain derivative enclosures must still validate both terms in \eqref{eq:accessbound}. Reporting a zero trace budget without the remaining residual budget would therefore be incomplete. Nor do we force the numerical continuation witness to equal the known immediate-settlement value at an initially stopped upper-boundary state: at that state every policy has value $g$ and regret zero directly.

\section{An auxiliary finite-Bellman policy-iteration bound}\label{sec:certifiediteration}
The following result is an auxiliary statement about a stationary finite Bellman problem, not the algorithm executed in the nonlinear experiments. The executed certificate-aligned algorithm is defined in Section~\ref{sec:aligned}. It is useful to distinguish three sources of approximation: policy evaluation, policy improvement, and approximation of a continuous-time transition problem. We state a finite Bellman result that makes the first two requirements quantitative. It does not assume that a particular stochastic-gradient optimizer attains them.

Consider a finite discounted Markov problem with rewards $r_a$, stochastic or substochastic transition matrices $P_a$, and contraction modulus $q\in(0,1)$. Absorbing settlement is incorporated in the reward. Write $T_a v=r_a+qP_av$ and $Tv=\max_a T_av$. A neural evaluation $v_n$ of policy $\pi_n$ and an improved policy $\pi_{n+1}$ are accepted with recorded defects
\begin{equation}
 \|v_n-T_{\pi_n}v_n\|_\infty\leq\epsilon_n,
 \qquad 0\leq Tv_n-T_{\pi_{n+1}}v_n\leq\eta_n\mathbf1.
 \label{eq:policydefects}
\end{equation}
On a continuous state space the same inequalities require a complete enclosure, not a finite collection of collocation residuals. On a finite state space they can be checked by enumeration. They give a stopping and refinement criterion for a learned representation without identifying its training loss with its Bellman defect.

\begin{theorem}[A quantitative certified policy-iteration recurrence]\label{thm:iteration}
Let $E_n=\|V^*-V^{\pi_n}\|_\infty$ for the finite Bellman problem. If \eqref{eq:policydefects} holds, then
\begin{equation}
 E_{n+1}\leq qE_n+\frac{2q\epsilon_n}{(1-q)^2}+\frac{\eta_n}{1-q}.
 \label{eq:iterationrate}
\end{equation}
Thus geometrically decreasing certified defects imply convergence of policy values, with the explicit convolution bound obtained by iterating \eqref{eq:iterationrate}. If both defects are bounded by $C r^n$, $r\in(0,1)$, the bound decreases at rate $O(\max\{q,r\}^n)$ when $q\ne r$, and $O(nq^n)$ when $q=r$.
\end{theorem}
The contraction and policy-improvement ingredients are classical. The operational content is the use of independently checked evaluation and continuous-action defects as acceptance criteria. A neural optimizer that does not achieve those defects has not met the hypothesis. In particular, the full-horizon experiments below are not assigned a convergence guarantee by virtue of being called policy iteration.

A time or state discretization requires an additional comparison. If a numerical optimal value differs from continuous-time value by at most $\zeta_h$, and the numerical value of the transferred policy differs from its continuous-time payoff by at most $\xi_h$, its continuous-time loss is at most $E_n+\zeta_h+\xi_h$. Neither term is assumed zero. An alternative is to bypass both transfers and certify the actual continuous-time feedback with Theorem~\ref{thm:accessibility}; that is the procedure used for the classical candidates below. At an inward stopping face, imposing an inconsistent Dirichlet approximation does not supply the required $\zeta_h$. Proposition~\ref{prop:traceconsistency} explains why this qualification is material rather than technical.

For a fixed positive discount rate, $q=e^{-\rho h}$ approaches one as $h$ decreases. The factors in \eqref{eq:iterationrate} must therefore be included in any accuracy allocation. The theorem is not a dimension-free complexity result. The original nonlinear economy still uses a full rectangular certificate; the structured extension in Section~\ref{sec:inventory} avoids a state tensor cover for a different, explicitly proved reason.
