\begin{proposition}[A computable fixed-witness obstruction]\label{prop:fixedwitness}
Let $v$ satisfy the upper-comparison trace conditions and let
$h_v=v(0,2,2)-g(0,2,2)$ be its upper-wealth trace excess. Write
$\Delta=6.45-.1\log2-25.1e^{-8}$. Any nonnegative, time-dependent bound on its positive optimal residual must satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^1 e^{-.04s}e_+(s)\,ds\geq(\Delta-h_v)_+ .\label{eq:fixedwitnessfloor}
\end{equation}
This is a lower bound on the certificate component of this fixed witness, not a lower bound on the regret of its policy.
\end{proposition}
The final accessible critics have verified trace excesses only about $.052$--$.097$. Their lower floors from \eqref{eq:fixedwitnessfloor} range from $6.274882$ to $6.320490$. Consequently, refining their covers or increasing arithmetic precision cannot close the original accuracy gap. A materially different continuation witness is required. The all-face class has the stronger structural obstruction $h_v=0$.

The actor--critic interchange sharpens this diagnosis. Pairing the initial actor with the final critic for seed 17100 gives exactly the same retained bound, $7.339624178645005$, as the final actor/final critic pair. Pairing the final actor with the initial critic gives $23.718685694842687$. Thus the certificate descent cannot be attributed to a demonstrated increase in the actor's true payoff. Both facts are retained rather than reinterpreted as policy improvement.

\subsection{Unrestricted candidate generation and independent witnesses}
The new controlled Markov-chain and semi-Lagrangian candidates do not multiply their interior portfolio choices by the neural wealth-distance factor. Each solves over the original bounded action grid, including zero portfolio. The upper-wealth grid column represents the interior continuation trace: zero-portfolio continuation is computed and compared with immediate settlement for nonzero portfolio at that trace. The retained policies select zero portfolio on this column. The certified controls are the original-action-clipped, exact-real bilinear state interpolants, constant within each time interval. Their interpolation consequently has bounded $p/(2-x)$ and cannot hit the upper face before another stopping event; no interior distance multiplier is imposed.

Each candidate receives a separately trained witness and a full continuous-time residual check against the original continuous action set. Hence neither the discrete central value nor a common inaccurate neural witness is used as its accuracy certificate. Table~\ref{tab:r18classical} gives all six resulting bounds and separates candidate generation, witness construction, and verification. The method-specific witnesses remove the earlier common-witness confound but do not produce a sharp matched-accuracy frontier. In particular, the finest-grid bounds increase because their policy residual contribution is no longer zero. The numerical values of the discrete schemes remain diagnostics, not certified continuous-time values.
