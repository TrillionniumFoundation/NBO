\section{Budget-feasible welfare compensation in the original economy}\label{sec:wealthcompensation}
An absolute utility tolerance is not self-interpreting. The consumption-top-up experiment in the preceding section specifies an external financing convention, but it is not a budget-feasible wealth equivalent. We now give a sufficient initial-wealth compensation with an explicit feasible policy adjustment under the original budget and stopping rules. The object being compensated is the sharp deterministic time-control library, not the original neural policies.

Let $\pi=(c,\theta,0)$ be the selected library policy at $(u_0,x_0)=(2,1.25)$ and an arbitrary $k\in[.5,8]$. Add initial wealth $\Delta x$ and a constant consumption increment
\[
 d_c=\frac{\Delta x}{C_{.02}(1)},\qquad C_{.02}(1)=\frac{1-e^{-.02}}{.02},
\]
while retaining the preference control and zero portfolio. Before preference stopping, the wealth difference solves $\dot z=.02z-d_c$, $z(0)=\Delta x$, and has $z(1)=0$ and $z(s)\geq0$. No extra payoff, transfer after time zero, or modified consumption cap is introduced.

\begin{proposition}[Sufficient wealth compensation]\label{prop:wealthcompensation}
Suppose every selected original and compensated policy avoids wealth stopping before time one, satisfies $c+d_c\leq.8$, and has preference-exit probability at most $p_e$. Then
\begin{equation}
 J_k(0,2,1.25+\Delta x;\pi^+)-J_k(0,2,1.25;\pi)
 \geq d_c\,.8^{-1.2}C_{.04}(1)(1-p_e).\label{eq:wealthgain}
\end{equation}
Consequently, if the right side exceeds the original library's uniform regret bound $\varepsilon_{\rm lib}$, the compensated feasible policy satisfies
$J_k(0,2,1.25+\Delta x;\pi^+)\geq V_k(0,2,1.25)$ for every library-covered price.
\end{proposition}
The comparison is sufficient compensation with an explicit policy change. It is neither the exact minimum wealth equivalent of the unchanged feedback nor a calibrated empirical welfare estimate.

Directed calculations check every one of the 20 freshly generated library policies. The preference-exit bound is below $1.207\times10^{-29}$. The analytic sufficient wealth increment is at most $.007713143$; the implementation chooses the simple rational increment $39/5000=.0078$, or $.624\%$ of initial wealth. Its consumption increment is enclosed by $[.007878259998266659,.007878259998266711]$. All compensated consumption paths satisfy the original $.8$ cap, all terminal wealth values remain strictly above $.5$, and the global wealth upper bound is below $1.257958<2$.

The resulting uniform utility gain is at least $.010094048517141695$, greater than the original uniform regret upper bound $.009981645275150275$, with a verified slack exceeding $.00011240324$. An additional replay through the original stopped-payoff evaluator is retained separately. Thus the normalization is tied to a feasible economic experiment rather than to the numerical size of $.01$ alone. Its coverage remains the original library's central initial preference and complete price interval, not the full-state neural experiment.
