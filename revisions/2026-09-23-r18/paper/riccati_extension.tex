\subsection{A rigorously validated classical comparison for the quadratic model}\label{sec:validatedriccati}
The preceding quadratic model has only two independent gain equations. We retain it as a transparent validation case, not as the nonlinear high-dimensional benchmark. Its classical comparison is now certified rather than merely an RK45 diagnostic.

For each scalar mode, with coefficients $(a,q)=(-.2,1)$ or $(.1,1.5)$ and terminal gain $p(0)=.5$ in remaining time $h$, let $\kappa=\sqrt{a^2+q}$ and $y=(.5-a)/\kappa$. The exact gain is
\[
 p^*(h)=a+\kappa\frac{y+\tanh(\kappa h)}{1+y\tanh(\kappa h)}.
\]
It is increasing on $[0,1]$. Directed endpoint values therefore enclose its full range on each time cell. The implemented policy stores a dyadic midpoint gain on each cell; MPFR bounds its uniform gain error $\delta$. All stored gains are strictly above $.5$, so the moment estimate used in the quadratic performance-difference identity gives per-coordinate loss at most
\[
 \delta^2\left\{C_{.8}(1)+\frac{.01}{.8}(1-C_{.8}(1))\right\}.
\]
Total loss is this quantity multiplied by $d$. Table~\ref{tab:r18riccati} reports both units and includes the full generation-and-verification cost. In particular, the 128-cell classical policy has a $d=128$ total bound below $.002457$, and the 2,048-cell policy has a total bound below $9.624\times10^{-6}$. This is the appropriate rigorous comparison for a problem whose exact structure is so favorable to classical methods.
