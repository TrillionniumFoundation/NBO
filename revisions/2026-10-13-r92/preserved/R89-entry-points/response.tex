commit \nolinkurl{4b27b56f4514f6c1d77b69fe707f7202f8b06b19}, and reviews
R67 manuscript commit \nolinkurl{eacd3e217ae1e2e9ea6e154b48da7d103159d051}.

The new central sections are \textbf{Representation calibration and

\hypertarget{b1-m1-and-m10-the-nbo-contribution-and-the-role-of-a-predictor}{%
\subsection{B1, M1 and M10: The NBO contribution and the role of a
predictor}\label{b1-m1-and-m10-the-nbo-contribution-and-the-role-of-a-predictor}}

The referee is right that the validity of the safeguarded query
procedure does not identify a neural performance advantage. The revised
introduction keeps the original constructive NBO continuation central
and distinguishes it from the optional action-query proposal. A
continuation is fitted against its own attained future, qualifies
residual oscillation and original-optimum policy loss, and constructs a
complete robust controller. Exact conditional integration of a shallow
continuation does not require an optimal-value descendant tree. These
are constructive results about the NBO itself, not conclusions inferred
from the safe return of an optional predictor.

The new question is whether qualification charges the represented
function consistently and whether a policy-cost result depends on a
single numerically selected readout. The affine-exact qualification
proposition proves a common calibration for the two fixed bases. The
optimization-to-policy theorem and exact switch duals certify a class of
readouts rather than only an optimizer seed. The observed cost intervals
can then extend to those classes without adding a statistical union
bound. This develops the original operator-to-policy-to-economic-cost
chain while retaining the method-neutral query theorem as a
complementary result.

The title remains \emph{Neural Bellman Operators}. We do not claim that
every optional neural component improves work, or that the present
expected-cost identities prove results for all historical nonlinear
recursive objectives. Those distinctions identify the actual
mathematical objects rather than substitute a new topic.

\hypertarget{b3-and-m4-prospective-reuse}{%
\subsection{B3 and M4: Prospective
reuse}\label{b3-and-m4-prospective-reuse}}

The intervening completed material extends the original reuse
investigation to the retained sixty-four-block and witnessed-reuse
designs. The manuscript and appendix retain their model identities,
first fills, failed validations, fallbacks, descendants, load and record
costs. The bounded-cache contract is distinguished from a full-cover
fill theorem, and localized workloads are not counted as direct
policy-cost observations.

R81 adds neither a new reuse timing population nor a claimed neural

fully action-stable qualification sublevel. This deterministic evidence
transfer does not erase model-loading or verification costs and is not
an amortized runtime result. The two notions are stated separately.

\hypertarget{b4-and-m5-useful-precision-for-actual-original-law-costs}{%
\subsection{B4 and M5: Useful precision for actual original-law
costs}\label{b4-and-m5-useful-precision-for-actual-original-law-costs}}

The inherited recursive-policy comparisons and the later
attained-controller comparisons concern different policies. We preserve
that distinction. The R80 protocol froze nine complete actors, 524,288
new four-coordinate continuous-bin rows, forty-bit bins, two estimators
and ninety-four estimands before the new cost stream was evaluated. The
predeclared paired-width and equivalence margin is \(1/256\). Cash cost
and exact last-innovation conditional expectation target the same
original-law expected costs and share one simultaneous failure allowance
below \(1/100\); they do not double the sample count.

The new appendix displays all marginal, paired and interaction intervals
and decomposes width into endpoint sampling-variance allowances, the
finite-support term and actual mean numerical enclosure width. The
numerical term includes continuous-bin, acquisition and arithmetic
propagation; no invented additive attribution to separate intermediate
operations is asserted. The paired target is not retrospectively imposed
as a success condition on marginal costs. The finite-support term
dominates the fine calibrated basis comparison, so additional variance
reduction alone would not remove its principal uncertainty.

There are now identified own-controller gains and matched cost rankings.
For example, the fine calibrated ReLU controller improves on its own
legacy construction by an interval approximately
\([0.005159,0.006341]\). The calibrated fine ReLU-minus-Bernstein
difference is approximately \([0.000955,0.002013]\). The latter
identifies lower Bernstein cost while also satisfying the specified
equivalence margin; strict order and small-margin equivalence can both
hold. No zero-containing interval is relabeled as equality or
superiority, and the original unresolved R67 comparisons remain
unchanged.

\hypertarget{b5-m2-and-m3-executed-training-comparable-information-and-attribution}{%
\subsection{B5, M2 and M3: Executed training, comparable information and
attribution}\label{b5-m2-and-m3-executed-training-comparable-information-and-attribution}}

The intervening retained studies include the certificate-aware
fixed-feature readout, matched conventional features,
independent-context validation, longer reuse and trained vector
proposals. They are not relabeled versions of R67's L-BFGS fits. Their
hypotheses and complete evidence remain in the appendix. A
predictor-independent validation stream and recursively generated
endogenous contexts continue to have different statistical contracts.

The attained-continuation experiment supplies an additional
representation comparison. Both bases use nine coefficients, the same
coefficient limits, primitive information, robust action menus and
acquisition resolutions. Inspection of the qualification objective
showed that the old coefficientwise modulus charged a common affine
function ten times as much in its Bernstein representation as in its
ReLU representation. The new derivative-control moduli charge those
affine functions identically. The ReLU modulus is exact for its
piecewise-affine function; the Bernstein bound is valid but need not be
exact for a general polynomial. We therefore call this affine
calibration, not equality of nonlinear hypothesis classes.

The separately frozen factorial experiment retains the legacy actors and
reconstructs both fitted futures under calibrated qualification. Both
bases improve their own attained policy costs. The matched ordering
reverses: legacy ReLU has lower cost, but calibrated Bernstein has lower
cost at both resolutions. The two predeclared interactions identify the
change. Both findings are reported. This supplies a positive explanation
of how qualification affects actual economic decisions; it does not
protect a preferred ranking by discarding a stronger conventional
result.

\hypertarget{b6-and-m6-vector-controls-and-horizon-cost}{%

The original vector theorem, its primitive curvature and feasibility
premises, the R67 horizon-four descendant count, and subsequent trained
two-output comparisons are retained with their actual records. The
costly vector recursion is not reclassified as solved because a tensor
state lattice is absent. The main reading path points to the complete

The new action-stability theorem is executed for the two-date
construction with a fixed terminal actor. A separate first-disagreement

constructed. In particular, a changed fitted future changes upstream
labels and needs a new qualification; independent small optimization
gaps at different label problems do not automatically compose into the
new stability result.

\hypertarget{b8-fitting-reliability-versus-return-reliability}{%
\subsection{B8: Fitting reliability versus return
reliability}\label{b8-fitting-reliability-versus-return-reliability}}

The new theorem addresses a precise deterministic reliability question.
Let \(U\) be the attained feasible readout's qualification objective. A
verified switch lower bound exceeding \(U+\epsilon\) excludes every
competing action throughout that objective sublevel, including ties.
Since the unknown optimum is at most \(U\), every \(\epsilon\)-optimal
readout belongs to that sublevel.

Three of the four calibrated terminal-label problems have fully stable
first-date covers at zero extra allowance. Every exact minimizer of each
such problem yields the attained complete policy, and positive uniform
dyadic allowances are also reported. Larger allowances leave some cells
unresolved; coarse ReLU is partially unresolved even at zero. For
partial coverage the theorem gives an explicit original-law cost
enlargement based on the initial mass of uncertified cells. These
enlargements are not presumed small enough to preserve every empirical
sign.

Thus an attained-policy comparison can hold uniformly across qualified
optimizer classes without sampling optimizers or claiming a hidden-layer
convergence rate. Practical solver attainment is still separately
certified by its actual feasible iterate and dual gap. Safe return via a
conventional fallback remains a different property, and fitting warnings
are not removed.

\hypertarget{b9-and-m9-the-economic-decision}{%
\subsection{B9 and M9: The economic
decision}\label{b9-and-m9-the-economic-decision}}

The new actual-cost intervals give nonempty conditional charge regions.
For the fine calibrated basis comparison, a Bernstein per-deployment
premium below the lower ReLU-minus-Bernstein cost endpoint is covered by
its identified policy-cost saving; a premium above the upper endpoint
favors ReLU after charges. The intermediate region is unresolved. Both
estimators' marginal and pair intervals can be combined on their
original simultaneous event, and exact difference-constraint closure
sharpens these regions without additional sampling.

The attaining cost potentials and the zero-charge pure-choice regret
calculation for the nine-controller catalogue are deposited. Four-term
interaction intervals are reported separately rather than silently
treating the pair-constraint solution as sharp for a smaller set
including those extra restrictions. The preceding sharp
deployment-allocation and finite-cohort theorems remain available under
their original premises.

The amounts are normalized model units. We do not invent a monetary loss
scale, deployment count, processor price or installation charge, nor
substitute a sample count for an economic deployment horizon. The
economic advance is an identified constructed-policy cost and
conditional choice region, including an optimizer-class interpretation
where stability is certified. It is not an empirical calibration or a
general representation-specific purchase recommendation.

\hypertarget{preservation-exposition-and-release}{%
\subsection{Preservation, exposition and
release}\label{preservation-exposition-and-release}}

The article follows the original operator, attained continuation,
qualification, policy stability and economic cost chain. The technical
supplement retains the core proofs, and the Numerical and Development
Appendix compiles each relocated recursive, vector and historical
evidence section once. The new complete dual and coupling proofs and all
factorial outcomes are in that appendix. Original wrappers, prior PDFs
and every adverse scientific record remain preserved with their
provenance.

The release build checks ordinary sources, exact regressions, inherited
suites without skipped tests, all new bin trajectories and inference
quantities, all new switch duals, source manifests, original section
hashes and cross-references. A clean source-archive rebuild is a
separate check. Automated compilation and text checks are not described
as visual inspection or external mathematical approval. Any visual
inspection is separately reported against the actual PDF identities.

The submission therefore develops the original NBO paper positively:
affine-calibrated residual qualification is connected to attained policy
costs; exact optimization barriers yield policy-class stability; and
simultaneous economic intervals transfer to the qualified classes. The
new results coexist with the stronger calibrated conventional
comparator, unresolved sublevels and costly historical recursive trees.
None is removed to obtain a favorable conclusion.
