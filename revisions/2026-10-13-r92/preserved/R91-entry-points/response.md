We have not adopted the recommendation to replace the paper with a first-date finite-menu article. ## M3 / B2. Recursive propagation and the original objective

For each stored continuation, the revision verifies a lower original-minus-numerical action-value bound over every original feasible action and an upper bound for the implemented feasible action. Numerical residual span, greediness, the difference of those signed endpoints, and terminal span enter the original-policy loss certificate with their correct dated discounts. Recursive monotonicity and cash invariance establish the comparison with both the complete implemented policy and the original Bellman optimum. No linear expectation telescoping is asserted for nonlinear risk.

The formula contains the earlier symmetric transport allowance as a special case. A one-date singular-transition example attains the signed charge and shows a strict factor-two improvement over its symmetric counterpart. A two-date example shows why a common-action bias cannot be cancelled when it varies with the state: an earlier action changes which later bias is encountered. A separate four-outcome calculation distinguishes static and nested tail risk. The latter is used only when it is the original recursive primitive, not as a convenient replacement objective.

All feasible-action and own-future obligations remain explicit. The policy-specific upper endpoint does not license replacing the all-action lower endpoint by a selected-action check.
