\section{Arithmetic and exact polynomial conversion}\label{supp:arithmetic}
The interval backend is the inherited rational-Taylor binary64 implementation. Rational constants are first enclosed; elementary operations and affine transformations are outward rounded. Tanh and sigmoid bounds use the backend's exponential enclosure. Positive derivative ranges may be intersected with their analytic ranges, $0\leq\tanh'\leq1$ and $0\leq\mathrm{sigmoid}'\leq1/4$. The second derivative is then propagated by the exact chain rule. All stored weights are fixed binary64 numbers interpreted as exact reals.

For the direct polynomial policy, write $P(\xi)=\sum_{j=0}^8c_j\xi^j$, with $c_j$ the exact rational value of the stored float. On a rational cell $\xi=a+(b-a)s$, local power coefficients of the residual $R=xP-1$ are
\[
 q_k=\sum_{j=k}^n R_j{j\choose k}a^{j-k}(b-a)^k.
\]
Its Bernstein coefficients are
\[
 \beta_i=\sum_{k=0}^i q_k\frac{{i\choose k}}{{n\choose k}}.
\]
Every coefficient and cell endpoint is stored as an exact rational string in \path{results/jets/direct_exact.json}. The basis convex-hull bound, the positive lower bound on $x$, nonexpansiveness of action projection, and exact portfolio cancellation give the certificate in the article. This avoids the inherited ordinary floating multiplication in derivative coefficients. We preserve that old implementation and its output as historical material rather than changing a past calculation silently.

\section{Oracle precision and the original stopped gradient}\label{supp:gradient}
An interval decision for $F(y')-F(y)$ is a statement about a finite payoff difference. Dividing by a finite step gives a secant enclosure, not a derivative enclosure. To enclose a directional derivative one additionally needs a rigorously controlled local remainder, such as a verified Hessian bound along the relevant policy segment. No such complete stopped-payoff derivative remainder is supplied for the original economy here.

The article's reduced-gradient perturbation result explicitly bounds both numerical and exact derivative factors. Its root conditioning, price error, derivative errors, and offset-Lipschitz allowances must all be instantiated before it bounds the original executed economic gradient. Its proof is not an excuse to omit these quantities. Likewise, the payoff-polling theorem requires sufficiently narrow valid payoff intervals, smoothness on feasible poll segments, and an upper payoff bound. The actual online gate establishes safe accepted improvements with the available checker, while the theorem's shrinking-oracle hypotheses remain additional requirements for stationarity.

The exact quadratic regression test verifies the logic of the finite-poll estimate in a setting where the oracle conditions hold exactly. It is labeled as a mathematical implementation test, not a new original-economy optimizer experiment. The stopped reference has a stronger independent exact identity, but it is a separate model.

\section{Work accounting}\label{supp:cost}
For a batch of $m$ policies, a complete cost account has the form
\[
 T_{\mathrm{dual}}+\sum_{i=1}^m(T_{\mathrm{setup},i}+T_{\mathrm{proposal},i}+T_{\mathrm{check},i}+T_{\mathrm{bookkeeping},i}).
\]
The dual cost is amortized only if the same validated dual is reused legitimately. The imported held-out table reports proposal and scheduled-checkpoint times, not this entire expression. Carrier construction and shared preprocessing have their own records. The new online table measures actual interleaving of generation and checking from initial incumbent evaluation; it excludes construction of the inherited common dual and initial model object. The shadow continuation is separately timed and excluded from the production online latency. These distinctions prevent a sub-two-second proposal from being presented as an end-to-end certified solve.

R25 and R26 were executed in different recorded environments. In particular, the eightfold cell-count reduction is not reported as an eightfold wall-clock speedup. The local same-cover table includes both first-order and second-order calculations and file writing. Timings are observations of these runs, not a benchmark against an external implementation.
