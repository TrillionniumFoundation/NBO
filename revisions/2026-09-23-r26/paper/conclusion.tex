\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
A Neural Bellman Operator for the original stopped preference-adjustment economy remains the organizing objective. This revision adds an executed certification-coupled proposal mechanism, a conditional finite-work quotient-polling theorem, a moving historical-transport comparison, and sharper whole-domain verification in a stopped unspanned-risk economy. The experiments distinguish a real optimizer rollback from checkpoint replay and a verifier gain from a policy gain. They retain a strong direct benchmark and the adverse neural outcomes rather than selecting away the relevant comparison.

The next successful original-economy calculation must supply a current-state actor with a complete-domain certificate near the declared $.01$ tolerance and a separate payoff-improvement statement when such a statement is made. A proof that the original checker supplies arbitrarily narrow policy intervals, or instantiated stopped-gradient constants, would connect the verified-improvement component more tightly to the optimization analysis. The present results do not claim those conclusions. They provide reproducible methods, proofs, and evidence with which to pursue them, while preserving the original economic problem and every prior derivation for further scrutiny.
