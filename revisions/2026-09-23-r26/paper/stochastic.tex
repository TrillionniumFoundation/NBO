\section{Whole-domain verification in an unspanned-risk stopped economy}\label{sec:stochastic}
To test stochastic propagation, stopping, and current-state restart without the original financed decoder, consider a separate investment economy on
\[
 (t,x,z)\in[0,1]\times[.5,2]\times[-1,1],\qquad
 a\in[0,2.2],\quad p\in[0,1],
\]
with independent Brownian motions $W,B$:
\begin{equation}
 dX=(.02X+a+.04pX)\,dt+.25pX\,dW,
 \qquad dZ=.3\,dB.\label{eq:reference_dynamics}
\end{equation}
The $B$ shock is not spanned by the risky wealth exposure. Let stopping occur at first exit from the interior box or time $1$. Set $A(t)=.8+.2t$ and supply the smooth witness
\begin{equation}
 v(t,x,z)=A(t)(\log x+.1xz-.1z^2).\label{eq:reference_witness}
\end{equation}
At every stopping face, settlement equals $v$. Running reward is $b(t,x,z)-a^2/2$, where the model's transfer is
\begin{equation}
 b=.04v-v_t-.02xv_x-\frac{.3^2}{2}v_{zz}
 -\frac{v_x^2}{2}+\frac{.04^2v_x^2}{2(.25)^2v_{xx}}.\label{eq:transfer}
\end{equation}
All these coefficients are bounded on the closed box, and $v_{xx}=-A/x^2<0$. This is a manufactured model: the transfer is chosen to make the value independently known. The experiment validates policy approximation and continuous verification in a stochastic stopped setting; it is not an exercise in discovering an unknown value function or calibrating an empirical economy.

\begin{theorem}[Exact stochastic value and restart regret]\label{thm:reference}
For the model \eqref{eq:reference_dynamics}--\eqref{eq:transfer}, the optimal current-state actions are
\begin{equation}
 a^*(t,x,z)=A(t)(1/x+.1z),\qquad
 p^*(t,x,z)=.64(1+.1xz).\label{eq:reference_controls}
\end{equation}
They lie in the declared action bounds, and the unrestricted stopped value is $v$. For every admissible current-state policy $\pi=(a,p)$,
\begin{equation}
 v(t,x,z)-J^\pi(t,x,z)
 =\E_{t,x,z}\int_t^\tau e^{-.04(s-t)}
 \frac{(a-a^*)^2+.0625A(s)(p-p^*)^2}{2}\,ds.\label{eq:reference_regret}
\end{equation}
Consequently, a uniform upper bound $\overline d$ on the integrand gives
\begin{equation}
 0\leq v-J^\pi\leq \overline d\frac{1-e^{-.04(1-t)}}{.04}
 \label{eq:reference_certificate}
\end{equation}
at every original state of this auxiliary economy and at every restart time, without stored initialization weights or a financing root.
\end{theorem}
The state--time statement is stronger than a list of simulated starting states. It follows from a complete cover and a stopped verification identity. The tabulated start times below are evaluations of the same uniform formula; no interpolation between those times is used in the proof.

\subsection{Centered second-order action certificates}\label{sec:jets}
Let $e_j=\pi_j-\pi_j^*$ be an action error on a rectangular cell with center $m$ and half-widths $r_i$. Interval propagation supplies point enclosures of $e_j(m)$ and $\partial_i e_j(m)$, and whole-cell enclosures of $\partial_{ik}e_j$. Denote valid upper absolute bounds by $E_j$, $G_{ij}$, and $H_{ikj}$.

\begin{theorem}[Second-order cell enclosure]\label{thm:jets}
If $e_j$ is twice continuously differentiable on the cell, then throughout it
\begin{equation}
 |e_j|\leq E_j+\sum_iG_{ij}r_i
 +\frac12\sum_iH_{iij}r_i^2+\sum_{i<k}H_{ikj}r_ir_k.\label{eq:jet}
\end{equation}
The minimum of this bound and a valid first-order mean-value enclosure remains valid. Substituting such componentwise error bounds into the nonnegative quadratic integrand in \eqref{eq:reference_regret}, maximizing over a complete cover, and applying \eqref{eq:reference_certificate} yields a whole-state--time certificate.
\end{theorem}
For each tanh or sigmoid layer, the implementation propagates
\[
 D(\phi\circ q)=\phi'(q)Dq,\qquad
 D^2(\phi\circ q)=\phi'(q)D^2q+\phi''(q)Dq\,Dq'.
\]
All affine operations and transcendental enclosures use the inherited directed interval arithmetic. Saved binary64 network weights are interpreted as exact real coefficients; displayed economic decoder constants are exact rationals. The certificate therefore concerns the real-valued policy represented by those coefficients, not an unspecified hardware rounding path at deployment. The control scales are exact $2.2$ and $1$. A separate floating-point comparison with automatic differentiation tests the implementation of the chain rule; it does not replace the interval proof.

Both neural policies are the unchanged R25 $3$--$12$--$12$--$2$ policies. They were fitted using the known actions on a $5\times9\times7$ tensor rule. R26 does not retrain them. It verifies nested covers of sizes $512$, $4096$, and $32768$, retaining the unsuccessful coarse certificates. Every endpoint and center of these covers is dyadic. The code saves every cell, its action-error bounds, its quadratic deficit bound, and a file digest. No sampled cell is substituted for an unverified remainder of the domain.

\subsection{Exact rational verification of the classical reference}\label{sec:bernstein}
The retained direct policy approximates $1/x$ by a degree-eight polynomial $P(\xi)$, $\xi=(x-1.25)/.75$, uses $a=A(P(\xi)+.1z)$ with projection onto $[0,2.2]$, and uses the exact portfolio expression in \eqref{eq:reference_controls}. Projection cannot increase distance to the feasible optimum. The polynomial coefficients are the unchanged stored binary64 coefficients, interpreted exactly as rational numbers.

The inherited direct verifier formed derivative coefficients with the ordinary floating multiplication $i\,c_i$ before treating them as point intervals. That operation does not itself enclose the exact product of the stored coefficient and $i$. We do not retroactively label the old derivative-coefficient computation as a directed enclosure. Instead we remove the derivative calculation from the current certificate.

\begin{lemma}[Exact residual-polynomial certificate]\label{lem:bernstein}
Let $R(\xi)=((5+3\xi)/4)P(\xi)-1$. On a cell $[a,b]\subset[-1,1]$, express $R(a+(b-a)s)=\sum_{i=0}^n\beta_i {n\choose i}s^i(1-s)^{n-i}$ with exact rational coefficients. Then
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{\xi\in[a,b]}|P(\xi)-1/x|
 \leq\frac{\max_i|\beta_i|}{(5+3a)/4}.\label{eq:bernstein}
\end{equation}
With $E$ the largest bound over a complete cover, the direct policy's regret is at most $\tfrac12 E^2(1-e^{-.04(1-t)})/.04$ for every starting state and time.
\end{lemma}
The computation uses 64 one-dimensional cells. The $t,z$ dependence is handled analytically through $A\leq1$, and portfolio error cancels exactly. The resulting bound is $1.2978908373252045\times10^{-7}$. Its small value is not a claim about superiority of a general classical Bellman solver; it is the correct strong baseline for this structured, known-value problem. In particular, it prevents a verifier improvement from being reported as neural-policy superiority.
