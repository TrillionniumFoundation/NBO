\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction56}
A continuation approximation matters because it changes an economic decision. Its fitting error, the loss of the policy eventually implemented, and the resources needed to verify that policy are different objects. This paper develops Neural Bellman Operators to connect them. A continuation is constructed using its own fitted future, feasible action witnesses are retained through implementation, and a computable policy account determines what has been achieved for the original economic problem.

The first contract is accuracy against the Bellman optimum. Under effective primitive moduli, the constructive backend returns a feasible policy with a prescribed error account. Theorems~\ref{thm:construct49} and \ref{thm:compile49} establish the construction and its continuous original-owner implementation; the separated resource budget identifies the roles of state coverage, action search and integration. A native envelope and its identical affine--ReLU circuit are two encodings of one construction. This identity is useful for implementation, but does not itself establish an advantage over another numerical method.

The trained backend supplies a second, linked route. Learned nonlinear features propose actions, while verification determines which proposals can be deployed. In the investment model, uniform-innovation integration turns a one-hidden-layer ReLU continuation into a rational piecewise-polynomial action objective. Exact fitted minimization is therefore a finite calculation rather than a second unconverged training problem. The fitted-witness transfer theorem separates center optimization error from acquisition, continuation, continuous-action covering and numerical verification errors. An exact witness removes the first of these errors, not all of them.

We develop two exact implementations of that witness. Lossless interval screening excludes only lattice actions that cannot attain the minimum, and exact comparison preserves the original smallest-index tie rule. Conditional transcript invariance then preserves the acquired policy, its certificates and a prospective controller's stopping decisions. The additional finite-difference construction in Section~\ref{sec:search60} avoids a favorable-separation premise. On each nonnegative scalar-action piece, the objective is a quartic with a nonnegative fourth-order coefficient. Its second forward difference is nondecreasing. A concave prefix and a convex tail can consequently be searched by rational sign comparisons, including flat objectives, adjacent ties and signed cancellation. No isolated real root or unique surviving action is required.

The action dimension and the state dimension play different roles. The scalar finite-difference result does not become a multivariate theorem by increasing the number of state coordinates. We therefore give a separate exact integration formula for finite mixtures of box-uniform shocks and a terminating rational subdivision result on constrained action lattices. The latter retains the original lexicographic witness, including when an exact fallback is needed. Its worst-case work and the exponential dependence of the integration formula on the number of independent uniform components are explicit. The two-control experiment implements this extension with a shared capacity constraint and one or two shocks; it is distinct from the complete scalar-action policy services.

The policy account is computed on locally split, non-tensor observation partitions. A one-state residual and value cache encloses a discontinuous incumbent's continuation. A signed sensitivity calculation for a smooth installed reference retains useful continuation direction without storing a full state-pair table. The nonlinear investment family has a dimension-uniform reference-stability bound, but the work required for a prescribed accuracy still depends on dimension, horizon and the acquired partition. A discontinuous returned actor is handled through every intersected leaf, not by differentiating away its jumps. The separate sample-split approximately-null calculation qualifies inferred nuisance structure and the remaining Bellman comparison under the same maintained-model confidence set.

A prospective service can stop at an economic target before it establishes a small all-state optimality gap. We keep those contracts separate and connect them by a direct Bellman calculation. Whole-state cells and complete continuous-action covers give a lower bound for the original optimum; feasible upper candidates give an implemented policy. Centering both bounds at the same reference value retains pointwise cancellation before a width is formed. A new fixed-policy verification applies that lower bound to the actual policies already returned by the prospective services, without retraining, replacing an action or changing an initial distribution. The convergence argument includes the fixed-cover stability property used by the executable endpoint maps; mere inclusion of a true value is not mistaken for stability under propagated errors.

The empirical analysis distinguishes an implementation comparison from a policy comparison. Matched exact solvers can return the same policy at different computation costs. Different generators can instead return different policies, whose economic ranking requires direct cost evidence. Complete services in dimensions two, four and eight and horizons two, four and six pay for fitting, all attempted searches, whole-cell verification, sequential cost inference and durable return. The current comparison includes common-action, native quadratic, screened ReLU and native ReLU configurations at the same declared targets. Cold compilation and repeated-use work are reported separately. Earlier tree, compiled-witness, tensor-FVI and adaptive comparisons remain part of the evidence, including their unfavorable outcomes and failed targets.

A broad stress catalogue examines crossing features, cancellation, flatness, exact ties, endpoint resolution and terminating fallbacks. Eight implementations separate several computational mechanisms. A further native two-by-two experiment crosses activation reduction with exhaustive or finite-difference search, holding the programming language and exact objective fixed. Its repeated queries and batch-size interventions isolate effects that a comparison between an unreduced exhaustive implementation and a reduced piecewise implementation cannot isolate. These query experiments do not replace the complete-service clocks. Likewise, fewer exact comparisons do not by themselves imply a lower running time, a cheaper conventional comparison, or a better economic policy.

\paragraph{Related methods.}
Approximate and fitted policy iteration have established approximation-error analyses \citep{munos2003,munos2008,scherrer2015}. Conservative and safe improvement relate a policy update to its incumbent \citep{kakade2002,pirotta2013,metelli2021}, and tree-based fitted iteration is an important conventional alternative \citep{ernst2005}. The action-contrast development distinguishes fixed-policy residual transport from state bisimulation metrics, reward transformations and value-equivalent model classes \citep{ferns2004,ng1999,grimm2020}. These are the inherited principles on which the policy account builds.

Interval exclusion and subdivision are standard verification ideas \citep{moore2009}. Discrete convex analysis supplies a broader language for finite-difference structure \citep{murota2003}; the signed neural objective here need not be globally convex. The specific additional result is an exact witness construction for its concave-prefix/convex-tail pieces, with the original tie rule, explicit rational work and a transfer to the same acquired economic policy. The paper's contribution is the combined construction--witness--verification--return chain, not a claim that safe improvement, interval pruning or exact arithmetic is intrinsically neural.

The article establishes this chain for finite-horizon expected costs. The complete development article and proof companion retain the recursive-preference, temporal-self, diffusion and game applications under their original hypotheses. All prior theorem statements, original introduction files, experimental records and adverse comparisons remain in the revision and its preservation map. The current introduction organizes that material around the economic contracts being proved and measured; it does not substitute a different model for Neural Bellman Operators.
