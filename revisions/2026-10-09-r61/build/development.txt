4        A continuation value is useful to an economist because it changes a decision. Its     4

5    prediction error is not the loss of the policy actually implemented. Nor is a smaller     5

6    upper bound on that loss evidence that one policy has lower cost than another. When       6

7    observation, action selection and verification are costly, the numerical procedure        7

8    and the economic decision have to be evaluated together.                                  8

9        We develop Neural Bellman Operators as a policy-evaluation and feasible- 9
10   improvement method. The economic object remains a controlled economy with                 10

11   policy-specific continuations. The operator constructs each date’s continuation us- 11
12   ing its own fitted future, returns a feasible policy, and accounts for its loss against   12

13   the Bellman optimum. A general signed policy sandwich permits arbitrary fitted            13

14   candidates. A constructive affine–ReLU backend additionally produces the candi- 14
15   date from primitive moduli, without assuming that a nonconvex training algorithm          15

16   eventually succeeds. Exact compilation retains the original action witness through        16

17   off-grid evaluation and state-dependent feasibility repair.                               17

18       The principal question in this revision is how that construction produces a cer- 18
19   tified economic answer with finite resources. State coverage, action search and           19

20   integration have different error coefficients. Tying them to one grid size conceals       20

21   the allocation problem. We derive a separated primitive budget and an optimal             21

22   continuous allocation for its leading work proxy, including lower resource bounds         22

23   and dyadic implementation. The empirical comparison then records the complete             23

24   positive-tolerance frontier of a fixed common resource ladder, not only an isolated       24

25   favorable target. All failed earlier attempts belong to the work required to return a     25

26   policy.                                                                                   26

27       The next question is what the returned policy does. We evaluate the actual policies   27

28   at the declared first crossings, even when two generators cross at different reso- 28
29   lutions. Independent interval simulation preserves the original continuous initial        29

30   and innovation laws and encloses discontinuous actors and robust capacity repair. 30
31   Conditioning on construction information permits simultaneous cost inference after        31

32   this selection. The result does not infer a policy ranking from two certificates and      32
                                 NEURAL BELLMAN OPERATORS                                3

1    does not extrapolate three deterministic timing repetitions into an optimizer-success    1

2    probability.                                                                             2

3      Two economic decisions make this distinction operational. First, a controller          3

4    replacement is worthwhile only if its actual gain exceeds the replacement fee. We        4

5    identify the directional gain intervals and the full break-even fee account, retaining   5

6    but no longer relying on a single generous charge. Second, observation precision is      6

7    chosen on the same finite bit grid on which the implemented policies are evaluated. 7
8    A simultaneous upper-net-cost selector has a regret bound relative to the best           8

9    precision in that catalogue. The bound holds for every nonnegative information           9

10   price on one coverage event. This is a result about actual net policy cost, different    10

11   from minimizing a conservative all-state loss account.                                   11

12     These advances do not depend on calling a classical envelope neural. A native          12

13   min-plus envelope and its exactly equivalent ReLU circuit are one method with            13

14   two encodings. The original-owner compiler is available to both. We compare that         14

15   method with uniform multilinear fitted-value iteration (FVI) and two separately          15

16   recorded conventional refinement rules. The original curvature-bisection block           16

17   remained uniform; the later graded block was nonuniform. A new residual-driven           17

18   comparator below records its realized geometry and all pilot work. Each generates        18

19   its own future labels and receives the same feasible action menu, continuous-law         19

20   integration and one-sided economic criterion. The evidence must determine the cost       20

21   of the returned policy as well as certificate sharpness. No uniform neural dominance     21

22   is built into the design.                                                                22

24     Related methods. Lipschitz extension supplies the cone construction (McShane,          24

25   1934), and separable distance transforms supply its linear-work nodal engine             25

26   (Felzenszwalb and Huttenlocher, 2012). Approximation in min-plus spaces has              26

27   an established dynamic-programming literature: Lakshminarayanan and Bhatnagar            27

28   (2014) study min-plus projected Bellman equations and supremum-norm errors. 28
29   Bellman-inequality methods likewise construct lower value bounds and policies;           29

30   Wang et al. (2015) use iterated inequalities with linear or semidefinite optimization. 30
31   Our argument does not claim priority for these principles. Its subject is their con- 31
32   nection to an own-future feasible policy, original-owner continuous compilation,         32
     4

1    acquired-state implementation, complete resource allocation and direct economic           1

2    comparison of the returned objects. The empirical-Bernstein component uses the            2

3    bounded-sample inequality of Maurer and Pontil (2009); conditional selection and          3

4    numerical path enclosures specify the estimand to which it is applied.                    4

5        The broader NBO program includes controlled diffusions, recursive utility, en- 5
6    dogenous preferences, temporal selves and games. Those results and their as- 6
7    sumptions are retained in the complete development edition and its full proof             7

8    supplement, together with the original training, matrix-factor and precision experi- 8
9    ments. The present article supplies a self-contained constructive theorem–evidence        9

10   chain. Its direct-cost statistical theorems concern expected discounted costs, not an     10

11   undeclared extension to every nonlinear recursive application. This organization          11

12   preserves the paper’s economic subject while making the current contribution and          12

13   the actual comparison burden explicit.                                                    13

14       The additional improvement result addresses the economically important case           14

15   where the certified construction returns a more costly policy than a conventional         15

16   competitor. We preserve that incumbent and permit only actions whose actual               16

17   reference advantage is certified nonpositive after the numerical and observation          17

18   allowances. At the final date of the original investment model, exact continuous- 18
19   law integration makes this gate executable without assuming a successful policy- 19
20   value fit. Both generators receive the same repair. The resulting experiment isolates     20

21   removable final-action loss from the policy gap that remains; it does not rename the      21

22   compiler as a uniquely neural advantage.                                                  22

23       The relevant continuation error can be smaller than its global range. We show that    23

24   a coupled-residual certificate controls the error in feasible action comparisons, and     24

25   use it in a stronger finite-sweep bound for the same Bellman optimum. A verified          25

26   nonconstant action-null component need not consume any improvement budget. 26
27   The original investment dynamics admit an explicit one-unit ReLU example. An              27

28   exhaustive observation-cell diagnostic holds the returned economic policies fixed         28

29   while varying this nuisance; it tests certificate robustness, not another expected-cost   29

30   ranking.                                                                                  30

18     Policy iteration, invariance, and state metrics. Approximate policy iteration already   18

19   has a substantial error-propagation theory; Munos (2003) studies approximation            19

20   errors in policy iteration. Conservative policy iteration connects improvement to         20

21   an incumbent and a performance criterion (Kakade and Langford, 2002), and safe            21

22   policy iteration develops related monotonic-improvement guarantees (Pirotta et al.,       22

23   2013). We do not claim the general idea of safe improvement. Our finite-horizon           23

24   statement requires an acquired-cell feasible action, a verified directed advantage        24

25   enclosure, and a lower cover of the entire continuous feasible action set. Those          25

26   conditions identify the error that enters each pass and make the economic cost of         26

27   verification explicit.                                                                    27

28     Couplings also occur in bisimulation metrics: Ferns, Panangaden, and Precup             28

29   (2004) relate state distances to optimal values. Here the transported object is the       29

30   signed evaluation error of a fixed incumbent, and the pair computation may use            30

31   any verified coupling with the correct marginals. It is neither a proposed state- 31
32   aggregation metric nor an assumption that a transport optimum can be obtained             32

                               4. COST-DIRECTED FEASIBLE IMPROVEMENT                                          7

8      A policy certificate answers a safety question relative to the optimum. It need                        8

9    not answer the comparison with an installed controller. We now make actual cost                          9

10   reduction a constraint on the improvement step. The continuation representation,                         10
