1        This paper develops Neural Bellman Operators (NBOs) along that chain. The          1

2    constructive implementation evaluates each continuation against its own fitted         2

3    future and retains feasible action witnesses. Its exact affine–ReLU realization and    3

4    primitive error budget do not require unrestricted nonconvex training to succeed. 4
5    A complementary recursive implementation queries the original Bellman problem          5

6    where action search requires information. A trained proposal can change query          6

7    locations, but verified endpoint bounds and protected conventional refinement          7

8    determine validity. These are distinct uses of neural approximation; success of a      8

9    common certificate is not evidence that every neural component improves complete       9

10   work.                                                                                  10

11       The investment economy has state-dependent capacity, bilinear transitions and      11

12   one or two independent continuous innovations. Primitive curvature bounds the          12

13   transition’s convexity defect and supplies regularity of the original Bellman value. 13
14   Recursive certification can dispense with a stored state lattice, but not with the     14

15   descendant cost of an original-value query. A strong-convexity upper chord can         15

16   certify a rounded action before another endpoint query; the method pays for safe- 16
17   guarded refinement whenever the screen fails. A fixed-feature readout has a convex     17

18   certificate-aware objective and an auditable optimization gap. Neither a neural        18

19   continuation nor an arbitrary proposal is assumed convex.                              19

20       The attained continuation provides a separate route to original-law cost eval- 20
21   uation. Its residual oscillations and implemented greedy gaps control both the         21

22   observation range and original-optimum policy loss. The terminal correction allows     22

23   an inexact fitted terminal value, and state-independent shifts cancel. For shallow     23

24   affine–ReLU continuations, conditional expectations under the original one- and        24

25   two-innovation laws are finite positive-part moment calculations. Explicit state- 25
26   and-action covers qualify residuals without querying the optimal Bellman tree. 26
27   Conditional on the actually fitted future, qualification-optimal readouts minimize     27

28   that verified account; exact primal–dual checks certify the achieved gap rather than   28

29   relying on solver status. Backward construction then returns the complete acquired     29

30   controller that is evaluated.                                                          30

31       Purchased original-value information can also be reused. A lower Bellman bound     31

32   and a feasible upper witness are transported only after paying for state displace- 32

1    ment, capacity repair and action rounding. Failed checks invoke the original verifier. 1
2    The finite-cover fill bound states its retention and precision premises explicitly; the    2

3    executed bounded cache receives only the guarantees its actual contract supports. 3
4    Predictable qualifications and time-uniform cost intervals separately address when         4

5    to stop purchasing information. Fixed-controller costs and conditional average             5

6    costs of changing controllers are not interchanged, and failed numerical evaluations       6

8      The evidence distinguishes these constructions. The retained action-readout cat- 8
9    alogue compares certificate-aware ReLU and equally sized Bernstein proposals,              9

10   independent-context validation, sixty-four-block reuse and trained two-control ex- 10
11   tensions. The recursive-controller cost comparisons meet their declared equivalence        11

12   margin without identifying strict superiority; adverse cold-start, fitting and reuse       12

13   outcomes remain visible. Localized witnessed-reuse workloads count first fills and         13

14   every descendant on a miss. They are not policy-cost observations or a long-horizon        14

15   neural speed result.                                                                       15

16     The later original-law experiment evaluates the attained continuation actors             16

17   themselves. ReLU and Bernstein constructions at each acquisition resolution use            17

18   nine coefficients, common limits, primitive information and a matched acquisition          18

19   contract. Their actual fitted futures are propagated backwards. Two estimators of the      19

20   same complete-policy costs use one frozen simultaneous family: direct cash cost and        20

21   exact last-innovation conditional integration. The resulting intervals identify lower      21

22   ReLU-controller costs at both matched resolutions and order the five constructed           22

23   controllers. This is a positive finite comparison between specified attained fits, not     23

24   an ordering of all conventional or neural methods.                                         24

25     The economic decision uses those same costs. Sharp difference-constraint bounds          25

26   retain all paid marginal and paired information when choosing a controller at              26

27   declared resource prices. Deployment allocation extends the decision when neither          27

28   acquisition precision is uniformly best after its fee. The controller is selected before   28

29   state observation, so a share means a mixture of complete feasible policies rather         29

30   than averaged actions or network coefficients. The new allocation theorem gives            30

31   an attained linear-programming certificate, exact setup-cost thresholds and an             31

32   implementable finite-cohort choice. It remains valid for data-dependent prices             32

5        Relation to existing methods. Lipschitz extensions and distance transforms under- 5
6    lie the constructive envelope (McShane, 1934, Felzenszwalb and Huttenlocher, 2012). 6
7    Approximate and safe policy iteration have established error analyses (Munos, 2003,       7

8    Pirotta et al., 2013, Scherrer et al., 2015), as does convex dynamic-programming          8

9    approximation (Yang, 2020). Learned warm starts and optimization-based control            9

10   supply related numerical methods (Sakaue and Oki, 2023, Sambharya, Hall, Amos,            10

11   and Stellato, 2023, Agrawal, Barratt, Boyd, and Stellato, 2020). Value-function con- 11
12   trol variates and telescoping identities are established evaluation tools (Munos, 2006,   12

13   Jiang and Li, 2016). We connect these ideas to a verified residual-oscillation account    13

14   for an attained own-future continuation, continuous-law numerical enclosures and          14

15   the implemented controller’s cost.                                                        15

16       Time-uniform concentration and off-policy confidence sequences have their own         16

17   theory (Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe, and Sekhon, 2020, Karampatziakis, Mineiro,             17

18   and Ramdas, 2021). Our observations are direct original-law contrasts, and the            18

19   contribution identifies the paid numerical qualification needed to use those concen- 19
20   tration tools. Minimax-regret decisions have an established economic role (Manski,        20

21   2004); shortest-path difference constraints are classical (Floyd, 1962). Our deploy- 21
22   ment result uses the common attaining cost potentials of the paid interval system         22

23   to obtain an exact allocation and installation account. It is conditional on qualified    23

24   costs, not a new unconditional statistical-risk theorem.                                  24

                  4. ORIGINAL-OPTIMUM ACCURACY IN THE INVESTMENT ECONOMY                        4

5      The exact witness theorem answers an implementation question conditional on              5

6    a continuation. An economist also needs to know how close the complete imple- 6
7    mented policy is to the original optimum. We now establish that connection in the          7

8    investment economy itself. The argument exploits a property not used by the earlier        8

9    cell-range calculation: despite the bilinear transition, the Bellman objective remains     9

10   jointly convex. This permits a second-order interpolation account and transfers            10

11   verified nodal decisions to an implementable policy over the entire state domain. It       11

12   does not assume that a fitted neural continuation is convex.                               12

9                                                                                                             9
       Neural Bellman Operators connect attained continuation approximation to fea-
10                                                                                                            10
     sible policy decisions under the original economic law. The constructive readout,
11                                                                                                            11
     uniform qualification and direct controller-cost experiment form one chain; optional
12                                                                                                            12
     query prediction and witnessed reuse form distinct work mechanisms. Exact inte-
13                                                                                                            13
     gration removes optimal-value descendants from evaluation of a qualified shallow
14                                                                                                            14
     continuation, while the original fitting, qualification and every failed reuse remain
15                                                                                                            15
     paid operations.
16                                                                                                            16
       The matched continuation experiment identifies lower ReLU-controller costs at
17                                                                                                            17
     both acquisition resolutions. Its joint intervals also determine the constructed-policy
18                                                                                                            18
     ordering and conditional purchase regions. The deployment theorem extends those
19                                                                                                            19
     observations to sharp expected-regret allocations, additional installation costs and
20                                                                                                            20
     finite cohorts. It does not turn decision-theoretic regret reduction into a newly
21                                                                                                            21
     observed cost gain. Actual monetary conclusions require the stated loss scale,
22                                                                                                            22
     deployment count and resource charges. All historical adverse selector findings and
23                                                                                                            23
