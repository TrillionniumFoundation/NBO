\section{Recursive utility and a controlled BSDE verification bridge}\label{supp:bridge109}
The NDU formulation studies Neural Differential Utility through a preference-state FBSDE under general martingale information \citep{QiNDU2025}. Its information process and preference dynamics cannot be replaced silently by an AVaR innovation or a Brownian motion. The result below establishes a separate, explicit bridge for a Brownian Markov subcase with a decoupled augmented forward state. It is a verification theorem for that declared recursive-utility problem, not a claim that every coupled NDU system has this form or that the investment experiment solves it. The classical Lipschitz BSDE foundation is due to \citet{PardouxPeng1990}; its recursive-utility connections are developed by \citet{ElKarouiPengQuenez1997}.

Fix a finite horizon $T$, a Brownian natural filtration, and an admissible progressively measurable action process $a_s$ valued in a compact set. The augmented economic and preference state solves
\[
 dX_s=b(s,X_s,a_s)\,ds+\sigma(s,X_s,a_s)\,dW_s.
\]
Assume bounded coefficients, uniform Lipschitz continuity in the state, and the usual measurability; the selected feedback policy, if any, must also define an admissible well-posed state process. The state may include latent preference coordinates. There is no dependence of $b,\sigma$ on the backward pair in this subcase. Let $g$ and $f(s,x,0,0,a)$ be bounded. The scalar cost driver satisfies
\[
 |f(s,x,y,z,a)-f(s,x,y',z',a)|\le L_y|y-y'|+L_z\|z-z'\|,
\]
and the original policy criterion is $Y_0^a$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bsde109}
 Y_s^a=g(X_T)+\int_s^T f(v,X_v,Y_v^a,Z_v^a,a_v)\,dv
                  -\int_s^T Z_v^a\,dW_v.
\end{equation}
Changing the sign of a utility to a cost requires the corresponding sign change in this driver and terminal condition. It does not identify different utility functionals.

For a bounded $u\in C^{1,2}$ with bounded derivatives needed below, set
\begin{align*}
 z_u(s,x,a)&=\sigma(s,x,a)^{\mathsf T}\nabla u(s,x),\\
 \mathcal H^a u&=\partial_su+b(s,x,a)^{\mathsf T}\nabla u
 +\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\sigma\sigma^{\mathsf T}D^2u)
 +f(s,x,u,z_u,a).
\end{align*}
On the whole-space formulation there is no artificial boundary. A bounded-domain version requires an invariant state domain, or a specified stopping boundary whose terminal error is included in the same account; deleting the boundary term is not licensed.

\begin{proposition}[Original-recursion residual certificate]\label{prop:bsde109}
For each admissible action process the forward equation and \eqref{eq:bsde109} have a unique square-integrable adapted solution. Suppose uniformly over the declared state-action domain
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal H^a u&\ge-e_-(s)\quad\hbox{for every admissible }a,\qquad
 \mathcal H^{\pi}u\le e_+(s),\\
 -\eta_-&\le g-u(T,\cdot)\le\eta_+,
\end{align*}
where $e_\pm$ are nonnegative integrable deterministic bounds and $\eta_\pm\ge0$. Then the implemented admissible policy satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bsdegap109}
 0\le Y_0^\pi-\inf_aY_0^a
 \le e^{L_yT}(\eta_-+\eta_+)
       +\int_0^T e^{L_ys}\{e_-(s)+e_+(s)\}\,ds.
\end{equation}
No existence of an optimizing control is inferred merely from BSDE solvability; the infimum in this certificate is sufficient. Additional compactness and continuity of the control-to-solution map are separate optimal-control assumptions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For completeness, the forward Picard iterates have squared maximal differences on a short interval bounded by a fixed Lipschitz constant times the interval length and the preceding difference, using the Brownian isometry and maximal inequality. Choose the interval so that this coefficient is less than one. The fixed point is a strong adapted solution; the same difference estimate proves uniqueness. Concatenating finitely many intervals covers $[0,T]$. Bounded coefficients give the required second moments. This argument applies to the stipulated admissible control process and not to a feedback for which forward well-posedness has not been established.

With this forward process fixed, consider a trial pair $(y,z)$ in the weighted square-integrable space. Brownian martingale representation of
$g(X_T)+\int_0^T f(s,X_s,y_s,z_s,a_s)\,ds$
defines the linear backward solution $(Y,Z)$ with that prescribed driver. For two trial pairs, It\^o's formula to $e^{ks}|\Delta Y_s|^2$, their common terminal condition and expectation give
\[
 \E\int_0^T e^{ks}\{k|\Delta Y_s|^2+\|\Delta Z_s\|^2\}\,ds
 \le2\E\int_0^T e^{ks}|\Delta Y_s|
       (L_y|\Delta y_s|+L_z\|\Delta z_s\|)\,ds.
\]
Young's inequality bounds the right side by the sum of
$(k/2)|\Delta Y_s|^2$, $(4L_y^2/k)|\Delta y_s|^2$ and $(4L_z^2/k)\|\Delta z_s\|^2$, integrated with the same weight. In the norm
$\E\int e^{ks}\{(k/2)|Y_s|^2+\|Z_s\|^2\}\,ds$,
the map's squared-norm contraction factor is at most
$\max\{8L_y^2/k^2,4L_z^2/k\}<1$ for sufficiently large $k$.
The weighted space is complete, and for finite $T$ its norm is equivalent to the usual one. Banach iteration proves existence and uniqueness; Brownian maximal estimates applied to the backward equation give the square-integrable supremum of $Y$. This is the Lipschitz subcase; neither general-martingale representation nor fully coupled forward dynamics has been assumed for free.

For verification fix any admissible action process and apply It\^o's formula to $u(s,X_s)$. Put $\Delta Y=Y^a-u(s,X_s)$ and $\Delta Z=Z^a-z_u(s,X_s,a_s)$. Subtracting equations shows that this pair solves a linear BSDE with terminal error $g(X_T)-u(T,X_T)$ and driver
\[
 A_s\Delta Y_s+\theta_s^{\mathsf T}\Delta Z_s+\mathcal H^{a_s}u(s,X_s),
 \qquad |A_s|\le L_y,\quad\|\theta_s\|\le L_z.
\]
Such measurable divided differences are obtained by first changing $y$ at fixed $z$, then changing $z$; when a denominator is zero set the corresponding coefficient to zero. Bounded $\theta$ satisfies Novikov's condition. Under the measure with density
$\exp\{\int\theta\,dW-\tfrac12\int\|\theta\|^2ds\}$,
$W-\int\theta ds$ is Brownian. Multiplying by $\exp(\int_0^s A_vdv)$, localizing the martingale and passing to expectations yields
\[
 \Delta Y_0=\E^{\theta}\!\left[
 e^{\int_0^TA_vdv}(g-u(T))(X_T)
 +\int_0^T e^{\int_0^sA_vdv}\mathcal H^{a_s}u(s,X_s)\,ds\right].
\]
The passage to the limit is justified by bounded coefficients and terminal/residual bounds; the exponential density has moments of every finite order, and the localized linear BSDE has its unique square-integrable limit. Alternatively the displayed conditional-expectation process is the unique linear solution by the contraction argument just proved.

The multiplier is positive and bounded above by $e^{L_ys}$. The all-action lower residual and terminal lower error therefore give
$Y_0^a\ge u(0,X_0)-e^{L_yT}\eta_- -\int_0^Te^{L_ys}e_-(s)ds$
for every admissible control. The policy-specific upper conditions give the analogous upper bound for $Y_0^\pi$. Taking the infimum in the first inequality and subtracting proves \eqref{eq:bsdegap109}. The nonnegative lower loss follows because the implemented policy is admissible.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What this bridge permits.}
A neural critic and actor may be judged by the displayed original driver, its full computational graph $z_u=\sigma^{\mathsf T}\nabla u$, its terminal or stopping-boundary discrepancy, and the all-action versus implemented-action residuals. No claim of uniform neural approximation, discretization consistency, a contraction for a fully coupled FBSDE, or convergence of a training optimizer follows from this verification result. A continuous-time NDU application would first verify its own Markov augmentation, driver assumptions, admissible policy class and numerical residual bounds. The finite investment catalogue supplies no data for that different objective. This preserves the intended connection to recursive utility and stochastic control without replacing the original economics by an easier unlabelled model.
