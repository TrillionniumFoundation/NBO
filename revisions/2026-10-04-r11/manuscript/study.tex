\section{The Economic Value of State-Dependent Consumption}\label{sec:r11study}
The quantitative question is whether conditioning consumption on the evolving, densely coupled capital state improves upon a common feasible consumption plan enough to justify the computational intervention. This question is posed in the original benchmark economy; the exercise is not a calibration to household or firm data. The relevant comparison is the schedule itself, not just another neural implementation. All reported gains are differences in the original discounted utility criterion.

\subsection{Design and primary evidence}
The final design uses ten independent training seeds in each of dimensions ten, twenty, and fifty. Each seed supplies an NBO actor, a direct-policy actor, and an affine actor. The parameter vector and dense matrix-generation rule are unchanged from R10. The primary candidate architecture has width 32, two hidden layers, radius $.1$, and 120 outer iterations. Each selected policy is evaluated from $y=0$ using 8,192 fresh paths and 1,024 time cells. A family allocation of 2,000 one-sided statements at total probability $.05$ covers the primary comparisons and all declared sensitivity rows. The final noise seed differs from the exploratory bank used during development. This is a design fixed before the final remote computation, not preregistration before all research.

\subsection{Economic normalization and stress}
Let $A=\int_0^T e^{-\rho t}dt$. We report $100\{\exp(\Delta/A)-1\}$ as a flow-consumption-equivalent normalization of a utility difference. It describes an externally financed log-flow supplement holding the reference resource allocation and other payoff terms fixed. It is not a self-financing change in consumption and is not an estimated welfare percentage for a calibrated population. A positive lower gain also bounds an admissible additional implementation charge in utility units. This interpretation makes both the magnitude of the learned benefit and its practical limits visible.

\subsection{Connection to the other economic applications}\label{sec:r11applications}
Recursive utility, endogenous preference formation, temporal selves, and dynamic games remain applications of the NBO evaluation--improvement distinction, not consequences of the additive-noise capital theorem. Their full model equations, original calculations, counterexamples, tables, and proofs have been relocated without deletion to the supplement. The recursive problem requires its utility-domain and comparison conditions. The preference certificates refer to their specified nodal and continuous-action objects, with continuous-state, time, and stopping transfer still separately accounted for. The strategic results bound deviations in the saved finite dynamic games. The sophisticated-self problem uses its own equilibrium deviation condition.

This organization preserves the original research program while placing one complete computation and its new policy-specific guarantee at the centre of the article. Neither the broader title nor the retention of those applications enlarges the scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:r11certificate}.
