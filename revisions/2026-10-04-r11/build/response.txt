            Response to the 4 October 2026 Referee Report

                                            R11 revision


     We thank the referee for the careful review of R10 and the independent reconstruction of its
continuous-capital bound. The report identifies the central distinction that motivates this revision:
a guarantee supplied by a feasible schedule and an architectural tube does not measure what the
fitted policy accomplishes. We address that distinction with a new policy-specific verification result
and a new final computational design in the same capital economy. The title, broader economic
subject, and original applications are retained. Their full equations and prior evidence are not
deleted; the main article and supplement have been reorganized with an exact-source relocation
map.
     The report considered the R10 evidence commit beginning 8ded2629; the present revision starts
from its review commit beginning 3ad498fa. The report is an owner-commissioned advisory
review, not an editorial decision of the Econometric Society. The response below concerns its
substantive mathematical and numerical objections.


Principal changes
Theorem 9 replaces the common tube allowance with an independently verified lower bound on a
fitted policy’s improvement relative to the analytical schedule. It combines an exact paired payoff
identity, the explicit continuous-time transfer estimate in Proposition 8, a proved clipping-tail
account, and a finite-family empirical Bernstein bound. The policy-specific endpoint depends on
the saved policy and its fresh outcomes. It can distinguish a useful policy from an unsuccessful one
inside exactly the same tube.
     The deployment rule is explicit. A controller updates an internal state from observed inno-
vations and holds its action between dates. The economic state remains the original continuous
diffusion. This is not an unannounced identification of an Euler policy with continuously observed-
state feedback. The guarantee is conditional statistical inference at each declared initial state, not a
deterministic state-uniform PDE certificate.
     Section 8 implements NBO evaluation using current-policy rollout and pathwise costate tar-
gets, followed by a separately differentiated actor update. Ninety primary fits cover ten seeds,
three dimensions, and three methods. The final design includes stronger direct-policy and affine
alternatives, longer budgets, tuned projected critic greedification, checkpoint frontiers, radius and
architecture tests, initial-state stresses, and deliberately poor policies. The protocol distinguishes
development from the independent final noise bank.
     The final raw-data replay contains 90 primary policies and 230 two-sided evaluation records.
Among the 30 primary NBO policies, 30 have positive lower improvement endpoints; their lower
endpoints range from 0.000716 to 0.001193. The largest NBO regret upper endpoint is 0.026672.

Blocking comments
B1: The best guarantee does not require the learned actor or critic. We agree about the logical
content of the R10 guarantee and preserve it as the analytical benchmark. It is no longer the sole
guarantee attached to a fitted policy. The new endpoint is Gd − Lϕ , where Lϕ is an independently
computed lower improvement bound for the policy actually deployed. In contrast to Gd + Dd,ϵ , it
changes with the weights and the observed paired return distribution. When Lϕ > 0, the guarantee
improves upon the schedule’s bound itself. The proof does not assume training succeeds. The critic
is used to generate a candidate through policy evaluation, while verification remains independent
of its fitted residual. This separation is intentional, rather than evidence that a critic residual was
certified when it was not.

B2: The actor has no robust gain over the schedule and is more expensive. The final experiment
now reports the schedule as the primary economic comparator and uses ten seed-level policies per
dimension. A mean Euler difference is not treated as a continuous-time gain: the sampling, clipping,
forward-arithmetic, and time-discretization terms are all subtracted before the improvement state-
ment is made. All seed rows, including unsuccessful targets, are retained. Training, verification,
and deployed decision costs are reported separately. The schedule remains the zero-training-cost
choice. The economic question is whether the verified additional gain justifies a computational
charge, not whether learning can be made costless. The R10 negative comparisons in dimensions
twenty and fifty remain in the supplement and are explicitly discussed in the new main text.

B3: Weakly tuned comparisons and unstable direct greedification. The primary design compares
the NBO actor with both direct policy optimization using the same nonlinear actor and an affine
state-dependent policy. It records equal simulator visits but does not claim equal total work: five
critic and five actor updates per NBO batch differ from a direct gradient step through the simulator.
Checkpoint cost–accuracy frontiers and 600-iteration direct-policy and affine runs expose this
difference. Additional direct-HJB critics are fitted at 200, 600, and 1,200 steps; a declared validation
search selects their budget and conservative improvement fraction. A projected direct-full policy is
labelled a hybrid, not an unrestricted direct-full solution. These experiments do not assert that a
common update count proves NBO superiority. They allow simpler and actor-free alternatives to
remain competitive in the reported record.

B4: The certificate cannot distinguish good and poor policies. Theorem 9 is policy-specific
after training. Its finite-family guarantee includes the candidate-selection and sensitivity rows
declared in the protocol. The certificate accepts neither a sampled maximum as a uniform bound
nor a measured state range as a tail cutoff. The clipping envelope follows from bounded action
separation and a Gaussian concentration calculation. The complete bias bound refers to the original
economic diffusion. The raw arrays, output clipping counts, source weights, lower and upper
payoff endpoints, and intermediate constants are stored. Zero and deliberately saturated corrections
provide explicit checks that a shared tube does not produce a shared post-training assessment.

B5: Too few seeds, conditional confidence intervals, and missing Euler error. The number of
primary training seeds is increased from three to ten. Every fitted policy is assessed separately.
Across-seed ranges and standard deviations are reported as algorithmic variability, while the
simultaneous statistical endpoints are conditional on the fitted objects. No confidence interval for
an average over three actors is presented as training robustness. Proposition 8 explicitly bounds
the policy-value transfer rather than extrapolating from grid differences. The 256-, 512-, and
1,024-cell records remain available as separate refinement diagnostics. Development used seed 2027
and a disjoint noise bank; the final protocol states when it was fixed and makes no retrospective
preregistration claim.

B6: A model-specific theorem within a broad economic paper. The capital primitives and full
adapted comparison class are preserved. The new theorem advances the verification of that
application; it is not claimed for recursive utility, stopping problems, or games without their
additional hypotheses. The original preference, recursive-utility, temporal-self, strategic, and trace
sections are retained in full in the supplement, with a main-text account of their connection to
the NBO maps. This keeps the paper’s subject intact while distinguishing a complete theorem–
algorithm–experiment chain from the different verified objects in other applications. In particular,
no capital result is substituted for a missing continuous-transfer term in the preference model.

B7: Economic significance and the depth of the numerical contribution. The revised economic
question concerns the value of state-dependent consumption relative to the common schedule
in the same dense nonquadratic economy. Equation 40 decomposes that value into production,
curvature cost, and terminal dispersion. The tables report utility differences and a clearly defined
externally financed flow-consumption-equivalent normalization. Positive lower gains also quantify
a permissible implementation charge in utility units. This is a quantitative conclusion in the stated
stylized model, not an invented population calibration. The post-training guarantee is attached to
the actual measured policy rather than an arbitrary member of a safe class. The final comparisons
determine the size of the gain and which simpler alternatives achieve comparable outcomes; the
paper does not declare a universal actor advantage.


Major comments
M1: Title and abstract. The title Neural Bellman Operators is retained because the paper continues
to develop the original evaluation–improvement–verification construction and its economic ap-
plications. The abstract now states the policy-specific result and the precise sampled-innovation
deployment rule. It no longer relies on inheriting an architectural bound as evidence of learned
economic improvement. The untrained schedule and its stronger structural benchmark remain
explicit.

M2: Safety, solution accuracy, and method superiority. These are separated throughout. Theo-
rem 7 concerns an architectural envelope; Theorem 9 gives a conditional, policy-specific economic
endpoint; the algorithm and baseline tables compare executed methods and costs. None of these
statements is used as a substitute for another. Passing the reproducibility checks is not journal
acceptance or a theorem of algorithmic superiority.

M3: Schedule and tube economic conversions. The schedule’s bound and flow-equivalent
conversion are printed alongside the original tube allowance and the new post-training endpoints.
The normalization holds other payoff terms and the reference allocation fixed and is labelled
externally financed. It is not described as a self-financing resource counterfactual.

M4: Radius–performance–guarantee frontier. The saved 0.05, 0.10, and 0.15 radius comparisons
report both paired payoffs and policy-specific bounds. Frozen-correction rescaling is distinguished
from fresh fitting. Each row receives the same declared statistical family allocation and its own
action range, transfer bound, and tail envelope. The additional width-16 and width-64 actors are
actually retrained.

M5: Diagnose direct-full failure. The revision adds budget and conservative-improvement
selection for both direct critic families, and it retains controlled derivative-perturbation and action-
saturation diagnostics. These diagnostics identify sensitivity of greedification; they are not labelled
independent estimates of the true continuous critic-gradient bias. A direct-full critic followed by a
radius projection is an explicitly named conservative hybrid. This provides a repair experiment
without treating the failure of an untuned full greedy policy as evidence that the NBO actor is
universally better.

M6: Approximate policy iteration and conservative improvement. The discussion now directly
cites conservative policy iteration, safe policy improvement, and approximate modified policy
iteration. The use of separate evaluation and improvement maps and the identity that converts a
verified improvement into a smaller regret bound are not claimed as new principles. The specific
contribution is the operational continuous-economy transfer and policy-specific error account,
paired with the executed rollout-costate NBO implementation. The empirical Bernstein bound is
attributed to its source rather than restated as a new concentration theorem.

M7: The critic’s role and diagnostic relationships. The actor uses a learned marginal continuation
value fitted against current-policy rollout costates. A held-out value/costate diagnostic is recorded
for every primary NBO seed. These stochastic-target discrepancies and their relationship to final
payoff are reported separately from the certificate. They are not described as exact derivative errors.
Verification deliberately does not trust the critic; the policy has to earn its independent payoff
bound.

M8: State-distribution stress. The final design includes positive and negative mean shifts, in-
creased dispersion, a joint shift–dispersion case, and a fixed Student-t(3) quantile profile with
heavier cross-sectional tails. All exact initial vectors, saturation frequencies, and internal-state
coverage frequencies are saved. These are controlled pointwise stress profiles, not an inferred guar-
antee under every random initial-state law. The original economic diffusion remains unbounded;
no observed state range is promoted into an invariant economic domain.

M9: Conditional arithmetic. The supplement places the complete contract in one subsection:
exact-real interpretation of stored coefficients, binary64 elementary arithmetic and dot-product
bounds, polynomial transcendental kernels, interval scalar and spectral calculations, inward action
projection, forward rounding, and the ideal-iid sampling assumption. The optional compiled
kernels disable fast-math transformations. Unit tests and independent scalar quadrature stress the

Reproduction and scope
The source and evidence commits, executed environment, complete result arrays, fitted weights and
checkpoints, test outputs, generated table hashes, and compiled PDFs are bound by the revision
manifests. The source branch and the final evidence/referee branches are distinct. Final-run
numerical failures and unmet economic targets are different fields. No failed accuracy target is
rewritten as a successful computation, and no unsuccessful seed is replaced by a more favourable
one.
    The result remains conditional statistical verification of a precisely specified controller in the
retained capital economy. General optimizer convergence, a universal ranking of neural and direct
methods, a calibrated empirical welfare claim, and continuous-transfer guarantees for the other
applications are not inferred from it. These distinctions define what the revised results establish;
they do not alter the paper’s topic or substitute a different research project for the NBO paper.
