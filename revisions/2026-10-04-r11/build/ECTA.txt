                                            1. INTRODUCTION
25                                                                                                 25

26     Quantitative economic models increasingly place several margins of adjustment               26

27   inside the same dynamic decision problem. An investor chooses consumption and                 27

28   risk exposure while managing a preference state. A sophisticated consumer chooses             28

29   today knowing that tomorrow’s self will reconsider. A firm invests while antici- 29
30   pating the investment policies of its rivals. These problems share a computational            30

31                                                                                                 31

32     Qian QI: qiqian@pku.edu.cn                                                                  32
     2

1    structure: a continuation value must correctly evaluate a policy, and that policy must      1

2    satisfy the appropriate optimality or equilibrium condition. The two requirements           2

3    are distinct. A perfectly evaluated policy need not be optimal, and an attractive           3

4    policy evaluated by an inaccurate continuation value need not improve welfare.              4

5        This paper develops Neural Bellman Operators (NBO) around this distinction. A           5

6    critic represents continuation utility. A feasible actor represents the controls that       6

7    enter the economic generator. The critic is fitted to a policy-evaluation equation,         7

8    including its terminal and boundary data. Holding the critic and its derivative chan- 8
9    nels fixed, the actor improves its own Hamiltonian. An independent maximization             9

10   step measures the remaining feasible-action gap. The resulting error decomposition          10

11   concerns economic values and deviations, rather than only parameter gradients or            11

12   the scalar quantity minimized by an optimizer.                                              12

13       The motivation remains continuous-time economic control, including recursive            13

14   utility and costly preference formation in the motivating model of Qi (2025). We do         14

15   not replace these problems by a prediction task. Instead, we make explicit how a            15

16   neural approximation is connected to the original decision problem. In the smooth           16

17   case, the connection follows from a verification bound. In the nonsmooth case, it           17

18   follows from a monotone Bellman approximation and a comparison principle. The               18

19   actor and critic may be trained by different numerical procedures in either case. 19
20   Their economic interpretation is determined by the equations they satisfy and by            20

21   the errors that are verified after training.                                                21

22       The paper makes four connected contributions. First, it specifies a neural policy- 22
23   evaluation and improvement map with explicit derivative routing, boundary condi- 23
24   tions, and an indexed evaluation guard. The guard bounds a selected-policy target           24

25   residual by construction; it does not claim convergence of Adam or an uncorrected           25

26   network. Second, it makes the complete-action error account operational through             26

27   signed interval derivatives and verified face domination, with natural union bounds         27

28   at stopping switches. A matched enclosure experiment isolates this calculation from         28

29   policy training. Third, it carries the accounts through the original preference, capital,   29

30   and strategic applications: nested-grid retraining, a model-specific compensating           30

31   transfer, an absolute continuous-time capital benchmark, and finite dynamic best            31

32   responses. Comparison, monotonicity, interval inclusion, and approximate policy             32
                                 NEURAL BELLMAN OPERATORS                                3

1    iteration remain inherited principles. The new results concern the specified guarded     1

2    map, its verified output, and its measured economic accuracy, not an unqualified         2

3    claim that a neural actor dominates classical or actor-free alternatives.                3

4      Fourth, an independently verified paired improvement tightens the continuous- 4
5    capital policy bound after training. The evaluation block uses current-policy rollout    5

6    and costate targets, while verification refers to a separately specified innovation- 6
7    driven sampled controller. A new transfer estimate does not require a global deriva- 7
8    tive bound for the learned policy. The final experiment measures improvement rela- 8
9    tive to the analytical schedule with ten training seeds, explicit time-discretization    9

10   and sampling uncertainty, and stronger tuned baselines. It preserves all earlier         10

11   adverse evidence.                                                                        11

12     The numerical program uses generic multilayer critics and actors as well as clearly    12

13   separated analytical laboratories. The finite preference and game studies train actors   13

14   using exhaustively constructed continuation-payoff labels; their results establish       14

15   neural policy representation, not avoided maximization. The continuous-action            15

16   preference study instead differentiates the actor’s own payoff, records local im- 16
17   provement as a hybrid policy, and reserves global action covering for independent        17

18   verification after training. Direct optimization and an actor-free search ablation       18

19   expose which parts of the computation produce the measured policy quality. Ad- 19
20   ditional gated and projection comparisons exploit the same economic problem              20

21   rather than weakening the baseline. Dense nonlinear capital experiments use paired       21

29                                                                                            29

30     Neural residual methods build on numerical dynamic programming and function            30

31   approximation, not on a new principle of optimality. Projection and related methods      31

32   exploit economic structure to approximate a Bellman equation; see Judd (1998). 32
     4

1    Derivative approximation by smooth neural networks provides useful represen- 1
2    tation results (Hornik et al., 1990), but does not establish that a particular finite       2

4        The differential critic is closely related to the neural PDE approach in Sirignano      4

5    and Spiliopoulos (2018). Their DGM formulation includes differential and boundary           5

6    conditions and treats HJB equations. The deep BSDE method of Han et al. (2018) also         6

7    includes an HJB application. The distinguishing object here is the combination of           7

8    an explicit feasible actor, block-specific evaluation, and economic error accounting        8

9    across control and equilibrium problems. An analytical formula for the maximizing           9

10   action is helpful when available, but is not a universal dividing line between neural       10

11   PDE methods. Likewise, recursive dependence on the value is a nonlinear zeroth- 11
12   order term; full nonlinearity in the PDE sense concerns the highest derivatives.            12

13       Recursive utility separates intertemporal substitution from risk aversion (Epstein      13

14   and Zin, 1989, Duffie and Epstein, 1992). Its infinite-horizon formulation requires         14

15   particular care about utility-process selection and transversality; Herdegen et al. 15
16   (2021) explains why a stationary algebraic solution alone is insufficient. The time- 16
17   inconsistency extension is an equilibrium problem in the sense of Björk et al. (2016),      17

18   not an ordinary control problem with a relabelled flow payoff. Finally, stochastic          18

19   approximation results require their own step-size, stability, and limiting-dynamics         19

20   conditions (Borkar, 2024). They do not convert constant-step Adam into a conver- 20
21   gence theorem for economic optimality.                                                      21

25   policy. The resulting bound concerns the original diffusion rather than a nodal             25

26   surrogate. Its stronger structural benchmark is reported alongside every fitted             26

27   policy.                                                                                     27

12     The quantitative question is whether conditioning consumption on the evolving,        12

15   original benchmark economy; the exercise is not a calibration to household or           15

22   twenty, and fifty. Each seed supplies an NBO actor, a direct-policy actor, and an       22

23   affine actor. The parameter vector and dense matrix-generation rule are unchanged       23

24   from R10. The primary candidate architecture has width 32, two hidden layers,           24

25   radius .1, and 120 outer iterations. Each selected policy is evaluated from y =         25

26   0 using 8,192 fresh paths and 1,024 time cells. A family allocation of 2,000 one- 26
27   sided statements at total probability .05 covers the primary comparisons and all        27

28   declared sensitivity rows. The final noise seed differs from the exploratory bank       28

32   original calculations, counterexamples, tables, and proofs have been relocated with- 32

4    still separately accounted for. The strategic results bound deviations in the saved       4

5    finite dynamic games. The sophisticated-self problem uses its own equilibrium             5

                                       10. CONCLUSION
14                                                                                             14

15     Neural Bellman Operators connect policy evaluation, feasible improvement, and           15

16   independent economic error accounting. The capital application now distinguishes          16

17   an architectural performance envelope from a policy-specific post-training certifi- 17
18   cate. A paired payoff identity, explicit diffusion-transfer estimate, and finite-family   18

19   sampling bound identify the value of a deployed neural correction relative to an          19

20   analytical schedule. The economic comparison remains the original continuous              20

21   problem and its full adapted policy class.                                                21

22     The rollout-costate implementation evaluates the current policy before improving        22

23   its Hamiltonian. Direct-policy, affine, and tuned critic-greedy comparisons expose        23

24   the cost and accuracy of that implementation without presuming its superiority. A         24

25   positive verified gain is evidence about a particular deployed policy; it is neither an   25

26   optimizer convergence theorem nor a claim about every initialization. The reported        26

27   improvement is a quantitative result in the stated stylized economy, not an empirical     27

28   calibration.                                                                              28

29     Recursive utility, endogenous preferences, sophisticated temporal selves, dy- 29
30   namic games, viscosity selection, and stochastic traces remain within the paper’s         30

31   economic program. Their full equations and evidence are retained in the supple- 31
32   ment, with their own domains and verification hypotheses. Extending the new               32
     38
