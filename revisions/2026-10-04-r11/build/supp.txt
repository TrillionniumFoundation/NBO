                          5.1. Smooth approximation and what it establishes                      8

9      A derivative-approximation result concerns a smooth target on a compact domain            9

10   and a family of networks whose capacity can increase. It does not imply that a fixed        10

11   network attains zero residual, that optimization finds its best approximation, or           11

12   that a nonsmooth target has classical derivatives everywhere. In a smooth problem,          12

13   increasing the representation class can reduce approximation error in the value and         13

14   derivatives. In a nonsmooth problem, NBO instead evaluates a consistent positive- 14
15   weight Bellman equation, or solves an explicitly regularized equation before taking         15

16   a justified limit.                                                                          16

17     The hard-terminal architecture is v(t, x ) = G ( x ) + ( T − t) N (t, x ). It exactly en- 17
18   forces v( T, x ) = G ( x ) independently of training. A lateral Dirichlet face needs an     18

19   additional compatible lifting and vanishing factor. For a reflected state, the Neu- 19
20   mann condition is a different constraint. A loss on only interior points leaves all of      20

21   these boundary questions unanswered. The regression test evaluates the terminal             21

22   architecture at randomly chosen states and checks exact equality, not merely a small        22

23   mean terminal error.                                                                        23

24     The recursive Epstein–Zin domain is bV > 0. An exponential output ensures                 24

25   positivity, while an interval transformation can keep bV between declared positive          25

26   barriers. If bG > 0, one terminal-compatible positive representation is                     26

27                                                                                               27
                           bv(t, x ) = exp [log(bG ( x )) + ( T − t) N (t, x )] .
28                                                                                               28

29   Its terminal value is exact. It does not by itself impose a lateral exit payoff or a        29

30   two-sided barrier. These additional conditions must be built into the architecture or       30

31   measured explicitly. Clipping an invalid utility argument inside a fractional power         31

32   changes the equation and is not an acceptable hidden numerical repair.                      32

                         9. GAMES AND INDEPENDENT BEST RESPONSES
8                                                                                               8

9      A fixed rival profile turns each player’s problem into an ordinary controlled            9

10   Markov problem, so the main verification results apply player by player. The               10

11   derivative graph must implement this reduction. During player i’s actor step, every        11

12   rival policy and every derivative of player i’s critic is detached. During player i’s      12

13   critic step, all players’ actions are detached. A sum of these reporting losses can be     13

14   logged, but differentiation of a joint-profit objective is not equivalent to those block   14

15   steps.                                                                                     15

16     The two-firm static diagnostic has best response qi = (1 − q j )/2 when interior. At     16

17   the joint-profit allocation (1/4, 1/4), the deviating firm chooses 3/8 and improves        17

18   profit from 1/8 to 9/64, a gain of 1/64. The own-action derivative is 1/4 at the           18

19   joint optimum. These numbers verify a graph-level distinction; they are not a              19

20   computation of the dynamic capital game.                                                   20

21     For the dynamic problem, a reported equilibrium should include an indepen- 21
22   dent solution of each fixed-rival best-response problem, or an upper bound on              22

23   the unilateral Hamiltonian gap over the entire verification domain. A finite list of       23

24   unsuccessful deviations does not supply such an upper bound. Multiple initializa- 24
25   tions, asymmetric profiles, boundary actions, and the market-size normalization are        25

26   part of the same experiment. The specification in the main text retains the dynamic        26

27   Cournot application but does not invent a dynamic Nash table from the static               27

28   arithmetic.                                                                                28

                              12.2. Application-specific hypotheses
13                                                                                          13

14     The stopped consumption diffusion is uniformly nondegenerate on its compact          14

15   domain; controls and coefficients are bounded and its endpoint data are continuous. 15
16   The smooth networks and strictly positive consumption map meet the regularity          16

17   and log-domain requirements of the verification argument. Both stopping-face           17

18   errors and the full action interval are checked. The upwind reference error is not a   18

19   premise of its continuous certificate.                                                 19

20     The NDU computation is a positive-weight, finite-action, finite-horizon economy      20

21   with prescribed discrete stopping and folded preference transitions. Positivity,       21

22   all terminal and wealth-face values, and every finite action are evaluated. The        22

23   continuous reflected PDE’s corner compatibility, continuous-action coverage error,     23

24   and diffusion approximation error are not inferred from these tests. Expanding a       24

25   feasible box changes the constrained economy; densifying a fixed box does not. The     25

26   sensitivity record keeps these operations separate.                                    26

27     The dense capital computation uses bounded nonlinear drift in log coordinates,       27

28   bounded positive consumption fractions, constant diffusion, and finite horizon. Its    28

29   flow has at most linear log-state growth and its terminal payoff at most quadratic     29

30   growth; these are integrable under the declared bounded-drift diffusion. The fitted    30

31   box is a sampling domain, not a state constraint. Its sampled differential residuals   31

32   do not check comparison barriers or uniform errors on Rd . Euler payoff estimates      32
                                       NBO SUPPLEMENT                                 19

1    carry both sampling error and unbounded-state/time-discretization issues not           1

2    covered by the reported Monte Carlo standard error.                                    2

3      The dynamic capital game is a fully enumerated finite approximation with             3

4    stopped joint boundaries. Stage equilibria and dynamic fixed-rival best responses      4

                          13. DESIGN, BUDGETS, AND REPRODUCIBILITY
11                                                                                          11

12     The stopped-consumption study uses float64 and the existing generic derivative       12

13   core. Identical seed labels fix initialization and collocation construction across     13

14   methods. L-BFGS iteration caps are matched, but line-search closure counts need        14

15   not match. Those counts, fit times, and separate verification times are saved. The     15

16   grid reference uses exact maximization within each upwind drift region, not the        16

17   learned actor. Reference comparisons omit four endpoint-adjacent nodes only for        17

18   finite-difference derivative comparisons; all endpoints enter the boundary check       18

19   and the interval certificate.                                                          19

20     The NDU actor predicts one of the finite feasible triples. Its normalized Bellman- 20
21   loss objective gives economically costly action mistakes more weight than ordinary     21

22   classification would. Absorbing boundary rows are excluded only from actor fitting,    22

23   because their actions have no effect; they remain in policy evaluation and verifi- 23
24   cation. The failed initial classification implementation is archived with its actual   24

25   outputs. Principal seed 11 misses the declared policy-regret target even after the     25

26   improvement; its result is not discarded. The direct finite-Bellman comparator uses    26

27   the same critic class and total inner-iteration cap.                                   27

28     The dense-capital primary study uses exact Hessians; the trace follow-up actu- 28
29   ally backpropagates through Hessian-vector products. Product and single-bank           29

30   implementations use four probes in total, but only the product splits them into        30

31   conditionally independent banks. The Monte Carlo regression separately checks          31

32   the value and gradient of the product objective with tolerances .012 and .025. An      32

29     At a given state and time, the initial action box covers the entire feasible set. Bisec- 29
30   tion replaces one closed box by two closed boxes with the same union. Their shared        30

31   face is harmless. Induction on the number of subdivisions preserves coverage. A           31

32   pruned box is retained logically through the inequality u B ≤ ci + τ, where u B is        32

2    feasible incumbent. If the incumbent later increases, the previous pruning inequality     2

3    remains true. Consequently every feasible action is either in an unresolved box or        3

4    in a pruned box with payoff at most the current threshold. Taking the maximum of          4

5    the threshold and all unresolved upper bounds dominates the action supremum,              5

6    whether or not the supremum is attained. Termination on a numerical budget does           6

7    not change this argument.                                                                 7

20     Neural Bellman Operators organize computation around policy evaluation, feasi- 20

24   economic claim concerns the policy actually deployed and the error that has been                          24

25   bounded.                                                                                                  25

27   records the indexed corrections it requires. Signed derivative enclosures reduce                          27

28   complete-action verification work without dropping unresolved boxes or differen- 28
29   tiating across a stopping discontinuity. Their numerical contribution is identified                       29

30   using identical frozen policies and matched one-step comparisons. Fresh training                          30

31   and recertification on genuinely nested grids are kept distinct from the full-domain                      31
