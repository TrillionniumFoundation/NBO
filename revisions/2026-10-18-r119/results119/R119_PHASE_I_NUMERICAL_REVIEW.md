# R119 Phase I: independent numerical review of the complete frozen experiment

**Identity:** source and protocol commit `1f33c2667e0dff89699c6d6a01d03eacfecf3f4e`. This is post-execution reporting of already deposited raw files; no scientific evaluator, fitted model, policy, sample, query acquisition, or timing was rerun.

## Main result and scientific implication

The active-tail construction has a correct independent implementation audit and a stronger exact point-information account. In the original investment experiment it lowers median complete-service time relative to the fixed 128-bin compiled service in 12 of 16 arm/risk cells. Against the stronger inherited geometric full-support service it is faster in only one of 16 cells, by about 0.53%; that cell wins only three of seven paired clocks. ReLU and its exactly equivalent conventional hinge are slower with active tails against both existing services at both risk levels. These results establish no neural-specific advantage or practical general adoption region for Phase I.

All three service comparisons return exactly the same complete original policy within every arm, state and risk: 2,304 identical pairs for each comparison. Every within-arm economic objective contrast is exactly zero. A change in certificate width or service workload therefore does not constitute a policy improvement in this episode. The method remains an original Bellman certificate construction; no objective, information set, continuation controller or economic model was changed.

The strongest computational diagnosis is the mismatch between cheaper risk information and complete decision cost. The 43 rejected ReLU proposals at each risk and 35/37 rejected spline proposals exhaust the entire six-stage tail schedule, then invoke the same inherited repair. Phase I pays tail localization, empty-tail arrays, repeated certificates and eventual repair. The all-arm primary risk-point count falls from 654,836 under fixed compiled service to 293,844 under active tails, but rises from 223,767 under the stronger geometric service. All-arm primary serving time is 8.42052 s compiled, 6.79235 s geometric and 7.31623 s active-tail.

This diagnosis can justify a separately frozen Phase II that stops unnecessary tail work earlier and evaluates only positive-weight tails. Such a new design is informed by these observed results. It must preserve this entire Phase I, reacquire its declared models, retain all arms and complete work, and identify its own prospective source; it is not an unseen confirmation of Phase I.

The primary source-bound campaign completed 6,912 catalogue decisions and 10,752 timing decisions in seven fresh, sequential processes. Its internal elapsed time was 85.63236 s; parent external execution receipt records the larger wrapper wall time. The native exact record audit passed 444,053 checks across 384 raw service files and 25,161 attempted certificates. This second, separately written post-execution audit checked all 384 content hashes and passed 152,056 further exact arithmetic, feasibility, qualification, reference and identity checks. No exact rational fallback was entered in a primary decision.

Independent whole-support economic references used 1,024 bins and 983 distinct original state/action/risk queries, costing 9.49030 s. They share the previously independently checked terminal compiler but use a risk partition independent of active tails. Their outputs are outward original-policy and original-optimum bounds, not exact regret observations. The largest reference policy-regret upper bound is 0.00285148834, below the unchanged 1/256 requirement.

The fitting metric is not ordered like economic performance. At alpha 1/2 the degree-three polynomial’s recorded fitting loss is about 0.134957, compared with the quadratic fit’s 0.512575, yet its mean original-regret upper bound is approximately 0.000215969 versus 0.000153657. It does not turn post-execution fitting comparisons into a new acquisition experiment.
