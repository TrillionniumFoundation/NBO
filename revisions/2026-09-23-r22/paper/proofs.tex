\section{Proofs for nonsynchronized policy comparison}\label{app:r22proofs}
\subsection{Weighted variances and the Jensen upper}
For weights $\lambda$ summing to one, the exact identity
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i\lambda_i\|z_i-\bar z\|^2
 =\sum_{i<k}\lambda_i\lambda_k\|z_i-z_k\|^2,
 \qquad \bar z=\sum_i\lambda_i z_i,
 \label{eq:r22variance}
\end{equation}
follows by expansion. In particular, $\sum_{i<k}\lambda_i\lambda_k=(1-\sum_i\lambda_i^2)/2\leq3/8$ for four experts. For scalar points with range $D$, the weighted variance is at most $D^2/4$: translate the range to $[0,D]$, use $z^2\leq Dz$, and maximize $D\bar z-\bar z^2$.

On $A_t=\{|.05W_t^u|\leq.2\}$ the auxiliary preferences lie in $[1.78,2.42]$, since their means lie in $[1.98,2.22]$. The same interval contains their weighted average. Consumption and its average lie in $[.5,.8]$. Taylor's formula with integral remainder, expanded about the weighted average, eliminates the weighted linear term. Taking absolute Hessian bounds gives
\[
 f(\bar u,\bar c)-\sum_i\lambda_i f(u_i,c_i)
 \leq\tfrac12H_{uu}\operatorname{Var}_\lambda(u)
 +H_{uc}\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_\lambda(u)\operatorname{Var}_\lambda(c)}
 +\tfrac12H_{cc}\operatorname{Var}_\lambda(c).
\]
The common preference shock cancels in every pair difference, so its weighted variance equals the variance of the deterministic means and is at most $D_j^2/4$ on slab $j$. Identity~\eqref{eq:r22variance} and the directed pair integrals imply
\[
 \int_{I_j}e^{-.04t}\E[\operatorname{Var}_\lambda(c(t))]dt\leq V_j^c.
\]
The expectation may be restricted to $A_t$ without increasing this nonnegative upper bound. Cauchy--Schwarz, on the joint time-probability measure $e^{-.04t}dt\,dP$, bounds the cross term by $\sqrt{V_j^uV_j^c}$. Correlation of the Brownian motions is immaterial to this argument; independence has not been assumed.

On the common no-exit event, all preferences lie in the original domain. The joint utility is concave there by the determinant calculation in the retained proof, and its range is contained in $[-6,0]$. Thus the nonnegative Jensen gap on $A_t^c$ is at most six. The two-sided Mills inequality gives $P(A_t^c)\leq2\phi(4)/4$ for every $t\leq1$. The effort contribution is exactly $k\operatorname{Var}_\lambda(\theta)/2$; for $k=2$ it is bounded by $T_j^2/4$. These observations prove the running part of \eqref{eq:r22gamma}.

At the terminal date the exact subaccount wealth is its reserve $R_i$. The quadratic preference term has Jensen gap $.02\operatorname{Var}_\lambda(m_i(1))\leq.005D_T^2$. Since $-(\log x)''=1/x^2\leq1/R_-^2$, the log-wealth term contributes at most $.0125D_R^2/R_-^2$. Both are discounted by $e^{-.04}$.

The event $E=\{\sup_{t\leq1}|.05W_t^u|<.58\}$ prevents preference exit for every expert and the mixture; the financial decoder prevents wealth exit. The reflected Gaussian tail bound gives $P(E^c)\leq p_E$. On $E$, stopped and full-horizon expressions agree. On its complement, both the mixture payoff and a convex combination of expert payoffs lie in $[-15,1]$, so replacing their difference costs at most $16p_E$. This proves the upper bound with \eqref{eq:r22gamma}. Joint concavity on $E$ and the same complement allowance prove the lower bound. Subtract the old upper from the new lower to obtain \eqref{eq:r22gain}. Finally, $V\leq W$ and convexity of $W$ give the regret upper. If $r_1=V-J^1$ and $r_0=V-J^0$, then $r_0\geq r_1+d$ and $0\leq r_1\leq B$, whence $r_1/r_0\leq B/(B+d)$.\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Certification of the pair moments}\label{app:r22quadrature}
With $v=\sqrt t$ and $z$ standard normal under the physical measure,
\[
 \log Y_t=-.025v^2+.3vz.
\]
For fixed slab coefficients the integrand of a pair moment is
\[
 2v e^{-.04v^2}\phi(z)
 \left[.3\sigma\{b_i-s_i(-.025v^2+.3vz)\}
       -.3\sigma\{b_k-s_k(-.025v^2+.3vz)\}\right]^2.
\]
The checker uses rationally isolated Gauss nodes on directed cells in $v$ and $z$, with $|z|\leq8$. The Gaussian tail omitted from a pair moment is at most $.09\,2\phi(8)/8$; using this bound separately on every time slab is conservative.

We justify the analytic constant used by the inherited quadrature-error routine for this new integrand. In simultaneous complex discs of radii $1/16$ in $v$ and $1/2$ in $z$, centered at real $v\in[0,1]$ and $z\in[-8,8]$, the imaginary part of the logistic argument is bounded by
\[
 6.5\left\{.025\,2\frac{17}{16}\frac1{16}
       +.3\left(\frac{17}{16}\frac12+\frac{17}{2}\frac1{16}\right)\right\}
 <2.094<\frac94<\frac{3\pi}{4}.
\]
Consequently the logistic function has no pole in these discs and its modulus is at most $\sqrt2<1.5$. The complex consumption modulus is below $.95$, the Gaussian density modulus is below one, and $|2v e^{-.04v^2}|<3$. The pair integrand therefore has modulus less than $11$, hence less than the deliberately conservative Cauchy constant 100. The machine check obtains the still sharper product upper bound \RXXIIComplexBound{}; the proof uses 100 throughout.

All cell endpoints, node-isolation errors, tensor-product quadrature remainders, and Gaussian tails are added outward. The global quadrature remainder is applied to each slab separately, never divided by the number of slabs. The resulting six pair integrals enter \eqref{eq:r22moments}; numerical similarity of policies is not used as a substitute for a pair bound. This construction establishes an arithmetic certificate conditional on the stated analytic proof. It is not proof-assistant verification or independent validation of the economic model.

\subsection{A simpler range certificate, retained as a cross-check}
An alternative bound needs no pair integrations. Put $L=97/40$. The Gaussian representation yields $P(|\log Y_t|>L)\leq2\phi(8)/8$ for $t\leq1$. On $|\log Y_t|\leq L$, the maximum range of affine logits is attained at an endpoint of $[-L,L]$:
\[
 A_j=\max_{\ell\in\{-L,L\}}\left\{\max_i(b_{ij}-s_{ij}\ell)-\min_i(b_{ij}-s_{ij}\ell)\right\}.
\]
The maximal difference of two logistic values whose arguments differ by at most $A_j$ is $\tanh(A_j/4)$, attained at symmetric arguments. Hence consumption range is at most $C_j=.3\tanh(A_j/4)$. The running Jensen upper on slab $j$ is
\[
 M_j\{H_{uu}D_j^2+2H_{uc}D_jC_j+H_{cc}C_j^2+kT_j^2\}/8,
\]
with the additional six-unit allowance for the union of preference and factor tails. The same terminal and stopping corrections apply. This proves the independently executable range formula without requiring equal slopes or drifts. The range and pair-moment bounds are both retained; either can be used, or their smaller certified upper can be selected.

\section{Proofs for global verification and cover complexity}\label{app:r22global}
\subsection{Residual corrections}
For $w^+=v+\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell\psi_\ell$, linearity of $D^a$ gives $R^aw^+=R^av+\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell D^a\psi_\ell$. Nonnegative coefficients permit interval upper bounds to be summed without reversing any inequality. On a state box $B$ and an action cell $C$, sufficient linear constraints are
\[
 \overline{R^av}(B,C)+\sum_\ell\alpha_\ell\overline{D^a\psi_\ell}(B,C)\leq0.
\]
For $w^-=v-\sum_\ell\beta_\ell\psi_\ell$, sufficient constraints are
\[
 \underline{R^\pi v}(B)-\sum_\ell\beta_\ell\overline{D^\pi\psi_\ell}(B)\geq0.
\]
A complete action cover or an exact continuous-action oracle is required; sampled maximizing actions do not suffice. Trace inequalities must be checked on their specified stopping faces. Discounted It\^o's formula, localization, and the integrability conditions already stated for stopped verification yield $J^a\leq w^+$ for every admissible $a$ and $J^\pi\geq w^-$. Taking the supremum over $a$ proves Proposition~\ref{prop:r22cone}. The argument is unchanged for a vector diffusion with nonreplicable shocks, provided the corresponding generator, admissibility, and integrability conditions hold.

For a feasible fallback, $D^a1=-\rho$ and $D^aC_\rho(T-t)=-1$. Nonnegative constants dominate bounded stopping-trace discrepancies, and nonnegative multiples of $C_\rho$ dominate finite positive and negative residual errors. For time-slab corrections $\psi_j(t)=\int_t^T e^{-\rho(s-t)}1_{I_j}(s)ds$, one has $D^a\psi_j=-1_{I_j}$ away from slab endpoints. Values match at the endpoints, so piecewise discounted It\^o integration applies. This proves the time-slab correction program and recurrence~\eqref{eq:r22suffix}. The envelope's monotonicity within a slab proves the all-start-time maximum rule.\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Expert covers and adaptive verification}
A simplex represents each point by nonnegative barycentric weights on $d+1$ vertices. Under the stated affine-mixture conditions, weighted subaccounts preserve the state law and admissibility. Concavity and the separately bounded common-event complement transfer vertex payoffs; convexity of the upper comparison transfers its upper values. No statement about arbitrary nonlinear state mixtures is implied. A regular $d$-dimensional grid has $(n+1)^d$ distinct vertices; the standard ordering of fractional coordinates divides each of its $n^d$ cells into $d!$ simplices. Active price evaluation requires one set of at most $mq_tq_z$ integrand evaluations per active expert, proving the stated cost and storage bounds. The exact conditional-price object need not possess a closed-form elementary integral.

For the adaptive interval statement, subdivide until $Ch^\alpha<\eta$. The strict margin then separates every interval upper from the failure threshold. A dyadic cover reaches such a diameter after finitely many levels, with a number of boxes of the indicated order. The assertion is conditional on uniform consistency and a positive margin; it does not prove that the full-domain error objective has been attained. Nor does reducing the number of active experts to $d+1$ eliminate the number of distinct vertices needed for a global library.\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Root invariance and supervisory recovery}
For a fixed quadrature rule, the financed budget function $P(a)$ is continuous and strictly increasing in the common offset $a$ whenever finite logistic coefficients are used. Its limits are $.5\int_0^1e^{-.02t}dt$ and $.8\int_0^1e^{-.02t}dt$. The chosen budget lies strictly between them. A finite bracket therefore exists. Shifting every raw intercept by $h$ and the root by $-h$ leaves the exact delivered consumption stream unchanged. Centering the intercepts is a numerical choice of this redundant common-offset coordinate, not a change of the policy family. Geometric bracket expansion remedies a failed fixed bracket, but the independent financing check remains necessary because the proposal uses finite quadrature and finite arithmetic.

Exact rollback restores the proposal state. If the next proposal is a deterministic function of that state, it repeats after rejection. A supervisory state outside the rolled-back proposal state is therefore necessary for deterministic recovery by step-size modification. In the executed recovery rule, the state-hash comparison precedes the learning-rate change. The observed recovery is a finite execution result; no convergence rate or universal acceptance guarantee follows from it.
