\subsection{A numerical financing failure and its repair}\label{sec:r22root}
In the retained local preflight of the registered experiment, the neural L-BFGS-B proposal for ensemble 22000 at $(1.98,1.26)$ terminates numerically but fails the independent reserve check. Its reserve enclosure is approximately $[.4774000812,.4774000935]$, below the required $.5$. The fixed proposal root bracket $[-10,10]$ does not contain the budget root for those raw coefficients. The correct decision is rejection. The frozen failing parameters, actor, and check are retained as a regression fixture; any corresponding rejection in the reference execution also retains its incumbent in Table~\ref{tab:r22crossed}. Optimizer convergence is not a financing theorem.

A subsequent, separately specified diagnostic centers the raw intercepts, expands the budget-root bracket until it encloses the quadrature budget, and performs 80 bisections before implicit differentiation. The independent checker is unchanged. A common intercept shift of 1,000 leaves the delivered policy invariant to within \RXXIIShiftError{} in the numerical diagnostic. Re-budgeting the frozen failed network restores a valid reserve but does not make that network a competitive optimum. Rerunning the four configurations in the selected block gives the complete diagnostic results in the supplement. They are not substituted for the prospective primary outcomes. In particular, the repaired neural L-BFGS-B run remains less accurate than direct L-BFGS-B.

\subsection{The gate is exercised, and rollback alone is not recovery}\label{sec:r22stress}
The prospective stress test uses seed 22300, Adam learning rate $.5$, and eight successive blocks of 25 gradient evaluations at the first vertex. It accepts \RXXIIStressAccepted{} blocks and rejects \RXXIIStressRejected{}. Each rejection restores network parameters, optimizer state, and the Python, NumPy, and PyTorch random-number states exactly. This validates the restoration path but also exposes a limitation: with the same deterministic proposal rule, rejected candidates can repeat after rollback. Repeated rejections are not independent adverse draws.

The follow-up recovery test uses the root-safe objective and a separate supervisory controller. After a rejected block, exact restoration is checked first; only then does the controller halve the learning rate. The controller itself is not rolled back. This execution accepts \RXXIIRecoveryAccepted{} blocks and rejects \RXXIIRecoveryRejected{}, with accepted improvement after the first rejection. All proposal, incumbent, learning-rate, and state-hash records are retained. Explicit no-op and state-corruption tests are listed as fault-injection regressions, not empirical optimizer outcomes. These tests demonstrate an active gate and one recovery mechanism; they do not establish broad convergence or architecture robustness.

\subsection{Current-state execution on the full domain}\label{sec:r22state}
The global experiment does not use the four-expert decoder. It refines the accessible-face architecture whose actor and critic both take $(t,u,x)$, starting from the recorded R17 seed17107 checkpoint. Joint L-BFGS-B uses 400 gradient evaluations on the complete $4\times16\times16$ differentiable interval-surrogate cover. A separate MPFR checker evaluates every original-domain cell, with no skipped or failed cells and with the original continuous action comparison. The current-state dependence of the actor is also checked by diagnostic derivatives; those pointwise derivatives are not offered as the coverage proof.

The initial full-domain bound is $7.2783190635$ and the new bound is \RXXIIGlobalBound{}. The discounted suffix calculation in \eqref{eq:r22suffix} extends that bound to every starting time, rather than using an unnecessarily undiscounted sum. The run is an explicitly charged warm-start refinement, not a new from-scratch success. Because the witness and actor change together and no separate sharp policy-payoff interval is obtained for this full-state actor, the bound decrease is not claimed to prove actual policy improvement. The original $.01$ full-domain objective remains unmet; regional bounds in Tables~\ref{tab:r22crossed}--\ref{tab:r22conditional} do not replace it.

\subsection{Deployment and economic interpretation}
The online calculation for a regional mixture is not merely a network forward pass. The network is compiled to slab coefficients; conditional prices and their factor derivatives require integration. A separate ordinary-float timing diagnostic evaluates the price and hedge with eight time nodes and sixteen Gaussian nodes per remaining slab. Four experts use at most 8,192 quadrature nodes per query and 1,536 bytes for their coefficient arrays, excluding reserves, indexing, and language-runtime overhead. The measured median time is \RXXIIRuntimeMs{} milliseconds. Duplication to 8, 16, and 32 experts tests computational scaling only; it does not enlarge the certified economic domain. These timings are not rigorous online approximation-error certificates.

We use \emph{sufficient re-budgeted wealth increment} for the earlier $.002$ result. The counterfactual increases initial wealth, retains learned slopes and preference-drift shapes, recomputes the consumption budget offset, and re-verifies the financed stream. It does not keep the entire original policy map fixed and is not a minimum compensating variation or an estimated structural welfare parameter. The earlier price-transport, wealth, and synchronized-initialization calculations remain a posteriori properties of frozen policies; only the previously registered training design has confirmatory status.
