\section{Introduction}\label{sec:r22intro}
A numerical policy for a dynamic economic problem must earn its accuracy in two different comparisons. It must improve the payoff of the policy it replaces, and its payoff must be close to the unrestricted optimum. A neural representation can help generate a candidate without supplying either comparison. Conversely, a better Bellman witness can improve an error bound without improving the underlying policy. We develop Neural Bellman Operators that keep these tasks separate and test the contribution of the representation rather than identifying it with the performance of a particular optimizer.

Our application is the original stopped consumption--portfolio economy with costly preference adjustment. We retain its continuous action set, correlated shocks, stopping payoff, and full-domain accuracy objective of $.01$. The central new experiment crosses neural and direct representations with Adam and L-BFGS-B, starting each pair from exactly the same delivered policy. Each of three ensembles has independently initialized corner experts. This design distinguishes an optimizer-conditional representation comparison from the earlier comparison of two end-to-end configurations.

The main mathematical contribution is a continuum policy-comparison theorem based on verified pair moments. The theorem bounds the Jensen benefit of the old mixture without requiring equal expert slopes, equal preference drifts, or synchronized random initialization. The variance identity converts the unknown mixture benefit into six Gaussian integrals per time slab. Directed quadrature and a Hessian bound then control the entire initial-state rectangle, not a sampled state grid. Applied to all three new neural--Adam ensembles, the theorem proves initial-to-final payoff improvement of at least \RXXIIGainMin{} and regret below \RXXIINeuralRegret{}. Under the same Adam optimizer and gradient-call cap, it also proves a uniform payoff advantage of at least \RXXIIConditionalGain{} over the corresponding direct-parameter mixture. This is a conditional advantage; direct L-BFGS-B remains the stronger end-to-end configuration in these experiments.

Two further executions address operational and global-state questions. A predeclared high-learning-rate test exercises actual rejection and exposes deterministic repetition after exact rollback. A separately recorded supervisory repair restores the complete state before changing the learning rate and resumes accepted improvement. In addition, a current-state network and witness, both taking $(t,u,x)$, are jointly refined from an explicitly identified historical warm start. An independent complete-domain MPFR audit tightens their certificate to \RXXIIGlobalBound{}. This is progress on the original witness, not a relabeling of the regional policy theorem and not closure of the $.01$ objective.

The paper states a constructive residual-correction program that does not require a replicable consumption stream, and quantifies both the local and global costs of a simplicial expert cover. These results separate the general verification mechanism from the financial decoder used by the successful regional policies. We preserve the earlier results, proofs, adverse comparisons, and continuation-trace analysis. The technical supplement records their historical status and provides a proof-to-code map for the new results.
