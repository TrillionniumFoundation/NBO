\section{Coupled action comparisons and signed recursive gains}
\label{sec:coupled119}
The common-density comparison below develops a mathematical obligation
already frozen in the R111 research protocol.  Its atom treatment and the
computable active-tail defect complete that interface here.  The separate
R111 and R112 numerical campaigns retain their original identities and
completion status.  A common density exploits information shared by two
actions; it does not confer a neural-specific capability.

\subsection{Tail witnesses compare the original policy costs}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be two bounded original continuation outcomes on the same
innovation space and write $D=Y-X$.  For a common convex cash-invariant
risk with probability-law dual representation, put
\[
 k_X(q)=\rho(X)-\{\mathbb E_qX-\psi(q)\}\ge0.
\]
For any common feasible $q$, exact cancellation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coupledidentity119}
 \rho(Y)-\rho(X)=\mathbb E_qD+k_Y(q)-k_X(q).
\end{equation}
If the original risk sets or penalties differ across actions, this
particular common-density identity is unavailable unless both differences
are included explicitly.  A similar-looking objective under a different
law is not a comparison in this theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Atom-safe coupled action certificate]
\label{thm:coupled119}
Suppose $0\le k_X(q)\le s_X$, $0\le k_Y(q)\le s_Y$, and
$d_-\le\mathbb E_qD\le d_+$ are verified for a common feasible
density.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coupledrisk119}
 d_- -s_X\le\rho(Y)-\rho(X)\le d_+ +s_Y.
\end{equation}
For the original AVaR, let $\eta$ be an $\alpha$-quantile of $X$,
$t=1-\alpha>0$, and choose a measurable $r\in[0,1]$ equal to one
on $\{X>\eta\}$ and zero on $\{X<\eta\}$, with $\mathbb Er=t$.
Thus $q_X=r/t$ is a maximizing density, including a possible fractional
selection on $\{X=\eta\}$.  For any constant $c$ and verified
$\|D-c\|_\infty\le\delta$, the exact nonnegative remainder obeys
\begin{align}
 0&\le\rho(X+D)-\rho(X)-\mathbb E_{q_X}D\notag\\
 &\le\frac1t\mathbb E\!\left[
 (X-\eta+D-c)_+-(X-\eta)_+-r(D-c)\right]
 \label{eq:hingeremainder119}\\
 &\le\frac1t\mathbb E(\delta-|X-\eta|)_+.
 \label{eq:bandremainder119}
\end{align}
If $P(|X-\eta|\le z)\le Cz^\kappa$ for $0<z\le\delta$, with
$C>0$ and $\kappa\ge0$, the last bound is at most
$C\delta^{\kappa+1}/\{t(\kappa+1)\}$.  Positive quantile atoms
in general preclude an exponent $\kappa>0$; no differentiability
of an actor or risk quantile has been assumed.

For the original investment actions $a,b$, choose one original-law
tail cut $u$ and apply Theorem~\ref{thm:investmenttail119} to bound
the two deficits by $\kappa_a,\kappa_b$.  If
$[d_-,d_+]$ encloses the integral of $f_b-f_a$ under that same
density, their optimal-future contrast lies in
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actioncontrast119}
 h(b)-h(a)+\beta[d_- -\kappa_a,\ d_+ +\kappa_b].
\end{equation}
The implemented complete-policy contrast is enclosed after enlarging
this interval on each side by $\beta\varepsilon_1$.
For exactly the same realized action and same future policy the
contrast is exactly zero.  A strictly negative upper endpoint, together
with the unchanged original qualification predicates, licenses a
strict policy improvement from the completed incumbent.
\end{theorem}

The integral of $f_b-f_a$ must itself be enclosed.  A difference of two
convex functions need not be convex, so applying convex midpoint--secant
rules directly to that difference is invalid.  Valid alternatives include
separately enclosed integrals, a verified modulus for their paired
difference, or a separately implemented exact formula.  All acquisitions
needed to obtain the tighter paired enclosure belong to the service cost.
The centered remainder in \eqref{eq:hingeremainder119} completes the
R111 hypothesis using the standard AVaR hinge representation
\citep{rockafellaruryasev2002}; it does not retroactively convert an
unexecuted tail-aware fitting experiment into new evidence.

\subsection{Transporting a signed policy contrast}
The cost difference between two complete policies has its own recursive
equation.  Let $v_t=J_t^\pi$, $D_t=J_t^\nu-J_t^\pi$, and
\[
 b_t=T_{\nu,t}v_{t+1}-v_t.
\]
At each fixed state and action $\nu_t(x)$, the original risk set and
penalty are the same for the two continuations being compared.  Define
$k_t^{v}(q)$ by centering that original risk at the successor
continuation $v_{t+1}$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:signedrecursion119}
 D_t=b_t+\beta_t\sup_q\left\{\mathbb E_qD_{t+1}-k_t^v(q)\right\}.
\end{equation}
This is the signed policy-difference specialization of the retained
reference-centered operator.  It preserves the reference penalty instead
of replacing it immediately by a worst-case physical or adverse law.

\begin{proposition}[Only near-maximizing reference tails can affect gain]
\label{prop:nearactive119}
At a fixed original action, suppose a verified bracket implies
$m\le D\le M$ and $w=M-m>0$.  The risk difference in
\eqref{eq:signedrecursion119} is unchanged when its supremum is
restricted to $\{q:k^v(q)\le w\}$.  No attainment assumption is
required.  If only a stored reference $f$ is available and a verified
bound gives $\operatorname{osc}(v-f)\le r$, it is sufficient to
retain $\{q:k^f(q)\le w+r\}$.  On this set, the penalties
\[
 \max\{0,k^f(q)-r\}\le k^v(q)\le k^f(q)+r
\]
give computable lower and upper risk-difference bounds when combined
with valid continuation bounds.  For $w=0$ the difference is the
known constant $D=m$, by cash invariance, and no zero-slack optimizer
is presumed to exist.
\end{proposition}

Statewise bounds on $b_t$ and on the continuation contrast can therefore
be propagated backward using the retained penalties, with the declared
terminal contrast as the boundary condition.  This narrows the relevant
dual laws when possible gains are small.  A finite catalogue does not
establish the required statewise premises, and this statement supplies
no uncharged original-policy evaluation oracle.  Constant continuation
allowances still compose by discounted sums; observed physical-state
frequencies cannot replace the original conditional risk operators.
