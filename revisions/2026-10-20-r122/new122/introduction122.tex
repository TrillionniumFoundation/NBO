\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction122}
A numerical decision rule is useful when its information changes an economically
valuable action at an acceptable total cost. A small approximation residual
alone does not answer that question. The distinction becomes especially sharp
when a fitted continuation is used inside a constrained recursive decision:
its level can be accurately estimated while its action contrasts are wrong,
and an expensive certificate can establish the safety of an otherwise
uncompetitive policy. This paper studies these issues within Neural Bellman
Operators (NBO), which construct and learn continuation operators, return
feasible actors, and account for loss under the original recursive objective.

Our application is an investment economy with interacting productive states,
state-dependent capacity, continuous innovations and an explicitly nested
risk preference. The Bellman recursion, information available to the controller,
feasible actions and original objective are fixed throughout each comparison.
An implementation must pay for its continuation information, numerical risk
integration, training, rejected proposals, qualification and eventual decision.
Neural and conventional representations receive the same economic targets and
the same certification opportunities. This makes computational adoption an
empirical and mathematical question rather than a consequence of a model label.

\paragraph{The contribution.}
We derive an exact connection between a paid lower quadratic support, an
original-value upper enclosure, and the action derivative compatible with that
information. The support's slope need not be the derivative of the true Bellman
objective. When the support gap is small enough relative to both feasible side lengths,
the interior uncertainty scales as its square root; the exact finite-domain
bound becomes asymmetric near a capacity boundary.
The interval is sharp in its explicitly specified information class. It
therefore identifies both what an already completed certificate can buy and
what its representation label cannot reveal. For the exact support interface,
Supplement, Proposition~\ref{prop:informationminimax122} matches this upper
guarantee with a lower bound for every deterministic or randomized method
receiving the same information. The robust proposal is minimax over that
stated function class; after the implemented-future allowance, retention is
optimal when the possible guaranteed gain does not cover that allowance.
This is an information-class result, not a lower bound for algorithms allowed
the additional exact investment formulas.

The interval supplies a feasible policy-improvement mechanism. A robust upper
quadratic model produces a small, completely recorded candidate menu. At the
realized rounded action, the method checks positive original-policy gain,
complete-policy regret and objective width. These checks use the existing
payload without querying the original risk at each candidate. An unchanged
future controller enters through its explicit error allowance; its numerical
error is not silently differentiated. A second service purchases finer support
information at the same anchor only under a fixed, costed refinement protocol.
The mathematical analysis also separates the precision needed for an action
sign from the stricter precision needed for an objective enclosure, and gives
a priced stopping dominance condition under a compulsory incremental cost.

The same original-policy advantage also has a recursive account.
Supplement, Theorem~\ref{thm:dynamicgain122} propagates nonnegative action
advantages computed against a common incumbent continuation through a
centered lower risk operator. Its verified subsolutions give lower bounds on
the complete-policy gain even when several dates change. The construction
requires the inequalities at every relevant state, and a lower bound on the
complete risk infimum; one favorable risk witness or a finite sample is
insufficient. A stationary extension uses an explicitly invariant Banach
space and a discount below one. These statements supply an analytical bridge
to a longer original Bellman recursion, not an unexecuted numerical claim
or an optimal-convergence guarantee.

These results extend the paper's original operator construction and policy
account. The own-future construction uses acquired continuations at every date,
returns feasible witnesses, and propagates their errors through the economic
recursion. The retained continuous-risk theory states the information interface
and lower bounds; the retained stochastic-control and nonlinear recursive-
utility results make their additional assumptions explicit. The new experiment
implements a two-date specialization. It does not claim that an unexecuted long
neural recursion, a nonlinear-utility problem, or a different information set has
been numerically solved by that specialization.

\paragraph{Why change the action calculation?}
The preceding independently evaluated experiment reduced its finite-design
marginal-fitting objective in all 28 paired fits, yet increased the enclosed
sample mean original economic cost in all 28. Its simultaneous population
mean-gain intervals all contained zero. Those results remain in the development
companion with their own immutable source and sample. They motivate a distinct
question: can the original-risk information already paid for at a proposed
action deliver a safer and more valuable action directly? The present study
freezes this mechanism before drawing its new states. It reuses the actual
stored actors, fully charges their acquisition and fitting, and does not
replace the earlier adverse evidence.

\paragraph{What the comparison identifies.}
Quadratic and polynomial readouts, splines, an ordinary hinge basis, a grid
representation and a direct original-model solver are evaluated alongside the
ReLU actor. The matched hinge and ReLU representations have the same features
and feasible policies; agreement between them is evidence about their common
information, not a neural-specific gain. The primary economic question is the
change in original policy value. The adoption question additionally charges
all acquisition, certification, rejected calculations and complete service
work. Population uncertainty is reported separately from descriptive clocks
and hypothetical reuse volumes. Every declared cell and comparator is retained,
including unsuccessful refinements and empty adoption regions.

\paragraph{Relation to established methods.}
The classical Lipschitz extension supplies the constructive backend
\citep{mcshane1934}. Inexact first-order information and its accuracy requirements
have an established optimization literature \citep{DGN2014Inexact122}, as do
sharp interpolation inequalities \citep{THG2017Interpolation122}. We do not
claim generic smooth descent, hinge geometry or bounded convex readouts as
neural inventions. The present contribution is the constrained conversion of a
specific paid Bellman payload into original-policy gain and qualification,
with the implemented-future allowance and a reproducible full-cost comparison.
Sections~\ref{sec:core49}--\ref{sec:construction49} give the original operator
and construction; Section~\ref{sec:supportaction122} gives the new information
and action mechanism; Section~\ref{sec:numerical122} evaluates its economics.
Complete proofs for the current main results appear in the technical supplement.
The development companion
preserves the complete historical theorem and evidence record without
substituting it for the current experiment.
