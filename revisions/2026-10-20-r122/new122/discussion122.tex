The information result distinguishes three possible sources of computational
value. A representation can locate a promising action; a paid original-model
certificate can license its replacement; and reuse can make the complete
service attractive at a stated computation price. These are different claims.
The first is a learning question, the second is an action-information question,
and the third is an economic deployment comparison. A lower fitting residual
cannot establish all three. Neither can a successful local replacement establish
a neural advantage when an ordinary hinge or a conventional online solver
receives the same information and feasible choices.

The future work required by NBO remains substantive. A long learned recursion
must retain its continuation level as well as its action contrasts, pay for its
own future information, and verify the resulting policy account at every date.
The present two-date application supplies an executed instance, not a numerical
certificate for those longer recursions. The Brownian nonlinear-utility and
FBSDE connections remain conditional on the stated existence, comparison,
regularity and information assumptions. Different recursive preferences are
not interchangeable economic objectives.

The sharp interval also supplies a concrete research direction. When its
uncertainty straddles zero, no representation label resolves the derivative
using only the recorded quadratic support and value bound. Additional action
information, a different allocation of precision, or exploitable original
structure is required. When the derivative sign is resolved but the objective
width is not, the remaining obstacle is a different one: curvature uncertainty
and the deployed-future allowance impose their own floor. A useful new neural
operator must learn or acquire information that changes these feasible
comparisons at lower complete cost, against equally informed strong numerical
methods. The current proofs, complete menu and preserved negative results
make that question testable without changing the original economic objective.
