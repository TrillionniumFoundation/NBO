\section{Exact primitive validation of original-optimum regret}
\label{supp:independentopt120}
The R119 independent catalogue enclosure bounded $Q_x(a)$ and
$J_x(a)$ at fixed actions.  The following additional construction
also provides an independent lower bound on $\inf_a Q_x(a)$.
It uses the retained original curvature proposition and explicit
primitive gradients; it is a validation strengthening, not a
new claim that a scalar fixed-action lower value already
bounded the all-action optimum.

\begin{proposition}[An independent rational all-action witness]
\label{prop:independentopt120}
Fix the original $d=2$ model and a rational original state and
root action.  At a terminal anchor $y$, choose any feasible
rational $v$ and any feasible original tail density $q$.
Let $W$ be the exact terminal objective under this fixed
density, and let $(p_y,p_v)$ be its state and action gradients.
For arbitrary rational $\lambda,\nu\ge0$, define
\begin{align}
 e&=p_v+\lambda-\nu,\qquad
 c=W+\lambda\{v-A(y)\}-\nu v-e^2/4,\notag\\*
 r&=p_y-\frac{\lambda}{8d}\boldsymbol1.
 \label{eq:independentstate120}
\end{align}
Then $V_1(y')\ge c+r^\top(y'-y)$ for every original state
$y'$.  At a boundary one may choose
$\lambda=\boldsymbol1_{\{v=A(y)\}}(-p_v)_+$ and
$\nu=\boldsymbol1_{\{v=0\}}(p_v)_+$; no exact stationarity
is required.

Partition the root innovation interval into $n$ equal cells,
with midpoints $z_i$ and successors $y_i=F(x,a,z_i)$.
Acquire such supports $(c_i,r_i)$, and choose rational
masses $q_i\in[0,1/(nt)]$ summing to one.  Put
\begin{align}
 W_0&=S(x)+h(a)+\beta\sum_iq_i c_i,\notag\\
 p_0&=h'(a)+\beta\sum_iq_i r_i^\top\boldsymbol b,\qquad
 a_*=\operatorname{proj}_{[0,A(x)]}(a-p_0/2),\notag\\
 B_0&=W_0+p_0(a_*-a)+(a_*-a)^2 .
 \label{eq:independentopt120}
\end{align}
Then $B_0\le V_0(x)$.
Together with any separately valid original complete-policy
upper bound $U_J(a)$, this proves
\[
 0\le J_x(a)-V_0(x)\le U_J(a)-B_0,\qquad
 |a-a_0^*(x)|^2\le U_J(a)-B_0.
\]
The maximum of finitely many such lower bounds acquired at
the same original state is also valid.  No support or optimum
bound is transferred across distinct states without an
additional justified state comparison.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed feasible original density, the joint terminal
state-action objective $W_q$ has the lower curvature
$m_x\|dy\|_2^2+2\,dv^2$, where
$m_x=(4-5\beta/4)/d>0$.
This follows directly from
$\nabla^2S\succeq(4/d)I$, $h''\ge2$,
$\nabla^2g\succeq0$, and $|\partial_jg|\le10/d$:
the only potentially negative transition-chain term is
$-\sum_j\partial_jg(F)\,dy_jdy_{j+1}/8$,
bounded below by $-5\|dy\|_2^2/(4d)$.
The other chain Hessian is positive semidefinite even in a
joint state-action direction.  Integration under the fixed
nonnegative density preserves this bound.  Matching first
derivatives extend it across squared-hinge junctions.
Dropping the positive state-curvature term gives
\[
 Q_1(y',v')\ge W_q(y',v')
 \ge W+p_y^\top(y'-y)+p_v(v'-v)+(v'-v)^2.
\]
Replace $p_v$ by $e-\lambda+\nu$.
For a feasible $v'\in[0,A(y')]$,
$-\lambda v'+\nu v'\ge-\lambda A(y')$.
The affine capacity and the inequality
$e(v'-v)+(v'-v)^2\ge-e^2/4$ therefore give
$Q_1(y',v')\ge c+r^\top(y'-y)$.
Taking the infimum over the original feasible $v'$ proves
\eqref{eq:independentstate120}.
In particular the intercept must be this valid global-support
intercept; a larger scalar lower bound obtained by clipping a
quadratic at only the anchor state cannot be attached to $r$
without a further proof.

Here are explicit gradients suitable for an independent
rational implementation.  Put $m=F(y,v,0)$.
The original terminal polynomial in the innovation is
$g(m)+kz+5z^2$, whose coefficients can be reconstructed by
evaluating the primitives at $-R,0,R$.
For its exact clipped quadratic tail with left cut $u$, let
\[
 L=2R\alpha,\quad B=2Rt,\quad
 \mu_1=-L(2u+L)/(2B),\qquad
 G_j=\partial_jg(m)+(10/d)s_j\mu_1 .
\]
The squared hinge is constant along the zero-sum shock,
and direct differentiation of the remaining quadratics gives
$\partial_jg(m+\boldsymbol s z)
 =\partial_jg(m)+(10/d)s_jz$.
Thus $G_j$ is the exact fixed-density mean of that derivative.
With cyclic indices,
\begin{align}
 p_v&=2v+16v^3+\beta\sum_j b_jG_j,\notag\\
 p_{y,j}&=\partial_jS(y)
  +\beta\left[
       G_j\left\{\frac12+\frac{1-y_{j+1}}{16}\right\}
       +G_{j-1}\left\{\frac18-\frac{y_{j-1}}{16}\right\}
        \right].
 \label{eq:independentgradient120}
\end{align}
These formulas differentiate the fixed density, not its
optimizing cut.  The density supplied by the independent
quadratic threshold construction is exactly feasible and
maximizing at the anchor, but feasibility alone suffices for
the global support proof.  All quantities are rational at
the stated inputs.

For the root, the masses $q_i$ define a piecewise constant
feasible AVaR density with value $nq_i$ on cell $i$.
For another feasible root action $w$ and any $z$ in cell $i$,
\[
 F(x,w,z)-y_i=\boldsymbol b(w-a)+\boldsymbol s(z-z_i).
\]
Integrating its affine support over the symmetric cell
removes the last term exactly.  The same fixed feasible
density consequently yields
\[
 Q_x(w)\ge
 S(x)+h(w)+\beta\sum_iq_i
                 \{c_i+r_i^\top\boldsymbol b(w-a)\}
 \ge W_0+p_0(w-a)+(w-a)^2 .
\]
Its quadratic minimum over the full original capacity
interval is \eqref{eq:independentopt120}.
Taking the infimum of $Q_x$ proves the optimum lower bound.
For equal cells with integral $nt$, selecting the largest
$nt$ values of $c_i$ with fixed ties and assigning mass
$1/(nt)$ to them is feasible and maximizes this finite
linear witness.  This sorting changes the chosen original
density, not the original continuous innovation law.

Every implemented feasible policy costs at least $V_0$,
so $J_x(a)\le U_J(a)$ gives the regret inequality.
The original $Q_x$ is strongly convex in its action with
modulus two.  Its constrained minimizer obeys the boundary
variational inequality.  Hence
$Q_x(a)-V_0(x)\ge|a-a_0^*(x)|^2$.
Use $Q_x(a)\le J_x(a)\le U_J(a)$ and $B_0\le V_0(x)$
to prove the action-distance bound.  A maximum of valid
lower bounds on the same scalar optimum remains a valid
lower bound.  This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

The independent fixed-action upper value is still constructed by
the original midpoint/duplicated-endpoint convex-order argument
in Proposition~\ref{prop:independentvalidation119}, with
$\beta\varepsilon_1$ added once for the actual future policy.
The new all-action witness does not assert containment of a
fine production interval inside an arbitrarily selected coarse
one, nor equality of any fine endpoint to the truth.  It supplies
its own exact, reproducible bounds on the original optimum,
regret, and action error.
