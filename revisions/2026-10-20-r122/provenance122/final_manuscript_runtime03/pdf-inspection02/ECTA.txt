NEURAL BELLMAN OPERATORS
QIAN QI
Peking University
Neural Bellman Operators construct continuation values and feasible ac-
tors for a specified recursive economic objective. We develop an action-level
use of information already purchased by an original Bellman certificate. A
quadratic minorant and a value enclosure imply a sharp derivative interval
on a constrained action domain. A robust feasible replacement then certifies
original-policy improvement, regret and objective width without a new
value query at the candidate action. The result distinguishes support accu-
racy, marginal accuracy, implemented-future error and computational cost.
We retain complete own-future construction and recursive-risk foundations,
with qualified links to stochastic control and nonlinear recursive utility.
The numerical application uses the original two-date investment economy
with continuous innovations and nested average value at risk. Fifty-six
previously acquired actors and an online solver use common original ob-
jectives, feasible controls, qualification tolerances and improvement rules;
matched-budget representations share the acquired labels. A separately
frozen experiment compares neural, matched hinge, quadratic, polynomial,
spline and grid representations on 32 new states with three process clocks.
Independent rational evaluation, simultaneous population inference and
complete cost–value menus distinguish safe policy changes from neural-
specific adoption. All 824 accepted changes pass independent gain and
containment checks; 102 of 116 own-control mean gains have positive si-
multaneous lower bounds. Matched hinge and neural decisions coincide,
while all complete three-clock neural adoption domains are empty. The
resulting information bounds and recursive gain account identify what
additional action information a competitive neural decision service must
supply.
KEYWORDS: Dynamic programming, Neural approximation, Recursive
risk, Policy qualification, Computational economics.

1. INTRODUCTION
A numerical decision rule is useful when its information changes an economically
valuable action at an acceptable total cost. A small approximation residual alone
does not answer that question. The distinction becomes especially sharp when a
fitted continuation is used inside a constrained recursive decision: its level can
be accurately estimated while its action contrasts are wrong, and an expensive
certificate can establish the safety of an otherwise uncompetitive policy. This paper
studies these issues within Neural Bellman Operators (NBO), which construct and
learn continuation operators, return feasible actors, and account for loss under the
original recursive objective.
Our application is an investment economy with interacting productive states,
state-dependent capacity, continuous innovations and an explicitly nested risk
preference. The Bellman recursion, information available to the controller, feasible
actions and original objective are fixed throughout each comparison. An imple-
mentation must pay for its continuation information, numerical risk integration,
training, rejected proposals, qualification and eventual decision. Neural and conven-
tional representations receive the same economic targets and the same certification
opportunities. This makes computational adoption an empirical and mathematical
question rather than a consequence of a model label.
The contribution.
We derive an exact connection between a paid lower quadratic
support, an original-value upper enclosure, and the action derivative compatible
with that information. The support’s slope need not be the derivative of the true
Bellman objective. When the support gap is small enough relative to both feasible
side lengths, the interior uncertainty scales as its square root; the exact finite-domain
bound becomes asymmetric near a capacity boundary. The interval is sharp in its
explicitly specified information class. It therefore identifies both what an already
completed certificate can buy and what its representation label cannot reveal. For the
exact support interface, Supplement, Proposition S.22 matches this upper guarantee
with a lower bound for every deterministic or randomized method receiving the
same information. The robust proposal is minimax over that stated function class;
after the implemented-future allowance, retention is optimal when the possible

The interval supplies a feasible policy-improvement mechanism. A robust upper

The same original-policy advantage also has a recursive account. Supplement,
Theorem S.24 propagates nonnegative action advantages computed against a com-
mon incumbent continuation through a centered lower risk operator. Its verified
subsolutions give lower bounds on the complete-policy gain even when several
dates change. The construction requires the inequalities at every relevant state, and
a lower bound on the complete risk infimum; one favorable risk witness or a finite
sample is insufficient. A stationary extension uses an explicitly invariant Banach
space and a discount below one. These statements supply an analytical bridge
to a longer original Bellman recursion, not an unexecuted numerical claim or an
optimal-convergence guarantee.
These results extend the paper’s original operator construction and policy account.
The own-future construction uses acquired continuations at every date, returns
feasible witnesses, and propagates their errors through the economic recursion.
The retained continuous-risk theory states the information interface and lower
bounds; the retained stochastic-control and nonlinear recursive- utility results
make their additional assumptions explicit. The new experiment implements a
two-date specialization. It does not claim that an unexecuted long neural recursion,
a nonlinear-utility problem, or a different information set has been numerically
solved by that specialization.

Why change the action calculation?
The preceding independently evaluated exper-
iment reduced its finite-design marginal-fitting objective in all 28 paired fits, yet
increased the enclosed sample mean original economic cost in all 28. Its simulta-
neous population mean-gain intervals all contained zero. Those results remain in
the development companion with their own immutable source and sample. They
motivate a distinct question: can the original-risk information already paid for at
a proposed action deliver a safer and more valuable action directly? The present
study freezes this mechanism before drawing its new states. It reuses the actual
stored actors, fully charges their acquisition and fitting, and does not replace the
earlier adverse evidence.
What the comparison identifies.
Quadratic and polynomial readouts, splines, an
ordinary hinge basis, a grid representation and a direct original-model solver are
evaluated alongside the ReLU actor. The matched hinge and ReLU representations
have the same features and feasible policies; agreement between them is evidence
about their common information, not a neural-specific gain. The primary economic
question is the change in original policy value. The adoption question additionally
charges all acquisition, certification, rejected calculations and complete service work.
Population uncertainty is reported separately from descriptive clocks and hypo-
thetical reuse volumes. Every declared cell and comparator is retained, including
unsuccessful refinements and empty adoption regions.
The completed experiment accepts 824 original-policy changes, all confirmed by
the independent gain and containment checks. The fixed simultaneous family has
positive own-control mean-gain lower bounds in 102 of 116 cells. All 768 matched
ReLU/hinge primary pairs agree in their decisions and certificates, and all 96
complete three-clock neural adoption menus are empty. The numerical contribution
is thus a verified change in original actions and values, together with a complete
account of its cost and competitive limits. It does not assign the shared information
mechanism to neural representation alone.

4. REFERENCE-CENTERED RISK KERNELS AND COMPLETE-POLICY QUALIFICATION
A datewise worst-case error account can charge incompatible states at succes-
sive dates. Under nonlinear recursive preferences, replacing that account by an
expectation under the physical transition law is not valid. We instead retain the con-
ditional risk evaluation and center its dual representation at the actual stored NBO
continuation. The resulting state-dependent certificate has an original-optimum in-
terpretation and a finite occupation representation. It extends, rather than replaces,
the signed qualification of Section 2.

5. CONTINUOUS-LAW QUALIFICATION IN THE INVESTMENT MODEL

mented terminal controller rather than a hypothetical exact optimizer determines
policy cost. This application joins continuation construction, the original-optimum
comparison and a representation comparison on one problem. It does not replace
the general NBO construction or the other economic applications.
