\section{An institutional information theorem (Report Section 9)}
Theorem~\ref{paper-thm:r19-information} isolates the information question requested by the referee. In one economy the feasible responses form the simplex generated by $k$ distinct outputs; in the other, a zero-output response is also feasible. Every response is privately optimal at the center. Both economies have the same exact value at every perturbation with at least one nonnegative coordinate. This includes every signed coordinate query. Adaptive selection of those queries cannot help because its entire transcript has the same distribution in the two economies.

When the purchaser values every output positively, the worst-response return is strictly positive in the first economy and zero in the second. A safe offer with intermediate return is therefore optimal in opposite economies. The theorem derives the positive minimax regret under the uninformative messages. A single query opposite to the purchaser's joint payoff vector separates the economies exactly when its value gap exceeds twice the adversarial observation error. The theorem also computes the expected value of this information under an arbitrary prior. If one output no longer accrues value to the purchaser, the same distinction ceases to matter. Thus institutional output incidence changes both the necessary query direction and the value of acquiring it.

The example is implemented by a finite dynamic contract, not an unattainable geometric object. Proposition~\ref{paper-prop:r19-sharp} then gives a sharp response set under arbitrary consistent exact value messages by constructing a policy for every admitted feature vector. The known-dynamics occupancy problem is distinguished from that information experiment. We do not present its linear-programming duality or the performance-difference identity as new theorems. The proposed novelty is the exact institutional separation and its procurement loss, noise threshold, and information value. Whether this contribution meets the journal's significance standard remains a substantive editorial assessment, not an inference from a passed software check.

\section{The common-fee outer relaxation (Report Section 11)}
Theorem~\ref{paper-thm:r19-cell} defines the action value explicitly and states why it is convex: affine fee rewards, fee-independent feasible actions, and nonnegative kernels preserve convexity of continuation values. Actual feasible endpoint policies produce the lower planes. Their payoff and surrender-moment intervals are inserted with the signs appropriate to each side of the cell; endpoint action upper intervals generate a chord majorant. A rationally checked convex combination gives a uniform lower action-loss margin.

The new result aggregates these margins through discounted occupancies. At zero tolerance it specializes to an exact-response exclusion graph. At positive tolerance the feasible occupation measures satisfy one total loss budget. We retain rare, conditionally costly actions when their total loss fits that budget, and rule out the erroneous practice of permitting a full tolerance at every date. The proof is the global performance-difference identity with certified local lower bounds.

The result remains an outer relaxation. A graph can combine actions requiring incompatible charges; such combinations can only raise the upper purchaser bound. The manuscript does not describe that graph as the actual common-fee response correspondence. Fee--surrender products and participation inequalities remain separately constrained. The R19 rational fixtures test the global loss interpretation and an endpoint margin. The retained settlement continuum certificate is rechecked with its original value-message algorithm; it is not retrospectively claimed to have executed the new positive-tolerance refinement. Accordingly, there is no fabricated table assigning old cells to an unexecuted new graph bound.
