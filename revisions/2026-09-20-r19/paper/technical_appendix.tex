\section{Proofs for the Additional Information and Operator Interface}
\label{sec:r19-interface-proofs}
\subsection{Dynamic occupancy information}
For a fixed finite polyhedral action representation, each history-dependent randomized policy induces nonnegative discounted occupancies satisfying the flow equations. Conversely, at a state with positive occupation, divide its state--action occupations by their sum to obtain a Markov action distribution; use any feasible distribution at a zero-occupation state. Forward induction recovers the given occupancies. The reward and response functionals therefore range over precisely the stated occupancy polytope. Restricting the reward functional to at least $W_M-\eta$ proves the response representation in Theorem~\ref{thm:occupancy}.

Multiply each Bellman loss by the corresponding occupancy. The continuation terms cancel the next-date state occupations by the flow equations, leaving the initial optimal value minus the policy payoff. This proves the single global-loss identity. All terms are nonnegative, so zero total loss restricts positive occupations to zero-loss actions. With positive tolerance it constrains their sum, not their separate maxima. The information inclusions follow by including the true model in the consistent collection and then applying the finite-query theorem to each consistent model. For strictness, a one-date menu with responses $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ has joint upper response one. Adding $(1,1)$ preserves its coordinate support values but raises joint upper response to two. Thus the oracle-consistent collection is strictly larger than the known-model response set.

\subsection{Directed support and the vertex-gap assertion}
The support problem is a feasible, bounded linear program with the additional constraint $r^\top x\geq W_M-\eta$. Attach a nonnegative multiplier $\lambda$ to this inequality in its maximization form. Linear-programming strong duality gives the first equality in Theorem~\ref{thm:directed}; maximizing the remaining affine functional over the occupancy polytope gives its value-query representation for $\lambda>0$. The objective is continuous at $\lambda=0$, so an infimum over positive multipliers also includes the boundary case by a limit.

To verify the gap assertion, compare vertices. After subtracting $\lambda W_M$, an optimal vertex contributes its response functional. A suboptimal vertex contributes its response functional minus at least $\lambda\delta$. Its response advantage over any optimal vertex is at most $R_w$. Consequently every positive $\lambda\geq R_w/\delta$ prevents a suboptimal vertex from exceeding the best optimal-response vertex. A maximum of an affine functional is attained at a vertex, proving exactness. When all vertices are privately optimal, the private-payoff term is constant and every positive multiplier gives the same response support. Replacing the perturbed value by an upper enclosure and the center value by a lower enclosure proves the interval version; multiplication by $\lambda$ charges the two errors explicitly.

\subsection{Endpoint response graphs and purchaser accounting}
Convexity of an action value follows from its affine current reward, a nonnegative transition kernel, and convex continuation values. Its endpoint chord is therefore a majorant. Each fixed feasible endpoint policy has affine payoff at all fees; its plane is a minorant for the value. A strict endpoint separation from a convex combination of the two planes excludes the action everywhere on the interval because the tested difference is affine. Exact optimal policies use only nonexcluded actions along their positive-occupation histories. The graph can combine incompatible charge-specific actions, so optimizing over it yields an upper bound rather than an implementing policy.

For the accounting specialization, use the main paper's fee-free payoff $B+dA$, not $B$ alone. With zero quality premium, exact response implies $W=B+dA-FH$. The inequality $q\geq G_0-W+\alpha_C C(E)$ holds even if its right side is negative. Thus purchaser payoff is no greater than
\[
 B+(d+b)A-(1-\rho_F)FH-G_0-C(E)-D(m).
\]
When $0\leq\rho_F\leq1$, $H\geq0$, $F\geq a$, and $E\geq F/\zeta$, replace the fee in the nonpositive term by $a$ and capacity cost by $C(a/\zeta)$. Maximizing the remaining functional over the containing graph proves Proposition~\ref{prop:intervalgraph}. The positive-part participation rule has not been removed; its inequality is what makes the bound valid in both binding and slack cases. A nonzero quality premium must be retained in the maximized reward. The main paper's whole-cell loss theorem provides the global positive-tolerance extension.

\subsection{Operator residuals, signed error budgets, and barriers}
For Proposition~\ref{prop:operatorresponse}, set the terminal upper and lower error budgets to their corresponding terminal allowances. Monotonicity and the row-mass bound imply that replacing $W_{n+1}$ by $v_{n+1}+E^+_{n+1}$ raises its Bellman value by at most $\beta_nE^+_{n+1}$. Add the checked positive residual allowance to obtain the upper recursion. Apply the same argument with a negative displacement for the lower recursion. For a feasible fixed policy its own affine, monotone operator gives the analogous lower payoff. The directed-query inequality then charges the two initial errors with their multiplier. This proof requires a bound on the actual action supremum, not a residual on the actions visited during training.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:directederror}, let $R=c-A^\top\mu$ and let $B$ denote the fixed feasible box. For each perturbed feasible point,
\[
 c^\top x=\mu^\top Ax+R^\top x
 \leq\mu^\top b+\mu^\top D\xi+h_B(R)
 \leq U_0+h_\Xi(D^\top\mu).
\]
This proves the bound even when stationarity is inexact, because its box residual is charged. A lower-minus-upper comparison combines the correctly oriented signed coefficients of shared errors before taking their support. Subtracting two unrelated upper bounds does not itself bound a payoff difference. A shared scalar shift with coefficient sum zero cancels algebraically; independent errors require an absolute-sum allowance. Neither type of dependence may be assumed without a model for $\Xi$.

For Proposition~\ref{prop:continuousbarrier}, apply It\^o's formula to the discounted upper candidate stopped at the agent's stopping time and integrate its residual inequality. The upper terminal, boundary, or elective-stop condition bounds the corresponding payment by the candidate plus $B^+$. Nonnegative residual allowances permit replacing the random stopping time in their integral by the fixed terminal date. Taking the supremum over admissible policies gives the upper value inequality. Apply the reversed residual inequality to the stated feasible lower policy and use its payment lower condition to obtain the lower bound. Bounded candidates justify optional stopping directly; localized candidates require the stated uniform-integrability or moment argument. In the main paper's Brownian example the derivative bounds have quadratic growth and the admissible controls imply the required second moments, so that argument is supplied rather than assumed.

\section{Structural Zero-Surrender Certificates and Reproduction}
\label{sec:r19-reproduction-proof}
\subsection{Why the initial upper endpoints are certificates}
The canonical arrays define exact binary64 rational reward and transition inputs. The pre-existing data-dependent arithmetic derivation checks every sparse row and bounds Bellman and selected-policy errors on the entire parameter range used here. The new calculation charges $\varepsilon=10^{-7}$, larger than the independently derived bound. At each state-date pair retain every feasible action whose computed action value is within $4\varepsilon$ of the computed Bellman value. Since both value errors are enclosed, every exactly optimal action is retained.

At the first date, retain the corresponding near-maximizing financial knots. A maximum over a continuous piecewise-affine segment is attained at an endpoint, or the segment is flat and both endpoints attain it. The support of a transition row for an interior point is contained in the union of its endpoint supports. Thus propagation from all retained knots contains the states reached by every exact first action in the closed representation. The independent attainment check separates all zero-share knots from a positive knot after charging the errors, so the positive class has an actual attaining optimum in the reported cases.

Propagate reachability using every strictly positive stored transition entry of every retained action. Mixture endpoints with positive law weight are both included. This is a support computation, not a floating-point test of whether a small computed probability is zero. If no reached state-date pair admits a retained surrender action, every exact best response has zero elective surrender. It follows that the optimal value equals the no-surrender value and that the tested fee is an upper endpoint for the initial implementation threshold. Conversely, an evaluated feasible policy whose lower payoff exceeds the no-surrender upper value proves a strict lower endpoint. Both arguments cover the actual initial contract and do not require every state in the grid to be reachable.

The reader should distinguish this structural conclusion from a small weak value discrepancy between two models. A diffusion can reach states outside a finite transition graph. The graph certificate proves a statement about the hash-identified array model. It is not transferred to a diffusion merely by observing close values. The explicit Brownian certificate uses continuous obstacles and a stopped verification identity instead.

\subsection{Machine-checking boundaries}
The independent checker reconstructs Bellman recursions and fixed-policy evaluations without importing the producer, its Bellman engine, or its policy-evaluation routine. It shares the immutable array loader and the pre-existing roundoff derivation, a dependency recorded explicitly. A second checker reconstructs neural and polynomial forward passes from stored parameters, checks bank-selection values, and verifies the exact control's Bellman greediness. These checks establish inference and payoff provenance, not an independent reimplementation of the training optimizer.

The logarithm enclosure uses positive rational series with a proven tail bound. The independent analytic check instead decomposes $\log2$ into $\log(4/3)+\log(3/2)$ and evaluates two different rational series. It checks the PDE identities symbolically, the obstacle factorization algebraically, and the participation-adjusted margin by exact rational inequalities. Mutations to a reported policy payoff and a claimed interval-exclusion margin are rejected. The checks run under optimized Python as well, so validation does not disappear with disabled assertion statements.

The release process regenerates tables from their identified JSON and witness files, checks every current source dependency, compiles the manuscript, supplement, response, and preservation compendium, and records PDF page counts, warnings, and hashes. The older full fee-cover certificate is separately rechecked read-only. A report's successful status means precisely that these specified checks passed; it is not a substitute for referee assessment of the economic contribution or for the still-distinct CRRA diffusion-inclusion and high-dimensional computational questions.
