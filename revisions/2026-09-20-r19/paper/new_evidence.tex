\subsection{Initial implementation: new independent brackets}
\label{sec:r19-threshold-evidence}
Table~\ref{tab:r19-enforcement} separates an initial contract from the uniform state-wide requirement. It keeps the same law, operating benefit, arrays, controls, terminal settlement, and first-date financial representation as the preceding application. Only the surrender charge is searched. Each lower endpoint has a feasible policy whose payoff, after the arithmetic allowance, strictly exceeds the no-surrender upper value. Each upper endpoint has a Bellman action supergraph containing every exact optimum, with no reachable surrender action from the initial mandate. These are logically different certificates. Their combination brackets the actual initial threshold; equality of two rounded values is not used as a stopping test.

The positive-mandate intervals imply a fee reduction exceeding 0.0717. The nonpositive-mandate intervals also separate strictly. No-surrender private values rise in both classes. The uniform thresholds are much larger, because they cover eligible states not reached by the initial optimal contract. Both uniform requirements exceed the administrative fee ceiling of one, whereas every initial threshold bracket lies strictly below that ceiling. A demand for state-wide implementation would therefore exclude contracts that are implementable from the actual initial state. The distinction concerns feasible procurement, not merely a tighter numerical estimate. We do not substitute a uniform threshold for the initial fee in the purchaser's capacity cost. Nor do we infer the initial ordering solely from nested action sets: the table proves it for the stated numerical target with its own witnesses.

At the common charge 0.765 the adjusted optimal-action supergraph has no reachable elective surrender under either initial mandate. The no-adjustment contract, in contrast, has a strictly positive option value relative to no surrender. The generator records feasible first actions and the lower and upper payoff bounds underlying that statement. The independent checker recomputes the Bellman values and selected-policy payoffs without calling the generating solver. The first-date positive witnesses use positive financial knots; the separate attainment check compares their values with all zero-share knots after charging the arithmetic allowance.

For a positive global tolerance, the exact zero-surrender conclusion is replaced by Theorem~\ref{thm:r19-enforcement}'s explicit bound. Using an upper endpoint $\overline F^{\rm init}$ of a proved threshold bracket gives the conservative bound $H\leq\eta/(F-\overline F^{\rm init})$ whenever $F>\overline F^{\rm init}$. This replacement preserves the economic comparison while identifying precisely how much behavioral error it can bear. It is not obtained by adding a local tolerance to an equality graph.
