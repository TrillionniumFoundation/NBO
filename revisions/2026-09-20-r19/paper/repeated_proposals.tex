\subsection{Repeated fits and the exact-policy control}
\label{sec:r19-repeated}
The new study uses the unchanged two-state-variable settlement economy, all 1,565 common actions and three frozen proposal actions, and eight operating intervals. In each adjustment regime, teachers solve the model at laws 0.12 and 0.13. The training sample contains 22,638 date--state rows. A two-hidden-layer network with 32 units in each layer is trained for 45 epochs at each of seeds 1901, 1902, and 1903. The tests are laws 0, 0.125, and 1 at the same benefit, fee, and first-date mandate conventions. The extremes are extrapolations from the narrow teacher-law range; their adverse outcomes are not removed.

The controls are the nearest teacher policy, the quadratic classifier, the better independently evaluated policy from the complete two-policy teacher bank, and the exact dynamic program's returned policy. Bank selection is made for the known initial contract, not after observing its future trajectory. All methods use the same feasible first-date optimization and are checked against the same mathematical upper problem. The exact program already produces a policy while producing its upper reference; the comparison includes that policy rather than discarding it to create an artificial benefit for a learned proposal.

The offline column includes the teachers used by the method and its own fitting. The workload column sums proposal, feasible-policy evaluation, and upper-reference calculation over the three test laws. It does not silently amortize fitting over a larger deployment. Independent verification is timed separately for the complete evidence set and is required before release. The largest reported gap ranges over the three laws and both initial mandates, including all unfavorable responses of the selected policy relative to the upper value. These are policy-value gaps; they are not a claim that a selected policy reveals every optimal response.

The stored weights reproduce the neural policies under an independently implemented forward pass. A separate checker reevaluates every deposited policy and recomputes the Bellman references without importing the producer or its evaluation routines. It checks the exact-control policies' Bellman greediness as well. The parameter-storage account includes stored weights or policy arrays, not the canonical transition arrays shared by all methods. The process-peak memory account includes loading and computation; it is not relabeled as exclusive neural memory.

This experiment supplies repeated fits and a matched exact-policy comparator, but not a state-dimension scaling frontier. Three seeds do not identify a population failure probability. The results preserve the distinction between an operator architecture that can propose useful policies and a certificate that must remain valid when those policies are poor. No neural superiority claim is inferred from the accuracy of the independently computed upper reference.
