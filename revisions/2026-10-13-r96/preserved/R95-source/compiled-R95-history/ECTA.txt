26                                                                                                26

27     A continuation approximation matters through the economic decision it produces. 27
28   A small prediction error, a feasible controller, a bound on original-optimum policy          28

29   loss and a saving in computation are different achievements. This paper develops             29

30   Neural Bellman Operators (NBOs) to connect them. The benchmark is the original               30

3        An NBO fits each date against its own attained future. Our constructive back- 3
4    end uses primitive state and action covers, certified integration and feasible action    4

5    witnesses to return an affine–ReLU continuation and an explicit original-optimum         5

6    error budget. It does not assume that unrestricted nonconvex fitting succeeds. On        6

7    a tensor cover, original-owner compilation preserves both the represented func- 7
8    tion and the owner’s feasible action at off-grid states. A complementary recursive       8

9    implementation uses verified curvature and protected conventional refinement,            9

10   allowing trained proposals to change optional query locations without weakening          10

18   first obtain these inequalities from own-future residuals and explicit acquisition       18

24       Under nonlinear recursive preferences, a physical-expectation identity is gener- 24
25   ally not available. We use monotonicity and cash invariance for the policy com- 25
26   parison. Centering the original conditional-risk dual at the stored continuation         26

27   preserves the nonnegative cost of changing the risk kernel. State-dependent error        27

28   functions can then yield a substantially smaller loss bound than a scalar worst-state    28

29   charge. The exact finite risk-occupation representation and strict support exclusions    29

30   implement this idea under their stated hypotheses. A frozen reference maximizer          30

31   is not substituted for the original nonlinear risk evaluation, and equality at an        31

1      A general comparison theorem is not yet an executable numerical method. The          1

2    continuous investment application supplies the missing primitive construction. The     2

3    original transition is nonlinear in the state, the feasible capacity depends on that   3

4    state, actions range over an interval and shocks have a continuous law. We prove       4

5    joint curvature for the one-date risk objective, derive global affine value supports   5

6    with an explicit multiplier-stationarity allowance, and establish a state modulus. 6
7    These results yield an all-action lower witness and a complete-policy upper cost       7

8    under the original continuous innovation law. Innovation bins are sets on which        8

9    inequalities hold, not new point masses in the economic model.                         9

10     This application also permits construction, learning, qualification, computation     10

11   and policy-cost inference to be evaluated on the same problem. At each of two fixed    11

12   risk levels, oblique ReLU, additive spline and projected-quadratic continuations       12

13   use the same labels and nominal feature counts; conventional online risk dynamic       13

14   programming pays for its original continuation queries. Both an own-continuation       14

15   target and a common original-optimum target are required. Training and calibration     15

16   are separated from the 4,096 initial-state observations used in the evaluation, and    16

17   all 16,384 arm-state records are retained. The ReLU fits have narrower whole-square    17

18   residual brackets, but every simultaneous policy-cost interval includes zero. All      18

19   four policies are equivalent at the prespecified normalized margin; no strict cost     19

20   superiority is identified.                                                             20

21     Stored continuations reduce measured work on this workload even after pay- 21
22   ing for the original-value lower witness. This is not a uniquely neural result: the    22

23   conventional fitted representations obtain comparable reductions, and the projected- 23
24   quadratic features have lower effective rank. Likewise, strict continuum screening     24

25   removes candidate bins but is slightly slower once its proof is paid for. The ar- 25
26   ticle distinguishes these observations from analytical certificate improvements,       26

27   from construction overhead and from a full cross-architecture or high-dimensional      27

28   implementation claim.                                                                  28

30     Relation to existing methods. Lipschitz extensions and distance transforms pro- 30
31   vide established construction tools (McShane, 1934, Felzenszwalb and Hutten- 31
32   locher, 2012). Approximate policy iteration, safe improvement and convex dynamic       32
     4

1    programming have established error and implementation analyses (Munos, 2003,              1

2    Pirotta et al., 2013, Scherrer et al., 2015, Yang, 2020). Learned warm starts and         2

3    optimization-based control address related computational questions (Sakaue and            3

4    Oki, 2023, Sambharya et al., 2023, Agrawal et al., 2020). Our construction retains        4

5    the continuation’s own future, the feasible action owner and the original-optimum         5

6    accounting obligations. An exact neural encoding and a native encoding of the same        6

7    function are not two different economic methods.                                          7

8        Risk-averse Bellman equations and policy iteration are established results            8

9    (Ruszczyński, 2010, 2014); feature-based risk evaluation and structured improve- 9
10   ment are also established research directions (Ruszczyński and Yang, 2025). We do        10

11   not claim conditional-risk duality or elementary centering as new. The theorem- 11
12   level comparison in Section 9.5 distinguishes those tools from the state-dependent        12

13   qualification object, its risk-distorted occupation representation and its constructive   13

14   all-action implementation. Standard convex duality supplies the support principle         14

15   (Boyd and Vandenberghe, 2004); independently verified multipliers, the original           15

16   feasible menu and the paid error set determine whether it licenses an exclusion           16

17   here.                                                                                     17

18       Recent feature-based risk learning also includes multipattern risk approxima- 18
19   tion and high-probability regret analysis (Ruszczynski and Zhang, 2026). Those            19

20   results concern learning in finite-state risk models under their feature and sampling     20

21   assumptions. Our fixed-accuracy horizon construction instead uses the specified           21

22   continuous investment primitives and pays for deterministic state, action and in- 22
23   novation resolution. A rational-operation bound for that construction is not an           23

24   episode-regret bound and does not supersede the learning result.                          24

25       The retained economic accuracy and selection results similarly use established        25

26   concentration, control-variate and decision-theoretic tools (Munos, 2006, Jiang and       26

27   Li, 2016, Howard et al., 2020, Karampatziakis et al., 2021, Manski, 2004, Floyd, 1962). 27
28   Their role is to connect verified optimization accuracy to a sufficient economic          28

29   stopping region. A narrower certificate for an unchanged controller is not a new          29

30   economic observation. The full earlier investment catalogue, including adverse            30

31   rankings, unresolved comparisons and the effect of affine calibration in both ReLU        31

32   and Bernstein bases, remains in the appendix.                                             32
