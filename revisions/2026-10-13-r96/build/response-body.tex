\section{Response to the referee: Neural Bellman Operators,
R96}\label{response-to-the-referee-neural-bellman-operators-r96}}

We thank the referee for the detailed report on R95. The controlling
report is commit \path{2fcf0bbd5926ee927c14a5f6d122fb50bb420988},
reviewing the manuscript at
\path{85263fd95ccbb60f0cf1875c0f29f1b7c2558a34}. We revise that original
paper and retain its title, author, own-future neural construction,
original economic technology, complete-policy optimum and broader
applications. We address the request for focus by reorganizing complete
sections, not by substituting a different paper or deleting adverse
evidence.

The principal new result is Proposition 15, an executable continuous-law
AVaR enclosure using the convexity of the original value, with a
second-order resolution bound. Theorem 17 connects a globally qualified
complete service to a prespecified simultaneous inference budget.
Proposition 18 gives the corresponding acquisition-and-reuse decision
region. The new experiment commits its protocol, then its actual
rank-matched models and selector, before opening a new confirmation
stream. Its outcomes and actual verification status are reported by the
source-bound tables and \texttt{RELEASE96.json}; a submitted workflow is
not counted as successful execution.

The exact equality in Corollary 16 also shows that the integration-rate
exponent and leading constant are sharp for the stated midpoint and
endpoint rules, already on explicit two-unit convex ReLU continuations.
The full threshold proof and exact-rational witnesses are retained. This
is not a lower bound for every possible integration algorithm or a claim
of neural empirical superiority.

\hypertarget{m2-b1.-one-central-contribution-without-replacing-nbo}{%
\subsection{M2 / B1. One central contribution without replacing
NBO}\label{m2-b1.-one-central-contribution-without-replacing-nbo}}

The main argument is now own-future feasible NBO construction,
original-law complete-policy qualification, executable continuous-risk
precision and a controlled economic comparison. The new introduction
states which part is a constructive neural representation, which
guarantee is representation-neutral, and which empirical comparison
concerns storage. The benchmark and original topic remain unchanged.
Validity does not require pretending that a non-neural encoding of the
same continuation has different economic properties.

The new precision result is not credited with inventing convex
quadrature order, the AVaR threshold formula, or Hoeffding's inequality.
Its role is to connect these tools to an executable original-law policy
interval, a complete service and a prospective economic resolving-power
requirement. The paper's own-future construction and wider original-law
results remain substantive components of this chain.

\hypertarget{m3-b2.-matched-representation-controls}{%
\subsection{M3 / B2. Matched representation
controls}\label{m3-b2.-matched-representation-controls}}

The new primary slice gives each stored representation exactly six
independent design columns, 1,024 common training labels per risk level
and a 128-byte executable payload. The payload stores the family
identifier, selected feature geometry and readout; it is not padded with
unused parameters to imitate matching. All three families use three-step
searches over 32 candidates. Every attempted candidate solve, rejected
rank-deficient design, active coefficient count, condition number and
fitting cost is retained. Spline knots adapt to training covariates and
feature selection uses training labels only.

This repairs the previous rank-six versus rank-35 comparison. It does
not claim to match active statistical degrees of freedom or realized
processor time, nor to exhaust all rank, label and compute frontiers.
Identical candidate schedules and stored sizes are controls, not proof
that every family receives an identical optimization problem. The online
original-risk continuation remains a paid comparator. A broader adaptive
cached-convex-regression and multi-budget Pareto frontier remains a
further comparison rather than a conclusion of this one controlled
slice.

\hypertarget{m4-b7.-new-remotely-frozen-confirmation}{%
\subsection{M4 / B7. New remotely frozen
confirmation}\label{m4-b7.-new-remotely-frozen-confirmation}}

\texttt{PROTOCOL96.md} fixes the model, representations, admission rule,
candidate search, sample size, statistical rule, margin, ablation,
timing workload and audit sample before construction. The actual model
binaries, source identities and calibration selector are then committed
before evaluation. The evaluator refuses to open the confirmation stream
unless the freeze is present in the checked-out commit and all protected
source and model hashes agree.

The new streams use a separate SeedSequence namespace from R94 and R95.
There are 4,096 independent initial states per risk level, paired across
all four services. No sample extension, arm replacement, refitting or
selector change is permitted after evaluation. A rejected
\emph{proposal} follows the already declared, paid fallback of its
complete service and remains recorded; it is not a silently replaced
experimental arm. A second-architecture replay of that stream, if
completed, adds no independent economic observations.

\hypertarget{m5-b3b4.-economic-margin-and-prospective-power}{%
\subsection{M5 / B3--B4. Economic margin and prospective
power}\label{m5-b3b4.-economic-margin-and-prospective-power}}

The new margin is one percent of the maximum first-date investment
charge: \texttt{1/1280}, eighty times smaller than \texttt{1/16}. This
is an explicit requirement of a model-relative purchaser. It is not an
estimated monetary welfare scale or a claim of external calibration. The
design requires every complete service to have an original-optimum loss
at most \texttt{1/256} everywhere on the declared initial domain.
Therefore any paired true policy contrast lies in
\texttt{{[}-1/256,1/256{]}}.

The new integration rule supplies a cost-interval width target of
\texttt{1/8192}. With 4,096 initial states per risk level and a sampling
radius of \texttt{1/4096}, the twelve fixed intervals have family
coverage at least 95 percent, and their prospective half-width allowance
is at most \texttt{3/8192}, less than half the economic margin. The
proof does not use observed extrema or sample variance to invent a
favorable support. Outward arithmetic is separate, and the nondyadic
economic margin is rounded inward for classifications. Whether any
actual comparison establishes equivalence or superiority is determined
by the retained outcomes, not by the power calculation alone.

\hypertarget{m6-b5.-acquisition-and-reuse}{%
\subsection{M6 / B5. Acquisition and
reuse}\label{m6-b5.-acquisition-and-reuse}}

The model records separate common-label, search, cover,
serialization/reload and calibration costs. Fresh-process workloads
include interpreter startup, loading, source/model checks, proposals,
original-law admission, all fallback, output and shutdown. Independent
rational verification is charged as a separately identified campaign. An
amortization calculation must state whether that campaign is included in
the acquisition charge; it cannot disappear silently.

The new purchase proposition gives the joint policy-quality,
workload-count and computation-price region. It covers nonpositive
savings denominators instead of dividing by an assumed positive speedup.
The supplied prices and future workload remain external economic inputs.
Observed timing extrema yield conditional sensitivity calculations, not
probabilistic bounds on an unobserved deployment. Missing cost
measurements are not assigned zero in a claim of complete economic
superiority.

\hypertarget{m7.-repeated-fresh-process-timing}{%
\subsection{M7. Repeated fresh-process
timing}\label{m7.-repeated-fresh-process-timing}}

The protocol specifies five fresh processes per service--risk pair on a
fixed 32-state timing workload, forty processes in total. Parent elapsed
time includes startup and shutdown. CPU model, reported frequency,
architecture, thread settings, logical primitive counts and maximum
resident memory are retained. Frequency is recorded, not claimed to be
controlled. The descriptive range is not presented as a confidence
interval for arbitrary future machines, and the workload is not counted
as additional policy-quality evidence.

A separately triggered ARM64 replay uses the identical frozen models and
source. Completion and any cross-architecture comparison require its
actual receipt; an available workflow alone is not evidence. The full
construction is not rerun or counted as an independent fit during
frozen-policy replication. The manuscript therefore separates repeated
within-machine evidence from the stronger full-construction
multi-architecture claim.

\hypertarget{m8-b6.-original-vector-and-horizon-obligations}{%
\subsection{M8 / B6. Original vector and horizon
obligations}\label{m8-b6.-original-vector-and-horizon-obligations}}

The retained horizon-uniform proposition still applies to the original
scalar and two-control/two-innovation economies under its hypotheses,
with explicit state/action/innovation and bit-growth costs. The new
convex-order rule additionally has a product-innovation version, with a
sum of coordinate precision allowances and a product evaluation cost. It
does not automatically apply to a nonconvex min-of-cones continuation.

The new primary execution remains a two-date scalar-control experiment.
It is not relabeled as the requested full vector or long-horizon
execution. The original adverse vector measurements are retained
unchanged, and a full matched vector implementation and independent
endpoint comparison remain an empirical obligation. This distinction
preserves the original NBO research program and develops its available
precision tools without treating notation as an executed scalability
result.

\hypertarget{m9-b8.-independent-endpoint-and-inference-implementation}{%
\subsection{M9 / B8. Independent endpoint and inference
implementation}\label{m9-b8.-independent-endpoint-and-inference-implementation}}

The new rational checker does not import the production interval, actor,
quadrature, fitting or inference modules. It uses a separately
implemented threshold representation and original primitive
reconstruction, independently reconstructs stored candidate proposals,
and builds finer policy enclosures and an original-action lower witness.
Eight randomly selected rows in each service--risk stratum give 64
complete endpoint audits. Their selection rule is fixed before
confirmation. Finer independent enclosures must be contained in the
production enclosures, not merely overlap them.

All 32,768 row bounds and all twelve simultaneous paired mean intervals
receive a separate exact-rational check. The inference implementation no
longer reuses the production statistical formula. It proves its coverage
constant using a rational exponential-series inequality. The checker
still does not independently re-fit the learned stack or reconstruct the
conventional proposal optimizer in every row. Hash identity, exact
statistical replay and the random endpoint audit are reported as
distinct objects. The broader independent learning and largest-vector
implementation remains separate.

\hypertarget{m10-b10.-prospective-certificate-ablation}{%
\subsection{M10 / B10. Prospective certificate
ablation}\label{m10-b10.-prospective-certificate-ablation}}

Global norm, signed scalar and joint original-value support builders are
fixed before the new stream. The global builders use the declared
whole-state cover; the joint builder pays for original-value witnesses.
The comparison concerns the same \emph{proposed} policies. If a service
subsequently rejects a proposal, no scalar certificate of the proposal
is silently attached to its replacement policy. The ablation retains its
separate work, target-pass counts, actors and cover choices.

This is a prospective comparison of three specified implementations. It
is not an assertion that all three builders were tuned optimally to the
full accuracy--work frontier. The previous retrospective R95
re-accounting and the adverse paid screening experiment remain in the
archive with their original status.

\hypertarget{m11-b12.-economic-consequence-and-its-scope}{%
\subsection{M11 / B12. Economic consequence and its

The revision connects a substantially tighter model-relative quality
requirement to a total-cost purchase inequality, rather than identifying
broad-margin equivalence with welfare. Certification changes the
permissible policy support, the attainable simultaneous resolution and
the set of adoption decisions justified by a given cost-and-price
account. Actual interval signs and reuse scenarios are reported in the
source-bound tables.

The model is not externally calibrated to field data. The normalized
margin and externally supplied computation prices are explicit economic
assumptions. A field-calibrated welfare claim requires that additional
evidence; the new theorem and experiment do not fabricate it. The
manuscript remains an NBO economic-method paper and does not replace its
subject with a qualification-only topic or a no-go claim.

\hypertarget{technical-comments-112}{%
\subsection{Technical comments 1--12}\label{technical-comments-112}}

The convexity hypotheses now distinguish the true value, fitted
continuation and actual controller. \texttt{CONSTANTS96.json} maps
mathematical constants to code and tests. Per-service records retain
rejected proposals, exact fallback entries, maximum innovation
resolution and all qualified rows. The comparison table reports paired
numerical midpoints, numerical half-widths and simultaneous intervals.
The controlled six-column, 128-byte budget is explained without a
universal frontier claim. The dyadic initial distribution is separated
from continuous subsequent innovations in the abstract, main argument
and conclusion. Preparation and serialization are model-specific;
adverse screening is preserved. Whole-domain residuals are not
substituted for queried-state admissions. Tables carry the actual source
commit and frozen-input identities. Analytical horizon regularity
remains distinct from measured scalability. The independence statement
explicitly separates all-row exact inference, sampled numerical
endpoints and unreplicated portions of the learning and vector stack.

Finally, the targeted current bibliography corrects the author of the
2025 value-learning paper to Shangzhe Yang and the author and title of
arXiv:2605.00654 to Tiangang Zhang and \emph{Reinforcement Learning with
Markov Risk Measures and Multipattern Risk Approximation}. The prior
entries remain in the preserved bibliography. These corrections use the
primary publisher and preprint records, not an assumed title inferred
