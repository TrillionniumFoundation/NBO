                   1.2. B1, M1: The NBO contribution and the role of learning
8                                                                                              8

9        The revised article distinguishes the constructive own-future ReLU continua- 9
10   tion from the trained query selector and from the method-neutral verifier. It does        10

11   not infer neural computational superiority from an exact ReLU encoding. The               11

12   original operator, construction, witness-preserving compiler and acquired-policy          12

13   statements remain in the active theorem chain, with their arguments preserved in          13

14   the supplement and the complete earlier editions.                                         14

15       The positive new contribution is operational. A strong-convexity upper chord          15

16   and a global action-search lower bound can certify an actual proposed action before       16

17   a new Bellman query. A margin loss bounds the endpoint work left unresolved by            17

18   the proposal. A fixed-feature ReLU readout can minimize a convex certificate-aware        18

19   objective with a directly computable optimization-gap certificate. These additions        19

20   do not rely on successful nonconvex hidden-layer training or on the unknown               20

21   location of the optimal action. They develop the existing NBO question rather than        21

22   substituting a different paper or claiming exclusivity for generic safe improvement. 22

                     1.4. B4, M3: A computable training-to-query connection
17                                                                                                17

18     The earlier localization proposition contains an unknown action error and a                18

19   derivative at the unknown optimum. It is retained as an analytical result, but the           19

20   new section Operational prediction certificates and readout training supplies a              20

21   different, computable condition.                                                             21

22     For consecutive queried actions l < u, verified upper endpoints and a primitive            22

23   strong-convexity bound define a quadratic upper chord C. If the outward value of             23

24   C at the actual rounded proposal minus the existing global lower bound is at most            24

25   the local tolerance, the proposal is certified without a new endpoint query. Its proof       25

26   uses only the original objective’s strong convexity and valid endpoints. A failed            26

27   screen leaves the protected-refinement and full-policy guarantees intact.                    27

28     The score is then used to define a margin loss. If a failed screen can require at          28

29   most K (z) further endpoint queries in context z, the additional count is bounded            29

30   by K (z) times the clipped squared margin loss. This is a deterministic statement            30

31   for every specified context catalogue. A separate finite-family validation theorem           31

32   gives an expected-work bound under an explicitly declared, independent, predictor- 32
     4

1    independent context law. Recursively generated on-policy contexts are not assumed        1

2    to satisfy that law merely because they are stored in a transcript.                      2

3        The fixed-feature ReLU construction goes further. Nonnegative partition-of-unity     3

4    features and a box readout produce a feasible action inside a fixed context interval. 4
5    The squared positive-part certificate surplus, weighted by the fallback work cap, is     5

6    convex in the readout. Its achieved objective bounds unresolved work, and its box        6

7    dual gap bounds the optimization error. The deposited exact-rational implemen- 7
8    tation returns both quantities after finitely many conditional-gradient iterations. 8
9    Exact tests include nonvacuous pre-query closure and successful certificate-aware        9

10   fitting examples.                                                                        10

11       This constructive readout is distinct from unrestricted hidden-layer learning. The   11

12   benchmark’s L-BFGS models are not relabeled as minimizers of the new objective. 12
13   Their action-label mean squared errors, warnings and actual certificate progress         13

14   remain separate empirical records. The theorem therefore supplies the requested          14

15   operational connection without asserting an unexecuted population generalization         15

16   result for the benchmark fits.                                                           16

27                                                                                             27

28       The manuscript gives a proved simplicial query for the already specified two- 28
29   control, shared-capacity investment extension. A semiconcavity minorant is formed         29

30   on each action triangle. Its lower bound is computed by an outward supporting             30

31   plane, so an approximate quadratic minimizer has no certificate authority. Verified       31

32   feasible upper witnesses and a continuous simplex cover give the same local-gap           32

5    innovations. No state lattice or stored reference policy is loaded. Query counts, de- 5
