\begin{abstract}
This paper develops Neural Bellman Operators for constructive policy evaluation and feasible improvement. An own-future continuation retains action witnesses and an explicit policy-loss account. In a nonlinear investment economy, recursive Bellman queries replace the state lattice while a trained selector proposes where to obtain information. We derive a computable pre-query certificate, a loss that bounds additional verification work, and a fixed-feature ReLU readout with a certified optimization gap. The same original-optimum account extends to two controls and two continuous innovations. A source-frozen study compares ten selectors, eight fitting seeds, prospective cached reuse, and actual callable policies under the continuous law. Complete work, certificate progress and policy cost are reported separately. Reuse savings depend on the task and comparator; expected policy-cost differences remain statistically unresolved. The purchase criterion combines these measured objects without assuming neural superiority or equating a tighter certificate with an economic gain.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction65}
A continuation approximation matters because it helps produce an economic decision. Prediction error, the loss of the implemented policy and the work needed to certify that loss are different objects. This paper connects them within a constructive Neural Bellman Operator (NBO). The economic target is a feasible complete policy under the original law, not an action that is optimal only for a fitted surrogate.

The first construction evaluates each continuation against its own fitted future and retains feasible action witnesses. Its affine--ReLU realization and primitive error account do not require a successful nonconvex optimizer. The second implementation queries the original Bellman problem recursively, only at states needed by its current action search. A trained selector proposes where to query; outward lower and upper bounds determine whether information is sufficient. These are two implementations within the same NBO development, not two encodings counted as independent methods.

The nonlinear investment economy has state-dependent capacity and continuous innovations. Its primitive curvature controls the convexity defect of the bilinear transition. This yields Lipschitz and semiconcavity bounds for the true Bellman value, and hence continuous-action certificates without a tensor state table. The trained continuation or selector is not assumed convex. Boundary queries, protected refinement, acquired-state feasibility and an explicitly costed rational recovery determine the complete policy's original-optimum guarantee.

The new operational question is whether a proposed action can be licensed before purchasing its Bellman value. Strong convexity gives an upper chord from two existing endpoint bounds. Subtracting the global action-search lower bound produces a computable regret certificate. If that certificate closes, the actual rounded proposal can be returned without another query. If it does not close, protected conventional refinement remains available. This criterion replaces an unobserved distance to the minimizing action by information present in the numerical transcript.

A corresponding margin loss measures unresolved verification work. Its achieved value bounds additional endpoint queries on a specified context catalogue. A separate validation result states the distributional conclusion and the required independence assumptions. We also give a fixed-feature ReLU readout whose certificate-aware objective is convex and whose optimization error is bounded by a computable box dual gap. This constructive training result does not assert convergence of unrestricted hidden-layer fitting. The numerical comparison retains its separately frozen L-BFGS, quadratic and other selectors, and evaluates their actual certificate progress rather than equating action-label fit with verification savings.

The empirical design tests the complete procedure. Ten selectors receive identical kernels and screens, including linear, spline, nearest-action, derivative-based and simple analytic controls. Eight predetermined fitting seeds separate training variability from two clock repetitions. A prospective reuse experiment loads a saved model for twenty-four new workloads and deliberately declines a stale acquisition identifier. Fresh common-path inference evaluates the actual callable controllers at every date under the original continuous law. A two-control, two-innovation extension reaches four dates and reports the resulting descendant cost and memory instead of inferring horizon scalability from state dimension alone.

An independent rational check also detected a path-accounting error in the preceding execution: mutable lower and upper cumulative-cost endpoints shared an array. We retained that execution, repaired the accumulator and froze a complete new run of the same catalogue. The random indices are unchanged to isolate the correction, so repeated streams are not counted as new independent observations. The active tables use corrected path enclosures and their own complete process clocks. This separates a numerical repair from a change in model, target or selected seed.

The resulting evidence distinguishes three conclusions. The query procedure returns certified policies without a stored state lattice. Learning can change the amount and location of verification work, and a saved selector can be assessed under actual reuse rather than an assumed amortization ratio. Neither conclusion alone identifies lower expected economic cost or representation-specific superiority. The purchase decision therefore uses observed work, direct policy-cost intervals and the declared price of computation as separate inputs.

\paragraph{Relation to existing methods.}
Lipschitz extension and distance transforms underlie the constructive envelope \citep{mcshane1934,felzenszwalb2012}. Approximate and safe policy iteration supply established error and performance analyses \citep{munos2003,pirotta2013,scherrer2015}, while \citet{yang2020convex} studies convex dynamic-programming approximation. Prediction-dependent warm starts and learned optimization-based control also have an established literature \citep{sakaue2023warm,sambharya2023,agrawal2020control}. We do not claim those general principles. The additional chain here runs from the original nonlinear economic primitives to an acquired-policy certificate, a pre-query action decision, an operational training loss and complete measured work. The verifier is method-neutral; the constructive neural representation and the empirical value of a fitted selector are distinct claims.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion65}
Neural Bellman Operators connect continuation construction to feasible decisions under the original economic law. The witness construction supplies an explicit own-future loss account. Primitive curvature also supports a recursive query implementation without a state lattice. A computable upper chord can license a predicted action before another Bellman evaluation, while the lower certificate retains authority over accuracy.

The operational loss measures verification work rather than distance to an unknown optimum. Its fixed-feature ReLU readout has a certified convex optimization account. The two-control query extends the same policy guarantee to the previously specified coupled-capacity economy. These are constructive statements, distinct from a claim that a particular fitted network wins a computational comparison.

The full study makes that comparison observable. It charges training, loading, invalid reuse, queries, path enclosures and durable records; evaluates actual complete policies under the continuous law; and records the growth of a two-innovation continuation tree. The current gain intervals do not identify a representation-specific economic advantage. Keeping that finding alongside the new positive certificates preserves the paper's original objective: a useful numerical decision must be feasible, accurate and worth its complete cost.
