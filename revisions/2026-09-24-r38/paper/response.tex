\subsection{R36-F3: exact operating value and a deployable outer bound}
Exact $V$ is now explicitly a reference input whose construction is charged. It is not needed for the general deterministic lower-bound theorem. Given checked $L_t\leq V_t\leq U_t$, define
\[
 \cN_t^{L,U}(x)=\{a:L_t(x)-T_t^aU_{t+1}(x)\leq\varepsilon\}.
\]
Every operating-feasible deterministic action lies in this set, because
$L_t-T_t^aU_{t+1}\leq V_t-T_t^aV_{t+1}\leq\varepsilon$.
The implementation-cost recursion on this outer class is therefore valid without exact access to $V$. Both the safe-witness and exact-value lower recursions are executed on all 42 configurations; their differences are reported. The theorem also gives monotone refinement of nested operating witnesses to the exact necessary-action relaxation. That limit remains a relaxation unless its minimizing policy is feasible or its value matches a separately feasible policy. We do not convert convergence of an outer representation into an unsupported global convergence assertion.

\subsection{R36-F4: deterministic, randomized, and history-conditioned classes}
Section 2 defines deterministic Markov policies, randomized Markov kernels, and history-conditioned policies with operating feasibility at each admissible decision history. Conditioning occurs before the current action lottery. Section 4 proves the deterministic lower bound for both deterministic Markov policies and deterministic history-conditioned policies with that stronger restart requirement. It does not apply actionwise screening to a lottery.

The distinction is substantive. Section 5 solves a two-period version with the same maintenance primitives, defer incumbent, and uniform initial distribution. The deterministic optimum is $3/2$. The randomized Markov optimum is approximately 1.3720075153 at tolerance 0.01 and 0.8600375764 at tolerance 0.05. Exact rational enclosures of the analytic logarithmic integral accompany these numbers. Randomized sufficiency of deterministic actions is therefore false in this model family. The 24 primary equalities are always described as deterministic optima for the stated initial objective. The separate frontier study gives exact history-conditioned, fixed-restart optima; it is not presented as the primary randomized Markov optimum.

\subsection{R36-F5: specify the objective and meaning of exactness}
The primary objective is expected discounted implementation cost under the uniform distribution on $[0,1]$, at date zero, using the deployed policy's own transitions. Its constraint is operating feasibility at every state and date. The proof objects contain rational lower and upper integrated costs, rational cost-to-go functions, a policy hash, lower-function hash, frozen-input hash, and exact all-restart feasibility checks. The printed supplement gives all 42 decimal intervals; \path{results/primary_exact_values.json} gives the corresponding fractions and hashes.

Twenty-four cases have exact integrated equality, but only 23 have function equality at every date and state. The exception is horizon 8, neural31003, tolerance $1/20$. The article and tables distinguish these scopes. An isolated-state discrepancy cannot be erased by an integral, and neither can a later-restart discrepancy. Exactness is decided in rational arithmetic, never by a decimal tolerance.

\subsection{R36-F7: local LP accuracy and distinct gap sources}
The local three-action, one-inequality problem is solved by feasible pure vertices and two-action mixtures. Its dual candidates are zero and nonnegative intersections of action lines. The cell constructor handles the resulting rational primal frontier by exact quadratic inequalities over whole cells, including endpoint limits; isolated points receive exact point-LP solutions. Directed dyadic coefficient encoding bounds representation error. The proof supplies a discounted accumulation bound for approximation to the continuous local restart relaxation.

The experiments separate five sources rather than assigning one residual to all of them. On all 42 configurations, the global support study compares constructed versus exact operating witnesses at fixed prices, then eight versus fifteen global prices at fixed exact value. The deterministic study compares safe versus exact necessary sets and the inherited versus enriched upper portfolio, and records infeasibility of the necessary-set selector. The local-LP diagnostic separately replaces the eight-price local approximation at three tolerances and two operating-witness choices. Its floor is fixed at zero. Thus it measures local multiplier/representation error; it is not an additive decomposition of the nonzero inherited R34 support floor, nor a proof that a structural global duality gap vanishes. Global prices $\lambda$ and local dual variables $\mu$ use separate notation and result fields.
