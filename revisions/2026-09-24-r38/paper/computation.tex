\section{Inputs, objects, and arithmetic contracts}
\subsection{Frozen primary inputs}
The primary cohort retains the seven R34 installed-rule families at each of three horizons and two operating tolerances. Neural fitting is not repeated. The raw-policy serialization and its SHA-256 are retained in every primary proof. All newly computed operating, implementation, and feasibility quantities are obtained from the explicit maintenance primitives rather than from an assumed correctness label on an old result. The inherited feasible R34 candidate is one attempted upper candidate and is reevaluated with the new exact policy evaluator.

The exact operating program is shared across the fourteen configurations at each horizon. Constructed $L/U$ operating witnesses, exact necessary-action lower costs, safe-witness lower costs, and seven named upper attempts are evaluated with Fraction arithmetic. A policy is eligible as an upper bound only after its entire operating-regret function passes the all-restart test. The policy's cost uses its own transition law. Integrated equality and complete function equality are distinct Boolean fields.

\subsection{Primary proof objects and separate verification}
Each \path{results/primary/*.json.gz} object specifies rational knots, open-cell affine coefficients, isolated-point values, terminal conditions, actions, operating values, cost functions, witnesses, exact integrals, and hashes. Decimal table entries are derived renderings. Rational strings are parsed as exact numbers; illegal actions, nonincreasing knots, inconsistent dates, malformed strings, or mismatched primitives fail verification.

The primary verifier is a separate SymPy implementation. It rebuilds the transition pullback partition and verifies every relevant open-cell endpoint limit and isolated point. It checks both Bellman equalities and inequalities, exact necessary-action membership, minimizing selectors, policy recursions, cost integrals, and exactness scope. It does not import the Fraction/PW implementation. Twenty corruption categories exercise the model, parser, partition, policy, numerical values, and metadata. The mutation ledger records each expected rejection rather than describing a test category without running it.

\subsection{Constrained frontier references}
The 70 fixed-restart frontier objects contain all recursively used history-conditioned feasible frontiers and the exact minimum initial cost. The verifier rebuilds successor candidate combinations in its own rational implementation and checks primal convex combinations and supporting lines. Candidate feasibility includes every downstream decision history, not only the initial operating constraint. Reusing a statewise frontier set does not force the selected continuation point to be the same after every history reaching that state.

The two analytic two-period uniform-initial randomized Markov references are separate entries in \path{results/frontier.json}. Their cost integrals reduce to rational terms and logarithms. The reported rational enclosures use an explicit 40-term logarithm series with a remainder bound. Those two analytic references are not included in the count of 70 independently checked finite-tree objects or in the 42 primary deterministic objects.

\subsection{Adaptive local LP}
The 12 local experiments vary the operating tolerance, the witness type, and the requested local accuracy. A fixed zero continuation support floor is used in this diagnostic. The constructor chooses a pointwise exact primal/dual pair, then proves feasibility and a gap bound over the entire affine input cell by rational polynomial sign tests. It validates dual-line changes and isolated points explicitly. Cells that fail are refined; a depth-cap event is an error rather than a certificate.

A preliminary unquantized local attempt encountered excessive rational-string growth. Its failure log is preserved in the history directory. The accompanying reconstruction source is labeled as a reconstruction, not as a byte-identical original run artifact. The published constructor uses 40-bit directed downward coefficients and includes the resulting analytic error allowance. The development execution subsequently added and passed a stronger full-dual/isolated-point validation; the publication execution reruns the complete final constructor and records its full time. The older local timing before that added validation is not used as the publication timing.

\subsection{Economic sensitivities}
Each of the 66 rational sensitivity objects retains the changed primitive, the installed rule, the common sufficient action restriction, both policy selectors, their separately evaluated operating/cost functions, and the exact initial-density integral. All zero savings remain in the output. The two sensitivity tolerances are explicitly separate from the unchanged primary tolerances. Scenario counts have no sampling interpretation.

\subsection{Nonlinear whole-box objects}
The nonlinear experiment stores 20 witness-array sets and 76 candidate-policy records. All Bellman enclosures use outward integer rounding at scale $2^{34}$. Monotone bilinear maps are bounded by box corners; the generated successor-cell spans are checked before the four-corner array stencil is used. Reward, terminal, and revision-indicator bounds cover complete boxes, including those crossed by an installed-rule threshold. Integer-range assertions reject an unsafe intermediate magnitude.

The pilot is a midpoint-compiled box action rule. It is not the exact continuous installed rule on boxes that cross an action threshold, and its implementation cost is consequently not assumed zero there. Restart and classical grid candidates are assessed with the same whole-box policy evaluator. A failed sufficient operating certificate does not identify an actual operating violation. The nonlinear arrays receive exact integer/read-only consistency checks, not the separate SymPy row/partition verification used for the one-dimensional primary cohort.

\section{Result inventory and the limits of audit independence}
There are 42 primary proof objects, 66 sensitivity objects, 70 fixed-restart frontier objects, 12 adaptive local-LP objects, 21 global-support bundles, and 42 gap-function bundles. The nonlinear archive separately stores witness and policy arrays. The canonical cross-execution comparison covers 187 objects in the primary, frontier, local-LP, support, and gap-function directories. It excludes timing ledgers and NPZ container timestamps; it is an exact object-identity check, not a second proof system.

Mathematical implementation independence is narrower and explicit. Forty-two primary objects and 70 frontier objects pass the separate SymPy verifier. Local-LP, sensitivity, and nonlinear checks do not acquire that label because they appear in the same publication. The independent checker and constructor still share the intended mathematical model, file format, and general Python execution environment. Mutation tests reduce specified implementation risks but do not prove freedom from all bugs.

\section{Publication and preservation checks}
The science workflow commits its actual execution outputs to the isolated R38 branch. The publication workflow starts from that committed state, materializes the authored paper source, rebuilds every generated table, and compiles the five root PDF objects. Every final log is checked for undefined references/citations, fatal errors, and materially overfull boxes. PDF metadata and extracted text are inspected programmatically; representative full-page renders are inspected separately during document preparation. The historical annex incorporates the earlier R34 article, supplement, response, computation report, and recursive history PDF without deleting their pages.

The preservation manifest checks that files inherited from the review base remain unchanged, except the root revision index whose exact former content is retained under the R38 history directory. Old review reports, protocols, source, failed runs, and manuscript PDFs are not overwritten. New scientific and publication files live in the R38 tree or use new R38 root names. No force-push or modification of a different branch is part of the publication procedure.

The publication manifest deliberately records the source commit and workflow identifier rather than attempting a self-referential hash of its own final Git commit. The final remote branch HEAD identifies the committed package containing that manifest. Its file-hash map covers the completed new artifacts; the external Git commit gives the authoritative review snapshot. A failed run retains its logs and is not described as a passing publication.
