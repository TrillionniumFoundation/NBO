\section{Action comparisons that can change the investment decision}
\label{sec:improvement120}
An accurate continuation approximation contributes economically when it
helps choose a better feasible action at an acceptable complete cost.
The preceding service experiments kept the implemented actions fixed.
We now construct an action comparison whose acquisition error decreases
with the distance between the actions, and use it to safeguard actual
policy changes.  The original nested AVaR criterion, information available
to the economic agent, capacity constraint, and future controller remain
those in Section~\ref{sec:continuous95}.

At a fixed state write
\[
 f_a(z)=V_1(F(x,a,z)),\qquad
 Q_x(a)=S(x)+h(a)+\beta\rho_\alpha(f_a).
\]
The actual complete-policy cost is $J_x(a)$, with the same implemented
last-date policy for every current action.  Its retained uniform guarantee is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:future120}
 J_x(a)=Q_x(a)+\eta_x(a),\qquad
 0\le\eta_x(a)\le\delta,\qquad
 \delta=\beta\varepsilon_1,\quad \varepsilon_1=2^{-20}.
\end{equation}
The function $\eta_x$ is not assumed convex or differentiable.

\subsection{A comparison modulus from the original technology}
The following constant concerns the original optimal continuation, not a
fitted critic or an empirical gradient bound.  This addition is restricted
to the original two-dimensional application.

\begin{theorem}[Original action comparison modulus]
\label{thm:modulus120}
For $d=2$, $\beta=15/16$, $R=1/32$, and
$\alpha\in\{1/2,3/4\}$, put $t=1-\alpha$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modulus120}
 M_\alpha=\frac{1413}{128}
       +\beta\left\{\frac{127}{16}
                 +\frac{19625\alpha}{20480t}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The original $V_1$ has an $M_\alpha$-Lipschitz gradient on the state
cube, with the continuous boundary extension.  Consequently, for every
original state and every two feasible actions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixedmodulus120}
 \operatorname{Lip}_z(f_b-f_a)
 \le E_{ab}:=\frac45M_\alpha|b-a|.
\end{equation}
Both conclusions include switches of the constrained terminal optimizer
and of its active risk region.
\end{theorem}

The proof normalizes terminal investment as $A(y)v$, $v\in[0,1]$,
and bounds the state semiconcavity of that fixed-domain objective.
It therefore does not differentiate a rounded actor or ignore the
capacity multiplier.  The earlier expected-cost smoothness result in
Section~\ref{sec:accuracy62} is retained with its original criterion;
the extra term in \eqref{eq:modulus120} pays for the movement of the
original AVaR tail.

Fix one feasible original-law tail density
$q_u=t^{-1}\boldsymbol1_{[-R,u]\cup[u+2R\alpha,R]}$.
Partition its nonempty tails into cells of lengths $h_i$, midpoints
$z_i$, and weights $w_i=h_i/B$, where $B=2Rt$.
For each of the two actions, acquire valid point-value intervals at
these midpoints and endpoints.  Let $L_a,L_b$ be their weighted
midpoint lower sums and $U_a,U_b$ their weighted endpoint-secant
upper sums.  Let $[M_-,M_+]$ enclose
$\sum_iw_i\{f_b(z_i)-f_a(z_i)\}$ using the paired midpoint intervals.
All of these acquisitions, including both action payloads, are paid.

\begin{theorem}[A paid action-aligned original-policy certificate]
\label{thm:paired120}
Define
\begin{align}
 e_{ab}&=\frac{E_{ab}}{4B}\sum_i h_i^2,\label{eq:pairradius120}\\
 [d_-,d_+]&=[L_b-U_a,\ U_b-L_a]\,
                  \cap[M_--e_{ab},\ M_++e_{ab}].
 \label{eq:pairintersection120}
\end{align}
This intersection contains $\mathbb E_P[q_u(f_b-f_a)]$.
If the two original tail deficits satisfy
$0\le k_a(q_u)\le\kappa_a$ and
$0\le k_b(q_u)\le\kappa_b$, the actual policy contrast satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:policycontrast120}
 J_x(b)-J_x(a)\in h(b)-h(a)
       +\beta[d_--\kappa_a-\varepsilon_1,
                     d_++\kappa_b+\varepsilon_1].
\end{equation}
Every numerical operation must enclose the displayed exact quantities
outwardly.  In particular, the separately acquired node errors and
the tail defects are retained.

With exact point values, $K$-Lipschitz convex $f_a,f_b$, an
integer $n\ge1$, and $h_i\le2Rt/n$, the fixed-density interval
has width at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixedwidth120}
 \min\left\{\frac{2KRt}{n^2},
                   \frac{E_{ab}Rt}{n}\right\}.
\end{equation}
For the original investment continuations one may take $K=15$;
their additional smoothness sharpens the same computed interval to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smoothpairwidth120}
 \min\left\{
   \frac{\min\{2KRt,\ 2M_\alpha R^2t^2\}}{n^2},
   \frac{E_{ab}Rt}{n}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The exactly identical realized action has contrast zero and requires
no new density or point values.
\end{theorem}

The second interval in \eqref{eq:pairintersection120} uses a Lipschitz
bound for a difference; it never treats that difference as convex.
The common-density risk identity was frozen in R111 and proved in
Theorem~\ref{thm:coupled119}.  The present construction supplies its
missing action-scaled integration bound and an executable refinement
gate.  Neither the identity nor generic safe policy iteration is claimed
as a new neural property.

\paragraph{What the information bound does and does not establish.}
For a fixed known feasible tail density, let the information consist of
exact point values of either function, $K$, an upper bound $E$ on the
Lipschitz constant of their difference, and the original uniform law.
One scalar value of one function counts as one query; obtaining both
values at a common point costs two.  With this convention there is an
algorithm using at most $4n+8$ values and having the width in
\eqref{eq:mixedwidth120}.  Conversely, for every deterministic adaptive
algorithm using at most $N$ such values, a known one-sided tail and a
consistent pair force interval width at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pairlower120}
 \min\left\{\frac{KRt}{48(N+1)^2},
                    \frac{ERt}{12(N+1)}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Thus the worst-case order for this fixed-tail interface is
$\min\{KRtN^{-2},ERtN^{-1}\}$, for $N\ge32$.
Supplement Proposition~\ref{prop:pairinformation120} gives the complete
quantifiers and proof.  Localization, original risk deficits, node
precision, and descendant Bellman solves are additional information
costs.  The lower bound is not an oracle lower bound for an agent already
given the algebraic formula or every additional smoothness restriction
of the investment model.

\subsection{Safe refinement, stopping, and the future-policy floor}
Start from a completed original-policy incumbent.  A proposed replacement
is accepted only when the upper endpoint of
\eqref{eq:policycontrast120} is strictly negative and its own policy
interval passes the unchanged regret target $\tau=1/256$ and width
target $1/8192$.  At the same state an original-optimum lower bound
may be reused across actions; an action's policy interval may not.
Refuse a candidate when its lower contrast endpoint is nonnegative,
or retain the incumbent when the prespecified work limit is exhausted.
Every refused trial remains in the complete cost.

\begin{proposition}[Uniformly safe completed-policy replacement]
\label{prop:safe120}
For every original state where the stated outward premises hold,
any finite sequence of these replacements preserves feasibility,
qualification, and weak improvement over its completed incumbent.
If accepted contrast intervals are $[\underline D_j,\overline D_j]$,
then the final original-policy gain satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gainaccount120}
 0\le\sum_j(-\overline D_j)
       \le J_x(a_{\mathrm{initial}})-J_x(a_{\mathrm{final}})
       \le\tau .
\end{equation}
A finite, measurable, statewise completed service satisfying these
premises therefore defines a uniformly safe policy rule.
\end{proposition}

The proposed support directions and fractions $1,1/2,1/4$ are
retained from R108--R110.  The new comparison ladder changes which
paid evidence can authorize their realized action changes.  A direct
original-continuation optimizer receives the same comparison mechanism.
The quadratic, polynomial, spline, grid, hinge, neural, and online
representations receive the same original formulas and certificate
access.  The current experiment does not change the critic fitting
objective or establish action-aware training convergence.

There is an explicit limit to refinement based only on
\eqref{eq:future120}.  Even an exact $Q_x(b)-Q_x(a)$ leaves the
sharp interface
\[
 J_x(b)-J_x(a)\in
 [Q_x(b)-Q_x(a)-\delta,\ Q_x(b)-Q_x(a)+\delta]
 \quad(a\ne b).
\]
It does not uniformly authorize strict improvement when
$Q_x(a)-Q_x(b)\le\delta$.  This is a restriction of the stated
continuation-error information, not an assertion that the actual
controller attains every endpoint or that more informative policy
evaluation cannot help.

Action alignment also controls risk-witness mismatch.  If
$m_z=181/64$, Theorem~\ref{thm:tailtransfer120} proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tailtransfer120}
 k_a(q_u)\le
 \left\{\sqrt{\kappa_b}
       +E_{ab}\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{2m_zt}}\right\}^{\!2}.
\end{equation}
At an exactly optimal candidate tail, the mismatch is quadratic in
$|b-a|$.  The implemented gate pays for and retains both direct
deficits, and intersects the incumbent deficit with this transferred
upper bound.  This can tighten the lower contrast and refusal rule;
the upper acceptance endpoint still uses the candidate deficit.
No saved endpoint acquisition or upper-gate gain is attributed to
that intersection.
Supplement Proposition~\ref{prop:resolution120} states the resulting
finite stopping guarantee, including the tail, node, future, and
qualification margins.  A capped unresolved comparison is recorded
as unresolved; it is not a certificate of optimality.

\subsection{From a safe decision rule to population economic gain}
A catalogue of successful gates is not by itself a population statement.
Conditional on frozen actors and a fixed statewise service, let
$X_1,\ldots,X_{64}$ be independent draws from the declared finite
uniform initial-state law.  Each complete service is applied separately
to each state, so its action and gain do not depend on the other sampled
states.  States may be shared across representations and risk levels.
Let $G_j(x)$ be the gain over the corresponding completed incumbent,
and let $[\ell_{ji},u_{ji}]$ be saved, valid gain intervals contained
in $[0,\tau]$.

\begin{proposition}[Simultaneous transfer of certified economic gains]
\label{prop:population120}
Consider the fixed $48$ gain groups and thresholds
$(\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3,\theta_4)
=(2^{-20},2^{-18},2^{-16},2^{-14})$.
For each group and threshold count only
$\boldsymbol1_{\{\ell_{ji}\ge\theta_k\}}$.
Invert its binomial upper-tail probability at error $1/7680$,
using an exact rational lower bracket endpoint, to obtain
$p^-_{jk}$.  Put $r=33\tau/128$ and
$\theta_0=0$.  With conditional probability at least $0.95$,
simultaneously for all groups,
\begin{align}
 \mathbb E G_j
 &\ge \sum_{k=1}^4(\theta_k-\theta_{k-1})p^-_{jk},
 \label{eq:staircase120}\\
 \max\{0,\overline\ell_j-r\}
 &\le\mathbb E G_j
 \le\min\{\tau,\overline u_j+r\}.
 \label{eq:meangain120}
\end{align}
Neither independence across groups nor disjoint samples across risk
levels are required.
\end{proposition}

The binomial inversion and bounded-mean concentration are classical
\citep{clopperpearson1934,hoeffding1963}; the proof verifies their
application to a uniformly safe original-policy gain, with all numerical
interval uncertainty and the prespecified multiplicity account retained.
The stored entropy bytes make the finite dyadic sample reproducible.
Their independence and uniformity are the sampling assumption, not a
mathematical conclusion established by a generator log.
Positive gain alone does not establish adoption: acquisition, fitting,
all attempted comparisons, inference, qualification, validation, and
repeated decision service must be charged against that economic gain.
