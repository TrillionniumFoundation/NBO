\section{A Verified Native Floating-Point Refresh}
\label{sec:r38native}
The storage schedule in Theorem~\ref{thm:r37precision} deliberately separates
an exact-arithmetic construction from its implementation. We now supply an
implementation that charges and verifies the target solve, input conversion,
matrix products, and output storage. It uses binary32 or binary64 proposals
and an outward binary64 verifier. Mixed-precision numerical linear algebra
and residual-based verification are established techniques
\citep{r38carson2018,r38rump2010}; the present application combines their
error accounting with a trainable continuation and an economic stopping
criterion. It is not a new claim of mixed-precision iterative refinement.

For a fixed stored target $M$, let certified bounds satisfy
$0<mI\preceq M\preceq LI$. The entries of the stored $M$ and $W$ are exact
binary coefficients for this step. Write $G=W'W$ for their exact Gram
matrix, and let $Z$ be any floating-point proposal for $M^{-1}G$.
Suppose interval computations provide
\[
 r_Z\geq\|MZ-G\|_F,\qquad
 e_P\geq\|\widehat W-\tfrac12W(3I-Z)\|_F,\qquad
 w_+\geq\|W\|_2.
\]
Here $\widehat W$ is the stored output of the native-precision update.
Residuals are evaluated against the original binary64 inputs, not their
lower-precision conversions.

\begin{proposition}[Complete-step floating-point enclosure]
\label{prop:r38float}
The perturbation in \eqref{eq:r37update} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r38float}
 \|\Delta M^{-1/2}\|_2\leq
 \nu:=\frac{e_P+w_+r_Z/(2m)}{\sqrt m}.
\end{equation}
If $\|E\|_2\leq\rho<1$, the next relative Gram error is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r38floatgram}
 \|E^+\|_2\leq\frac{3+\rho}{4}\rho^2+2\nu+\nu^2.
\end{equation}
Thus the squared-radius rule remains valid throughout the certified basin
$\rho<1$ whenever the complete-step noise is no larger than
$(1-(3+\rho)/4)\rho^2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$Z-M^{-1}G=M^{-1}(MZ-G)$, so its Frobenius norm is at most $r_Z/m$.
Subtract the ideal update from the stored output, apply the triangle
inequality, and then multiply by $M^{-1/2}$ to obtain
\eqref{eq:r38float}. In whitened coordinates $X=WM^{-1/2}$, the ideal
update is $X(3I-X'X)/2$, and its Gram error is
$(-3E^2+E^3)/4$. For $\rho<1$, its singular values do not exceed one:
the scalar map $s(3-s^2)/2$ is nonnegative and at most one for
$0\leq s\leq\sqrt2$. The perturbation therefore changes the Gram matrix
by at most $2\nu+\nu^2$. This proves \eqref{eq:r38floatgram}.
\end{proof}

An identity-scaled cold start is available without a target factorization.
In exact arithmetic, take the first $d$ rows of $W_0$ to be
$\sqrt{2mL/(m+L)}I$ and the remaining rows zero. Its relative radius is at
most $(L-m)/(L+m)<1$. In the implementation, outward bounds on the stored
scalar square verify this radius, including initialization roundoff. An
old factor is used only after a new-target Gram test gives a radius below
the declared gate $0.9$; otherwise the cold start is charged. The target is
immutable during a fit. Its numerical inverse may be cached once for each
of the two proposal precisions, but never carried unchanged across a target
change.

Near a machine precision floor, insisting on one more squared-radius step
can reject a factor that already meets its requested accuracy. The algorithm
therefore has two explicit acceptance tests. A proposal is accepted either
by \eqref{eq:r38floatgram} with the squared-radius allowance, or by a direct
outward terminal test
\[
 \|\widehat W'\widehat W-M\|_F/m\leq\tau_{\rm fit}.
\]
The latter is a terminal Gram certificate, not a claim of continued
quadratic contraction. Failed binary32 proposals are charged before a
binary64 attempt. If neither available precision passes either permitted
test, the service reports failure. No floating-point inverse is assumed
exact, no target Cholesky factor is supplied to the learner, and no exact
rational intermediate products are hidden in the timing.

The returned factor is not by itself a policy certificate. After every
changed actor, the implementation evaluates the entire returned policy
using the outward recursive comparison of
Theorem~\ref{thm:r27entropic}. It stops at the first passing full-policy
check, including a check of the initial policy. Thus actor solves and
nominal continuation proposals need not be exact: their actual stored
outputs are evaluated independently. This is an a posteriori executable
route. It is distinct from the a priori envelope allocation in
Corollary~\ref{cor:r37policy}, whose premises remain stated explicitly.
The reported Gaussian policies are the mathematical feedbacks represented
by their stored coefficients. A separate action-execution allowance is
required for online rounding, measurement, or actuator error.

\subsection{Work, storage, and the stopping target}
Let $J$ be the number of proposed updates, including rejected precision
attempts, and let $I$ count numerical inverse constructions. With dense
classical matrix arithmetic, a fixed-target fit uses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r38floatcost}
 O\{Id^3+J(pd^2+d^3)\}
\end{equation}
scalar work, including a constant number of matrix-ball products per
proposal and direct Gram checks. Caching gives $I\leq2$ per nonempty fit
for the declared native precision set; without caching, $I=J$.
Applications of an already formed inverse still cost $O(d^3)$ here and
are not treated as free. The proposal-only multiply--add proxy in the
records is $2pd^2+d^3$ per attempt; it excludes the verifier and inverse
construction and is never labeled total work.

If a policy service performs $C$ complete-policy checks, evaluates $E$
individual future dates for candidate construction, and solves $A$ actor
equations, its dense work additionally includes
$O\{(CT+E+A)d^3\}$. All failed checks and tuning trials enter these counts.
At fixed native precision, a live fit uses $O(pd+d^2)$ storage; retaining
all dated factors and gains uses $O\{T(pd+d^2)\}$, plus the audit trace and
runtime working memory. Factors are stored as binary64 after a proposal,
even when that proposal used binary32. Reduced proposal mantissas are
therefore not reported as a reduction in returned-factor storage.

Conditioning affects the cold radius, the residual factor $1/m$, and the
number of successful and rejected updates. In the exact noise-budget
regime, the update cap is \eqref{eq:r37cap} with the certified cold or warm
radius. The Gaussian moment margin, horizon, target-evaluation allowance,
and actor residual enter the policy tolerance through the earlier
recursive comparison, not through an unstated equality between Gram error
and economic loss. Consequently, \eqref{eq:r38floatcost} is a complete
arithmetic-operation account for the specified native kernel, not a
bit-complexity theorem for exact rational solves or a hardware-independent
wall-clock speedup.
