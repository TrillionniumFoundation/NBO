\section{Executing fitted neural action witnesses}\label{sec:study57}
The preceding study establishes a prospective economic return and a non-tensor verification procedure. Its candidate-attribution audit also identifies a necessary next comparison: the common action menu often determines the same policy regardless of the fitted generator. The algebraic solver in Theorem~\ref{thm:algebraic56} was tested there as a solver, not used to create the policies whose costs were reported. We now execute that solver in new prospective services and evaluate the resulting policies on new streams. The original investment laws, Bellman optimum and NBO research object are unchanged.

\subsection{A controlled action-search comparison}
The new protocol fixes the three tasks $(d,T)=(2,2),(4,4),(8,6)$ before production. It compares five modes. Common-only uses the nine robust actions without fitting a critic. ReLU-menu uses a genuinely fitted 32-feature continuation and adds its 17-menu proposal. ReLU-exact retains every one of those candidates and adds the exact fitted-critic lattice minimizer. Quadratic-exact supplies the analogous exact action search for its fitted quadratic continuation. ExtraTrees-menu supplies its own-future tree-based proposal. All modes receive the same exact terminal-action search, so an analytic terminal improvement is not assigned to neural representation.

The nonterminal verification rule is identical across generators: signed sensitivity of the installed zero-investment policy from Proposition~\ref{prop:tube56}. Its state boxes, innovation bins and primitive arithmetic do not depend on the fitted critic. Shared candidate actions therefore have identical upper endpoints. This design implements the premise of Corollary~\ref{cor:nested57}, rather than changing the candidate generator and its error certificate simultaneously.

Each mode is reconstructed independently for each task, target and one of three distinct training seeds. ReLU-menu and ReLU-exact use the same seed within an attempt, but neither loads the other's fit free of charge. Their fitted-parameter identities are checked. Different seeds produce different fitted neural objects. The three seeds are not a population of economic tasks, and their timing variation is not described as a controlled timing-only repetition. The preceding fixed-seed timing repetitions remain a separate experiment.

\subsection{Prospective economic targets}
The two targets require ten- and twenty-percent expected-cost reductions from the installed zero policy. Each target service starts from primitives. It has two declared construction attempts, with training rows $256,1024$, acquired leaves $32,64$, and innovation bins $4,8$. The same locally split non-tensor observation geometry and downward action spacing $1/4096$ apply to every mode.

At each attempt a complete candidate is certified against the installed policy and frozen before inference. Intermediate attempts are not deployed. In particular, refinement does not apply the smooth-reference derivative formula to an already changed discontinuous actor. Every attempt is safe relative to the installed zero policy; no ordering between the policies of different attempts is assumed.

The own-service stopping estimand is $J(\pi)-qJ(0)$, with $q=9/10$ or $4/5$, rather than a ratio of two estimated means. Its cumulative inference looks have $4096$, $16384$ and $65536$ paths. A nonpositive upper endpoint returns immediately. A positive lower endpoint advances the construction attempt; an unresolved interval advances the declared look. After the final unsuccessful attempt the service returns a budget-exhausted safe policy, not a claim that the target is impossible. Every attempted fit, exact search, verification, inference and durable output belongs to the service's surrounding process clock.

There are 90 separately executed target services. After each set of five modes has returned, a separate 65,536-path stream compares ReLU-exact with each other returned policy. This stream is independent of construction and stopping information under the declared independent-bin model. It evaluates the original continuous initial and innovation laws using forty-bit bin enclosures and analytic final integration. Negative path differences are retained. Full policy identities receive exact zero contrasts; other overlapping intervals are not treated as identities.

The new finite confidence family has error at most $1/100$. Its maximum possible interval count is
\[
 90\cdot2\cdot3\cdot3+18\cdot4=1692<2048,
\]
covering own cost, target contrast and gain at every possible look, together with the independent validation contrasts. The retained logarithm allowance is checked by exact rational arithmetic. The first-attainment argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:stop56} applies conditionally at every fresh stage. This finite-look account is not an undeclared confidence sequence valid at arbitrary future sample sizes.

The new execution supplies the previously missing connection between the trained representation's exact action witness and a deployed policy. It also measures the extent of that connection. A large number of positive optimization losses can coexist with few additional deployed actions because the common safe menu already determines most selected upper bounds. Even a strictly improved upper bound does not prove that the resulting policy costs less than its menu counterpart. The independent paired contrasts in Table~\ref{tab:paired-summary57}, and not the fitted objective or interval width, determine that economic comparison. Every individual interval is reported in the supplement.

The common-only control pays no fitting cost and is a substantive comparator. Quadratic-exact receives the same algebraic-search opportunity, while ExtraTrees retains its declared fitted-value construction. This prevents the exact solver's availability from being conflated with a special verification privilege. The experiment compares the complete returned procedures and identifies their components; it does not presume that fitting a neural continuation must beat a common-action or conventional-value alternative.

\subsection{Work, uniform accuracy and adoption}
The main table reports complete own-target process work at a matched economic target. It includes failed intermediate attempts and all inference actually needed to return. The supplementary work ledger separately records fitting, root isolation, candidate evaluation, signed verification, path evaluation, serialized bytes and resident memory. Different tasks run on different task runners; within each task processes are sequential, use one available core and one numerical-library thread, and rotate mode order. Available governor information is recorded, but clock frequency is not controlled.

The fresh paired validation is additional study work. Its recorded timer ends after endpoint evaluation and serialization, before the final comparison JSON is committed. It is therefore reported as a partial validation-service clock, not a complete process clock, and is not concealed inside the own-target time. The protocol's intended complete comparison-process timing was not implemented; this timing boundary does not change the prospective stopping rule or its fully recorded own-service work. The raw timing records are retained without retrospective adjustment.

Every returned policy also receives a one-sweep all-state loss account. At date $t$ it includes the old-reference continuation term $\beta_tE_{t+1}^{0}$ as well as the directed greedy allowance. The tables use a valid support bound for that reference error and cap the result by the original total-cost support. They do not substitute the local $U-L$ gap for loss against the Bellman optimum. A successful expected-cost target under the initial law is thus not mislabeled as exact Bellman convergence.

The gain from replacing installed zero investment remains an actual economic gain, subject to the stated confidence event and normalized primitives. Choosing between two returned methods requires the additional incremental-cost calculation \eqref{eq:incremental-adoption57}. It charges the measured work difference and any replacement fee, and uses the fresh paired policy-cost interval. The complete fee--computation-price regions follow from that interval without choosing a favorable fee after seeing the outcome. The experiment is a theoretical investment exercise, not an externally calibrated welfare estimate.
