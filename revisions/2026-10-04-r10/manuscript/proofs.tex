\section{Proof of the Continuous-Capital Guarantee}\label{app:r10proof}
All norms in this proof use the normalization $d^{-1}\sum_i z_i^2$. This normalization is important: the spectral norm of $B$ remains the ordinary Euclidean operator norm, while the stochastic moment of cross-sectional dispersion is of order one rather than order $d$.

\subsection{Logarithmic tangent remainder}
For $a>0$ and $x>-a$, twice integrating the second derivative gives
\[
 \frac{x}{a}-\log(1+x/a)
 =x^2\int_0^1\frac{1-r}{(a+rx)^2}\,dr.
\]
For $x\geq0$ the integral decreases as $x$ increases. For $x<0$ it is at least $1/(2a^2)$, its value at zero. Consequently, for every $m\in[\underline m,M]$ and interior $a<M$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r10loglemma}
 \log(m/a)-\frac{m-a}{a}
 \leq-\frac{\mu(a)}2(m-a)^2,
 \quad
 \mu(a)=\frac{2\{\log(a/M)+(M-a)/a\}}{(M-a)^2}.
\end{equation}
On the smaller interval $|m-a|\leq\epsilon<a$, the same integral also yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r10logupper}
 0\leq\frac{m-a}{a}-\log(m/a)
 \leq\frac{(m-a)^2}{2(a-\epsilon)^2}.
\end{equation}
These are global inequalities on the specified intervals, including their endpoints; no empirical concavity check is used.

\subsection{Synchronous comparison and baseline moments}
Construct $Y^m$ and $Y^0$ on the same probability space with identical initial state and Brownian drivers, where the second process uses $m_0$. With $\Delta Y=Y^m-Y^0$ and $\Delta m=m-m_0\bm1$, additive noise cancels. Since componentwise hyperbolic tangent is one-Lipschitz,
\[
 \|\Delta Y_t\|_d\leq\int_0^t
       \{\beta\|\Delta Y_s\|_d+\|\Delta m_s\|_d\}\,ds.
\]
Gronwall's inequality followed by Minkowski's integral inequality implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r10stability}
 \bigl(\E\|\Delta Y_t\|_d^2\bigr)^{1/2}
 \leq\int_0^t e^{\beta(t-s)}r(s)\,ds,
 \qquad r(s)=\bigl(\E\|\Delta m_s\|_d^2\bigr)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
All terms are integrable because controls and the nonlinear production term are bounded. For the baseline, common consumption, common drift constants, and common noise vanish under $\Pi$. Furthermore, $\|\Pi\tanh(BY)\|_d\leq1$ and
$\E\|\Pi W_T\|_d^2=(1-1/d)T$. Minkowski's inequality therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r10moment}
 \bigl(\E\|\Pi Y_T^0\|_d^2\bigr)^{1/2}
 \leq\|\Pi y\|_d+\kappa T+\sigma_I\sqrt{(1-1/d)T}
 \leq Q_d.
\end{equation}
This estimate is uniform in the initial mean. It does not suppose that the optimal policy is symmetric or that the dense economy separates.

\subsection{An exact payoff-difference identity}
Put $h(Y)=\overline{\tanh(BY)}$. Integrating the mean-state equation inside the discounted flow and terminal mean, and interchanging integrable expectations and time integrals, yields
\begin{align}
 J_y(m)-J_y(m_0)
 ={}&\int_0^T e^{-\rho t}\E\left[
       \overline{\log(m_t/m_0(t))}-w(t)\overline{\Delta m_t}
       -\frac\zeta2\{\bar m_t^2-m_0(t)^2\}\right]dt\notag\\
 &+\kappa\int_0^T e^{-\rho t}w(t)
        \E[h(Y_t^m)-h(Y_t^0)]\,dt\notag\\
 &-\chi e^{-\rho T}\E\left[
       2\langle\Pi Y_T^0,\Pi\Delta Y_T\rangle_d
           +\|\Pi\Delta Y_T\|_d^2\right].\label{eq:r10identity}
\end{align}
The martingale mean terms have zero expectation. The schedule satisfies
$m_0(t)^{-1}-w(t)-\zeta m_0(t)=0$. Thus all terms linear in $\overline{\Delta m_t}$ cancel. Equation~\eqref{eq:r10loglemma} bounds the first integral above by
$-\frac12\int e^{-\rho t}\mu(t)r(t)^2dt$; the remaining mean-adjustment square is nonpositive and may be dropped in an upper bound.

Next, $|h(Y^m)-h(Y^0)|\leq\|B\|_2\|\Delta Y\|_d$. Equations~\eqref{eq:r10stability} and \eqref{eq:r10moment}, Cauchy--Schwarz, and a change in integration order bound the potentially positive production and terminal-dispersion terms by $\int_0^T K(s)r(s)ds$. Dropping the nonpositive terminal square, we obtain
\[
 J_y(m)-J_y(m_0)
 \leq\int_0^T\left[K(s)r(s)
             -\tfrac12e^{-\rho s}\mu(s)r(s)^2\right]ds
 \leq\frac12\int_0^T e^{\rho s}\frac{K(s)^2}{\mu(s)}ds.
\]
Taking the supremum over the original adapted controls proves \eqref{eq:r10anchorbound}. Attainment of the supremum is not needed.

For a tube policy, $r(s)\leq\epsilon$. Reverse the payoff difference in \eqref{eq:r10identity}. Equation~\eqref{eq:r10logupper} and
$\E(\overline{\Delta m})^2\leq\E\|\Delta m\|_d^2$
bound the consumption and adjustment loss by
\[
 \frac{\epsilon^2}{2}\int_0^T e^{-\rho s}
       \{[m_0(s)-\epsilon]^{-2}+\zeta\}\,ds.
\]
The production and terminal cross terms contribute at most $\epsilon\int K(s)ds$. The terminal square now has positive sign and is bounded by
$\chi e^{-\rho T}\epsilon^2 A_\beta(T)^2$ using \eqref{eq:r10stability}. This proves $J_y(m_0)-J_y(m^\phi)\leq D_{d,\epsilon}$. Adding \eqref{eq:r10anchorbound} proves \eqref{eq:r10neuralbound}. Feasibility supplies the nonnegative lower bound.\qed

\subsection{Interval spectral and deployment checks}
For the stored exact-real matrix $B$, outward products and sums enclose each entry of $B^\top B$. The Schur complement recurrence for an $LDL^\top$ factorization is enclosed entry by entry. If every pivot interval has strictly positive lower endpoint, every exact pivot is positive, so $\lambda I-B^\top B$ is positive definite. Hence $\|B\|_2<\sqrt\lambda$. The proposed numerical singular value is not part of this proof; an unsuccessful interval factorization supplies no spectral bound.

If $\ell\leq m_0(t)\leq u$, then
$\operatorname{up}(u-\epsilon)\geq m_0(t)-\epsilon$ and
$\operatorname{down}(\ell+\epsilon)\leq m_0(t)+\epsilon$.
Every finite output after projection onto \eqref{eq:r10inward} therefore meets the theorem's tube condition under the declared arithmetic contract, regardless of errors in the neural proposal. The proof does not verify the interpreter or processor. The arithmetic assumptions, overflow checks, saved weights, and input-clock convention remain explicit components of the computational claim.
