\section{Training, Deployment, and Certified Capital Accuracy}\label{sec:r10study}
We execute the preceding construction on the unchanged capital economy in dimensions ten, twenty, and fifty. Each dimension has three fixed initialization seeds, 11, 29, and 47. The actor and critic have two hyperbolic-tangent hidden layers of width 32. The actor radius is $\epsilon=.1$. The critic incorporates the terminal payoff exactly and includes a generic neural correction to the discounted mean and dispersion terms. This is an approximation architecture, not an exact invariant solution class.

\subsection{A fixed protocol with strong comparators}
Each fit uses 600 critic updates, batch size 128, and the same state draws and initialization seeds across methods. The critic learning rate is .0015; the actor learning rate is .002. Two independent Gaussian trace banks, with two probes each, are actually used in the product residual objective. Negative sample losses are retained. Training states have $t\in[0,1]$ and each log capital in $[-1.5,.5]$. Exact-Hessian diagnostics at 128 held-out points do not enter the continuous certificate.

The direct-tube comparator removes the actor and globally maximizes the same critic Hamiltonian on the identical time-dependent tube. The direct-full comparator instead maximizes on the full primitive box $[.02,2]^d$. Both exploit the scalar monotone mean-consumption equation and use 44 bisection steps. They receive the same critic architecture, sample stream, trace banks, and update count. Their additional maximization work is counted. This is a matched-update design, not a claim that unequal wall times are equal compute budgets. The untrained feasible schedule $m_0$ is a fourth comparator.

There are 27 fitted policies, including all seeds and both direct alternatives. Every saved actor is deployed without local action search. The full deployment cost includes the inward scalar schedule guard; direct deployment also includes critic differentiation and bisection. Separate process runs record peak resident memory, training time, query counts, and optimizer histories. The raw proposals, guarded deployment routines, and the scalar benchmark are not treated as interchangeable policies.

The complete-action comparison class remains the original primitive box, not the actor tube.

The certificate is recomputed at 4,096 and 16,384 quadrature panels. The finer enclosure, verified matrix majorants, and all initial-dispersion and radius variants are saved. This refinement concerns the numerical integration of a proved continuous-economy bound, not a grid approximation to the state process. The spectral verification and scalar integration costs are charged explicitly. The certificate is common to the actor and direct-tube policy class; it is not a seed-specific estimate of achieved regret.

For an interpretable utility scale, consider an externally financed proportional supplement to every consumption flow, holding the policy-induced state law, adjustment cost, and terminal payoff fixed. A supplement $e$ raises discounted payoff by
\[
 \log(1+e)\frac{1-e^{-\rho T}}{\rho}.
\]
Thus an outward upper value of
$\exp\{\rho(G_d+D_{d,\epsilon})/(1-e^{-\rho T})\}-1$
is a sufficient flow-consumption compensation. This is a defined externally financed welfare comparison, not a claim that the same transfer is self-financing or that all economic models share a common utility percentage.

This experiment supplies a trained multilayer NBO policy, a strong direct comparator, a deployed-policy specification, and an independent continuous-economy guarantee in one coupled nonlinear model. It does not establish that the actor is universally preferable to a structured feasible schedule, that constant-step optimization converges, or that the same certificate applies to endogenous preference formation. The latter economy retains the separate nodal and continuous-transfer accounts developed above. Likewise, the dynamic-game deviation bounds retain their stated finite-game scope.
