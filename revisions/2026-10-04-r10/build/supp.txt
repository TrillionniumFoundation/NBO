8      This supplement expands the models and algorithms of the revised main                   8

9    manuscript. It does not introduce an alternative total loss or a different equilibrium    9

10   definition. Policy evaluation freezes all actor outputs. Policy improvement freezes       10

11   the critic’s value and derivative channels. For games, only the acting player’s objec- 11
12   tive is differentiated. For temporal selves, both exponential components evaluate         12

13   the same future policy. The earlier main and supplementary sources are preserved          13

14   in the revision archive for comparison; they are not alternative implementations of       14

15   the revised method.                                                                       15

17   structure classes. R6 trains generic multilayer policies in stopped consumption,          17

20   common-noise coverage records, and an independent arithmetic replay. R8 in- 20

                              3.1. Smooth approximation and what it establishes
26                                                                                                        26

27       A derivative-approximation result concerns a smooth target on a compact domain                   27

28   and a family of networks whose capacity can increase. It does not imply that a fixed                 28

29   network attains zero residual, that optimization finds its best approximation, or                    29

30   that a nonsmooth target has classical derivatives everywhere. In a smooth problem,                   30

31   increasing the representation class can reduce approximation error in the value and                  31

32   derivatives. In a nonsmooth problem, NBO instead evaluates a consistent positive- 32
                                              NBO SUPPLEMENT                                  3

1    weight Bellman equation, or solves an explicitly regularized equation before taking          1

2    a justified limit.                                                                           2

3      The hard-terminal architecture is v(t, x ) = G ( x ) + ( T − t) N (t, x ). It exactly en- 3
4    forces v( T, x ) = G ( x ) independently of training. A lateral Dirichlet face needs an      4

5    additional compatible lifting and vanishing factor. For a reflected state, the Neu- 5
6    mann condition is a different constraint. A loss on only interior points leaves all of       6

7    these boundary questions unanswered. The regression test evaluates the terminal              7

8    architecture at randomly chosen states and checks exact equality, not merely a small         8

9    mean terminal error.                                                                         9

10     The recursive Epstein–Zin domain is bV > 0. An exponential output ensures                  10

11   positivity, while an interval transformation can keep bV between declared positive           11

12   barriers. If bG > 0, one terminal-compatible positive representation is                      12

13                                                                                                13
                          bv(t, x ) = exp [log(bG ( x )) + ( T − t) N (t, x )] .
14                                                                                                14

15   Its terminal value is exact. It does not by itself impose a lateral exit payoff or a         15

16   two-sided barrier. These additional conditions must be built into the architecture or        16

17   measured explicitly. Clipping an invalid utility argument inside a fractional power          17

18   changes the equation and is not an acceptable hidden numerical repair.                       18

30     A fixed rival profile turns each player’s problem into an ordinary controlled              30

32   derivative graph must implement this reduction. During player i’s actor step, every          32

1    rival policy and every derivative of player i’s critic is detached. During player i’s      1

2    critic step, all players’ actions are detached. A sum of these reporting losses can be     2

3    logged, but differentiation of a joint-profit objective is not equivalent to those block   3

4    steps.                                                                                     4

5        The two-firm static diagnostic has best response qi = (1 − q j )/2 when interior. At   5

6    the joint-profit allocation (1/4, 1/4), the deviating firm chooses 3/8 and improves        6

7    profit from 1/8 to 9/64, a gain of 1/64. The own-action derivative is 1/4 at the           7

19                                                                                             19

20     At a given state and time, the initial action box covers the entire feasible set. Bisec- 20
21   tion replaces one closed box by two closed boxes with the same union. Their shared        21

22   face is harmless. Induction on the number of subdivisions preserves coverage. A           22

23   pruned box is retained logically through the inequality u B ≤ ci + τ, where u B is        23

24   an outward upper enclosure on that whole box, and ci is a lower enclosure at a            24

25   feasible incumbent. If the incumbent later increases, the previous pruning inequality     25

26   remains true. Consequently every feasible action is either in an unresolved box or        26

                              21.3. Deployment and arithmetic contract
6                                                                                                        6

7      The interval schedule guard uses the inherited binary64 arithmetic implementa- 7
8    tion with outward elementary operations and polynomial transcendental bounds. 8
9    The additional square-root routine verifies squared endpoints and handles a zero                    9

10   lower endpoint explicitly. An SVD proposes the spectral majorant, but the interval                  10

11   factorization must verify it. A bad matrix shape, nonfinite matrix, nonpositive pivot,              11

12   mismatched matrix hash, empty inward tube, or out-of-horizon clock produces                         12

13   an error. There is no fall-through from a failed arithmetic check to an empirical                   13

14   maximum.                                                                                            14

15     The actor’s neural proposal need not be evaluated by validated transcendental                     15

16   routines to maintain its action constraint: its finite output is projected onto the                 16

17   inward interval. The theoretical result concerns the resulting continuous-time feed- 17
18   back rule. Euler paths approximate its payoff, and their bias remains uncomputed. A                 18

19   sampled Euler path is not claimed to be an exact draw of the continuous controlled                  19

20   diffusion. A finite proposal failure is recorded separately from the mathematical                   20

21   fact that the specified fallback remains in the tube.                                               21
