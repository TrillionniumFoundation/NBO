         1.2. M1 and B1: The original neural computation and the role of safe improvement
16                                                                                             16

17       We retain the original NBO title and research object rather than replace the          17

18   manuscript with a differently titled safe-policy-improvement paper. The reason is         18

19   substantive: the article now distinguishes the constructive operator, a trained-neural    19

20   action computation, its lossless implementation, and the representation-neutral           20

21   verification rule that makes the returned action economically admissible.                 21

22       The constructive backend still produces an own-future continuation and feasible       22

23   witnesses from primitive moduli, with an explicit error and resource account against      23

24   the original Bellman optimum. The trained backend is not merely a ReLU encoding           24

25   of that min-plus construction. Its stored affine–ReLU continuation has analytically       25

26   integrated innovation expectations and an exact action-lattice minimizer obtained         26

27   by rational hinge partition and cubic-root isolation. The preceding R57 execution         27

28   actually deployed that minimizer and retained common-only, menu-neural, exact- 28
29   quadratic and tree-based controls.                                                        29

30       The new theorem reduces the work of computing precisely that trained-neural           30

31   witness. Features whose entire action range lies in one integrated activation region      31

32   are reduced exactly to polynomial coefficients or zero. Remaining lattice objec- 32
                                   RESPONSE TO THE REFEREE                                 3

1    tives are enclosed outward; only points proved unable to minimize are excluded. 1
2    Surviving values are compared rationally with the original tie rule. The proof            2

3    permits negative fitted weights and does not assume a convex fit. It also estab- 3
4    lishes a survivor-gap bound, a conditional output-cardinality bound, exact fallback       4

5    behavior and explicit arithmetic/storage costs.                                           5

6      The interval-screening principle itself is representation neutral. The NBO-specific     6

7    implementation is the exact reduction and evaluation of integrated trained ridges         7

10   generators. The manuscript does not infer a universal neural advantage from either        10

11   principle.                                                                                11

         1.3. M2 and B4: A prospective stopping algorithm, not a retrospective catalogue
15                                                                                             15

16     The earlier R54 catalogue remains historical evidence and is not relabeled as           16

17   a prospective service. The subsequent R55/R57 protocols introduced actual first- 17
18   attainment controllers, and the R58 matched experiment follows a separately frozen        18

19   prospective rule. Every solver/task/target/repetition service begins from primi- 19
20   tives and pays for its own fit, verification, inference and outputs.                      20

21     The targets require ten- or twenty-percent expected-cost reductions from installed      21

22   zero investment. The estimand is the signed contrast J(candidate)-q\J(installed),         22

23   with q=9/10 or 4/5, rather than a ratio of two estimated means. At each construction      23

24   attempt, the complete candidate is frozen before inference. A nonpositive upper           24

25   endpoint returns immediately. A positive lower endpoint advances the construction         25

26   attempt; an unresolved interval advances the next declared inference look. Final          26

27   failure returns a safe policy with budget-exhausted status, not a claimed successful      27

28   or impossible target.                                                                     28

29     The R58 design has two declared construction attempts, three cumulative infer- 29
30   ence looks and 36 separate complete processes. Its finite family covers all possible      30

31   cost, target-contrast and gain intervals. The audit reconstructs every first-attainment   31

32   decision and verifies that no look or attempt was performed after the corresponding       32
     4

1    stopping condition. Identical policies and streams yield identical stopping histories     1

2    in the two solver modes.                                                                  2

3        The new transcript-invariance theorem states its premise explicitly: controller       3

4    decisions depend on mathematical outputs, not elapsed time or solver diagnostics. 4
5    A wall-clock deadline is a different contract and need not produce the same stop- 5
6    ping history. Intermediate construction attempts are not deployed; all are verified       6

7    against the same smooth installed reference, not against a silently differentiated        7

8    discontinuous updated actor.                                                              8

            1.4. M3 and B2: Matched economic accuracy and the full computational bill
12                                                                                             12

13       The new comparison matches more than a nominal target. Corresponding modes            13

14   have identical fitted parameters, acquired partitions, action witnesses, selected         14

15   policies, whole-cell error accounts, interval-path endpoints and statistical decisions. 15
16   Their policy-cost difference is therefore exactly zero under every admissible initial     16

17   law. This is stronger than an unresolved paired confidence interval, and it does not      17

18   require a new paired sampling service.                                                    18

19       The complete recorded service includes process invocation, imports, scientific- 19
20   source checks, independently repeated fitting, every attempted action search, ver- 20
21   ification, own inference, serialization, process exit and log flush. The parent con- 21
22   troller’s later checkpoint write is separately identified and is not described as         22

23   zero overhead. The supplement distinguishes component medians from complete- 23
24   process medians and reports all output bytes and resident-memory peaks.                   24

25       Thirty of the 36 services attain the declared target. Six exhaust the budget at the   25

26   eight-state, six-date twenty-percent target. All are retained. The complete-process       26

27   algebraic-to-screened median ratios lie between approximately 1.028 and 1.101             27

28   across the six task–target groups. The unsuccessful group is explicitly a comparison      28

29   of complete exhaustion work, not successful first-attainment work. All three timing       29

30   repetitions are printed rather than selecting the best run.                               30

31       The R57 independent neural-versus-conventional cost comparisons remain the            31

32   evidence for those distinct policies. Equality between the two lossless implementa- 32

12                                                                                             12

13                                                                                             13

14                                                                                             14

15                                                                                             15
     runtime comparison.

        1.6. M5 and B5: Non-tensor multidimensional execution and explicit scaling costs
29                                                                                             29

30     The retained and new prospective services use the original nonlinear cyclic invest- 30
31   ment family at dimension/horizon pairs (2,2), (4,4) and (8,6). Their observations use     31

32   locally split binary partitions with 32 or 64 leaves, not unequal-spaced tensor grids     32
     6

1    presented as non-tensor. Robust capacities and downward action indices are valid          1

2    on entire leaves. The signed smooth-reference verifier and its dimension/horizon          2

3    work account remain common to all candidate generators.                                   3

4        R59 adds a distinct work account for trained-neural action recovery. For width m,     4

5    dimension d, N action-lattice points, m_c crossing ridges and s survivors, coeffi- 5
6    cient formation/reduction requires O(dm) arithmetic operations; interval screening        6

7    requires O(N(1+m_c)) interval operations; exact original-objective comparison re- 7
8    quires O(sm) rational operations. The vectorized implementation uses O(N(1+m_c))          8

9    interval working storage. Rational bit costs are explicitly separate.                     9

10       These bounds do not erase dimension, horizon, acquisition or lattice dependence. 10
11   Nor is the new screen claimed to be universally faster: valid wide intervals may re- 11
12   tain the whole lattice, and a fallback pays for the original exact solver. The recorded   12

13   multidimensional executions and operation counts are genuine; different task run- 13
14   ners do not establish a hardware-controlled asymptotic scaling law. The original          14

15   stronger Bellman-accuracy target remains in the theoretical and returned-policy           15

16   error account.                                                                            16

                 1.7. M6 and B6: Strong controls and an attributable intervention
20                                                                                             20

21       The manuscript retains the R57 five-mode experiment: common-only, ReLU- 21
22   menu, ReLU-exact, quadratic-exact and ExtraTrees-menu. Common-only legiti- 22
23   mately incurs no fictional fitting cost. Every mode receives the same exact terminal      23

24   analysis and critic-independent signed verifier. Matched ReLU-menu/exact param- 24
25   eters isolate the added witness, while the raw nested-candidate record attributes         25

26   strict endpoint and selected-action changes to that addition.                             26

27       The R58 experiment answers the next question within that already executed             27

28   neural generator: can the same exact witness be returned with less work? It changes       28

29   only the nonterminal solver. Each mode independently reconstructs the fit, and the        29

30   audit checks the resulting parameters rather than assuming identity from seeds. 30
31   The same actions enter the same verifier in the same order. This prevents a change        31

32   of verifier, fit, target or policy from being credited to the screening algorithm.        32

                1.10. M9 and B9: An economic consequence at an unchanged answer
17                                                                                           17

18       The executed targets concern actual expected cost under the original continuous     18

19   initial and innovation laws. The positive gain intervals relative to the installed      19

20   controller remain the basis for adoption decisions, including the eight-dimensional     20

21   case whose stronger twenty-percent target is not certified at the available budget.     21

22       The new implementation comparison has an especially direct economic interpre- 22
23   tation. When both solvers return exactly the same policy, its economic cost cancels     23

24   from their total-cost comparison. For specified computation charges and a non- 24
25   negative computation price, the total-cost difference is exactly the price times the    25

26   charge difference. Relative to installation, the same gain interval is shifted by the   26

27   implementation charge and installation fee. A lower specified computational charge      27

28   expands the supported adoption region without inventing a difference in policy          28

29   welfare.                                                                                29

30       The main article states and proves this result and reports the complete equal- 30
31   policy work measurements that make it operational in the finite study. Recorded         31

32   medians do not provide a distribution-free expected-runtime estimate, and no exter- 32
                                   RESPONSE TO THE REFEREE                                9

1    nal computation-price or installation-fee calibration is claimed. The contribution is a   1

2    constructive and executable method for obtaining a verified economic answer more          2

3    economically on the recorded matched workloads, together with its exact policy            3

4    and stopping invariance; it is not an asserted universal inability of conventional        4

5    methods to obtain that answer.                                                            5
