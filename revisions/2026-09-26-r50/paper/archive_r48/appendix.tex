\subsection{Operating-witness sensitivity of a price field}
Suppose $u$ is reconstructed with exact disadvantage $d$, and $\underline u$ with \eqref{eq:dlo48}, using the same nonnegative field. The inequalities
\[
 0\le d_{tia}-\underline d_{tia}\le w_t+\beta w_{t+1}
\]
follow directly from the operating enclosure (including the clipping at zero). Backward minimum stability yields
\[
 0\le u_{ti}-\underline u_{ti}\le\Gamma_t,
 \quad \Gamma_t=\sum_{s=t}^{T-1}\beta^{s-t}\norm{\lambda_s}_\infty(w_s+\beta w_{s+1}).
\]
The positive-part map is 1-Lipschitz, so the same $\Gamma_0$ bounds the change in the displayed clipped lower endpoint. Any directed approximation of the price expectation adds its certified one-step error to this recursion. In the executed model the expectation is an exact rational finite sum. This is a perturbation result for a \emph{fixed} field; the algorithm's cap-based rule is imposed before generating the field, so it does not presuppose stability of an exact-model price optimizer.

\subsection{Clipping can change the optimizing price}\label{app:clipping48}
The mask maximization is not merely a different display of the full-mask optimum. Modify the two-period example in Theorem~\ref{thm:separation} as follows. Keep $\beta=\varepsilon=1/2$ and deterministic transitions to state 1. At date 0, rewards of $G,B$ are $0,-1$, while costs are $(0,0)$ in state 1 and $(3,0)$ in state 2. At date 1, rewards of $G,B$ are $0,-1/4$ and costs are $(1,0)$ in both states. Let $\nu=(3/4,1/4)$ and $g=0$. The operating value is zero.

Let $l$ be the price at the reached terminal decision state. Its potential is
\[
 u_1(l)=\min\{1-l/2,-l/4\}.
\]
For this fixed $l$, the best root potentials use prices 0 and 3 and equal $u_{01}=u_1(l)/2$ and $u_{02}=3/2+\min(3,l)/4+u_1(l)/2$. The first claim follows from the zero-cost good action; the second follows by checking the positive slope before root price 3 and the negative slope thereafter. Thus the optimized full-mask objective is
\[
 3/8+\min(3,l)/16+u_1(l)/2,
\]
whose maximum $3/8$ occurs at $l=0$. Its clipped value there is also $3/8$. But $l=3$ gives potentials $(-3/8,15/8)$ and clipped value $15/32>3/8$. The latter is the actual common-policy optimum: take the terminal bad action with probability 1, use the root bad action with probability $3/8$ in state 2, and use the good action in state 1. Its operating shortfalls obey all restart bounds, its objective is $15/32$, and the displayed certificate matches it. Hence optimizing the un-clipped objective can select a strictly inferior clipped certificate. Enumerating the relevant initial masks fixes this mismatch without altering the validity of any field.

\subsection{A quantitative form of repaired Borel upper density}
The inherited upper-density result can be stated with a finite error modulus, although not with a universal computable partition-size rate. Fix a globally feasible policy $p$ and define finite dominating measures
\[
 \mu_0=\nu,\qquad \mu_{t+1}=\sum_{a=1}^m\mu_tP_t^a.
\]
Their total masses are $m^t$. For any Borel proposal $q$, let $e_t(x)=\TV(q_t(\cdot\mid x),p_t(\cdot\mid x))$ and $E_t=\int e_t\,d\mu_t$. Put $M=2(R\Ssum T+G)$, $s=(1-\beta)\varepsilon$, $c=1+M/s$, and
\begin{align*}
 A_t&=cE_t+\frac{Mc\beta}{s}\sum_{v=t+1}^{T-1}(\beta c)^{v-t-1}E_v,\\
 \mathcal B(E)&=K\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\beta^t\Ssum{T-t}A_t.
\end{align*}
Then exact continuation-best repair of $q$ gives a globally feasible $\widehat p$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L1modulus48}
 |C_0^{\widehat p}(\nu)-C_0^p(\nu)|\le\mathcal B(E).
\end{equation}
To verify this, write $a_t=\TV(\widehat p_t,p_t)$ and $\Delta_t=|J_t^{\widehat p}-J_t^p|$. The threshold deficit before repair is at most $Me_t+\beta P_t^p\Delta_{t+1}$ because $p$ is feasible. The best repaired continuation has margin at least $s$, so the repair mass is at most this deficit divided by $s$. Therefore
\[
 a_t\le ce_t+(\beta/s)P_t^p\Delta_{t+1},\qquad
 \Delta_t\le Mc e_t+\beta c P_t^p\Delta_{t+1}.
\]
Integrating and using $\mu_tP_t^p\le\mu_{t+1}$ proves $\int a_t\,d\mu_t\le A_t$. The implementation performance-difference expansion and the range bound $K\Ssum{T-t}$ then give \eqref{eq:L1modulus48}.

Rational simple proposals can approximate $p$ in these finitely many $L_1(\mu_t)$ norms; the displayed expression gives value convergence after repair. But the dominating masses and factors $c$ can grow rapidly, arbitrary Borel integrals need not be effectively evaluable, and a repaired simple proposal need not itself remain simple. Thus \eqref{eq:L1modulus48} is not a two-sided computable scheme for a diffuse initial law. The finite-observation theorem in the main text obtains such a scheme by exact closure, not by suppressing these difficulties.

\subsection{Exact finite products and common budgets}
For $p\in[l,u]$, $v\in[a,b]$, each product $z=pv$ obeys the sign-safe inequalities
\begin{align*}
 z&\ge ap+lv-la,& z&\ge bp+uv-ub,\\
 z&\le ap+uv-ua,& z&\le bp+lv-lb.
\end{align*}
The displayed four McCormick inequalities, not any sign shortcut, are reconstructed by the reader. Signed $B=\underline V-J$ variables are allowed. For a probability simplex, $\sum_a p_a v=v$ supplies an exact product conservation identity. These constraints preserve one continuation variable per successor. The occupation completion \eqref{eq:shared48} is an alternative exact formulation, while the implemented probability-box search enforces the same compatibility through the original Bellman equations. The occupancy LP itself is not falsely described as complete for $\KR$.
