\title{Certified Bellman Operators for Costly Policy Revision}
\runtitle{Shared Continuation, Witnesses, and Certification}
\begin{aug}\author[id=au1,addressref={add1}]{\fnms{Qian}~\snm{QI}\ead[label=e1]{qiqian@pku.edu.cn}}\address[id=add1]{\orgname{Peking University}}\end{aug}
\begin{abstract}
We study minimum-cost revision of an installed policy with operating protection after every state--date restart and one common randomized Markov continuation. We characterize the supremum of restart-price certificates by an occupation-flow linear program and identify the shared successor-budget equations that restore the original constrained problem. A strict separation example distinguishes the price relaxation from both a constant-multiplier bound and the constrained optimum. An end-to-end finite algorithm constructs operating enclosures, feasible policies, lower certificates, and covering refinements without access to exact optimal operating values or inherited incumbents. Its accuracy rule adapts arithmetic precision. For controlled finite-atomic transitions and a finitely supported initial law, an exact restriction--extension theorem transfers paired finite certificates to the full Borel-state problem. A source-frozen evaluation produces four target hits in twelve new primary environments for each of two independently checked formulations; the price formulation tightens all eight unclosed intervals. Two positive-cost observed-fleet instances of the original affine dynamics have paired continuum certificates. These finite-initial-law results do not close the thirty earlier positive-cost intervals under the uniform initial law. All runs, costs, and remaining intervals are reported.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro48}
An organization may promise operating protection whenever a decision rule is restarted, while evaluating the expense of implementing that rule under its actual initial population. These are different restrictions on a single policy. The first is a statewise performance contract; the second is an ex ante cost objective. Actions alter future states and therefore alter future implementation expense. A collection of inexpensive plans, each optimized for a different restart, need not define one policy at their common successor states.

We retain this distinction throughout. The decision variable is a Borel randomized Markov policy with one action distribution at each state and date. Its randomization is fresh conditional on that state and date; a hidden initial lottery over persistent plans is not substituted for the common continuation. The target is the least implementation cost subject to every restart's operating-value shortfall being at most a prescribed allowance. Implementation cost can charge departures from an installed rule and can include an administratively specified realized-action charge. A fixed organizational cost of administering lotteries is a separate contract term.

The central result is a theorem--algorithm connection. First, we identify exactly what the restart-price family optimizes. Its supremum is the dual of an occupation-flow relaxation in which an edge may allocate the successor regret budget differently from other edges arriving at the same state. The correction in the price Bellman recursion is therefore not a free-standing strong-duality theorem for the original constrained problem. Adding common successor-budget equations makes the occupation formulation exact. A rational two-period example has a constant-multiplier value of $5/4$, a restart-price supremum of $27/16$, and an original common-policy optimum of $7/4$. The equalities locate both the gain from restart prices and the coupling which the global algorithm must restore.

Second, we construct both sides of the interval from primitive inputs. Directed Bellman enclosures are computed before any price or policy is proposed. A backward conditional-cost minimization supplies an initial feasible policy; a local numerical proposal is repaired against its actual continuation and the upper operating enclosure. Price proposals use only the lower and upper operating enclosures. Two different bilinear formulations use one common set of state--date probabilities. An independent rational reader reconstructs the relevant inequalities and the entire covering tree. The stopping theorem accounts for the enclosure width, policy-box diameter, and deployment quantization. Exact operating ties create no inverse-gap singularity in this argument. The sufficient tree bound is exponential, and the experiment reports rather than conceals its consequences.

Third, a restriction--extension theorem gives a matched controlled-continuum result for a finitely observed initial population. With finitely many transition atoms per action, the set of states reached from that population under \emph{all} action and shock sequences is finite at a finite horizon. Restriction to that set preserves the operating problem. More importantly, every feasible common policy on the set extends to a feasible Borel policy on the entire state space by choosing an operating-best action against the already extended continuation off the set. This proves equality of the finite and continuum revision optima, not merely validity of one endpoint. The dynamics may move the continuous state in an action-dependent and noninvertible manner. No autonomous type label or transition smoothing is required.

This result has an exact boundary. A finite observed initial law is the objective law, not an empirical approximation to an unobserved uniform population. Atomic laws need not approximate a continuous law in the topology required for this all-restart constrained optimization. Accordingly, the two observed-fleet calculations below do not replace the original uniform-initial-law cohort. The thirty positive-cost intervals in that cohort remain open. The broad Borel upper-density theorem and the structural aggregation, reset, and fiber results retain their separate hypotheses and are preserved in the technical volumes.

The new evaluation is generated only after the final five numerical source files and protocol are frozen on the remote repository. Twelve primary environments vary state count, horizon, action count, discounting, allowance, initial occupancy, and tie geometry. Each method constructs its own enclosures and incumbent. Neither receives a saved policy, an exact operating value, or an earlier price. At the absolute target $10^{-3}$, both formulations meet four of twelve primary targets, including one zero-cost case; two positive-cost closures require branching. On all eight unclosed cases the price formulation returns a smaller certified interval, but it produces no additional primary target hit in this sample. The separate observed-fleet problems have horizons two and three and positive costs. Both are certified under the registered target, with the price formulation's widths below $10^{-8}$. These results show a complete working pipeline and an exact continuum transfer in the stated class; they do not establish uniformly practical global optimization.

\subsection{Relation to constrained-control duality and approximation}
Occupation measures express conventional expected constrained Markov decision problems \citep{Altman1999}. Finite linear-programming approximations can converge, with quantitative rates under suitable assumptions \citep{DufourPrietoRumeau2013,Saldi2018}. Our all-restart constraint is different from an initial-distribution average of regrets. Theorem~\ref{thm:flow} derives the precise occupation program corresponding to the proposed prices, rather than inferring an equivalence from the common use of multipliers. Its common-budget completion is a finite nonconvex formulation of the original policy object. Theorem~\ref{thm:separation} shows that dropping this completion changes the value even with action-independent transitions.

Information-relaxation duality relaxes information restrictions and uses penalties to recover bounds \citep{BrownSmithSun2010,BrownSmith2014}. The relaxation derived here instead separates incoming allocations of a shared successor regret. It does not relax nonanticipativity, and the policy remains Markov throughout. Constant prices reproduce an initial-regret Lagrangian; state--date prices can be strictly stronger, but their finite supremum is still the identified relaxation. This is a formal relation to established dual objects, not a claim to a new general principle of duality.

The lower-node constructions use classical McCormick inequalities \citep{McCormick1976}; the direct-value baseline uses aggregate continuation products. The enhanced construction uses signed witness-relative regret and disaggregated products with simplex identities. Both are read by the same independent arithmetic checker and searched by the same driver. This isolates a formulation comparison, not an externally authored solver competition. Verified residual correction follows the separation between numerical proposals and mathematical certificates emphasized by \citet{Rump2010}. The previously favorable native SCIP comparisons remain in the supplement with their uncertified lower bounds distinctly identified.

The continuum theorem below is not a generic state-quantization theorem. It is an exact forward-closure result, together with a globally feasible continuation extension. It complements rather than subsumes the approximation results of \citet{DufourPrietoRumeau2013} and \citet{Saldi2018}. The economic example is a designed observed-fleet service contract. Unlike the estimated replacement application of \citet{Rust1987}, it supplies neither estimated primitives nor empirical implementation costs. High-dimensional operating approximation of the kind studied by \citet{BrummScheidegger2017} remains an important separate test; the current enclosures use finite directed Bellman passes.

\section{The common-policy revision problem}\label{sec:model48}
Let $X$ be a standard Borel space, $A=\{1,\ldots,m\}$, and $t=0,\ldots,T-1$. The known transition kernels $P_t^a$ are Borel. Rewards, nonnegative implementation costs, and terminal rewards are Borel and satisfy
\[
 |r_t(x,a)|\le R,\qquad 0\le k_t(x,a)\le K,\qquad |g(x)|\le G.
\]
Throughout, $0<\beta<1$, $T<\infty$, and $\varepsilon>0$. Define $\Ssum h=\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}\beta^j$ and $\Ssum0=0$. A policy $p$ is a family of Borel probability vectors $p_t(\cdot\mid x)$. Its operating and implementation values and the optimal operating value satisfy
\begin{align}
 J_T^p&=V_T=g,& C_T^p&=0,\nonumber\\
 V_t(x)&=\max_a\{r_t(x,a)+\beta P_t^aV_{t+1}(x)\},\label{eq:value48}\\
 J_t^p(x)&=\sum_a p_t(a\mid x)\{r_t(x,a)+\beta P_t^aJ_{t+1}^p(x)\},\label{eq:policy48}\\
 C_t^p(x)&=\sum_a p_t(a\mid x)\{k_t(x,a)+\beta P_t^aC_{t+1}^p(x)\}.\nonumber
\end{align}
For a specified initial probability law $\nu$, the target is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target48}
 \KR(\nu,\varepsilon)=\inf_{p\in\cF(\varepsilon)}\int C_0^p\,d\nu,
 \qquad \cF(\varepsilon)=\{p:J_t^p(x)\ge V_t(x)-\varepsilon\text{ for every }t,x\}.
\end{equation}
An operating-optimal Borel policy exists by backward first-maximizer selection, so this feasible set is nonempty. We use an infimum when a general Borel optimizer has not been established. Finite models have a compact policy simplex and continuous constraints, hence attain the minimum.

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regret48}
 d_t(x,a)=V_t(x)-r_t(x,a)-\beta P_t^aV_{t+1}(x),\qquad D_t^p=V_t-J_t^p.
\end{equation}
Then $d_t\ge0$, $D_T^p=0$, and $D_t^p=\sum_a p_t(a\mid x)(d_t(x,a)+\beta P_t^aD_{t+1}^p(x))$. The economic restriction is $0\le D_t^p\le\varepsilon$ everywhere. Its initial-law analogue, $\int D_0^p\,d\nu\le\varepsilon$, is weaker and will be used only as a comparison.

For an installed rule $a^0$, a representative cost is $k_t(x,a)=w_t(x)\ind\{a\ne a_t^0(x)\}$. Physical switching from the previous action is a different friction and requires augmenting the state. A fixed lottery-administration fee $f$ can be compared with a certificate in a designed budget exercise, but is not silently included in $k$. Scaling implementation costs by $c>0$ scales $\KR,L,U$, and an appropriate absolute target by $c$, while leaving the operating constraint unchanged.
