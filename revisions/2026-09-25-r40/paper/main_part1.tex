\title{Certified Bellman Operators for Costly Policy Revision}
\runtitle{Costly Revision and Common Continuations}
\begin{aug}\author[id=au1,addressref={add1}]{\fnms{Qian}~\snm{QI}\ead[label=e1]{qiqian@pku.edu.cn}}\address[id=add1]{\orgname{Peking University}}\end{aug}
\begin{abstract}
We study the minimum implementation cost of revising an installed policy while preserving operating performance after every state and date. A common randomized Markov continuation must serve all restarts. We construct a backward feasibility repair with an explicit cost modulus. A witness-driven form avoids an exact operating solve. It yields convergent global policy search for finite-state problems and an end-to-end approximation bound for continuous-state problems admitting certified total-variation aggregation. Exact rational spatial search and a verified McCormick comparator target the same constrained objective. A prospectively fixed eight-environment experiment reports all budget stops. On the original 42 continuous-state development configurations, whole-domain lotteries certify strict improvement over every deterministic policy in \PrimaryStrict{} cases. Nonlinear two-state lower bounds become positive, but longer-horizon gaps remain material. Independent checks distinguish arithmetic validity from approximation assumptions. The results identify when local operating guarantees can be converted into global revision certificates without changing the policy class.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
An organization may wish to improve an installed rule without replacing every decision it makes. Implementation cost then depends both on the decisions changed today and on the states visited under the revised rule tomorrow. A feasible revision must preserve the operating objective, whereas a cost-effective revision must economize on intervention along its own induced state distribution. These two requirements create a constrained dynamic problem. Pointwise selection of inexpensive changes need not solve it.

The relevant continuation must also be specified. Two histories that meet at the same date and state use the same continuation under a Markov rule. Allowing a different continuation for each initial condition or each remembered randomization can reduce costs, but changes the economic object. Conversely, requiring a deterministic rule can increase implementation cost even when randomization is admissible. We therefore take a common randomized Markov rule as the central choice variable. Performance is protected after every date and state; implementation cost is integrated under a stated initial distribution. A performance guarantee at every restart is not a claim of cost optimality at every restart.

The main contribution is a constructive connection between these requirements and a complete global search. A policy that violates every operating constraint by at most $\delta$ can be repaired backward without adding a persistent random seed or a history variable. The repair improves operating value at every state. Its additional implementation cost is bounded explicitly by a modulus that vanishes with $\delta$. Strict discounting and a positive operating allowance provide the slack; no global duality assumption is used. This repair gives an error bound for rational policy lattices. For binary actions it also gives a stopping theorem for the interval Bellman branch-and-bound procedure used in the experiments. Unlike convergence to a necessary-action relaxation, the limiting object is the original common-continuation constrained optimum.

The witness-driven extension separates operating approximation error from policy-search error in one computable global bound. It supplies a positive-margin check and a constructive policy without passing the exact optimum to the repair routine. A further perturbation result converts model error into operating and cost allowances; strict randomized separations also yield break-even administration charges. These statements extend the numerical method rather than changing its economic objective.

A second result transfers finite-state certificates to a continuous state space. Under a certified total-variation approximation of the transition kernel by a within-cell reset kernel, cell averaging maps an arbitrary measurable Markov policy to a finite-state policy. Lifting reverses this construction. Uniform primitive errors give an explicit operating-tolerance adjustment and a cost adjustment. The repair modulus controls the remaining difference between the relaxed and tightened finite-state optima. The theorem makes the approximation assumption operational and visible. It does not treat weak convergence of an atomic transition law as total-variation convergence, and it does not silently replace discrete shocks by continuous noise.

The numerical analysis separates three questions. First, does complete search return sound intervals for the common Markov problem, and how do those intervals compare with a direct global mathematical-programming formulation? Second, does randomization change the conclusion in the original continuous-state maintenance configurations? Third, can a nontrivial revision lower bound survive nonlinear, two-state dynamics and nonzero operating-witness error? Each experiment retains the same objective on the two sides of its interval. The eight new finite-state environments and both solver implementations were fixed before those environments were generated. The original 42 configurations are explicitly a development cohort, not a prospective test.

The resulting distinctions are economically consequential. In the development cohort, \PrimaryStrict{} whole-domain randomized deployments cost strictly less than a certified lower bound for every feasible deterministic policy under the same uniform initial law. This is a statement about the value of admissible randomization, not about a neural label attached to the installed rule. The nonlinear experiment changes the zero lower bound into a positive bound; its finest horizon-32 interval is $[\NonlinearFineLower,\NonlinearFineUpper]$. That interval is informative but not tight. In the prospective finite-state comparison, both methods face the same node allowance and wall-clock limit. The stronger linear relaxation often improves the lower endpoint, but longer horizons retain substantial residual intervals. The theory supplies completeness, not a claim that complete search is inexpensive.

\subsection{Relation to the literature}\label{sec:literature}
Constrained Markov decision processes provide the natural starting point \citep{Altman1999}. Occupation measures are particularly effective when the constraints are expectations under a designated initial law. Our formulation additionally requires every restarted problem to meet its operating guarantee using one shared continuation. A separate occupation measure for each restart would need compatibility restrictions: at a shared date and state, its action probabilities cannot depend on which restart generated the occupancy. Dropping that restriction produces a different feasible class. Our finite formulation imposes it directly through a single policy variable for each date and state. This observation does not preclude occupation-measure methods; it specifies the coupling they must preserve in this problem.

Finite approximations to constrained control are not new. \citet{Saldi2018} proves value convergence for discounted and average-cost constrained problems, and develops approximately optimal policies and rates under additional continuity conditions. \citet{SaldiLinderYuksel2018} provides a systematic treatment of quantized stochastic control. Our transfer result concerns finite-horizon, all-restart constraints and combines the within-cell reset identity with an explicit Markov feasibility repair. It states the total-variation condition needed to average unrestricted measurable policies rather than assuming that a convenient policy sieve is automatically dense in the constrained optimum. The theorem is not a replacement for those approximation theories; its additional object is the common-continuation restart constraint and its computable tolerance-to-cost modulus.

Primal--dual policy methods also solve constrained expected-return problems. \citet{DingEtAl2020} establishes global convergence guarantees for natural policy-gradient methods in discounted constrained MDPs. \citet{NiKamgarpour2025} studies safe exploration under cumulative constraints. Those learning problems have information and feasibility requirements different from our known-model, all-restart certification problem. Safe baseline improvement \citep{Laroche2019} and approximation-error analysis for fitted dynamic programming \citep{MunosSzepesvari2008,Scherrer2013} further distinguish model uncertainty, policy approximation, and optimization error. We claim no sample-efficiency comparison against methods given less information.

The global optimization ingredients are classical. Convex envelopes of factorable products originate in \citet{McCormick1976}; the numerical LP proposals here use a modern simplex implementation associated with \citet{HuangfuHall2018}. We do not attribute novelty to bilinear envelopes, best-bound search, or weak duality. The contribution is a common-policy economic formulation whose local approximation errors can be repaired into an admissible deployment, with an explicit global stopping bound. We use the direct McCormick formulation as a serious same-object comparator, not as a deliberately weak price portfolio. A rational residual correction turns proposed LP multipliers into lower bounds even when floating-point dual feasibility is imperfect. This separation of numerical proposal and proof follows the broader program of verified computation \citep{Rump2010}.

Semi-infinite optimization emphasizes the difference between checking finitely many constraints and controlling them on a region \citep{HettichKortanek1993}. Our continuous-state certificates therefore include whole-cell inequalities and isolated switching points. Information-relaxation bounds supply another route to dynamic-programming lower and upper bounds \citep{BrownSmithSun2010,BrownSmith2014}; they are related to, but not identical with, the restart support functions used below. The support functions remain useful accelerators even though no finite price set is asserted to exhaust a global dual.

The economic motivation is adjustment rather than unconstrained operating optimization. Numerical economics has long emphasized model structure, approximation error, and state-space complexity \citep{Judd1998,BrummScheidegger2017}. Empirical replacement models such as \citet{Rust1987} discipline operating primitives with data. Our revision cost is instead the cost of departing from an installed decision rule, not a cost paid whenever the previous physical action changes. Adding physical switching costs would require including the previous action in the state. The present maintenance environments are designed, known-model economies. Their normalized operating and implementation units are not estimated monetary welfare, and their certificates are not estimates of empirical prevalence. The general convergence results, rather than a claimed calibration, carry the methodological argument.

\section{The economic problem}\label{sec:model}
Let $X$ be a standard Borel space, $A=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ a finite action set, and $t=0,\ldots,T$. Rewards $r_t(x,a)$, implementation costs $k_t(x,a)$, and terminal operating payoff $g(x)$ are bounded Borel functions. The transition kernels $P_t^a$ are known Borel probability kernels. Write $|r_t|\leq R$, $|g|\leq G$, and $0\leq k_t\leq K$. Unless otherwise indicated, $0<\beta<1$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bellman}
 T_t^a v(x)=r_t(x,a)+\beta P_t^a v(x),\qquad
 V_T=g,\qquad V_t=\max_{a\in A}T_t^aV_{t+1}.
\end{equation}
Finiteness of $A$ makes $V_t$ Borel and permits a Borel maximizing selector, for example the lowest indexed maximizing action.

A randomized Markov policy is a sequence of Borel kernels $p_t(\cdot\mid x)$ on $A$. Its values satisfy
\begin{align}
 J_T^p&=g,& J_t^p&=\sum_a p_t(a\mid x)T_t^aJ_{t+1}^p,\label{eq:operating}\\
 C_T^p&=0,& C_t^p&=\sum_a p_t(a\mid x)\{k_t(x,a)+\beta P_t^aC_{t+1}^p(x)\}.\label{eq:cost}
\end{align}
The same $p_t$ is used by all histories reaching $(t,x)$. Randomization is newly drawn conditional on the current date and state; a persistent hidden coin indexing different future policies is not introduced.

For an initial probability law $\nu$, the central object is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 \KR(\nu,\varepsilon)=\inf_{p\in\cF_R(\varepsilon)}\int C_0^p\,d\nu,
 \quad
 \cF_R(\varepsilon)=\{p:J_t^p(x)\geq V_t(x)-\varepsilon\ \forall t,x\}.
\end{equation}
A deterministic policy restricts $p_t$ to vertices of the simplex and defines $\KD$. An operating-optimal policy is feasible, so both classes are nonempty. On a finite state space, the product of policy simplexes is compact, $J^p$ and $C^p$ are continuous, and the feasible set is closed. The infimum in \eqref{eq:target} is then attained. We use infimum, not an unproved function-space minimum, in the Borel-state formulation.

For an installed rule $a_t^0(x)$, the principal cost specification is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:revisioncost}
 k_t(x,a)=w_t(x)\ind\{a\ne a_t^0(x)\},\qquad w_t\geq0.
\end{equation}
The cost is charged for the action actually drawn. Its expectation under a lottery is not an indicator that the lottery's most probable action differs from the installed action. The unchanged installed policy has zero revision cost even when it is operating-infeasible. Consequently, dividing an optimality interval by its revision cost is undefined, not an informative relative-gap statistic. We report $(U-L)/U$ when $U>0$, and report zero-cost exactness separately.

The uniform law in the development economy weights all normalized initial conditions equally. It is a numerical reference distribution, not an estimated stationary law. We also integrate exactly or outwardly under densities $2x$ and $2(1-x)$. These calculations change the initial objective while leaving the all-state operating constraints and installed rule unchanged. A pointwise cost certificate would cover arbitrary initial laws, including atoms; an almost-everywhere cost enclosure need not.
