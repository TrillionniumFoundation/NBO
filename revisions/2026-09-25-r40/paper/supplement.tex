\section{Details of the repair and complete-search arguments}
\subsection{Policy perturbation and terminal rewards}
Use total variation $\TV(p,q)=\frac12\sum_a|p_a-q_a|$ for action distributions. If an action-dependent scalar takes values in an interval of length $D$, replacing $p$ by $q$ changes its expectation by at most $D\TV(p,q)$. With $h$ stages remaining, a stage cost at distance $s$ can be affected by the current decision and by $s$ earlier transitions. Thus the cost perturbation constant is $K\sum_{s=0}^{h-1}(s+1)\beta^s$. Operating rewards contribute $2R\sum_{s=0}^{h-1}(s+1)\beta^s$, and the terminal reward contributes $2Gh\beta^h$. The maximum over all remaining horizons is necessary: $h\beta^h$ need not be increasing in $h$. Taking only the horizon-$T$ terminal term would not provide a uniform all-restart bound. The main article uses this maximum explicitly.

\subsection{Common-continuation repair and approximate minimizers}
Let the original policy be feasible at allowance $\varepsilon+\delta$. At each backward step, all next-date repairs have already been fixed. The old current action probabilities evaluated against that repaired continuation give $q_p\geq V_t-\varepsilon-\delta$. The best current action gives $q_*\geq V_t-\beta\varepsilon$. If $q_p<V_t-\varepsilon$, the required mixture share is
\[
 \alpha=\frac{V_t-\varepsilon-q_p}{q_*-q_p}
 \leq\frac{\delta}{(1-\beta)\varepsilon+\delta}.
\]
Mixing occurs only on the Borel set with a positive numerator and denominator. The first maximizer in a fixed action ordering is Borel because the action set is finite. Operating value weakly improves at every date and state. The same policy therefore satisfies all repaired constraints simultaneously. A coin selected once at time zero and remembered would not prove this result; the construction does not use such a coin.

For a general Borel model an optimizer need not exist. Apply the repair to a policy whose cost is within $\xi>0$ of the relaxed infimum. The repaired cost is at most that relaxed infimum plus $\xi+D_C\rho_\varepsilon(\delta)$. Letting $\xi\downarrow0$ proves the value modulus without assuming attainment. In a finite-state model, the product of policy simplexes is compact, the finite-horizon Bellman values are polynomials in its coordinates, and the feasible set is closed and nonempty. There the constrained minimum is attained. The pure-action finite-state problem is a finite enumeration; the randomized repair does not preserve deterministic policies and is not presented as a deterministic repair.

\subsection{Why necessary contractions preserve completeness}
Fix the already contracted next-date probability intervals. Their rectangular operating Bellman recursion has one maximizing Markov policy that attains its upper value at all states, by backward induction. The inequality used to contract a current interval is therefore necessary for every feasible policy remaining in the original box. Contracting a current coordinate leaves next-date values unchanged. Its retained endpoint set, when nonempty, still permits an operating value at least $V_t-\varepsilon$. Proceeding backward establishes this fact simultaneously at all dates of the contracted box. Capping an operating upper value by $V_t$ does not reduce a true rectangular maximum, since an unconstrained operating optimum already dominates every box-restricted policy.

The implementation also computes lower operating enclosures and may discard a box when a lower enclosure exceeds its upper enclosure. That is an inconsistency test, not a failure of one sufficient candidate. Lower operating clipping at $V_t-\varepsilon$ uses a property of every feasible policy. Such additional necessary tests cannot remove a feasible policy. The separate cost lower recursion uses the retained probability rectangle without imposing incompatible, restart-specific continuation choices. Ordinary cost Bellman minimization attains this rectangular lower value at every state.

Let $w$ be the largest retained interval width. The midpoint differs by at most $w$ in total variation from the rectangular operating maximizer and the rectangular cost minimizer. Hence its operating deficit is at most $D_Jw$, and its cost is at most the rectangular lower cost plus $D_Cw$. Repair adds at most $D_C\rho_\varepsilon(D_Jw)$. This proves the stated box error, including the effect of contractions.

For the finite stopping count, put $a=\beta^{T-1}w_*$. Any physical width exceeding $w_*$ has weighted width exceeding or equal to $a$. Each weighted coordinate starts at at most one and, whenever selected, is at least halved; contractions can only reduce it further. Along any path, after at most $\lceil\log_2(1/a)\rceil$ halvings per coordinate, no weighted width can remain above $a$. Because the largest weighted coordinate is selected, no more than $nT$ times that number of splits are needed before all physical widths are at most $w_*$. A positive-gap search never needs to refine a box whose repaired midpoint is already within the requested gap of its lower bound. There are finitely many possible nodes above this depth. The count can be enormous, and does not imply that the fixed-budget experiments exhaust it.

\subsection{Rational policy lattices and a deterministic benchmark}
For $m$ actions, rounding down the first $m-1$ probabilities and assigning the remainder to the last action gives a total-variation error at most $(m-1)/M$. The relaxed feasibility test at allowance $\varepsilon+D_J\theta_M$ retains a rounded version of every originally feasible policy. Its minimum cost minus $D_C\theta_M$ is a lower bound. Repair of its minimizing policy gives a feasible upper bound. The main theorem bounds the gap by $D_C[\theta_M+\rho_\varepsilon(D_J\theta_M)]$. Lattice levels need not be nested: taking the maximum of all lower endpoints and the minimum of all feasible upper endpoints yields nested certificates. The executed algorithm uses box search, rather than asserting that this exhaustive lattice was run.

\section{Continuous-state transfer without a hidden policy sieve}
\subsection{Averaging identity}
In the reset model, after a transition into $E_j$, the conditional within-cell law is exactly $\mu_j$, independently of the predecessor state and action. For an arbitrary Borel policy $p$, define $\bar p_t(a\mid i)=\int_{E_i}p_t(a\mid x)\mu_i(dx)$. At the terminal date the payoff is cellwise constant. If the averaged next-date values coincide with those of the finite model, integrating the reset Bellman equation over $\mu_i$ gives the finite Bellman equation with action probabilities $\bar p_t$. Backward induction proves the identity for operating values and costs. It uses the same averaged policy at every restart, not a separately optimized policy for each $i$.

Uniform primitive comparison bounds $|J^p-J_h^p|$ by $a_t$ and $|C^p-C_h^p|$ by $b_t$ for the very same policy. The comparison for optimal operating values is obtained directly from the contraction and monotonicity of finite-horizon Bellman operators. An original feasible policy, when averaged, therefore satisfies the finite constraints at allowance $\varepsilon+2a_h$. A finite policy feasible at $\varepsilon-2a_h$ can be lifted to the original model and satisfies its original constraints. The initial-mixture identity converts the integrated cost exactly in the reset model; $b_h$ controls the remaining original-model error. Combining both maps with the repair modulus produces the global error budget in the article.

\subsection{Verification obligations for an application}
The theorem needs a bound for every bounded Borel test function, not merely weak convergence for smooth functions. A verified uniform density approximation can provide such a bound through an $L_1$ estimate. For a compact domain with a Lipschitz density in both source and destination coordinates, piecewise-constant density integration gives a computable linear mesh modulus, with cell volume and domain diameter included in the constant. Discontinuous installed rules require an aligned partition or a separately bounded cost error. The approximation must retain the declared initial-law mixture. These conditions are sufficient, not automatically satisfied by a chosen mesh.

The affine and bilinear benchmark kernels contain atoms. Replacing their images by uniform cell measures does not furnish a vanishing operator-total-variation error. Their direct whole-domain certificates do not depend on this transfer theorem. We retain their unresolved structural gaps and do not present a mesh sequence as a proof of convergence for those atomic models. The stopped diffusion would additionally need continuous-action coverage, boundary settlement, time discretization, and diffusion/quadrature error bounds before the same end-to-end conclusion could be claimed.

\section{Additional witness, robustness, and arithmetic audits}
