\section{Error, work, and claims}
The witness-driven theorem exposes the sufficient margin $(1-\beta)\varepsilon-d$. Its complete lattice bracket separates witness width, operating residual, policy resolution, and the repair cost. It does not assert that every arbitrary witness has a positive margin, or that exhaustive search is inexpensive. The continuous-state transfer additionally requires a checked reset-kernel approximation in operator total variation. Atomic-shock weak convergence is not substituted for that assumption.

The finite comparison reports all 16 solver/case rows, including every budget or time stop. The unchanged primary cohort has \PrimaryStrict{} certified uniform-law deterministic separations, \PrimaryZero{} zero-cost optima, and \PrimaryPositive{} positive randomized intervals with nonzero widths. The local recursion improves \LocalImproveCount{} uniform lower endpoints. The finest nonlinear horizon-32 interval is $[\NonlinearFineLower,\NonlinearFineUpper]$ and retains relative width \NonlinearFineRelative\%. Its allowance is $1/2$. The four $1/20$ nonlinear rows remain lower-bound-only and are never combined with upper policies certified only at $1/2$.

The administration-cost calculation is a derived economic robustness margin under the known designed model. It is not calibrated monetary welfare. Positive per-date overhead below its certified threshold preserves a strict deterministic-versus-randomized ranking. Model-uncertainty margins require separately verified primitive error bounds; R40 does not manufacture empirical confidence regions. The stopped-control numerical target and its boundary/action-coverage obligations remain archived and unresolved at the requested allowance, rather than being deleted or treated as solved by a maintenance example.
