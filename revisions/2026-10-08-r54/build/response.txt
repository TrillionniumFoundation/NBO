22   three. Every earlier construction rung is charged, and the reconstructed policy              22

23   is checked against the original checkpoint. Each complete acquired incumbent                 23

24   then receives exactly T passes, with every date updated against the same old                 24

25   policy before simultaneous adoption. The implementation is therefore a full-sweep            25

26   calculation, not a sequence of relabeled terminal repairs.                                   26

31   the recursion Etk+1 ≤ β t Etk+1 + εkt , followed by its exact finite-horizon unrolling. An   31

               1.5. M4 and B6: Approximately null structure and false-null decisions
13                                                                                             13

14       The extension deposits 384 controlled cases formed by two incumbents, three           14

15   nuisance amplitudes and four values of each of three perturbations. The perturba- 15
16   tions change the ReLU direction, action exposure and a known action-dependent             16

17   innovation-mean coefficient. Each case exhausts every closed five-bit observation         17

18   cell and records the corrected and deliberately false-null decisions.                     18

19       The new quantitative bound uses the Lipschitz property of the nuisance compo- 19
20   nent and a verified coupling of the actual kernels. For affine common-innovation          20

21   exposure it is | M| |v⊤ B( a − b)|. The text derives the coefficient under simultaneous   21

22   direction and exposure perturbations and explains how an additional innovation            22

23   discrepancy must be charged. Exact zero is used only after the coefficient has been       23

24   checked algebraically.                                                                    24

25       The replay checks that all corrected selected actions remain feasible and have non- 25
26   positive true-advantage upper endpoints. The false-null record identifies harmful         26

27   actions only when their true-advantage lower endpoint is strictly positive. Accepted,     27

28   blocked and unchanged cases are all preserved. This addresses controlled approx- 28
29   imate nullity and known misspecification; it does not constitute discovery of an          29

30   invariant from a learned critic, nor inference for an unknown transition law. The         30

31   innovation-mean shift retains a known valid coupling, so it is not mislabeled as a        31

32   generic test of arbitrary marginal misspecification.                                      32

19                        1.10. B9: The economic decision and its units                          19

20                                                                                               20

21     The full-sweep policies create an actual controller-adoption question that the            21

22   exact-null amplitude ablation did not create. If the cumulative gain is in [l, u], a        22

23   replacement charge τ gives net gain in [l − τ, u − τ ] on the same simultaneous             23

24   event. The main article reports this entire break-even account. Positive lower gain         24

25   supports adoption for every fee below it; fees above the upper gain are ruled out;          25

26   the intermediate region remains unresolved.                                                 26

27     The investment primitives and fees are normalized theoretical quantities. We              27

28   do not portray them as estimated installation costs or a calibrated welfare result. 28
29   The substantive numerical object is an implementable policy change whose gain is            29

30   identified under the original law, with the construction, verification and decision         30

31   costs separately stated. Information acquisition and the original fee-selection results     31

32   remain part of the same economic framework.                                                 32
