\section{Primitive Adjustment Bounds and Decision-Certificate Replication}
\subsection{The Primitive Option Bound}
For $z=u-1>0$ and $a=\log c$,
\[
 U(c,u)=-\frac{e^{-za}}z,
 \qquad U_u(c,u)=\frac{e^{-za}(1+za)}{z^2}.
\]
Differentiating again gives the strictly negative second derivative in the main text. For finite positive consumption support the expectation can be differentiated term by term. Taylor's integral formula and concavity imply, for $|\theta|\leq\bar\theta$,
\[
 g_\sigma\theta-\tfrac12 M_\sigma\theta^2
 \leq F_\sigma(\theta)-F_\sigma(0)\leq g_\sigma\theta.
\]
Subtracting $k\theta^2/2$, evaluating the lower quadratic at
$\theta=g_\sigma/(k+M_\sigma)$ when this is feasible, and maximizing the upper quadratic on the whole line give the option bounds. The upper bound remains valid if the actual optimum is constrained. Subtracting the negative-class upper bound from the positive-class lower bound gives the relative-option inequality.

Since $D_A=A_+-A_->0$ is a primitive constant in this specialization,
\[
 \Delta^0(d)=B_+-B_-+dD_A,\qquad
 \Delta^{\rm adj}(d)=\Delta^0(d)+\Omega_+-\Omega_-.
\]
Both functions have the same strictly positive slope. Their unique indifference contracts therefore differ by $(\Omega_+-\Omega_-)/D_A$. This proves the stated threshold shift and the intervening interval of opposite rankings. Uniqueness here follows from the explicitly constant duration difference in the two-stage model; it is not carried over to the full stopped portfolio model.

\subsection{A Curvature Bound over an Entire Primitive Family}
Curvature does not have to be estimated by sampling preference indices. In the notation above,
\[
 -U_{uu}(c,1+z)
 =\frac{e^{-za}[(1+za)^2+1]}{z^3}
 =\int_a^\infty t^2e^{-zt}\,dt.
\]
Its second derivative with respect to $z$ is $\int_a^\infty t^4e^{-zt}\,dt\geq0$. The integral is finite for every $z>0$, also when $a<0$. Hence the negative second derivative of expected utility is convex in $z$, and its maximum on the preference interval is attained at an endpoint. It is affine in the probability of a two-point consumption lottery. Its maximum over the stated probability/preference rectangle can therefore be bounded at its four vertices.

With $u_0=2$, $\bar\theta=0.2$, $C_-=0.5$, and $C_+\in\{0.05,0.8\}$, the positive-class shadow is affine and negative for every $p\in[0.06,0.10]$. Its absolute value lies between $1.4820$ and $3.1180$; the absolute negative-class shadow is below $0.6138$; and $M_+<17.342$. Feasibility of the lower-bound trial follows from $3.1180/20<0.2$. For every $k\in[20,80]$,
\[
 \Omega_+-\Omega_-
 \geq\frac{1.4820^2}{2(80+17.342)}-\frac{0.6138^2}{2\cdot20}
 >0.00186.
\]
The bound is conservative because it takes adverse cost values separately in its positive and negative terms. Division by $D_A=0.5$ gives a reduction greater than $0.00372$. This proves a statement on the entire probability/cost rectangle, not on the finite set of displayed optimizations.

\subsection{The Full-Model Joint-Region Calculation}
The risk certificate uses all $1,617$ states, all eight decision dates, and the original $1,568$-action target, comprising the common union mesh and the three frozen neural proposals. The declared initial state is $(u,X)=(2,1.25)$, the adjustment cost is $k=2$, the contract interval is $[0.4,0.45]$, and the one-step shock-law mixture probability lies in $[0,0.25]$. This region was selected during model exploration; it is not a preregistered or exhaustive characterization of all reversals.

For each adjustment regime and each of the four parameter corners, one backward optimization yields both first-risk classes: dates after the first use the regime's common feasible menu, and the two first-date maxima enforce positive and nonpositive risky positions separately. This is eight corner optimizations, yielding sixteen feasible class policies. The no-adjustment menu imposes zero deliberate adjustment at every date and state but retains preference shocks. Later portfolio signs remain unrestricted. Endpoint class values feed eight localized count constructions, one for each contract endpoint, class, and adjustment regime. Each corner policy is independently evaluated at reward contrasts zero and one, giving its Bernstein intercept and duration coefficients; this covers arbitrary intermediate $d$ without a new policy solve.

Convexity of the optimal class value in $d$ validates linear interpolation of its upper polynomials, not of its optimal policies. Feasible-policy values are affine in $d$ exactly. The resulting degree-eight-by-degree-one tensor coefficients certify the entire rectangle. Subdivision is available if a sign interval contains zero; acceptance of the original closed rectangle in the deposited execution means no further subdivision is required. Pointwise replay at the corners checks implementations but is not the source of the continuum sign claim. All upper and lower tensor coefficients, policy indices, and policy-feature arrays are deposited, with a coefficient-only replay program that does not call an optimizer.

\subsection{Numerical Error Account}
The exact-arithmetic theorem is stated separately from its floating-point implementation. The application uses an a priori round-to-nearest allowance relative to the \emph{stored finite kernel and reward arrays}. It is not a claim of directed-rounding interval arithmetic, and it does not include errors in choosing the state/action grid, constructing the quadrature model, or approximating a diffusion. Those omissions do not change which finite target the certificate concerns.

Every stored continuation row is nonnegative with at most sixteen pre-aggregation entries. Let $\widehat\beta$ be the largest computed row sum plus $10^{-12}$. The program verifies $\widehat\beta\leq1+2\cdot10^{-12}$. This margin dominates summation error for sixteen nonnegative entries of this size. Let $R$ be twice the largest sum of the absolute stored base reward, duration reward at $|d|\leq1$, and effort reward at $k=2$, across both common kernels and every neural-proposal kernel. The use of $|d|\leq1$ covers the intermediate feature calculation at $d=1$, not merely the reported interval ending at $0.45$. Let $G=2\|g\|_\infty$ and
\[
 V=(G+HR)\max(1,\widehat\beta)^H,\quad
 q=4096(H+1),\quad \gamma_q=\frac{q\,\mathrm u}{1-q\,\mathrm u},
 \qquad \mathrm u=2^{-53}.
\]
The per-class allowance used in the calculation is
\begin{equation}
 e=2\gamma_q(1+2V+R)\max(1,\widehat\beta)^H.
 \label{eq:r7_roundoff}
\end{equation}

Here the operation budget is deliberately conservative. One continuation dot product requires at most sixteen products and fifteen additions; constructing its sparse row requires at most sixteen additions of original entries. Adding the three reward components, mixing endpoint laws, and mixing conditional-count branches takes fewer than forty additional scalar operations per path per date. An endpoint action maximum is nonexpansive in the sup norm: its error is bounded by the largest candidate error, not multiplied by the number of actions. Thus an endpoint solve, a selected-policy coefficient recursion, or a count recursion has fewer than $128H$ serial scalar operations along any contributing path. Computing intercept/duration features from values at zero and one adds one subtraction and one multiply-add per coefficient, with absolute magnitude bounded by $2V$. A degree-$H$ restriction uses at most two triangular de Casteljau passes, with fewer than $6(H+1)^2$ scalar operations per retained coefficient path even if counted without sharing. For $H=8$, these counts, subsequent differences, and coefficient extrema are dominated by $4096(H+1)$. Positive mixing and row-mass stability propagate the absolute bound by at most the displayed mass factor. The factors two in $R,G$ and outside $\gamma_q$ cover input rounding margins and the addition of separately computed paths. The formula is an absolute-error allowance; signed reward cancellation is not analyzed with a relative-error claim. No overflow or underflow occurs in the recorded value range.

The same allowance is charged separately to the upper and feasible lower construction. A class-value gap therefore includes $2e$, and a class-difference sign interval also includes $2e$. The relative-option conclusion adds the already enlarged difference intervals. The information-relaxation and policy-bank errors are controlled by their computed coefficient gaps, not absorbed into an unexplained floating-point tolerance. Independent selected-kernel replay checks the actual arithmetic discrepancy, but small observed replay errors are not substituted for the a priori allowance. Supplementary JSON records the value and reward magnitudes, row-mass bound, operation budget, and final allowance.
