TECHNICAL SUPPLEMENT TO NEURAL BELLMAN OPERATORS
QIAN QI
Peking University
This supplement supplies complete proofs for the original Neural Bell-
man Operator, its feasible own-future construction, recursive risk and
action accounts, stochastic-control foundations and Brownian recursive-
utility bridge. The retained action, continuous-risk information and policy-
improvement statements are included with their full proofs. The R121
addition derives the original integrated-risk marginal, constrained tail
movement, outward finite-risk derivative labels, exact finite-box training
bounds, feature-aware action contrasts and recursive anchor propagation.
It states the precise information interface, numerical qualification and pop-
ulation assumptions. Earlier proofs and economic models retain their own
scope; a result for one objective is not transferred to another by notation.
Statements introduced here are numbered S.*.
1. RETAINED ACTION AND CONTINUOUS-RISK COMPARISON STATEMENTS
The following statements retain their original assumptions and mathematical
content. Their move from the preceding main article to this supplement changes
presentation, not their economic criterion or execution identity.
2. ACTION-ALIGNED ACQUISITION AND COMPLETE-POLICY WORK
A continuation is acquired to make a decision, not to minimize an approximation
statistic in isolation. The retained investment experiment makes the distinction op-
erational: every certificate-oriented fit reduces its validated residual span, whereas
aggregate first-proposal rejections increase. We now construct an acquisition target
in the units of the admission inequality. The construction uses the same original
action set and risk law as the NBO policy account.

3. RESOLVING THE ACTIVE INNOVATION TAILS
The original continuous innovation law supplies information that an NBO must
share with its strongest conventional competitors. Convexity of a continuation along
that innovation implies that the adverse outcomes occupy at most two endpoint
intervals. Locating these intervals before quadrature changes which continuation
values must be acquired. In particular, the upper information bound need not pay a
factor (1 −α)−1 for resolving the entire innovation support when only a fraction
1 −α is adverse. The result below is a deterministic point-information theorem and
an outward Bellman implementation. It does not assert a neural representation
separation, a randomized optimum, or multivariate tractability.

4.2. Safe refinement, stopping, and the future-policy floor
Start from a completed original-policy incumbent. A proposed replacement is
accepted only when the upper endpoint of (25) is strictly negative and its own policy
interval passes the unchanged regret target τ = 1/256 and width target 1/8192. At
the same state an original-optimum lower bound may be reused across actions;
an action’s policy interval may not. Refuse a candidate when its lower contrast
endpoint is nonnegative, or retain the incumbent when the prespecified work limit
is exhausted. Every refused trial remains in the complete cost.

16. A CONTROLLED RECURSIVE-UTILITY BSDE VERIFICATION BRIDGE
The connection between recursive utility and backward stochastic differential
equations has a classical foundation (Pardoux and Peng, 1990, El Karoui et al.,
1997). For an NDU/FBSDE application, the preference-state dynamics, information
filtration and recursive objective must first be specified. The result below supplies a
precise Brownian Markov subcase with an augmented but decoupled forward state.
It does not identify a general coupled, general-martingale NDU system with the
investment model’s nested AVaR recursion. The two-date investment experiment
supplies no observations for this different continuous-time criterion.
Fix a finite horizon T, deterministic initial state X0 = x, a Brownian natural
filtration with its usual augmentation, and an admissible progressively measurable
