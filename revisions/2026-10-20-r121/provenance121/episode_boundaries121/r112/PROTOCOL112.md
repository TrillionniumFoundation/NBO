## Mathematical question and fixed algorithm

For the same state, law and fixed implemented future, write J(a)=Q(a)+eta(a), where Q is the original optimal-future action objective, Q is mu-strongly convex, and 0<=eta<=epsilon uniformly. Given a completed incumbent with l<=J(a), and an already-paid bound Q(b)<=U, prove for c=(1-t)a+t*b that

J(c)-J(a) <= D(t)=t*(U-l)-mu*t*(1-t)*(b-a)^2/2+epsilon.

The same-action contrast is exactly zero and is treated separately. This uses strong convexity of Q, not false convexity of implemented J. Prove the bound, its sharpness for this specified scalar information interface, its constrained minimizer and a recursive policy-improvement statement with explicit uniform premises. Distinguish this elementary convexity ingredient from the new paired original-law implementation and information account. Do not claim neural exclusivity or a general Bellman oracle lower bound.

Let H=mu*(b-a)^2/2. For every distinct already-paid value anchor b, generate t=clip((H-(U-l))/(2*H),0,1), form c, and round downward to the 30-bit action lattice within the anchor segment. Recompute the actual rational t after rounding. All calculations and comparisons are exact rational with outward binary endpoints. In the original investment problem use mu=2 and epsilon=(15/16)*2^-20, without fitting these constants to outcomes.

The new coupled postprocessor starts AFTER the entire budget-construct P3 service from R111, retaining all its work. It sharpens the incumbent lower bound with the existing original-law minorants, generates the above candidates from all paid optimal-future upper anchors, and computes their original-policy lower/upper envelopes. It may tighten a candidate upper bound by incumbent_upper+D. It accepts only a distinct action with D<0 AND the unchanged original loss and policy-width targets. Among accepted candidates choose the smallest outward upper endpoint, then smallest action. Otherwise return the complete P3 incumbent unchanged. Keep all rejected candidates, gain certificates, comparisons and rational arithmetic counts. There are no new Bellman/terminal queries in the postprocessor; computation, copying and certification are not free. This is compared with separate marginal-interval admission rather than being presumed superior.
