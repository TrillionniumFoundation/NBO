1                                       1. INTRODUCTION                                        1

2                                                                                              2

3        A dynamic decision maker buys an approximation because it changes the cost            3

4    of making a good decision. In a constrained investment problem, an accurate               4

5    continuation level can still give the wrong incentive to invest, while a continuation     5

6    with a large common level error can choose the correct action. Under recursive            6

7    risk, the difficulty is greater: changing a continuation can also change which shocks     7

8    receive adverse weight. A numerical method must therefore connect its acquired            8

9    information, its fitted critic, its feasible actor and its computational work to the      9

10   original recursive objective.                                                             10

11       Neural Bellman Operators (NBOs) address this chain by constructing and learn- 11
12   ing recursive continuation operators together with an account of their policy error. 12
13   The object is the Bellman operator of the specified economy. Its criterion, infor- 13
14   mation filtration, constraints and boundary conditions remain part of that object         14

15   throughout approximation and deployment. The paper retains both a constructive            15

16   own-future neural operator and an analysis of arbitrary acquired critics. It develops     16

17   their links with stochastic control and, under separate explicit assumptions, nonlin- 17
18   ear Brownian recursive utility. A result for the nested risk investment application is    18

19   not automatically a welfare improvement in a different expected-utility or nonlinear      19

20   recursive-utility model.                                                                  20

21       The central computational question is which continuation information is worth         21

22   acquiring. Earlier NBO calculations found a concrete mismatch: every certificate- 22
23   oriented fit in a retained comparison reduced a residual-span criterion, but the aggre- 23
24   gate number of rejected first actions increased from 644 to 757. The quadratic control    24

25   remained a strong complete-service baseline. The subsequent policy-comparison             25

26   calculations preserved that economic criterion and constructed safe actual improve- 26
27   ments, yet acquisition still used a value loss. Those results motivate a new learning     27

28   calculation in the original model. Their observations and costs retain their historical   28

29   identities in the development companion; the present sample does not reuse them. 29
30       We learn the marginal continuation cost after evaluating the original risk operator. 30
31   This order matters. In the two-date investment economy, the original optimal              31

32   continuation is the last-date Bellman image of the known terminal function. It is         32
                                 NEURAL BELLMAN OPERATORS                                   3

1    acquired through paid outward calculations. The root critic retains the known action       1

2    cost and learns the action-dependent part of the integrated continuation. A state- 2
3    only anchor completes its value level. The resulting actor is proposed by piecewise        3

4    minimization of that learned objective over the original capacity interval. Floating       4

5    critical-point calculations generate a proposal; admission requires a certificate for      5

6    the original complete policy. The same acquired values, features where relevant,           6

7    feasible actions and certification rules are available to the conventional controls.       7

8      The mathematical contribution has three connected parts. First, we derive a con- 8
9    tinuous original-action marginal and explicit moduli from the nonlinear investment         9

10   technology, binding capacity and moving AVaR tails. The central interval excluded          10

11   from the adverse tail can touch either shock boundary. Its variational inequalities        11

12   control movement of the tail without differentiating a numerical sort or an un- 12
13   constrained optimizer. Finite original-risk value enclosures then provide outward          13

14   derivative labels, with the precision cost of finite differences paid explicitly.          14

15     Second, a bounded readout is fitted to minimize the worst action-marginal dis- 15
16   crepancy over those outward labels. The finite training problem is convex for each         16

17   declared feature family. Exact primal and dual bounds certify the actual stored            17

18   coefficients without treating a floating solver’s success flag as a proof. An explicit     18

19   feature-range construction states how to extend the acquired information to off- 19
20   design action comparisons. Its integral along the feasible action path controls policy     20

21   loss and possible policy changes; a capacity-sensitive stationarity bound sharpens         21

22   the account when available. The argument applies to a nonconvex fitted polyno- 22
23   mial or spline through the all-action comparison, and never assumes that target            23

24   smoothness removes a fitted ReLU kink. This extension is a proved construction             24

25   whose numerical cell bounds and cost must also be supplied before it can certify an        25

26   off-design decision. The current experiment audits the finite-node fits and admits         26

27   policies through the separate original-model certificate; it does not claim to have        27

28   executed that global feature-cell calculation.                                             28

29     Third, we state what is lost if the critic’s anchor is discarded recursively. In         29

30   this very investment transition, a state-dependent continuation level that does not        30

31   change a last-date action can shift the root marginal by a known nonzero amount. 31
32   A full level-and-contrast recursion therefore carries the anchor error, the action gap     32
     4

1    and the future policy error separately. A noncash-invariant recursive preference          1

2    receives its own continuation Lipschitz account. It cannot inherit a monetary shift       2

3    identity merely because both criteria have a Bellman notation.                            3

4        Derivative-assisted economic dynamic programming already uses Hermite ap- 4
5    proximation with value and slope information (Cai and Judd, 2015). Derivative             5

6    supervision and task-based model learning also have established precedents (Czar- 6
7    necki et al., 2017, Donti et al., 2017, Elmachtoub and Grigas, 2022). The training        7

8    certificates use convex duality and affine supporting bounds; these general tools do      8

9    not themselves supply a claim of methodological novelty. The development history          9

10   also contains certificate-aware readouts and an action-null quotient analysis. The        10

11   additional results here concern their explicit construction and verification for the      11

12   original recursive risk operator, including tail changes, paid finite-risk information,   12

13   feasible actions and recursive value levels. A three-probe lower bound identifies         13

14   a limitation of that stated information interface; it is not a lower bound for every      14

15   algorithm with access to the investment formulas.                                         15

16       The numerical comparison changes the learning loss before changing the evalua- 16
17   tion sample. Two separately executed acquisition budgets use nested state designs. 17
18   Seven stored representations each have a value-loss and an action-marginal-loss           18

19   critic; the online original-model method is included at both risk levels. Quadratic,      19

20   polynomial, cubic spline, multilinear grid and semi-analytic controls form the            20

21   conventional menu. An exactly corresponding hinge encoding makes the neural               21

22   representation claim testable: a geometry shared with that control is not a uniquely      22

23   neural contribution.                                                                      23

24       The protocol fixes 64 new independent initial states, complete original-policy        24

25   evaluation, five fresh matched timing processes, exact independent reconstruction,        25

26   simultaneous positive and adverse gain inference and full acquisition/service cost        26

27   comparisons. It also fixes how unresolved and failed calculations are retained. The       27

28   empirical question is whether action-marginal learning changes decisions, rejections      28

29   and complete work enough to compensate for its acquisition cost. A reduction of its       29

30   own finite-node fitting objective alone does not answer that question.                    30

31       The completed comparison gives an adverse answer for the implemented learner. 31
32   MARGINAL lowers its independently checked finite-node residual in all 28 loss             32

15   the upper regions not ruled out by the intervals. The result leaves a specific com- 15
16   putational task: acquire and control the continuation errors that change feasible                  16

17   actions at less total cost than the strong method that resolves the original decision              17

18   directly. The path and capacity-sensitive bounds state sufficient conditions for that              18

19   task; the present experiment does not supply their unexecuted feature-cover or                     19

20   adaptive-selection costs.                                                                          20

25     The role of a Neural Bellman Operator is to acquire and reuse continuation           25

26   information that changes feasible economic decisions at an acceptable total cost. 26
27   The new construction supplies original-marginal regularity, paid derivative labels,    27

28   checked finite-box fitting gaps and sufficient action conditions under the original    28

29   recursive risk objective. The implemented MARGINAL learner nevertheless raises         29

30   the independently enclosed sample mean cost in every loss comparison, and its          30

31   timing improvements are a minority of the complete records. This campaign has          31

32   not established the intended neural economic or computational advantage. The re- 32

3      The action bounds specify a concrete route for further algorithmic work. An             3

4    original derivative enclosure can stop regret certification when its capacity-sensitive   4
