3        A dynamic decision maker buys an approximation because it changes the cost            3

4    of making a good decision. In a constrained investment problem, an accurate               4

5    continuation level can still give the wrong incentive to invest, while a continuation     5

6    with a large common level error can choose the correct action. Under recursive            6

7    risk, the difficulty is greater: changing a continuation can also change which shocks     7

8    receive adverse weight. A numerical method must therefore connect its acquired            8

9    information, its fitted critic, its feasible actor and its computational work to the      9

10   original recursive objective.                                                             10

11       Neural Bellman Operators (NBOs) address this chain by constructing and learn- 11
12   ing recursive continuation operators together with an account of their policy error. 12
13   The object is the Bellman operator of the specified economy. Its criterion, infor- 13
14   mation filtration, constraints and boundary conditions remain part of that object         14

15   throughout approximation and deployment. The paper retains both a constructive            15

16   own-future neural operator and an analysis of arbitrary acquired critics. It develops     16

17   their links with stochastic control and, under separate explicit assumptions, nonlin- 17
18   ear Brownian recursive utility. A result for the nested risk investment application is    18

19   not automatically a welfare improvement in a different expected-utility or nonlinear      19

20   recursive-utility model.                                                                  20

21       The central computational question is which continuation information is worth         21

22   acquiring. Earlier NBO calculations found a concrete mismatch: every certificate- 22
23   oriented fit in a retained comparison reduced a residual-span criterion, but the aggre- 23
24   gate number of rejected first actions increased from 644 to 757. The quadratic control    24

25   remained a strong complete-service baseline. The subsequent policy-comparison             25

26   calculations preserved that economic criterion and constructed safe actual improve- 26
27   ments, yet acquisition still used a value loss. Those results motivate a new learning     27

28   calculation in the original model. Their observations and costs retain their historical   28

29   identities in the development companion; the present sample does not reuse them. 29
