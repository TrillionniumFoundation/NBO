\section{A controlled recursive-utility BSDE verification bridge}\label{supp:bridge109}
The connection between recursive utility and backward stochastic differential equations has a classical foundation \citep{PardouxPeng1990,ElKarouiPengQuenez1997}. For an NDU/FBSDE application, the preference-state dynamics, information filtration and recursive objective must first be specified. The result below supplies a precise Brownian Markov subcase with an augmented but decoupled forward state. It does not identify a general coupled, general-martingale NDU system with the investment model's nested AVaR recursion. The two-date investment experiment supplies no observations for this different continuous-time criterion.

Fix a finite horizon $T$, deterministic initial state $X_0=x$, a Brownian natural filtration with its usual augmentation, and an admissible progressively measurable action process $a_s$ valued in a compact set. The augmented economic and preference state solves
\[
 dX_s=b(s,X_s,a_s)\,ds+\sigma(s,X_s,a_s)\,dW_s.
\]
Assume bounded coefficients, uniform Lipschitz continuity in the state, and the usual measurability. A chosen feedback policy must additionally define an admissible, well-posed state process; arbitrary discontinuous feedback is not licensed by these assumptions on open-loop controls. The state may include preference coordinates, but there is no dependence of $b,\sigma$ on the backward pair in this subcase. Let $g$ and $f(s,x,0,0,a)$ be bounded. The scalar cost driver satisfies
\[
 |f(s,x,y,z,a)-f(s,x,y',z',a)|\le L_y|y-y'|+L_z\|z-z'\|.
\]
The original policy criterion is $Y_0^a$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bsde109}
 Y_s^a=g(X_T)+\int_s^T f(v,X_v,Y_v^a,Z_v^a,a_v)\,dv
                  -\int_s^T Z_v^a\,dW_v.
\end{equation}
Converting a utility into a cost requires the corresponding sign changes in both the driver and terminal condition. Merely changing the name of the output does not identify different utility functionals.

For a bounded $u\in C^{1,2}$ with the bounded derivatives needed below, set
\begin{align*}
 z_u(s,x,a)&=\sigma(s,x,a)^{\mathsf T}\nabla u(s,x),\\
 \mathcal H^a u&=\partial_su+b(s,x,a)^{\mathsf T}\nabla u
 +\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\sigma\sigma^{\mathsf T}D^2u)
 +f(s,x,u,z_u,a).
\end{align*}
The whole-space formulation introduces no artificial boundary. A bounded-domain version requires an invariant domain or a specified stopped process with its stopping-boundary error included in the terminal account. Deleting that term is not justified by a small interior residual.

\begin{proposition}[Original-recursion residual certificate]\label{prop:bsde109}
For each admissible action process the forward equation and \eqref{eq:bsde109} have a unique square-integrable adapted solution. Suppose uniformly over the declared state-action domain
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal H^a u&\ge-e_-(s)\quad\hbox{for every admissible }a,
 &\mathcal H^{\pi}u&\le e_+(s),\\
 -\eta_-&\le g-u(T,\cdot)\le\eta_+,
\end{align*}
where $e_\pm$ are nonnegative integrable deterministic bounds and $\eta_\pm\ge0$. Then the implemented admissible policy satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bsdegap109}
 0\le Y_0^\pi-\inf_aY_0^a
 \le e^{L_yT}(\eta_-+\eta_+)
       +\int_0^T e^{L_ys}\{e_-(s)+e_+(s)\}\,ds.
\end{equation}
BSDE solvability alone does not imply the existence of an optimizing control. The infimum is sufficient for this certificate; compactness and continuity of the control-to-solution map are separate optimal-control assumptions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the forward process, the Brownian isometry and maximal inequality bound squared maximal differences of successive Picard iterates on a short interval by a fixed Lipschitz constant times the interval length and the preceding squared difference. Choose the interval so that this coefficient is smaller than one. Completeness yields a strong adapted fixed point, and the identical difference estimate proves uniqueness. Concatenating finitely many intervals covers $[0,T]$. Bounded coefficients give finite second moments. The argument uses the stipulated admissible control process; it does not establish well-posedness for a feedback whose closed-loop coefficients violate those conditions.

Fix this forward process. For a trial pair $(y,z)$ in the weighted square-integrable space, Brownian martingale representation of
$g(X_T)+\int_0^T f(s,X_s,y_s,z_s,a_s)\,ds$
defines the linear backward solution $(Y,Z)$ with that prescribed driver. For differences between the outputs of two trial pairs, It\^o's formula to $e^{ks}|\Delta Y_s|^2$, the common terminal value and expectation give
\begin{align*}
 &\E\int_0^T e^{ks}\{k|\Delta Y_s|^2+\|\Delta Z_s\|^2\}\,ds\\
 &\hspace{8mm}\le2\E\int_0^T e^{ks}|\Delta Y_s|
       (L_y|\Delta y_s|+L_z\|\Delta z_s\|)\,ds.
\end{align*}
Young's inequality bounds the integrand on the right by
$(k/2)|\Delta Y_s|^2+(4L_y^2/k)|\Delta y_s|^2+(4L_z^2/k)\|\Delta z_s\|^2$.
In the squared norm
$\E\int_0^Te^{ks}\{(k/2)|Y_s|^2+\|Z_s\|^2\}\,ds$,
the map is a contraction for sufficiently large $k$, with squared-norm factor at most
$\max\{8L_y^2/k^2,4L_z^2/k\}<1$.
This complete weighted space has a norm equivalent to the usual finite-horizon one. Banach iteration gives existence and uniqueness. Applying Brownian maximal estimates to the backward equation gives $\E\sup_{s\le T}|Y_s|^2<\infty$. No general-martingale representation or fully coupled forward equation has been inserted into this argument.

For verification, fix any admissible action process and apply It\^o's formula to $u(s,X_s)$. Put $\Delta Y=Y^a-u(s,X_s)$ and $\Delta Z=Z^a-z_u(s,X_s,a_s)$. Subtracting the equations yields a linear BSDE with terminal error $g(X_T)-u(T,X_T)$ and driver
\[
 A_s\Delta Y_s+\vartheta_s^{\mathsf T}\Delta Z_s+\mathcal H^{a_s}u(s,X_s),
 \qquad |A_s|\le L_y,\quad\|\vartheta_s\|\le L_z.
\]
Measurable divided differences first change $y$ at fixed $z$, then change $z$; set the relevant coefficient to zero when a denominator vanishes. The Lipschitz bounds imply the displayed coefficient bounds. Bounded $\vartheta$ satisfies Novikov's condition. Under the measure whose density is
$\exp\{\int_0^T\vartheta\,dW-\tfrac12\int_0^T\|\vartheta\|^2ds\}$,
$W-\int\vartheta ds$ is Brownian. Multiplication by $\exp(\int_0^sA_vdv)$ and expectation give
\begin{align*}
 \Delta Y_0=\E^{\vartheta}\!\bigg[&
 e^{\int_0^TA_vdv}(g-u(T))(X_T)\\
 &+\int_0^T e^{\int_0^sA_vdv}\mathcal H^{a_s}u(s,X_s)\,ds\bigg].
\end{align*}
Here is the integrability step. The bounded drift has an exponential density with all finite moments. Cauchy--Schwarz transfers the $L^2(P)$ supremum of $\Delta Y$ and the $L^2(P)$ square-root quadratic variation of $\Delta Z$ to $L^1(P^{\vartheta})$. After multiplication by the bounded finite-horizon factor, the localized stochastic integral is therefore an $H^1(P^{\vartheta})$ martingale. The terminal and residual terms are dominated by their bounded or integrable deterministic envelopes. The localized identity consequently passes to expectation and the limit, rather than assuming the stochastic integral has mean zero without an integrability check.

The multiplier is positive and at most $e^{L_ys}$. The all-action lower residual and terminal lower error give
\[
 Y_0^a\ge u(0,x)-e^{L_yT}\eta_- -\int_0^Te^{L_ys}e_-(s)\,ds
\]
for every admissible control. The policy-specific upper assumptions give the analogous upper bound for $Y_0^\pi$. Take the infimum in the first inequality and subtract. This proves \eqref{eq:bsdegap109}; admissibility of $\pi$ proves nonnegative loss.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Research interface, not objective substitution.}
An NBO critic and actor for this declared problem must be checked against the original driver, the full graph $z_u=\sigma^{\mathsf T}\nabla u$, the all-action versus implemented-action residuals, and every terminal or stopping-boundary discrepancy. The proposition does not prove uniform approximation by a neural architecture, convergence of a training optimizer, discretization consistency, or a contraction for a fully coupled NDU FBSDE. A latent-preference application would first establish its Markov augmentation, information filtration, driver regularity and admissible policy class. The same economic error accounting can then be developed for that problem; investment-catalogue results cannot be transferred to it by renaming a value function.
