\subsection{Constructive continuation learning}\label{sec:r25constructive}
The rank-basin bound in Section~\ref{sec:r24learning} applies to a general
warm start. A cold start can instead be chosen to satisfy a global invariant.
This gives a finite learning budget without assuming that the first critic
is already close to its evaluation target. The result concerns the same
trainable square-activation critic; it is not a fixed-dictionary argument.
Identity-based learning of positive-definite maps has a substantial antecedent
\citep{r25bartlett2018}. We give the calculation for the tied Gram factor used
here, rather than transfer a convergence theorem for a different network.

\begin{proposition}[An isotropic cold start]\label{prop:r25isotropic}
Let $0<m\leq L$, $mI\preceq M\preceq LI$, and $O'O=I$ for
$O\in\mathbb R^{k\times d}$, $k\geq d$. Choose $0<c\leq m$,
$W_0=\sqrt c\,O$, and $0<\alpha\leq(4L)^{-1}$. Under the hidden-weight
update~\eqref{eq:r24update}, put $C_j=W_j'W_j$ and $\rho=1-2\alpha c$.
Then, for every $j\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
 cI\preceq C_j\preceq C_{j+1}\preceq M,\qquad
 \|C_j-M\|_F\leq\rho^j\|cI-M\|_F.
 \label{eq:r25global}
\end{equation}
In particular, the least singular value never falls below $\sqrt c$.
For a target $e_*>0$, it suffices to take
\begin{equation}
 N=\max\left\{0,\left\lceil
       \frac{\log(\|cI-M\|_F/e_*)}{-\log\rho}
       \right\rceil\right\},
 \label{eq:r25budget}
\end{equation}
where $N=0$ when the initial residual is at most $e_*$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$C_0=cI$ commutes with $M$. The Gram update is
$C_{j+1}=[I+\alpha(M-C_j)]C_j[I+\alpha(M-C_j)]$, so commutation
persists. For a common eigenvector, write $r$ for the eigenvalue of $C_j$
and $\lambda$ for that of $M$. The next eigenvalue is
$r^+=r[1+\alpha(\lambda-r)]^2$. If $c\leq r\leq\lambda$, then
$r^+\geq r$. Moreover, with $u=\sqrt{r/\lambda}$,
$u[1+\alpha\lambda(1-u^2)]\leq1$: this function is increasing on
$[0,1]$ and equals one at its right endpoint. Finally,
\[
 \lambda-r^+=(\lambda-r)
       [1-2\alpha r-\alpha^2r(\lambda-r)]
       \leq\rho(\lambda-r).
\]
Induction and the Frobenius norm in the common orthonormal eigenbasis give
\eqref{eq:r25global}. Taking logarithms proves the budget.
\end{proof}

This initialization does not require the eigenvectors or a square root of
$M$. A dyadically scaled signed permutation supplies $W_0$ exactly from a
positive lower bound on $m$. A row-sum bound supplies $L$. Every hidden
weight is subsequently updated. Isometric orientations are symmetries of
the population problem, not independent draws of scientific evidence.
The guarantee holds for all such orientations; it does not concern an
arbitrary nonisotropic random start or a general nonlinear neural network.

\subsection{A finite backward NBO construction}\label{sec:r25backward}
A second issue is whether the fitted future remains the future of the returned
policy. In a finite-horizon additive problem, the order of construction can
remove that discrepancy. Start at the terminal date and finalize gains in
reverse chronological order. At date $t$, evaluate the already finalized
future to obtain $P_{t+1}$, train its continuation, solve the current actor
problem, and leave all later gains unchanged. Thus $P_{t+1}$ is an own-policy
target throughout, although it need not be an optimal continuation.

Let $\overline J_{\rm ref}>0$ bound the value of a specified feasible reference
policy on the initial domain. The zero-adjustment policy is sufficient in
the capital economy. Given an ideal-policy allowance $\varepsilon_0>0$, set
$\bar\eta=\varepsilon_0/\overline J_{\rm ref}$. At each nonterminal
continuation date, choose $m_{t+1}I\preceq dP_{t+1}\preceq L_{t+1}I$.
With $a_t\geq\|A_t\|_2$ and $b_t\geq\|B_t\|_2$, define
\begin{equation}
 \chi_t=\beta b_t a_t
       \left(1+\frac{\beta b_t^2L_{t+1}}{d r_t}\right).
 \label{eq:r25chi}
\end{equation}
For $\chi_t>0$, train to a positive Gram tolerance no larger than
\begin{equation}
 e_{t+1}^{\rm req}
       =\frac{d\sqrt{\bar\eta q_t r_t}}{\chi_t}.
 \label{eq:r25allocation}
\end{equation}
The terminal continuation is used exactly. An uncoupled date, $\chi_t=0$,
requires no continuation accuracy for its actor decision.

\begin{theorem}[Finite construction of an accurate neural policy]
\label{thm:r25finitepolicy}
In the capital economy of Section~\ref{sec:r23policy}, suppose the coefficient
bounds above are valid and the reference value is finite on the initial
domain. Train each nonterminal continuation by
Proposition~\ref{prop:r25isotropic} to~\eqref{eq:r25allocation}, and solve
its actor problem exactly. The resulting backward NBO policy is constructed
in finitely many hidden-weight updates and satisfies
\begin{equation}
 0\leq J_0^{\rm NBO}(x)-J_0^*(x)\leq\varepsilon_0
 \quad\text{on the initial domain}.
 \label{eq:r25policy}
\end{equation}
The comparison includes all adapted finite-cost policies and all real vector
actions. The update budget is the sum of~\eqref{eq:r25budget} over the
constructed targets, with $T$ actor solves and $T$ own-policy matrix updates.
No optimal-policy labels or unresolved outer policy-iteration limit are
required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof of the target invariant and all error terms is in
Supplement~\ref{sec:r25proofs}. Its central inequality is
\[
 \|D_t\|_F\leq\chi_t\|W_{t+1}'W_{t+1}-dP_{t+1}\|_F/d
                  \leq\sqrt{\bar\eta q_t r_t}.
\]
The coercive comparison yields $J^{\rm NBO}\leq(1+\bar\eta)J^*$.
Since $J^*\leq J^{\rm ref}$, the policy gap is at most
$\bar\eta J^{\rm ref}\leq\varepsilon_0$. Each own-policy target is
positive definite before the terminal date, because its stage cost is.
There are finitely many dates, and every inner budget is finite.
\end{proof}

The statement is a constructive, real-arithmetic guarantee for the trained
factor in a declared economic class. The conventional quadratic and the
Riccati recursion receive the same population information. The theorem does
not establish a cost advantage over those procedures. Its contribution is
the complete implication from a specified initialization and finite neural
training to an unrestricted policy-loss target. Finite-precision candidates
are accepted only after the independent outward own-policy check, including
the implementation allowance. A failed check is not repaired by appealing
to the real-arithmetic iteration count.
