\section*{Response to the Referee}
We thank the referee for distinguishing reproducibility, mathematical validity,
economic scope, and the incremental contribution of a neural method. This
revision responds to the R21 advisory report at commit
\begin{center}\small\texttt{281e57b18a2d88de68d2219da1a7194e90570d13}\end{center}
It incorporates the subsequent full-policy and learning derivations already present in the
repository. The title, Neural Bellman Operators, the controlled-economy
problem, all original economic applications, and the adverse numerical
findings are retained. We do not adopt a change of topic or replace the
original article by a stand-alone verification note.

The principal addition is a constructive learning-to-policy theorem. An
isotropic, explicitly available initialization yields a finite hidden-weight
training bound without the earlier local rank-basin assumption. A reverse-time
NBO construction makes each training target the actual future of the returned
policy. A feasible reference policy supplies an a priori error scale, so the
algorithm attains a prescribed lifetime policy loss after finitely many
updates in the declared capital class. This is a theorem about a trained
neural factor, not a fixed-feature regression or a certificate that merely
assumes a small final gradient. The full proof retains the distinction between
real arithmetic and an independently verified finite-precision candidate.

The revision also incorporates the existing all-state, full-vector policy
experiment and the symmetric residual-energy refinement. The new construction
protocol is committed before execution, and all declared cases, work accounts,
and final verification records are preserved. Neural and structural procedures
receive the same population information. The structural method remains
cheaper in the new execution; this comparison is not suppressed. A finite
neural construction theorem, accurate returned policies, and total-work
superiority are three different claims.

The article, complete technical supplement, economic applications, prior
numerical evidence, and this response are separate native Econometrica
manuscripts with a common reference system. All old source files remain
unchanged. Content relocated by the prior integration is reproduced in full,
not deleted. The new preservation audit checks the inherited source closure
and every inherited label. The earlier native-build failure is corrected by
displaying two long mathematical expressions, without weakening the overflow
checks or altering the journal class.

\section*{Major comments}
\paragraph*{B1. Incremental value relative to stronger alternatives.}
The referee is correct about the R21 changing-future frontier: all eight
cells retain the higher cost of adaptive NBO relative to fresh NBO and the
lower cost of cached SAA. The Prior Numerical Evidence companion reproduces
those results. We do not rename them as successful amortization evidence.
The new theoretical contribution is an explicit sufficient training budget
for a jointly trained factor and a completed policy construction. The new
experiment includes a structural recursion with no artificial information
restriction. Its lower cost is reported next to NBO in
Table~\ref{tab:r25construction}. This establishes the cost of the constructive
neural procedure in the tested class, rather than presuming neural dominance.
The original economic question and method remain unchanged.

\paragraph*{B2. Economic response bands and neural necessity.}
The article now separates the policy supplying an economic response from the
representation needed to compute it. The retained response bands use the
saved candidate actions and independent economic error bounds. The full-policy
study reoptimizes future gains after technology and valuation changes; its
bands are therefore not effects of a supplied scalar continuation mislabeled
as equilibrium responses. Conventional and structural response bands remain
visible. Neither a signed band nor a sharper residual certificate is offered
as evidence that a neural representation was necessary.

\paragraph*{B3. Executing the composition account.}
Section~\ref{sec:r23policy} establishes and executes an all-state coercive
policy certificate. Its local opportunity is the full-vector quadratic
advantage against the returned policy's own value. It covers the whole
state space, including states not visited by the candidate, and compares
with every adapted finite-cost policy. Section~\ref{sec:r25backward} adds a
finite algorithm that reaches the sufficient local allowances without an
unresolved outer policy-iteration limit. Supplement~\ref{sec:r25proofs}
identifies the local errors, continuation weights, coverage, comparison
class, model-transfer convention, and implementation allowance separately.
The zero class and transfer terms apply to the declared discrete-time
instance, not to every original nonlinear diffusion application.

\paragraph*{B4. Horizon, innovation support, and action dimension.}
The integrated capital study has 24 reoptimized dates, continuous correlated
Gaussian innovations, and ten or fifty real-valued vector controls. Its
independent verifier uses coefficient inequalities, not enumeration of a
finite shock tree or an action catalogue. The construction study uses this
same economy and target. These changes directly address the limitations of
the earlier four-date, finite-support scalar experiment, which remains in
the record with its original interpretation. They do not turn a structured
quadratic laboratory into an empirically calibrated nonlinear economy.

\paragraph*{B5. A result about the trained representation.}
The trainable-factor implication in Proposition~\ref{prop:r23factor} and the
finite local learning result in Proposition~\ref{prop:r24learning} are now
completed by Proposition~\ref{prop:r25isotropic} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:r25finitepolicy}. Every hidden coefficient is updated.
The cold start is constructed from coercivity and an isometry rather than
from optimal-policy labels, target eigenvectors, or a fitted fixed dictionary.
A finite update count leads to the prescribed full-policy target. The proof
is explicit about its square-activation structure and its initialization;
it is not claimed for arbitrary neural architectures. Prior identity-based
learning results are acknowledged rather than appropriated as a novel
principle.

\paragraph*{B6. Reliability and the meaning of the randomizer.}
The inherited full-policy study enumerates its registered finite randomizer;
its exact probabilities and expected capped operation counts refer only to
that support. The new construction theorem holds deterministically for all
isotropic orientations and admissible positive scales under its stated
coefficient premises. The new execution is a complete deterministic design,
not a population confidence study. Isometric orientations are explicitly
identified as symmetries, not independent fits. The two machine executions
have distinct clocks and are not treated as independent draws of training
randomness. No population reliability claim is inferred from a few streams.

\paragraph*{B7. The original economic applications.}
The nonlinear capital problem, recursive utility, endogenous preferences,
temporal selves, and strategic applications remain in the complete Economic
Applications companion and retain their original main-text connections. The
new additive capital construction does not establish their nonlinear
transfer or equilibrium premises by analogy. It strengthens the same NBO
learning-to-decision pipeline at a fully worked policy instance. We preserve
the applications and their mathematical scope rather than deleting them to
obtain an artificially narrower paper.

\paragraph*{B8. Manuscript organization and reading burden.}
The main article is organized around the NBO economic target, continuation
learning, full-policy verification, finite construction, and the decisive
numerical accounts. Full prior numerical sections appear in the Prior
Numerical Evidence companion; full economic applications and technical
records have their own companions. The journal-native class, author-year
citations, theorem structure, and synchronized references are retained.
Historical sources and labels are audited. The proposed wholesale deletion
or change of topic is not adopted; the reading hierarchy instead distinguishes
current argument from the complete supporting and historical material.

\paragraph*{B9. Constructed future policies and economic interpretation.}
The earlier future-change catalogue remains a specified-future comparison.
In the integrated capital study and the new backward construction, future
controls are reoptimized at every date for each declared regime. Training
targets are evaluated under that returned future, not under an externally
fixed candidate or the optimal future. The tracking economy remains a
transparent constructed instance. The revision does not describe its
technology and valuation experiments as estimates of industry parameters or
as calibrated welfare predictions.

\section*{Requested revisions}
\paragraph*{M1. A coherent NBO contribution.}
We retain the original topic and strengthen the neural method itself: a
specified initialization, finite hidden-weight updates, a finalized-future
invariant, and a full-policy accuracy theorem now form one construction.
The certificate remains method-neutral, and the structural comparator is
not removed. This is different from relabeling a verification package as a
neural advantage. Suitability for the journal remains a scientific judgment
for the next referee, not a conclusion of the release audit.

\paragraph*{M2. Every term in the policy certificate.}
Against the own continuation, the full-action error is
\[x'D_t'H_t^{-1}D_tx.\]
The domain is all of $\mathbb R^d$; the reported absolute loss is uniform on
the declared initial ball. Discount weights are powers of $\beta$.
The comparison class is already the unrestricted adapted finite-cost class,
and the declared model is discrete-time, so neither a neural policy-class
gap nor a diffusion-discretization term is hidden in a reported zero.
The relative implementation error is bounded independently and added at
stopping. Supplement~\ref{sec:r25proofs} gives the complete account.

\paragraph*{M3. Independent verification beyond a finite tree.}
The Gaussian innovation law has infinite support. Coefficient-level own-policy
evaluation and the coercive inequality cover every date and state. The actor
has ten or fifty coordinates and is checked over the full real action space.
The verifier receives no Riccati optimum. It encloses policy evaluation,
rounding, action residuals and implementation error directly. The old
finite-catalogue results remain separately labeled.

\paragraph*{M4. Prospective estimands and chronology.}
The new source and protocol were frozen at commit
\begin{center}\small\texttt{9bf608d5154dfb61ff0ab209c2b66d1f8dbcc148}\end{center}
before the first construction execution. The target is deterministic success and complete
work for every registered dimension, regime and scale. No unregistered rescue,
case dropping, seed extension or tolerance relaxation is used. Independent
machine execution is labeled as such, not as a new population sample. The
repository retains the previous finite-randomizer estimands unchanged.

\paragraph*{M5. Reuse, refresh, and stopping.}
The earlier adaptive frontier is retained. The symmetric residual-energy
refinement is explicitly a separate replay, not a new historical first-success
clock. The new construction uses a prespecified cold-start invariant and
error allocation instead of choosing a stopping rule after seeing the costs.
Its future target is finalized before training, so target drift cannot be
silently discarded. The revision does not claim to have found a generally
least-cost refresh policy; it supplies a constructive alternative with a
complete bound and a measured bill.

\paragraph*{M6. Information parity and complete work.}
The neural factor and structural procedure receive the same exact-population
model. The structural procedure may exploit the model fully. The new account
charges initialization, the reference-policy evaluation, all hidden updates,
Gram checks, actor solves, final outward verification, hashing, compression,
and durable attempted-service writes. Service-ledger and loop overhead are
reported separately; imports and environment setup are not attributed to a
particular method. Operation counts, explicit array bytes, saved candidate
bytes and process-wide memory are distinguished. There are zero simulated
transitions in this population-information study. All stronger comparators
from the earlier nonlinear studies remain in the evidence companion.

\paragraph*{M7. Verifier, candidate, and neural generator.}
The paper distinguishes an error certificate, an accurate returned policy,
and the work required to construct that policy. The new initialization and
training theorem concerns the neural generator. The coercive and
residual-energy bounds concern verification. The table concerns complete
measured work. No improvement in one category is used as an unproved result
in another.

\paragraph*{M8. Fixed versus reoptimized futures.}
Both descriptions now appear with their correct targets. The earlier catalogue
has supplied future policies. The full-policy and constructive studies return
reoptimized gain sequences. In the backward construction, own-policy targets
remain unchanged because later gains are finalized; this is not the same as
holding economic future controls externally fixed.

\paragraph*{M9. A stable publication hierarchy.}
The native main article contains the current argument. The supplement contains
complete proofs and computational records; the applications companion retains
the full economic development; the prior-evidence companion retains the full
historical comparisons. Original introductions, conclusions and responses
remain archived. The preservation check verifies that the revision changes
none of the inherited source bytes and loses none of their labels.

\paragraph*{M10. Reproducibility and adverse evidence.}
The revision retains source freezing, candidate hashes, full attempted-service
ledgers, independent certificate replays and failed checks. The new construction
has its own source commitment and immutable execution identity. The native
publication is built and checked without modifying the journal class or
accepting unresolved references. These audits establish what was executed
and reproduced; they are not a substitute for evaluating the paper's economic
and methodological contribution.
