\section{Finite Neural Construction: Complete Accounts}\label{sec:r25proofs}
\subsection{The finalized-future invariant}
At the terminal date, $P_T=Q_T$. Suppose all gains after date $t$ have been
finalized, and $P_{t+1}$ is their own value coefficient. Train
$\widehat P_{t+1}=W_{t+1}'W_{t+1}/d$ against $dP_{t+1}$, and set
\[
 K_t=(R_t+\beta B_t'\widehat P_{t+1}B_t)^{-1}
                       \beta B_t'\widehat P_{t+1}A_t.
\]
The inverse exists because $R_t\succeq r_tI\succ0$. Evaluate the supplied
current gain with the fixed future using~\eqref{eq:r23evaluation}. This
produces the own value of the enlarged finalized policy. No subsequent
step changes a finalized gain. Backward induction therefore gives the
returned policy's own $P_{t+1}$ at every training date. The mathematical
target drift in~\eqref{eq:r24drift} is zero, not an omitted error term.
In a rounded computation the nominal target and exact own value may differ;
the independent final interval evaluation encloses that difference.

\subsection{Residual and full-policy bound}
Proposition~\ref{prop:r25isotropic} gives
$0\preceq\widehat P_{t+1}\preceq P_{t+1}\preceq L_{t+1}I/d$.
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
 \|K_t\|_2&\leq\frac{\beta b_tL_{t+1}a_t}{d r_t},\\
 \|A_t-B_tK_t\|_2&\leq a_t
            \left(1+\frac{\beta b_t^2L_{t+1}}{d r_t}\right).
\end{align*}
The exact actor has $Z_t=0$. Subtracting its fitted and own-policy normal
equations gives
\[
 D_t=\beta B_t'(\widehat P_{t+1}-P_{t+1})(A_t-B_tK_t).
\]
Taking norms proves the displayed bound in
Theorem~\ref{thm:r25finitepolicy}. At the last action date the exact terminal
continuation makes $D_{T-1}=0$. If $\chi_t=0$, either the action has no
continuation channel or the uncontrolled next-state mean is zero; the same
identity gives $D_t=0$ directly. The all-state advantage bound follows from
$H_t\succeq r_tI$ and $Q_t\succeq q_tI$. Telescoping along an arbitrary
adapted finite-cost comparison policy, as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:r23coercive}, proves
$J^{\rm NBO}\leq(1+\bar\eta)J^*$. For an infimum not attained, apply the
comparison to an infimizing sequence; attainment is not needed. Thus
\[
 J^{\rm NBO}-J^*\leq\bar\eta J^*
             \leq\bar\eta J^{\rm ref}\leq\varepsilon_0.
\]
The reference policy provides an a priori scale and is not an optimal-policy
oracle. With a finite horizon, finite coefficients, coercive quadratic stage
costs and finite innovation covariance, its value is finite on every bounded
initial domain.

\subsection{Coverage, transfer, and implementation}
The six policy-account terms can now be read directly. The local full-action
opportunity is the quadratic form $x'D_t'H_t^{-1}D_tx$ against the finalized
future. The coefficient inequalities cover the whole of $\mathbb R^d$;
there is no visited-state substitution. The continuation weights are the
specified powers of $\beta$. The comparison is already over all adapted
finite-cost policies, so no neural policy-class approximation term enters
this declared instance. The model is discrete-time by definition, so no
continuous-time discretization error enters this instance either. These
zeros do not transfer to the original nonlinear diffusion applications.
Finally, the physical execution account remains the separately declared
relative action-error class of~\eqref{eq:r23stop}.

The implemented design assigns half the total tolerance to the ideal
reference-policy allocation and halves each sufficient Gram tolerance again.
These margins leave room for the explicitly computed implementation bound;
they are not treated as numerical rounding certificates. The accepted policy
must satisfy the full outward stopping inequality under its stored binary
coefficients. Both the proposal matrices and every final unsuccessful check
are retained.

\subsection{Arithmetic and complexity}
The update theorem is about real arithmetic. An exact implementation exists
for rational model coefficients: choose a rational scaled isometry, rational
step sizes strictly below the displayed upper bounds, and rational Gram
thresholds strictly below the sufficient tolerances. Squared Frobenius
residuals can then be compared exactly. Rational linear solves and own-policy
matrix updates preserve rationality. This supplies a finite construction,
but not a bound on fixed-precision wall time or a polynomial bit-complexity
claim. In particular, growth of rational numerators and denominators must
not be omitted from an implementation bill.

The executed double-precision procedure is instead an approximate candidate
generator with a fixed budget. It computes a Gram check every eight updates
and at the cap. The stored-target residual uses outward matrix products.
The final policy certificate independently reevaluates the returned future,
which encloses both target-evaluation rounding and actor-solve error.
Increasing precision would be a distinct, charged algorithmic choice; it is
not silently invoked when a frozen budget is exhausted.

Initialization, reference evaluation, spectral bounds, all updates, residual
checks, actor solves, final verification and durable candidate writes belong
to complete work. A factor step uses dense matrix products, whereas the
quadratic structural comparator can bypass factor training entirely. The
new result is therefore an explicit neural learning-to-policy guarantee,
not a representation-separation or simulation lower-bound theorem.
