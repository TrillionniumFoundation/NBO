\section{Prospective accuracy and complete economic policies}\label{sec:study62}
The original-optimum question is now asked before execution, for every declared generator and economic cell. The previous service catalogue stopped on an initial-law gain relative to zero investment. That remains a meaningful adoption question, but it is not a certificate against the Bellman optimum. The present experiment makes the latter the common accuracy contract and separately evaluates the actual cost of each complete returned policy.

\subsection{Design and actual returned objects}
The catalogue has five cells: two-state economies with horizons two and three, a four-state economy with horizon three, an eight-state economy with horizon two, and the nested two-state, two-control economy with horizon two. The original scalar law and discount $15/16$ are unchanged. The last cell implements both controls at every date, with the capacity simplex, cross-action cost and additional continuous innovation stated in Section~\ref{sec:accuracy62}. It is not the earlier isolated two-control action query.

For each cell, the common comparator constructs nodal Bellman lower and upper bounds from primitive costs and transitions. Each fitted generator constructs its own future labels using 512 training states per date. The ReLU fit has 32 learned hidden units and 120 optimization iterations; the quadratic fit uses the same number of states. Four seeds, 6201--6204, are fixed before production. Each fitted continuation supplies proposals on the same 32-leaf state partition. The scalar proposal uses the retained exact root-free action recovery. The two-control proposal uses conventional sequential quadratic programming from four fixed starts, followed by downward lattice rounding and a feasibility check. Its approximate objective and termination flag are not treated as a certificate. The original-optimum verifier supplies the postcheck for both types of proposals.

The finest proposed state grids have $n=64$ in two states, $n=16$ in four states and $n=4$ in eight states. Every preceding declared rung is executed and retained. Scalar and two-control action meshes have sixteen subdivisions, except the eight-state cell, which has eight. Every continuous innovation component has four bins, except that cell, which has two. No extra rung is added after a target fails. The five common tolerances are $1/2$, $1/4$, $1/8$, $1/16$ and $1/32$.

At each nodal state we preserve both the pure fitted proposal and the proposal augmented by the same common action family. The latter chooses the smaller verified original-continuation upper endpoint, retaining the common candidate on a tie. The nodal actions are then interpolated barycentrically, observed at forty bits and rounded downward at twenty action bits. These are explicitly new implementable policies. We do not call this operation revalidation of an unchanged discontinuous actor. The preceding fixed-policy study remains the place where the earlier actors are verified without changing them.

\subsection{Matched all-state accuracy}
Theorem~\ref{thm:policy62} is applied to every actual returned actor. Its allowance includes the entire continuous-action optimum, off-grid state error, continuous innovation integration, arithmetic, state acquisition, action rounding and all remaining dates. Neither an initial-state average nor a favorable cost difference can substitute for the maximum over states and dates in the stopping comparison.

\subsection{Direct policy costs and the adoption decision}
All final-rung actors are evaluated under the original uniform initial law and continuous innovation laws on 65,536 fresh common paths per cell. Forty-bit bins enclose each continuous state and innovation realization, and outward propagation encloses the cost of the implemented acquired policy. The path endpoints, not a fitted value at the initial state, enter the bounded empirical-Bernstein calculation. A single error budget of $1/100$ covers the prespecified finite family across all five cells, seeds and variants. Repeated seeds define different fitted objects; common paths induce dependence between their comparisons, which is allowed by the simultaneous coverage construction. Exact policy identities are reported as identities.

For an installation fee $\tau\ge0$, let $[l,u]$ be the simultaneous interval for zero-policy cost minus the proposed controller's cost. Adoption has net gain in $[l-\tau,u-\tau]$. Every fee below a positive $l$ is supported as beneficial; every fee above $u$ is ruled out; the remaining range is unresolved. The same calculation applies to replacement of an installed pure fitted controller by its augmented controller using the separately recorded paired contrast. These are actual complete-policy decisions in the normalized economy. The intervals do not estimate an institutional installation charge, and a gain over zero investment does not establish a gain over the common Bellman comparator.

\subsection{Complete work at a common economic contract}
The cost of verification is part of the method. The reference lower and upper arrays are computed from primitives for every rung. The fitting/proposal service, actor transfer, every selected-action verification and serialization are then added. To avoid inventing unobserved marginal costs, each method is charged the entire shared reference and the entire shared inference service. Pure and augmented policies are verified together; each is conservatively charged that joint work. Cold compilation is included in the complete-release total and separately identified in its receipt.

\paragraph{Closing the measured production boundary.}
The frozen per-method release field sums measured core components. The publication audit found that common-certificate bookkeeping, some output records and the final actor-file serialization were outside those individual clocks. We preserve the field unchanged as a component sum, rather than calling it an isolated complete runtime. The recorded outer production step spans 466 seconds at whole-second timestamp resolution; a transparent two-second reserve gives a 468-second accounting envelope. Subtracting the nonoverlapping component clocks leaves a shared unallocated residual. The table charges that entire residual to every method, in addition to its own component sum. These are conservative complete-production charges, not separately observed method runtimes or statistical timing confidence bounds. Remote publication, document production and replay have their own receipts. This correction changes neither a scientific result nor a measured service clock.


For each tolerance, a method is eligible precisely when its recorded actual-policy certificate attains that tolerance. Minimizing the conservative complete-production charge among eligible methods defines the finite release frontier. This answers which executed complete catalogue supplies a certified controller at the stated contract. It does not estimate the minimum work of a sequential algorithm that was never run. The recorded first-crossing verification prefixes remain separate from this complete-release quantity, since the present independent cost service evaluates the final catalogue jointly.

The component timings are descriptive for the recorded host and arithmetic engine; the shared-residual charge is an explicitly stated accounting convention. Processor frequency was not controlled, and hardware counters were unavailable. The exact native witness retains its rational operand and request/response diagnostics. The new interpolation kernel reports explicit array sizes and arithmetic allowances, but logical input/output bytes are not presented as measured memory-bus traffic. No worker repetition is counted as an additional independent economic observation.

\subsection{Reliability and interpretation}
The four seeds define a finite fitting catalogue, not a probability model for optimizer success in an unspecified future economy. A pure fitted actor that fails an original-optimum target remains a failure in that catalogue even when its augmented counterpart succeeds. The count therefore separates the ability of the fit to supply a sufficiently accurate policy from the ability of the common verified fallback to complete the economic contract.

This execution advances the original NBO objective from a conditional action witness to a full original-optimum account across state dimensions, horizons and a genuinely implemented two-control policy. The preceding scalar exact-search result retains its stated function-class and lattice assumptions. The new policy theorem is independent of those assumptions once a feasible nodal proposal has been supplied. This extends certification to other proposal generators without asserting a new exact solver for deep networks or arbitrary shock distributions. Earlier conventional wins, failed targets, adverse multi-action query timings and broader economic applications remain available under their original hypotheses.
