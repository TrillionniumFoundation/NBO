\subsection{Design}
Tasks are $(d,T)=(2,2),(4,4),(8,6)$. Each method/task/target combination is a separate from-primitives service. Targets $q=9/10$ and $49/50$ require ten- and two-percent expected-cost reductions relative to installed zero investment. The stopping statistic is the signed contrast $J(\pi)-qJ(0)$, not a ratio of plug-in mean estimates. Two resource stages use respectively 256 and 1,024 training states, 32 and 128 non-tensor leaves, and four and eight innovation bins. Actions are downward quantized at $1/4096$ using the lower capacity over the complete leaf. Partition geometry is chosen before the generator and is nested between stages.

Trained ReLU, quadratic and ExtraTrees fitted-value methods use their own future targets. The neural architecture and training are described in Section~\ref{sec:learned56}. ExtraTrees uses 16 trees, depth at most eight and minimum leaf size three; quadratic FVI fits a ridge-regularized quadratic. The original two-dimensional frontends are reconstructed at $N=8,16$, $K=4$ and $M=4$. Every method receives nine common robust candidate actions and one fitted proposal, as well as eight intervals covering the full continuous feasible action set. The new algebraic neural action solver is evaluated separately on saved critics; it was not substituted for the frozen experimental construction menu.

After one complete simultaneous sweep at a stage, inference uses cumulative looks of 4,096, 16,384 and 65,536 interval paths. It stops on the first covered target crossing, advances a stage at a strictly positive lower target contrast or exhausted look budget, and never executes a later stage after success. All computation, including failed stages and each method's own inference, is retained. Three isolated sequential processes repeat identical training and inference seeds, with rotated method order, a fixed available CPU core and one numerical-library thread. These measure reproducibility and clock variation, not a population of independently trained networks. Clock frequency is recorded where available but not controlled.

The trained ReLU proposal changes no deployed cell beyond the nine common actions in either cohort. The only tenth-proposal effect is one compiled-witness cell in the two-dimensional sensitivity task, repeated across targets and timing runs. These identities are checked explicitly rather than inferred from similar cost intervals.

The conventional generators receive the same acquired states, common candidate actions, signed reference information and economic stopping rule. The audit reconstructs the trained proposal and then removes the tenth candidate from the recorded gate to determine whether that proposal changes the selected action. This is a deterministic candidate-attribution diagnostic, not a fresh policy-cost experiment. Exact deployed-policy identities are checked across generators and across timing repetitions.

In the four- and eight-dimensional sensitivity tasks, the generators return policy-identical arrays within each target. In the two-dimensional task the compiled-witness return differs slightly, while the trained ReLU, quadratic, ExtraTrees and tensor-FVI returns coincide. Thus the policy improvement is not evidence that the neural generator dominates these alternatives. At the ten-percent target, quadratic FVI has the lowest recorded median complete-process work in each task. The neural backend supplies a genuinely trained nonlinear continuation and analytic integration, but those facilities must not be confused with an identified economic ranking.

The clocks include imports, primitive construction, critic fitting, acquired-policy preparation, verification, own inference, serialization and durable outputs. Method comparisons are within each task's controlled runner; clocks from different task runners are not a hardware-controlled scaling regression.
