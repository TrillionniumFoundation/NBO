\subsection{Positive interpolation and acquired actions}
The state mesh has $(n+1)^d$ vertices. Indices use row-major order, and a point on the upper face is owned by the last adjacent interpolation cell. All tensor weights are nonnegative. In the native kernel, multiplication by the power-of-two subdivision count and subtraction of the integer cell index precede the explicit tensor products. The binary64 format, nearest rounding mode, finite input arrays and state-domain membership are checked. The kernel is built without reassociation or floating-point contraction; neither a BLAS sum nor an unverified optimizer supplies the interpolated endpoint.

The arithmetic allowance expands each stored endpoint by the proved absolute summation bound. To enclose a transition rectangle, the algorithm evaluates its midpoint and adds the sum of its coordinate radii multiplied by the table's recorded Lipschitz constants. Those constants are obtained from outward adjacent differences. This enclosure applies to the possibly nonconvex numerical endpoint table; the separate second-order interpolation allowance concerns the true convex Bellman value. Confusing those two statements would omit a necessary error.

The actual policy first rounds a true state downward at forty bits, evaluates the positive interpolation of nodal actions at that acquired state, and rounds a lower numerical endpoint downward at twenty action bits. Nonnegative truncation removes a spurious negative endpoint at zero investment. Feasibility follows because each nodal vector is in the capacity simplex, capacity is affine, and downward state acquisition cannot increase capacity. The action error account includes state acquisition, the arithmetic lower expansion and action rounding. The path evaluator encloses this implemented rule, rather than evaluating an ideal infinite-precision actor and assigning its cost to a different controller.

The pure fitted nodal action is obtained by transferring the coarse exact or approximate proposal as a fraction of capacity, followed by downward quantum rounding and an exact dyadic capacity check. This transfer can change the original fitted-lattice optimum. The new theorem certifies the action actually transferred; it does not claim that this fine-grid nodal action remains the exact minimizer of the stored critic. Likewise, interpolation changes the old cell-constant actor. The earlier unchanged-actor revalidation remains a separate experiment with its own results.

\subsection{Separate continuous-action and innovation errors}
A lower Bellman bound covers the entire continuous capacity interval or simplex. It subtracts the second-order lattice allowance even when the best proposed nodal action is found exactly. The upper endpoint needs only a feasible action. The terminal function is evaluated directly on each transition enclosure, so no terminal state-interpolation allowance is charged in that operation.

For each innovation bin, the midpoint is its conditional mean under the original uniform law. Jensen's inequality and the proved semiconcavity supply two different directions of approximation. The lower calculation uses the former; the upper adds the conditional-variance allowance. With two independent components, the variances add and every Cartesian bin is included. The proof concerns the true Bellman continuation. No convexity of a fitted ReLU or of an arbitrary numerical endpoint array is assumed.

The nodal original-optimum bracket is not itself the complete policy bound. The latter adds the off-grid optimal-advantage allowance and the future policy error at every date. All lower arrays, selected-action upper arrays, exact rational constants, deployment allowances and recursions are deposited for every method, seed and rung. The common-augmented selector minimizes a certified upper endpoint for the original-optimum continuation. This is not the earlier directed incumbent-policy improvement gate: a tighter certificate does not establish statewise cost dominance over the common policy. Direct policy-cost contrasts are therefore retained.

\section{Combined resource and reliability account}\label{supp:resources62}
Let $w$ denote the number of scalar ridge and squared-hinge terms, $L$ the feasible action-lattice cardinality, and $B_c$ the input coefficient bit length. Combining the retained root-free witness bound with the new verification account gives the following conservative conditional work decomposition:
\begin{align*}
 W_{\rm complete}\le {}&W_{\rm fit}+W_{\rm transfer}+W_{\rm serialize}
                       +W_{\rm inference}+W_{\rm compile}\\
 &+O\!\left(TL_0\{w^2+w\log(w+1)+w\log(L+1)\}
                      \mathcal C_{\rm rat}(b)\right)\\
 &+O\!\left(T(n+1)^dK_Aq^s2^d d\right),
\end{align*}
where $L_0$ is the coarse proposal-leaf count and
\[
 b=O\!\left((w+1)\{B_c+\log(Q+1)+\log(L+1)+\log(w+2)\}\right).
\]
The scalar exact-search term applies only to the retained rational piecewise-quartic subclass. For several controls it is replaced by the actual constrained proposal cost, or by the product-lattice and shock-vertex costs of the exact retained extension when that extension is used. The new two-control production proposal uses SLSQP, not an unexecuted claim that exact subdivision has become fast.

For fixed dimension and horizon, the analytic state, action and innovation allowances are respectively of order $n^{-2}$, $A^{-2}$ and $q^{-2}$, plus the explicit deployment and arithmetic terms. Their constants are the stated Bellman regularity constants. Choosing each resolution sufficiently large therefore makes the original-optimum policy bound small for the common construction and its augmented policies. A pure fitted policy has the additional recorded proposal-loss term, which does not vanish simply by refining the verification mesh. The factors $(n+1)^d$, $K_A$, $q^s$ and $2^d$ remain in the computational account.

The native rational solver's stored diagnostics describe evaluated operands, candidate counts, exact sign queries, and serialized requests and responses. They do not measure every temporary denominator or internal normalization allocation. Similarly, the explicit interpolation-array lengths do not measure total bytes moved through hardware memory. Those unmeasured quantities are not assigned zero. The release records process peak resident memory for the full science process, not an independent peak-memory observation for each generator.

The finite four-seed catalogue permits an exact attained fraction within each declared task. It supplies no confidence interval for optimizer reliability on an unspecified population of economic models. Every source freeze precedes production, and every unsuccessful target remains in the record. The publication build replays arrays and inference arithmetic without fitting, drawing new paths, or replacing scientific service clocks.
