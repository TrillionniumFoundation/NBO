                                            1. INTRODUCTION
23                                                                                                23
       A continuation approximation matters because it changes an economic decision.
24                                                                                                24
     Its fitting error, the loss of the policy eventually implemented, and the resources
25                                                                                                25
     needed to verify that policy are different objects. This paper develops Neural
26                                                                                                26
     Bellman Operators to connect them. A continuation is constructed using its own
27                                                                                                27
     fitted future, feasible action witnesses are retained through implementation, and
28                                                                                                28
     a computable policy account determines what has been achieved for the original
29                                                                                                29
     economic problem.

5    of state coverage, action search and integration. A native envelope and its identical    5

6    affine–ReLU circuit are two encodings of one construction. This identity is useful for   6

9        The trained backend supplies a second, linked route. Learned nonlinear features      9

10   propose actions, while verification determines which proposals can be deployed. 10
11   In the investment model, uniform-innovation integration turns a one-hidden-layer         11

12   ReLU continuation into a rational piecewise-polynomial action objective. Exact           12

13   fitted minimization is therefore a finite calculation rather than a second unconverged   13

14   training problem. The fitted-witness transfer theorem separates center optimization      14

15   error from acquisition, continuation, continuous-action covering and numerical           15

16   verification errors. An exact witness removes the first of these errors, not all of them. 16
17       We develop two exact implementations of that witness. Lossless interval screening    17

18   excludes only lattice actions that cannot attain the minimum, and exact comparison       18

19   preserves the original smallest-index tie rule. Conditional transcript invariance then   19

20   preserves the acquired policy, its certificates and a prospective controller’s stop- 20
21   ping decisions. The additional finite-difference construction in Section 10 avoids       21

22   a favorable-separation premise. On each nonnegative scalar-action piece, the ob- 22
23   jective is a quartic with a nonnegative fourth-order coefficient. Its second forward     23

24   difference is nondecreasing. A concave prefix and a convex tail can consequently be      24

25   searched by rational sign comparisons, including flat objectives, adjacent ties and      25

26   signed cancellation. No isolated real root or unique surviving action is required.       26

27       The action dimension and the state dimension play different roles. The scalar        27

28   finite-difference result does not become a multivariate theorem by increasing the        28

29   number of state coordinates. We therefore give a separate exact integration formula      29

30   for finite mixtures of box-uniform shocks and a terminating rational subdivision         30

31   result on constrained action lattices. The latter retains the original lexicographic     31

32   witness, including when an exact fallback is needed. Its worst-case work and the         32

1    exponential dependence of the integration formula on the number of independent           1

2    uniform components are explicit. The two-control experiment implements this              2

3    extension with a shared capacity constraint and one or two shocks; it is distinct        3

4    from the complete scalar-action policy services.                                         4

5      The policy account is computed on locally split, non-tensor observation partitions. 5
6    A one-state residual and value cache encloses a discontinuous incumbent’s con- 6
7    tinuation. A signed sensitivity calculation for a smooth installed reference retains     7

12   intersected leaf, not by differentiating away its jumps. The separate sample-split       12

13   approximately-null calculation qualifies inferred nuisance structure and the remain- 13

15     A prospective service can stop at an economic target before it establishes a small     15

16   all-state optimality gap. We keep those contracts separate and connect them by a         16

17   direct Bellman calculation. Whole-state cells and complete continuous-action covers      17

18   give a lower bound for the original optimum; feasible upper candidates give an           18

19   implemented policy. Centering both bounds at the same reference value retains            19

20   pointwise cancellation before a width is formed. A new fixed-policy verification         20

21   applies that lower bound to the actual policies already returned by the prospective      21

22   services, without retraining, replacing an action or changing an initial distribution. 22
23   The convergence argument includes the fixed-cover stability property used by             23

24   the executable endpoint maps; mere inclusion of a true value is not mistaken for         24

25   stability under propagated errors.                                                       25

17       Related methods. Approximate and fitted policy iteration have established             17

18   approximation-error analyses (Munos, 2003, Munos and Szepesvári, 2008, Scher- 18
19   rer et al., 2015). Conservative and safe improvement relate a policy update to its        19

20   incumbent (Kakade and Langford, 2002, Pirotta et al., 2013, Metelli et al., 2021),        20

21   and tree-based fitted iteration is an important conventional alternative (Ernst et al.,   21

22   2005). The action-contrast development distinguishes fixed-policy residual trans- 22
23   port from state bisimulation metrics, reward transformations and value-equivalent         23

24   model classes (Ferns, Panangaden, and Precup, 2004, Ng, Harada, and Russell,              24

25   1999, Grimm, Barreto, Singh, and Silver, 2020). These are the inherited principles on     25

26   which the policy account builds.                                                          26

31   prefix/convex-tail pieces, with the original tie rule, explicit rational work and a       31

32   transfer to the same acquired economic policy. The paper’s contribution is the            32

6                                                                                              6

7      Neural Bellman Operators connect an own-future continuation to a feasible               7

8    action and an economic cost account. The constructive backend retains the original        8

9    Bellman-accuracy objective. The trained backend now has analytic innovation               9

10   integration and a terminating exact action-lattice search, while its proposals remain     10

11   subject to whole-cell true-advantage verification. These are complementary parts          11

12   of the same numerical method, not interchangeable claims about the name of a              12

13   function class.                                                                           13

14     The non-tensor cache and signed reference calculation turn the policy account           14

15   into an executable prospective service. Their proofs retain the cost of leaf member- 15
16   ship, critic evaluation, state dimension and horizon. The declared expected-cost          16

17   targets are attained in the sensitivity experiment, with genuine nonterminal action       17

18   changes and complete own-service clocks. Conventional generators share the same           18

19   verification treatment, and policy identities make their role observable. The finite ex- 19
20   periments identify successful returns and their costs; they do not establish universal    20

21   neural dominance or calibrated welfare effects.                                           21

23   a policy remain separate economic quantities. Keeping all three explicit permits a        23

24   decision maker to choose the appropriate contract without treating fitting accuracy,      24

25   certificate sharpness or a favorable timing observation as a substitute for the cost of   25

26   the implemented policy.                                                                   26
