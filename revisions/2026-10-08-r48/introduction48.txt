Three further results connect this construction to computation and economic implementation. First, a literal cone network repeats a scan over every state label whenever a Bellman query evaluates its future continuation. On a tensor cover, we compile this network once and answer an arbitrary continuous-state query from the cell's corner data. The compiler carries the original action witness and the original tie rule. Consequently the improvement in evaluation work does not come from changing the policy being evaluated. Classical separable distance transforms supply the nodal computation; the additional policy argument establishes the continuous off-grid identity and feasible witness realization. In the two-state construction this removes the redundant factor that makes dense cone evaluation scale as $N^6$ rather than $N^4$. It does not remove the state cover or give a capability unavailable to the identical native envelope.

Second, we make observation accuracy a priced economic input. A finite-bit sensor reports a state cell. Robust repair selects an action feasible throughout that cell. The policy theorem then gives an augmented loss account consisting of the full-information construction bound, an acquisition term decreasing geometrically in the number of bits, and a linear information charge. Exact neighboring-bit comparisons identify the precision minimizing this guarantee. This is an information-design result for the stated technology, not an assertion that minimizing a bound also minimizes the unknown actual loss.

Third, we evaluate actual constrained-policy cost differences, independently of their all-state regret certificates. The two installed controllers face common continuous innovations and the same observation technology. A prospective replacement charge turns simultaneous expected-cost intervals into a decision about whether either replacement recoups its cost. That question differs both from the sign of the policy difference and from exact equality. A complete matched construction study, an error-driven conventional comparator, and higher-dimensional nonlinear stress executions accompany the decision analysis. We charge compilation, adaptive pilots, unsuccessful resolutions, repair and output, and report the joint policy-comparison service separately. These distinctions permit a positive computational contribution to the original NBO program without confusing a sharper certificate, a cheaper representation, and a better economic policy.
