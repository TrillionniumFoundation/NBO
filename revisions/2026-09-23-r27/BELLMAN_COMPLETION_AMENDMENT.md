# Bellman completion amendment — after the frozen raw experiment

The raw unknown-solution inventory experiment has now been executed. Its unmodified policies and results are retained. Neural fitted Bellman iteration reaches the exact optimal policy in several small instances but its four-dimensional worst regret is about 0.3665; the exact tabular baseline is faster. This amendment is therefore explicitly post-diagnostic, not a prospective efficacy experiment and not a retuning of the frozen neural runs.

Add a representation-independent Bellman completion operator at tolerance epsilon=1/100. For each raw neural and polynomial policy, create a fresh model object and read only the frozen action table. Never read an optimal-value reference during completion. Sweep backward, evaluating all feasible actions against the already completed suffix policy. Retain the proposed action whenever its exact local Bellman deficit is at most eta=epsilon/(sum_{j=0}^{T-1}(99/100)^j); otherwise choose an exact greedy action. Save every local deficit and every changed action.

Independently compute the residual envelope B_T=0 and B_t=delta_t+beta max_a P_a B_{t+1} from the completed policy's exact value and local Bellman deficit. This provides a whole-state/restart-time regret certificate without access to V*. Freeze the completed policy and its residual certificate before checking it against a freshly generated exact optimal reference. The theorem is a finite-horizon Bellman-residual argument, not a claim to have invented policy iteration or dynamic programming.

Apply this operator without selection to all 34 retained candidate policies. Report the raw and completed frontiers separately, the changed-action fraction, completion cost, residual-check cost, exact-reference evaluation cost, and the full standalone pipeline. Charge neural fitting and compilation in full. Do not claim a neural speed advantage if tabular dynamic programming remains faster. This finite-state result does not close the original continuous-time current-state whole-domain target.
