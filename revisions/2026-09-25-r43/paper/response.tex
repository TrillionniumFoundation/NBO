\section{Summary of the substantive revision}
We agree that an exponential completeness theorem alone does not establish a practically useful numerical method. We also agree that exogenous aggregation cannot explain the controlled atomic maintenance evidence. R43 therefore adds two results that address the mathematical structure of controlled transitions directly.

First, relative to a strictly separated operating-optimal action, feasible departures are rare and implementation savings lie in a cone relative to operating regret. Eliminating the reference probability makes both factors in the remaining McCormick products proportional to the operating allowance. Reference-directed repair also changes only the rare nonreference probability mass. These facts give an explicit quadratic root-relaxation error bound as the allowance shrinks at fixed primitives. The proof identifies the action-gap and discount constants; it does not suppress them or claim a uniform polynomial algorithm.

Second, an observed covariate with invertible autonomous evolution labels finite controlled problems. The full Borel common-policy optimum disintegrates over these fibers. Validated fiber perturbations and complete finite solves yield convergent continuum certificates without smoothing atoms. The executed persistent-size economy has genuinely action-dependent condition transitions and a paired global continuum interval. It is not the original action-dependent continuous-condition model, and the article does not claim otherwise.

All 41 newly constructed endpoint objects pass independent reconstruction, covering 6,648 all-restart inequalities. All four continuum sums and four mutation rejections are retained. Both the eight controlled case specifications and source hashes were registered remotely before evaluation. A subsequent directed-arithmetic amendment is disclosed, with the original sources and partial results retained. The evidence is consequently described as predeclared cases with a disclosed amendment, not as an untouched code-frozen holdout. The full R42 article, supplement, response, computational results, and earlier history remain unchanged in the branch ancestry.

\section{Responses to the fourteen scientific findings}
\subsection*{F1: Theory and the continuous-state examples}
\textbf{New response.} The main section ``Controlled atomic models with observable covariates'' proves exact disintegration and validated partition convergence for action-dependent regime kernels. The proof treats all Borel common policies, constructs measurable near-optimal families through the finite lattice and repair, and lifts policies using a label observable in the current state. It never substitutes total-variation smoothing for an atomic transition. The new controlled continuum experiment executes this route with 17 independently checked finite fibers and four exact weighted sums. Its 16-cell interval is $[\RFiberLower,\RFiberUpper]$.

\textbf{Inherited response and remaining boundary.} R42 already executed the complete nonzero-error density-transfer budget and exact exogenous atomic aggregation. These remain intact. Neither the new observable-fiber assumption nor the inherited exogenous identities applies to the original action-dependent continuous-condition maintenance laws. The new theorem is a substantive controlled atomic extension, not a claim to have closed that particular model class.

\subsection*{F2: Severe repair constants and exponential completeness}
\textbf{New response.} The savings-cone lemma and rare-departure repair yield a quantitative $O(\varepsilon^2)$ root width at fixed strict operating-action gap and fixed $\beta<1$. This is an improvement over a tolerance-independent probability rectangle and a repair charge proportional only to the mixing fraction. The main theorem displays the full $E_D$, $E_S$, $D_C$, $\underline d$, and discount dependence. The supplement reports these constants even where they are numerically uninformative.

The three-orders-of-magnitude allowance study compares the same model and both relaxations. At $\varepsilon=10^{-4}$ the scaled width is at most \RScaleGap{}; the unscaled width is \RScaleUnscaled{}. No empirical slope is substituted for the theorem. Vanishing action gaps and high discount factors remain explicitly adverse, and generic completeness remains exponential. The inherited allowance ladder continues to handle finite undiscounted problems separately.

\subsection*{F3: The stronger McCormick comparator}
\textbf{New response.} We use McCormick global programming as a component of the improved method, rather than attempting to defeat it with a weaker baseline. The new comparison holds the reference elimination, probability caps, operating-regret bounds, LP engine, tolerances, and candidate repair fixed. Only the savings cones and their product rectangles differ. The full table contains all 16 root computations, including \RControlledCaps{} LP caps. There is a material interval reduction in \RControlledImproved{} of the eight pairs. In the eight-regime, 32-period, four-action case the scaled interval has relative width \RLongRelative\%.

This establishes a useful structural tightening in a stated class, not blanket solver dominance. A stronger relaxation can be slower, and a time-capped scaled call can return a worse certificate. The old R40 McCormick comparison remains unchanged. The new work is an ablation of verified root relaxations, not a completed benchmark against every off-the-shelf branch-and-bound implementation. Construction, proposal, arithmetic, checking, dimensions, bytes, and cumulative high-water memory are reported with their actual meanings.

\subsection*{F5: One-shot bounds for the atomic primary model}
\textbf{New response.} Observable-fiber disintegration and the partition proposition provide a genuine fixed-allowance convergence mechanism for controlled atomic continuum models. Finite solver widths, primitive errors, and covariate partition errors have separate roles. We retain that model, its full evidence, and its open width instead of replacing it with a claim about a different policy class.

\textbf{New response.} The paper now states a theorem-level distinction rather than relying on repository engineering. McCormick envelopes, bound tightening, and linear programming are not new.
