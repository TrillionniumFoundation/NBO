 (3x/4,3x/4+1/20),\quad

The seven installed rules, horizons 4, 8, and 12, and allowances $0.01,0.05$ are unchanged. Neural and spline rules are fixed computational incumbents, not estimated economic policies. \section{Verification, economic interpretation, and limits}\label{sec:verification}
A certificate has three distinct burdens. Its mathematical reduction must preserve the target policy class. Its finite arithmetic must check the asserted inequalities with the correct direction. Its publication must correspond to the checked objects. Hashes address the third burden, not the first two. Independent arithmetic addresses part of the second burden and can expose a model-contract error, but is not a proof that every economic assumption is appropriate.

The finite-state verifier uses SymPy rationals and imports neither search implementation nor the optimizer. It rebuilds the common Markov equations, primitive contracts, every policy-box split, every necessary contraction, the LP residual bounds, and the global leaf minimum. The continuous-state verifier independently checks the new lotteries, whole-cell costs, support supersolutions, and local primal vertices, using the prior independent primitive parser rather than the constructor's piecewise-affine operations. The nonlinear verifier uses symbolic rational corner geometry and arbitrary-precision scalar integer induction instead of the constructor's vectorized box operations. Its recorded coverage now includes the complete finest $(32,256)$ object, not merely selected rows. The R42 structural checker separately reconstructs every new finite primal and dual certificate without importing its constructor. The verification report specifies precisely which objects passed; it never substitutes cross-run equality or a mutation test for an inequality proof.

The synthesis preserves all prior scientific material. Deterministic bounds, history-conditioned frontiers, the two-period analytic randomization example, adaptive local-LP analysis, 66 economic sensitivities, failed runs, and the stopped-control derivations remain available in full. The separate historical volume preserves the prior article and technical supplement verbatim; the current supplement supplies a direct map of the new results. The older 349-page historical annex remains unchanged as an archival object rather than a prerequisite for reading this article. The stopped consumption--portfolio problem is not relabeled solved: its inherited full-domain bound of approximately 7.1818 does not meet its 0.01 target. A killed Bellman comparison supports witness propagation but supplies neither missing action coverage nor a diffusion-discretization error bound \citep{KushnerDupuis2001}.

The economic conclusion established by the new primary calculation is precise. When randomized action assignment is admissible and costs attach to realized departures, common Markov lotteries can strictly reduce the minimum implementation cost relative to every deterministic feasible rule. This conclusion holds on the certified configurations and initial laws. It does not estimate how frequently organizations permit such lotteries, how costly they are to administer, or the monetary welfare effect of a real deployment. Fixed randomization overhead, parameter uncertainty, and empirical calibration would require additional primitives and certificates. The joint-perturbation holdout study tests a fixed algorithm beyond its development seed, but is not a statistical generalization theorem.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
A local operating requirement becomes a global revision certificate only when continuation compatibility and the cost of restoring feasibility are controlled together. Backward repair supplies that connection. A date-dependent allowance ladder covers finite undiscounted problems, while exact operating-action restrictions handle zero tolerance. These extensions retain the same common randomized Markov objective rather than replacing it by a necessary-action relaxation.

For endogenous finite-state dynamics, the operating-action disadvantage provides a probability bound, and implementation savings obey a regret cone. Their combination with reference-directed repair yields a quadratic small-allowance bound for a root relaxation at fixed action separation. This is a structural rate, not a polynomial guarantee for the unrestricted global problem. In the controlled audit, tighter relaxation geometry can materially improve a certificate, but difficult high-discount cases and solver stops remain in the evidence.

Observable-covariate disintegration supplies a separate controlled atomic continuum route. It preserves every Borel common policy by using a label recoverable from the current state, and its partition bounds converge when the finite fiber solves and primitive errors are refined. Exact exogenous aggregation remains a tractable complementary class; its twelve executed service economies retain tightly closed positive-cost intervals. The density exercise retains all primitive, solver, and repair terms, including a failed sufficient tightening condition.

These results do not make the original maintenance gaps disappear. Its 27 deterministic separations remain valid, all 30 positive-cost randomized intervals remain open, and the nonlinear fine interval remains wide despite complete independent arithmetic verification. Generic completeness, a small-allowance rate, observable-fiber convergence, exact structural solvability, and a designed policy-class comparison answer different questions. Stating their assumptions and retaining their unsuccessful executions permits the method to advance without altering its economic objective or converting a certificate into an unsupported empirical claim.
