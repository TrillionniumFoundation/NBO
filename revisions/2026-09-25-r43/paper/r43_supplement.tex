\section{Controlled-model primitive contract and complete accounting}
The controlled cases use the exact generator in \texttt{regret.py}, independently reconstructed by \texttt{audit.py}. Regimes are $i=0,\ldots,n-1$. For each regime and action, four integer weights are drawn, in that order, from
\[
 (\max\{1,38-10a\}+u_1,\;36+u_2,\;12+7a+u_3,\;2+11a+u_4),
 \qquad u_j\in\{0,1,2,3\}.
\]
Normalize these weights and assign them to successors $\max(0,i-1)$, $i$, $\min(n-1,i+1)$, and $n-1$, combining coincident successors. The seeded standard-library pseudorandom generator is advanced in regime/action order. The stage reward is
\[
 r_{ia}=\frac{i}{n-1}-c_a-\frac{a v}{100000},\qquad
 c=(0,9/100,8/25,11/20),\quad v\in\{1,\ldots,19\},
\]
where $v$ is drawn immediately after the four transition weights. The installed action is zero for $i\geq\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ and is the seed modulo two otherwise. The implementation cost is $(1+i/(n-1))$ times the indicator of a realized departure. The terminal operating value is $i/[2(n-1)]$, and the initial law is uniform. Transitions, rewards, and costs are constant over dates; Bellman disadvantages generally are not. All primitives and every declared seed are retained in the protocol and compressed proof objects.

\section{Directed certificate arithmetic and independent reconstruction}
Write the root relaxation as $\min c'z$ subject to $Ez=e$, $Az\leq b$, and $\ell\leq z\leq u$. Any rational vector $y$ and any rational $\mu\geq0$ give the lower bound
\[
 y'e-\mu'b+\sum_j\min\{h_j\ell_j,h_ju_j\},\qquad h=c-E'y+A'\mu.
\]
Add the exact reference cost $\nu'H_0$ and truncate at zero. A floating-point LP success flag is neither necessary nor sufficient for this inequality. Numerical equality marginals and sign-corrected inequality marginals are merely proposals for $y,\mu$.

Accumulating all distinct rational denominators before rounding is unnecessarily expensive. The implemented reader and constructor instead use denominator $2^{40}$. They round each constant contribution downward. For each residual coefficient, they accumulate dyadic lower and upper enclosures $h_j^-\leq h_j\leq h_j^+$. The safe contribution is the downward-rounded minimum of
\[
 h_j^-\ell_j,\ h_j^-u_j,\ h_j^+\ell_j,\ h_j^+u_j.
\]
Taking all four products is necessary when a savings coordinate can be negative. This procedure is valid for arbitrary proposed multipliers with the required sign, and keeps denominator growth under control. The complete lower endpoint, including reference cost, is retained as a rational number.

For the upper endpoint, the constructor repairs backward toward the reference action. It reserves a local inward margin before rounding nonreference probabilities down to denominator $2^{40}$; the unused probability is assigned to the reference action. At a fixed repaired continuation, the rounding-induced regret change is at most $(m-1)2^{-40}\max_a|q_a-q_{a^*}|$. If the reserved target leaves insufficient reference slack, the constructor uses the feasible reference action. These are proposal choices. The independent reader ignores the repair routine and checks the serialized probabilities by the original operating and implementation Bellman equations. Every probability is nonnegative and sums to one, and every date/regime satisfies the original, unmodified allowance.

The independent endpoint reader imports neither the constructor nor NumPy, SciPy, or any optimizer. It reconstructs the operating optimum, reference policy, disadvantage and savings coefficients, all variable bounds, all product envelopes, and every cone and Bellman constraint. A further independent audit reconstructs the declared random primitive sequence and checks each complete proof against its registered parameters. It also independently recomputes the continuum weighted sums. These checks do not mechanically prove the analytic repair, rate, or disintegration theorems. Four retained mutations test rejection of a forged endpoint, negative action probability, negative inequality multiplier, and invalid transition mass.

For the general observable-fiber theorem, the bijectivity of the covariate evolution has an economic content: the label that selects a continuation is recoverable from the current observed state. Noninvertible maps can merge different labels. Selecting a different continuation for each merged label would add memory and would not prove the stated common-Markov result. Likewise, an action-dependent continuous-condition transition is not covered merely because it has finitely many successors. The original affine and nonlinear condition models are therefore retained as separate, open-width evidence.
