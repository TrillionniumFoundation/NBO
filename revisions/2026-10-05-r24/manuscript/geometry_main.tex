\subsection{Preserving action curvature in the certificate}\label{sec:r24geometry}
The all-state certificate used above bounds $D_t'H_t^{-1}D_t$ by a scalar
multiple of the identity. This is convenient, but it discards the action
curvature and the direction of the policy residual. The following refinement
retains both. It changes the verifier, not the economic target or the
candidate class, and it is available to every candidate generator.

\begin{proposition}[Residual-energy certificate]\label{prop:r24energy}
At one date, let $H\succeq rI$, $r>0$, and let $Q\succ0$. Choose any matrix
$V$ of the same shape as $D$, and define
\begin{align}
 R_V&=D-HV,\nonumber\\
 S_V&=V'HV+V'R_V+R_V'V+\rho_V^2 I/r,
 \qquad \rho_V\geq\|R_V\|_F.\label{eq:r24energy}
\end{align}
Then $0\preceq D'H^{-1}D\preceq S_V$. Hence every upper bound
\begin{equation}
 \eta_V\geq\lambda_{\max}(Q^{-1/2}S_VQ^{-1/2})
 \label{eq:r24generalized}
\end{equation}
certifies $g(x)\leq\eta_V x'Qx$ for every state. When
$Q=\operatorname{diag}(q_1,\ldots,q_d)$, it suffices to use
\begin{equation}
 \eta_V=\max_i q_i^{-1}\sum_j |(S_V)_{ij}|.
 \label{eq:r24rowsum}
\end{equation}
Taking the maximum of the certified date bounds gives the same unrestricted
adapted-policy guarantee as Theorem~\ref{thm:r23coercive}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substituting $D=HV+R_V$ gives the exact identity
\[
 D'H^{-1}D=V'HV+V'R_V+R_V'V+R_V'H^{-1}R_V.
\]
The last term is at most $\rho_V^2 I/r$ in the positive-semidefinite order.
This proves the first statement and \eqref{eq:r24generalized}. For diagonal
$Q$, the symmetric positive-semidefinite matrix in
\eqref{eq:r24generalized} is similar to $Q^{-1}S_V$, whose spectral radius
is bounded by its induced maximum-row-sum norm. This yields
\eqref{eq:r24rowsum}. The earlier coercive-policy comparison now applies
without modification.
\end{proof}

The computation may choose $V$ by solving the midpoint system $HV=D$.
The solve is only a proposal: its error is charged through $R_V$.
Neither a numerical inverse nor an unverified eigendecomposition is accepted
as a proof. Outward matrix balls enclose every product, residual, row sum,
and positive division. The same terminal, covariance, integrability, and
implementation premises as the original certificate remain in force.

The choice $V=0$ recovers the original Frobenius/coercivity bound. Thus the
minimum of that bound and a successfully verified residual-energy bound is
valid. There is no statistical selection step: both are deterministic upper
bounds for the same stored candidate. An unavailable midpoint solve leaves
the original certificate in place and is recorded as an unavailable
refinement, not as a successful energy calculation. The test suite also
passes deliberately inaccurate solve proposals to the verifier; residual
charging must still protect the final inequality.

\paragraph{What a sharper bound establishes.}
A smaller sufficient upper bound can resolve previously unsuccessful checks
without changing the policy. It does not establish that the original policy
had large loss, that a neural representation was necessary, or that complete
work would fall after adding the cost of the new check. In particular,
replaying a sharper verifier on stored candidates cannot recreate a new
first-success wall clock. The supplementary calculation therefore reports
certificate changes separately from the original prospective method costs.
The original 1,088 checks, including all 608 unsuccessful checks, remain
unaltered in the primary record. Only a separately executed prospective
service could turn the refined stopping rule into a new work comparison.
