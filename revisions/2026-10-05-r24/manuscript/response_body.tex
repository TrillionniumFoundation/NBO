\section*{Response to the Referee}
We thank the referee for the detailed assessment of the manuscript reviewed
at commit \texttt{2789166}. This revision continues \emph{Neural Bellman
Operators}. It retains the original economic models, their proofs, and the
complete adverse numerical record. We have not substituted a different
paper about certification for the paper under review.

We agree that a verifier's validity, a returned policy's accuracy, and the
value of a candidate-generation method are different propositions. The
revision makes the distinction operational. The inherited, source-frozen
full-policy study supplies an all-state dynamic comparison with continuous
Gaussian innovations and vector actions. The new analysis adds an explicit
finite-step theorem for a trained hidden factor, charges drift between its
training target and the returned policy's actual continuation, and refines
the policy certificate without discarding action curvature. Every original
candidate is examined symmetrically. The new calculation is explicitly
post-execution; it neither replaces historical clocks nor supplies a new
prospective work-superiority experiment.

The article, Technical Supplement, Economic Applications, and Prior Numerical
Evidence are separate native journal-class documents with linked references.
The new response corresponds to the R21 report dated 5 October 2026, not to
a new journal editorial decision. The repository's R22 and R23 branches are
retained. The new publication has its own source identity and preservation
account. The principal new statements are
Proposition~\ref{prop:r24learning}, equations~\eqref{eq:r24drift}--\eqref{eq:r24allocated},
and Proposition~\ref{prop:r24energy}; the complete new computational record
is Section~\ref{sec:r24record} of the supplement.

\section*{Blocking concerns}
\paragraph{B1. Work to accuracy under a changing future.}
The unfavorable R21 frontier is unchanged. Adaptive NBO costs more than fresh
NBO in every original changing-future cell; cached sample-average optimization
is the least-cost fully certified procedure in all eight cells. These facts
remain in the article's prior-evidence summary and in the complete Prior
Numerical Evidence document. They are not relabeled as neural amortization.
The inherited full-policy experiment instead solves every future as a dynamic
problem, compares cold and warm neural and conventional representations, and
includes a Riccati comparator. Its original service clocks and all failed
checks are retained. The new residual-energy calculation measures how much
of a failed sufficient check reflects a loose certificate. It does not infer
that adding the new check would reduce end-to-end cost. A method-level neural
cost advantage is not claimed from this calculation.

\paragraph{B2. What the economic contrast establishes.}
The earlier specified-future response bands and the sharper enumeration
bands remain together. In the full-policy instance, future investment rules
are reoptimized at all 24 dates; response enclosures now concern the complete
dynamic optimum. The structural comparator remains visible, and the paper
states that certifying a response with NBO does not establish that the
response requires NBO. We retain the economic question and strengthen its
policy interpretation rather than treating a successful sign certificate
as evidence of representation necessity.

\paragraph{B3. End-to-end policy composition.}
The all-state cost-weighted argument in
Theorem~\ref{thm:r23coercive} and Corollary~\ref{cor:r23capital} is retained
in the article and instantiated in the full-policy capital study. Own-policy
evaluation supplies the continuation; a full vector-action completion of the
square supplies local advantage; coefficient inequalities cover every state,
not a visited-state catalogue. The comparison is with all adapted finite-cost
policies. Class approximation and value transfer are zero for the declared
discrete-time instance because neither a restricted policy class nor a
continuous-time approximation defines that comparison. The implementation
allowance is separate and conditional on its stated relative action-error
contract. No one of these zeros is transferred to a different nonlinear,
diffusion, or strategic model. The new bound preserves exactly this target.

\paragraph{B4. Support, horizon, and action dimension.}
The scalar finite-law exercise remains as an auditable nonlinear laboratory.
The inherited additional study has continuous unbounded Gaussian support,
24 reoptimized dates, and ten or fifty simultaneous real-valued controls.
Its verifier covers the full state space by coercivity, with a reported
uniform loss bound on the declared initial ball. This addresses the finite
catalogue and scalar-action limitations of the reviewed experiment. Quadratic
structure still makes a strong dynamic-programming baseline available; the
revision does not describe a structured problem as computationally intractable
merely because its shock law is continuous.

\paragraph{B5. A result for the trained neural procedure.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:r24learning} is a finite-step statement for actual
hidden-weight gradient updates of a square-activation scalar critic. The
factor is trainable, can be rectangular, and need not commute with the target.
The basin, step size, geometric residual bound, rank lower bound, and sufficient
inner-loop budget are all explicit. Equation~\eqref{eq:r24arithmetic} gives
the separate perturbation allowance needed for implemented steps. Equations
\eqref{eq:r24drift} and \eqref{eq:r24learningpolicy} then charge the difference
between the training target and the returned policy's own continuation.
This closes the logical gap between fitting an earlier target and certifying
the actual returned future. The result is not a global convergence theorem
for arbitrary architectures or arbitrary outer policy-iteration schedules.
Nor is it a separation theorem against the matched quadratic class. The
numerical comparison therefore continues to expose the cost of factor training.

\paragraph{B6. Method-level estimands and reliability.}
The full-policy study's registered randomizer is uniform on sixteen specified
seeds and is exhaustively enumerated. Its success probability and expected
operation counts refer exactly to that finite probability space. Repeated
deterministic comparator outputs are not independent replications. Wall
clocks are the realized clocks of the executed services, not population
expectations. The new audit examines every stored attempted candidate and
reports deterministic certificate and basin counts. It supplies no additional
population inference and does not retrospectively change the registered
randomizer or stopping rule.

\paragraph{B7. The economic applications.}
Recursive utility, endogenous preferences, temporal selves, and games retain
their full models and verification accounts in Economic Applications. The
new finite-step and all-state results are identified with the additive capital
instance in which their premises are proved. They are not presented as
equilibrium or nonlinear-utility certificates for other models. Preserving
these applications does not authorize transferring constants across economic
objects. Their distinct domain and deviation conditions remain explicit.
A common architecture is not used as a substitute for a common empirical
performance result.

\paragraph{B8. Organization and preservation.}
The main text follows one argument: the economic target, continuation error,
policy loss, trained-continuation control, and the full-policy experiment.
The three earlier main-text numerical studies remain unabridged in Prior
Numerical Evidence. The technical and economic companions preserve the
original derivations and applications rather than silently deleting them.
The revision adds a reading map and a source-and-label preservation audit.
This is a reorganization of the original paper, not a claim that the entire
research archive has become a short article. We have not removed substantive
content merely to reach an arbitrary page count.

\paragraph{B9. Interpretation and discipline of the counterfactual.}
The original finite-law regimes remain specified-future experiments. The
full-policy capital regimes change future technology or valuation while
resolving future investment. Their response bands therefore answer a dynamic
question, but their primitive coefficients are declared laboratory inputs,
not estimates from an observed industry. Neither an empirical reform effect
nor a quantitatively calibrated welfare conclusion is claimed. The numerical
and analytical contribution is judged at this explicitly stated economic
target, with the structural comparator included.

\section*{Major comments}
\paragraph{M1. A single paper and contribution.}
We retain the original NBO topic. Certification defines the accuracy that
candidate construction must achieve; it is not a substitute topic. The
finite-step hidden-factor result concerns the learner, the residual-energy
result concerns verification, and the service frontiers concern complete
methods. These three roles are now separated in the article and response.

\paragraph{M2. Every term in the policy account.}
The full-policy instance states own-policy local advantages, all-state
coverage, discounted conditional evaluation, unrestricted adapted comparison,
zero class and transfer allowances for the declared discrete-time model, and
the separate implementation envelope. The complete prospective record is
retained. The new action-curvature inequality strengthens the same account;
it introduces no new unmeasured domain or deviation assumption.

\paragraph{M3. A genuinely dynamic comparison.}
The continuous-support, 24-date vector-control instance supplements rather
than replaces the original capital economy. Its Riccati baseline prevents
continuous support from being used as an argument that strong alternatives
must be infeasible. Enumeration is not used as its policy oracle or verifier.

\paragraph{M4. Advance specification of the method-level target.}
The original full-policy freeze, initialization randomizer, check budgets,
and tolerance remain binding. The new record states a separate
post-execution analysis scope before running its all-candidate replay.
No historical result is advertised as a new independent fit or as a
prospectively chosen refined stopping rule.

\paragraph{M5. Refresh policies.}
The earlier adaptive rule's domination by fresh refitting is retained.
The full-policy study compares cold and warm variants with symmetric own-policy
information and failed-check charges. Equation~\eqref{eq:r24warm} supplies
an explicit sufficient reuse basin and equation~\eqref{eq:r24budget} supplies
its inner training budget; equation~\eqref{eq:r24drift} charges the subsequent
change of future. These analytical conditions do not retroactively optimize
the already executed refresh rule or prove a new service-cost frontier.

\paragraph{M6. Contribution-isolating frontiers.}
The original frontiers preserve fitting, evaluation, actor solves, failed
and successful verification, writes, stored candidate sizes, and separately
reported loop overhead. The matched conventional quadratic has the same
population evaluation information as the neural factor, and Riccati remains
a strong structural comparator. The new refinement is applied to all methods
and all attempts, without selecting favorable neural cells. Its own
postprocessing time is reported separately and is not substituted for any
primary clock.

\paragraph{M7. Candidate accuracy and construction value.}
The revised abstract and conclusion distinguish a valid certificate, an
accurate returned policy, a trainable representation's finite-step implication,
and comparative method work. Only the corresponding evidence is used for
each statement. In particular, a newly resolved historical sufficient check
is not a new observation of algorithmic superiority.

\paragraph{M8. Future-policy interpretation.}
All references to the old finite-law economic contrast retain the specified
future-policy qualification. The new full-policy contrast concerns
reoptimized futures and is labeled as such. The difference is economic, not
merely a change in the notation for a task catalogue.

\paragraph{M9. Reading burden.}
The article provides the main theorem chain and current full-policy result.
Linked companions carry the complete technical derivations, economic models,
and prior evidence. A reading map identifies which material establishes the
central claim and which material preserves prior applications and experiments.
We have preserved the original paper instead of adopting the suggested new
title or deleting substantive derivations. The continued breadth of the
archive is disclosed rather than hidden.

\paragraph{M10. Evidence and publication standards.}
Every original candidate digest and stopping decision is checked. The new
evidence has its own source commit, tests, ledger hashes, all-candidate records,
and deterministic-analysis label. Unsuccessful original and auxiliary checks
remain available. Native journal-class compilation produces all five linked
documents and checks references, citations, duplicate destinations, missing
glyphs, and overfull boxes. The publication manifest and preservation audit
bind the delivered scientific source and PDFs to the new revision.

\section*{What is new, and what is not inferred}
The new contributions are the finite-step trained-continuation theorem with
its target-drift and arithmetic accounts, the residual-energy certificate,
and their source-bound all-candidate analysis. The full-policy experiments
are inherited and independently replayed, not new fitting runs. The old
nonlinear evidence is preserved unchanged. The revision does not infer
universal neural superiority, global nonconvex convergence, calibrated
empirical welfare, or equilibrium accuracy outside the models and premises
actually examined. These distinctions support the original NBO research
program by making each positive result independently assessable.
