This paper develops Neural Bellman Operators for policy evaluation and feasible
improvement in controlled economies. Centered continuation errors connect
learned values to decisions, and own-policy Bellman comparisons connect local
accuracy to complete policies. For a trainable square-activation critic in a
multisector capital economy, we construct learning budgets from economic
primitives before any evaluation target is computed. A joint backward
induction yields finite hidden-weight training and a prescribed policy-loss
bound, including under nonlinear recursive entropic preferences. An independent
residual-centered verifier checks the rounded returned policy over all real
states, full vector actions, and adapted finite-cost deviations. Experiments
separate candidate accuracy, constructive trainability, and complete work,
retaining conventional quadratics, simulation, and structural dynamic programs
as comparators. Reoptimized-future policy experiments complement the original
finite-law reuse studies. The controlled-diffusion framework and the recursive,
endogenous-preference, temporal-self, and strategic applications retain their
full accounts; their distinct domain and equilibrium obligations are not
inferred from the capital experiment.
