\section*{Response to the R12 Referee Report}

We thank the referee for the careful examination of the development snapshot and for identifying the evidence needed to assess the revised method. This revision develops the same paper, \emph{Neural Bellman Operators}. Its central economic subject remains policy evaluation and feasible improvement in continuous-time models with recursive utility, endogenous preferences, temporal selves, and strategic interaction. We have completed the evidence account, extended the mathematical connection between evaluation and economic performance, added a finite-observation implementation theorem, and reorganized the article around these results and their numerical tests.

The report reviewed the development commit beginning \texttt{39344d49} and is preserved at the review commit beginning \texttt{65110ed2}. The report accurately identifies what was absent from that development snapshot. The present numerical record comes from the completed primary calculation at source \texttt{95053e72} and the completed fixed-work extension at source \texttt{c8299feb}. Their original generating sources remain separately identified. The present integration binds their recovered weights, raw arrays, protocols, worker records, independently recomputed endpoints, and generated tables to the manuscript; it does not describe the recovered fits as newly trained by the publication commit.

\section*{Principal results and changes}

The completed primary study contains ninety fitted policies, with ten streams in each of dimensions ten, twenty, and fifty for NBO, direct policy optimization, and affine policies. All 180 primary schedule comparisons, covering both the origin and the declared finite population, have positive simultaneous lower improvement endpoints. All 120 direct NBO-minus-comparator intervals contain zero. These results establish improvement over the feasible analytical schedule for the recorded policies and leave their direct method rankings unresolved at the reported precision. Both conclusions appear in the main text and in every seed-level table.

The fixed-work extension contains twenty-eight fits, fifty-six policy evaluations, and forty-two direct comparisons, including one repeated environment. It compares exact iteration and simulator-visit budgets, reports common certified-gain targets, and includes raw-costate improvement without critic regression. The independent-reference mechanism calculation finds larger critic mean squared error and larger critic-greedy Hamiltonian gaps in all six principal dimension--stream panels. The corresponding economic comparisons are reported separately. This is a measured property of the fitted evaluation block, and it is retained in the interpretation of the method.

Proposition~\ref{prop:r14economiccostate} strengthens the theoretical connection: it links costate regression error, rollout bias, and feasible-action error under the candidate's discounted state occupation law to economic improvement. Proposition~\ref{prop:r14randomization} specifies the comparison filtration. Proposition~\ref{prop:r14sensor} supplies an explicit transfer from a protected innovation controller to a controller using discrete noisy state measurements. Eighteen completed sensing cells accompany that theorem as implementation diagnostics. A four-grid scalar reference replay reproduces twenty-eight original arrays exactly in the recorded environment and adds equation residuals, full action diagnostics, and five initial-state comparisons.

\paragraph*{B5. Establish and test the critic mechanism.}
The new welfare inequality in Proposition~\ref{prop:r14economiccostate} makes the missing connection explicit. Its performance-difference identity is evaluated under the candidate policy's state law. The bound retains three distinct terms: regression error relative to the conditional rollout mean, the bias between that mean and the continuous-time policy costate, and incomplete feasible-action maximization. Equation~\ref{eq:r14costateresidual} gives a separate sufficient costate bound from derivatives of the continuous fixed-policy residual and terminal error, under stated smoothness and global derivative hypotheses.

The empirical mechanism comparison now uses a common independent reference. The saved raw action uses coarse replicate zero. We exclude its paired fine replicate from the reference mean and evaluate all saved actions against fine replicates one through fifteen. The original arrays and original diagnostics are retained. Tables~\ref{tab:r14costates} and~\ref{tab:r14hamiltonian} show that the critic's MSE exceeds the raw-costate MSE in all six principal panels, with ratios approximately 1.06--1.99; its greedy Hamiltonian gap is also larger throughout. The reference variance and nested-grid sensitivity are reported. These states follow the declared held-out design, so their diagnostics are not inserted into the occupation-weighted welfare theorem. The revision supplies a theorem for the mechanism and an independent test of its fitted implementation; it does not report a critic advantage that the data fail to establish.

\paragraph*{B6. Specify a finite-observation implementation.}
The exact continuous-observation proposition remains an explicitly stated benchmark. Section~\ref{sec:r14filtration} defines the economic and product filtrations, proves that independent private randomization leaves the optimal expected payoff unchanged, and establishes causality of the history reconstruction. Equality of the reconstructed controller's law does not mean pathwise recovery of an unobserved Brownian null component.

Section~\ref{sec:r14sensing} additionally defines a controller receiving a noisy capital observation at a finite set of dates and holding the resulting action between dates. It receives neither Brownian coordinates nor a continuous drift integral. Proposition~\ref{prop:r14sensor} propagates the sensor moment bound, a global Lipschitz bound for the real reference actor, protected-map arithmetic, clipped innovations, and internal-state error into a payoff allowance in the original continuous economy. Its proof retains cancellation in the accumulated local drift martingale and clipping errors. The eighteen executed cells use two decision grids and three noise levels in each dimension. Their fine-Euler differences diagnose implementation; the separate outward allowance supplies the conditional transfer. A payoff interval transfers only from the exact protected parent on the same grid. The 1,024-cell certificate is not reassigned to a 64- or 128-cell parent.

\paragraph*{B7. Delimit and strengthen the absolute reference.}
The scalar reference has been replayed from its frozen policies on all four original grids and domains. All twenty-eight original arrays reproduce exactly in the recorded replay environment. The additional state comparisons matter. On the finest domain-six grid, NBO's loss at zero is approximately $3.44\times10^{-6}$ and DPO's is $1.16\times10^{-4}$. \paragraph*{B8. State the economic contribution of the whole paper.}
The revised main text restores the motivating preference-formation problem explicitly. Section~\ref{sec:economic-scope} states its reflected preference process, consumption and portfolio dynamics, conditional policy rules, cardinal normalization, and valid adjustment-cost envelope. It then explains the recursive-utility, temporal-self, and dynamic-game versions of evaluation and improvement. Their complete derivations and numerical evidence remain in the supplement. The original economic agenda is therefore visible alongside the principal capital verification study.

The capital fee exercise translates a verified gain into a defined resource decision. Equation~\ref{eq:r12fee} specifies gross withdrawals, the service's resource charge, and household consumption. The fee threshold is exact for this log-utility model; it is not a claim of empirical calibration or a valuation of processor time in dollars. Its role is to make the verified utility difference interpretable. The paper's methodological contribution combines a specified Bellman computation, economic error bounds, an observation transfer, and reproducible method comparisons. These claims are evaluated on their mathematics and measured results, without treating a broad ranking of algorithms or an unobserved empirical economy as an established conclusion.

The anchor proof also applies directly in the enlarged filtration. The information assumptions and observation clock are specified before the implementation results.
