# Mathematical audit and proposed R14 additions

## Scope and source record

This audit reads the latest R12 referee report in `reviews/2026-10-04-econometrica-numerical-methods-r12/referee_report.md`, the root `ECTA.tex`, R10 continuous-capital theorem/proof, R11 policy-specific theorem/proof and costate-learning description, R12 `theory.tex`, `proofs.tex`, and `code/observation.py`, and the authored `sensing_proof.tex` and `work_and_sensing.tex` from `origin/revision/econometrica-nbo-r13-development-2026-10-04`. The proposed changes preserve the model, the NBO evaluation–improvement map, the primitive action box, and all inherited theorem content.

The TeX additions were written in scratch for the parent agent to integrate. They do not assert that NBO dominates another candidate-generation method. The mathematical results are estimator-agnostic; independently verified method differences remain the empirical test of the method contribution.

## B5: costate inequality and the critic mechanism

**The R12 inequality is correct.** On the convex action set, strong concavity and the first-order variational inequality at the critic-greedy action apply even when its maximum is on the boundary. The decomposition in the existing proof gives exactly

\[
 H_q(m_q)-H_q(\widetilde m)
 \leq\delta+\|q-\widehat q\|_d\sqrt{2\delta/\mu}
             +\frac{\|q-\widehat q\|_d^2}{2\mu}.
\]

This is the square displayed in the manuscript. The normalization `q = d D_y V` is consistent with the economic Hamiltonian. The Hessian bound `mu = upper_action_bound^(-2)` is valid, including the positive-semidefinite adjustment-cost contribution. Also, `sup |tanh''| = 4/(3 sqrt(3)) < 1`; the R11 generator constant using `|tanh''| <= 1` is valid.

**The conditional-projection identity is correct but does not establish fitted-critic improvement.** It identifies regression risk for the discretized conditional target. It does not eliminate the difference between the discretized target and the continuous policy costate, does not certify the critic's fitted regression risk, and does not change the state distribution at which errors are measured.

The new `costate_extension.tex` supplies the missing economic link:

1. An exact discounted performance-difference identity, evaluated along the improved policy's physical state process.
2. An occupation-weighted bound on the total greedy action loss. Its three terms are actor optimization error, critic regression error, and rollout discretization bias. The proof applies the R12 inequality and two applications of Minkowski's inequality.
3. Explicit conditional cross-bank identities for regression risk and rollout-mean variance. A finite sample cross-bank estimate can be negative. The state distribution must equal the candidate's discounted occupation law, or be connected to it by a justified change of measure, before it estimates the theorem's occupation-weighted risk.
4. A derivative-residual sufficient condition for continuous-time policy-costate accuracy, proved by differentiating the fixed-policy Feynman–Kac error representation and bounding the stochastic-flow Jacobian. This is an alternative route to a rigorous continuous costate bound; the current held-out diagnostic is not claimed to satisfy its global hypotheses.

The performance-difference proposition explicitly assumes a `C^(1,2)` policy value, the prescribed terminal payoff, a zero-mean discounted Itô stochastic integral, and integrable drift and terminal terms. Finiteness of the displayed risk quantities alone is not substituted for these requirements.

## B6 and M6: observation filtration and randomized comparator

**The R12 observation construction is correct under its exact assumptions.** Its right inverse and null-space completion give identity bracket; the scalar private Brownian randomizer is necessary for the particular componentwise clipping implementation. No pathwise recovery of the original null-space driver follows.

The original text's reference to an “enlarged observation filtration” should identify the probability space and filtration. `filtration_and_observation.tex` does so:

- Work on a canonical product space with economic Brownian motion `W` and an independent standard-Borel randomizer `R`. The latter can include a private Brownian path and sensor-noise stream.
- The observation filtration is generated by the observed state history and private Brownian history, and is a subfiltration of the full product filtration. The integrated-drift residual `M` is adapted to it and is a martingale by iterated conditional expectation.
- Cross bracket with the private Brownian motion is zero. Lévy characterization then applies in the actual observation filtration, rather than in an unspecified filtration.
- The control is constructed cell by cell, so each recovered increment is used only after the corresponding observed interval has ended. Strong well-posedness on each held-action interval establishes the closed-loop law.

The same fragment gives an explicit proposition that the expected-payoff supremum is unchanged by independent randomization. Its proof fixes the entire independent randomizer realization on the canonical product space and applies Fubini and strong uniqueness. The claim is equality of suprema. A “best realization” need not exist, and no attainment assertion is needed. A sensor error must be a causal functional of this product filtration; the informal phrase “not future-looking” alone is not the admissibility definition.

## M7: finite noisy observations

The R13 finite-observation proof is structurally valid as a conservative finite-grid bound, provided all arithmetic bounds are established. Its main text has notation collisions: `kappa` is reassigned to network arithmetic while it already denotes production intensity; an undefined `lambda` replaces the economic production coefficient in `beta` and the dispersion bound; `nu` is reassigned to sensor noise although the capital model uses `nu^2` for total diffusion variance; and `c` can mean either drift intercept or clipping threshold.

The new `finite_sensing.tex` and `finite_sensing_proof.tex` fix those collisions and strengthen the bound. The sensor policy observes only `O_k = Y_sensor(t_k) + eta_k`, then applies the protected actor directly and holds that action. No drift reconstruction is needed for this implementation.

### Strong-error improvement

The R13 cellwise triangle argument accumulates local stochastic drift errors to an `O(sqrt(h))` forcing and clipping errors to a term growing with the square root of the number of cells. Applying stochastic Fubini and Itô isometry to the accumulated martingales yields instead

\[
 b_k=h\{Kt_k/2+H\sqrt{t_k/3}\}
       +\overline\sigma\sqrt{t_kv_z}+k\xi_h.
\]

The sensor-minus-internal-state error is bounded by

\[
 e_{k+1}=(1+\beta h)e_k+h a_k^\Delta+(b_{k+1}-b_k),\qquad
 a_k^\Delta=\min\{2\epsilon,L(e_k+\nu_{obs})+2\delta_\pi\}.
\]

The clipping contribution uses the exact trace of the diffusion covariance in the normalized norm. Its independence is needed only across Gaussian coordinates and cells. It need not be independent of the local-drift martingale, because the proof uses the triangle inequality only after each accumulated norm has been bounded.

### Economic-payoff improvement

The proof uses the exact schedule-relative payoff identity, rather than separately bounding flow-state and terminal-state means. Along the segment between the two actions,

\[
 \left\|\nabla_d R_t(m)\right\|_d
 \leq r_k(t)\{[m_0(t)m_{-,k}]^{-1}+\zeta\},\quad
 r_k(t)=\epsilon+|m_0(t)-m_0(t_k)|.
\]

Thus the action-difference coefficient is proportional to the tube radius plus schedule movement. The final bound consists of production difference, consumption-deficit difference, and terminal-dispersion difference. It retains the original economy and original physical Brownian state. The lower bound `m_{-,k}` is uniformly at least the primitive positive lower action bound.

The bound is a finite-grid theorem. A convergence conclusion requires vanishing observation error, activation/representation error, and per-horizon recurrence error, and clipping second moment tending to zero. No convergence to zero is asserted with fixed clipping, fixed arithmetic, or fixed sensor noise.

## Executable diagnostic

`sensing_diagnostic.py` is an implementation of the new outward transfer account and a separately labelled finite-Euler diagnostic. It imports unchanged historical kernels, performs no fitting, and accepts only saved NBO iteration-80 checkpoints for dimensions 10, 20, and 50. The parent specified training stream 7919, 128 paths, 64/128 actor cells, eight fine Euler cells per decision interval, normalized observation RMS levels 0/.001/.01, and fresh base noise seed 1410047919. Those choices are fixed in the script and written to `PROTOCOL.json` before runtime loading or outcomes.

The parent and sensor use the same protected polynomial-tanh actor and inward guard. Their physical Euler diagnostic paths use untruncated shared Brownian increments. Only the parent's internal coarse Euler increments are clipped. The original physical state is not capped. The global actor norm and roundoff account use saved weights; they are not estimated from sampled derivatives. Input representation is included in the protected map's arithmetic account, while sensor error before that map is the separate `nu_obs` assumption.

The script saves path-level payoffs, differences, terminal states, observed action/state discrepancies, input-noise hashes, exact weight hashes, kernel hashes, deterministic allowance components, and per-cell majorants. The Monte Carlo standard errors describe finite-Euler sample dispersion; they omit fine-Euler bias and are not presented as confidence intervals for continuous payoffs. The script does not transfer any historical schedule-relative endpoint to a changed activation or time grid. Such a transfer would first require a certificate for the exact protected parent on that grid.
