\section{Restart-price evidence and precision experiments}\label{sec:priceevidence47}
The empirical question is whether prices that respect shared continuations yield useful certificates where strict action separation fails, and whether those certificates improve a complete search. We distinguish the original frozen evaluation from the subsequent development experiment. The latter reuses every one of the sixteen original models; it is retrospective evidence, not a second untouched sample. The original twelve primary cases comprise four sizes in each of three designed families. Four further cases perturb one tie construction. Their shared origin matters for interpretation.

\subsection{All cases, a fixed target, and the cost of reuse}
Table~\ref{tab:prices47} reports the constant-price certificate, the restart-price certificate, the price-strengthened aggregate root, and the complete price-strengthened search. The absolute target is always $10^{-3}$ in the serialized cost units. The full search retains the 120-second and 2,047-node limits, with a 15-second limit per LP proposal. Previously certified upper policies initialize it; the additional local proposal is disabled. Its incremental search time therefore does not include the earlier work that produced those incumbents. These runs are not an equal-total-work comparison with the original B and A algorithms.

Price-strengthened search reaches the target in six of twelve primary cases and three of four tie stresses. Of the primary successes, I0 has zero cost, Q0, I3, and Q3 close at the root, and M0 and Q1 require branching. For M0 the price root width $0.003537$ falls to $0.000331$ after nine nodes; Q1 falls from $0.006410$ to $0.000954$ after 125 nodes. These are actual covering-tree improvements. They do not imply that branching is effective in every case: M3 processes only 23 nodes, while T3 reaches 2,047 nodes with a remaining width $0.093208$.

The largest inventory case I3, with sixteen states, thirty-two dates, and four actions, now closes with width $0.000247$, compared with the original aggregate width $0.004768$. The aggregate model already closed Q3 in the original experiment. Such comparisons are paired developments on observed instances, not estimates of out-of-sample superiority. A scalar intersection of all independently certified old and new endpoints closes nine of sixteen rows. It leaves seven rows open and requires all contributing work. The complete individual results, intervals, resource records, and original adverse comparisons are retained in the supplement.

\subsection{Operating ties and the source of residual width}
The exact-tie case T0 has an operating-optimal face of dimension 32. The restart certificate has width $3.0701\times10^{-11}$, without branching, compared with the original Bellman width $0.004995$. The first two perturbations T1 and T2 retain certified widths below the target. T3 does not. This is a constructive improvement at a genuine tie rather than an invocation of an inverse-disadvantage bound outside its assumptions.

The gap identity is checked with exact fractions for all sixteen restart-price policies. It does not assign every failure to arithmetic. For example, the saved components distinguish unused initial allowance from price changes on successor edges; the latter remains appreciable in several open inventory and queueing cases. A large price-flow component indicates that one separable affine price field is not representing the jointly feasible future-regret pattern tightly. Branching and additional valid price fields can refine that representation, but their effectiveness has to be measured rather than inferred from the identity. The full decomposition includes the clipping improvement and all three nonnegative components.

There is no claim here that native SCIP lower bounds have become exact. The price certificate is a separately reconstructed Bellman bound. Intersecting it with other valid bounds does not post-certify SCIP's branch decisions, relaxations, or native global lower endpoint. The original numerical SCIP comparison and its stronger, uncertified lower values remain visible in Section~\ref{sec:evidence45} and the supplement.

\subsection{Paired certificates with approximate operating witnesses}
To test the interface in Section~\ref{sec:witness47}, we construct lower and upper operating Bellman witnesses at four precision levels, $b_V=16,24,32,40$, for each unchanged model. A backward min/max-directed arithmetic pass verifies the operating enclosures. The lower price constructor uses only their conservative disadvantage, and the upper constructor repairs a saved policy using the upper witness and its own continuation. The operating optimum is not substituted for the enclosure inside either endpoint construction. Probability rounding uses $b_p=40$ and lower-potential rounding uses 44 bits.

All sixty-four objects pass an independently implemented checker. Every checker invocation binds the original model and protocol by hash, verifies the operating sub- and supersolution inequalities, reconstructs the lower potential in unshifted coordinates, and evaluates one deployed policy at all restarts. Its upper check is the stronger $\overline V_t-J_t^p\le\varepsilon$. The checker imports neither the constructor nor a numerical optimizer. At 16 bits the sufficient repair guard fails in three models, but the actual row inequalities still certify the produced policies; no uniform upper perturbation bound is asserted in those three rows. All other rows satisfy the guard and their measured endpoint changes lie within the displayed analytic budgets.

Four price-only intervals meet the target at every precision: I0, T0, T1, and T2. This audit is not sixty-four independent economic environments or a new global-search benchmark. It is a paired error-propagation experiment with deliberately controlled operating accuracy. T0 has an exactly represented zero operating value and is not evidence for a difficult oracle. Finite directed Bellman witnesses are cheap in these models. The experiment validates the interface and exposes its quantitative sensitivity; it does not establish performance for a high-dimensional learned operating value whose global enclosure is itself expensive.

\subsection{Interpretation and the remaining optimization gap}
The principal conclusion is specific and positive. Restart prices furnish a gap-decomposable common-policy certificate without action separation; their finite representation resolves previously open tie rows, can strengthen a complete covering search, and admits an independently checked operating-witness error budget. The method remains a portfolio: on several open cases an older formulation supplies the strongest certified endpoint. Its scientific contribution is not a uniform speed ranking or a claim of polynomial global search.

The motivating controlled continuous-state problem remains part of the article. The price theorem applies to its bounded Borel kernels, including moving atoms, while the simple-policy density and repair theorem controls feasible upper approximation. Neither statement alone supplies a convergent, implementable two-sided continuum enclosure. The original thirty positive-cost intervals and the nonlinear two-state results are retained with that boundary. No calibrated economic application or sampling experiment has been added by relabeling the designed finite models.
