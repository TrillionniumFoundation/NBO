\section{Statistical and economic interpretation}\label{supp:interpretation65}
The workload generation rule and named boundary replacements are fixed in each source-frozen protocol. Its dyadic average has an exact reproducible value but is not an independent continuous-law estimate. The earlier bounded-sample cost intervals remain attached to their original frozen policies and their declared simultaneous families \citep{maurer2009}. The query-service catalogue adds neither new confidence intervals for expected gains nor additional optimizer replications to that earlier study.

A complete cold-start time is a directly observed service cost on the recorded machine. A median of two conventional runs or four learned runs is descriptive. Fixed affinity and single-thread configuration remove some sources of variation but do not control CPU frequency, system interference or another platform. We therefore retain all clocks, logical operation counts and process memory, and make no hardware-uniform timing probability claim.

For a decision maker with a declared dollar conversion per model-loss unit, a price per computation second and a deployment fee, an independently valid gain interval can be converted to a monetary purchase interval as shown in the article. A uniform regret bound instead supports an accuracy constraint. An accuracy constraint is not a measured welfare gain. Likewise, a conditional arithmetic break-even workload count does not establish that an unobserved future distribution will produce the same query savings.
