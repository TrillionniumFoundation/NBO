\hypertarget{neural-bellman-operators-revision-r83}{%
\subsection{Neural Bellman Operators --- Revision
R83}\label{neural-bellman-operators-revision-r83}}

We thank the referee for the detailed distinction between a valid
safeguarded numerical method and evidence that its neural proposal is
economically useful. We revise the original \textbf{Neural Bellman
Operators} paper. Its title, controlled economy, constructive own-future
continuation, original Bellman optimum, feasibility requirements and
broader development are retained. The response uses the report on R67
dated 11 October 2026 (review commit
\nolinkurl{4b27b56f4514f6c1d77b69fe707f7202f8b06b19}, manuscript
\nolinkurl{eacd3e217ae1e2e9ea6e154b48da7d103159d051}). This commissioned
advisory review is not represented as a decision of the Econometric
Society.

The report predates substantial deposited work. R69--R73 develop and
execute qualification, protected reuse and predictable evaluation;
R77--R81 add complete attained controllers, higher-precision
original-law costs, affine-calibrated qualification, independently
verified switch barriers and economic allocation. We preserve their
statements, assumptions and adverse observations rather than relabeling
them as fresh experiments. The completed R81 manuscript is retained
within the pinned R82 attempt. R83 integrates the subsequent actionwise
development and adds a new continuum-of-tolerances economic stopping
theorem, an exact frontier algorithm and an independent finite-catalogue
verification.

The principal new result is constructive. An action-switch lower bound
identifies which acquired actions remain possible throughout an
optimization sublevel. Original-law conditional cost differences price
those actions individually. Their exact threshold knots then determine
the entire region of verified optimization errors for which an economic
ranking, equivalence statement or replacement decision is valid. A
verified primal--dual gap places a numerical output in that region
without a probability model for the fitting algorithm. This connects the
accuracy of a completed NBO continuation to an actual economic endpoint;
it is not merely an extra gate for an optional warm start.

The article contains \textbf{Economic accuracy of approximately
optimized continuations}, \textbf{Economically sufficient optimization
accuracy}, and \textbf{Economic stopping regions in the investment
economy}. The Numerical and Development Appendix contains the interval
proofs, all refinement levels, all eighteen frontier targets and
fixed-reference gains. The core Technical Supplement remains intact.
Every one of the 35 inherited active sections remains byte-identical and
compiled once in its original document. Original wrappers, historical
companion editions and the interrupted R82 attempt are preserved
separately.

\hypertarget{m1-b1-the-contribution-and-the-role-of-neural-construction}{%
\subsection{M1 / B1: The contribution and the role of neural
construction}\label{m1-b1-the-contribution-and-the-role-of-neural-construction}}

The original paper now distinguishes three contributions rather than
assigning all of them to the neural proposal. The constructive NBO
produces an attained own-future continuation and its feasible action
witnesses. A common verifier supplies original-optimum accuracy
independently of whether a proposed warm start helps. A
representation-sensitive qualification rule and its optimizer-class
transfer determine which constructed continuations can be used and what
their implemented policies cost. The nonlinear investment study compares
the returned complete controllers, not a neural label attached to an
unchanged classical envelope.

We retain the original title because the constructive neural operator
remains a substantive object of the paper. Its primitive error account,
feasible witnesses, affine--ReLU construction and acquired
implementation are not replaced with a different model or a generic
certification framework. At the same time, the actionwise and stopping
theorems are available to conventional representations. The numerical
evidence cannot be converted into a neural advantage by making those
common theorems representation-specific in the exposition.

The new positive conclusion is that verified approximate optimization
can preserve an identified economic decision even when it does not
preserve every acquired action. The fine-grid comparison below applies
to an entire continuum of qualified readout outputs, with an explicit
sufficient stopping frontier and no further fitting or sampling. Its
meaning is stronger than reproducing one fitted coefficient vector and
different from claiming a universal neural ranking.

\hypertarget{m2-b2-strong-matched-baselines-and-the-qualification-objective}{%
\subsection{M2 / B2: Strong matched baselines and the qualification
objective}\label{m2-b2-strong-matched-baselines-and-the-qualification-objective}}

The retained qualification development includes fixed-feature ReLU and
conventional Bernstein readouts, exact convex primal--dual
qualification, matched label-loss ablations in their declared
construction services and realized whole-policy costs. The subsequent
affine-calibrated rule isolates a representation-dependent qualification
charge instead of attributing an outcome solely to a basis name. Both
own-future dates, feasibility rules, action menus, acquisition
resolutions and callable identities are deposited.

The R80 factorial catalogue compares both bases, both qualification
rules and both resolutions, together with the zero controller.
Calibrated qualification improves both bases and reverses their earlier
matched cost ordering. Both the earlier ReLU advantage under the legacy
rule and the Bernstein advantage under the calibrated rule remain
visible. The calibrated services did not repeat the complete label-loss
ablation; their metadata state that boundary explicitly. Their
construction clocks therefore are not used as a matched universal speed
comparison with the legacy services.

R83 extends these attained-controller conclusions to outputs satisfying
verified objective guarantees. At excess objective \(2^{-16}\), the
fine-grid ReLU-minus-Bernstein class interval is
\texttt{{[}0.000496,\ 0.002503{]}}, rounded outward. The original binary
uncertified-mass transfer gives \texttt{{[}-0.421558,\ 0.424526{]}} from
the same attained-cost interval. Individually pricing the possible
switches identifies the ordering throughout the stated sublevels,
although some actions remain unresolved. No new candidate is selected
because it produces a favorable realized comparison.

These classes hold the fitted labels and terminal actor fixed. They are
not a statement about all jointly refitted hidden layers or arbitrary
new future controllers. This distinction preserves the constructive
theorem while making the new optimization guarantee precise.

\hypertarget{m3-b3-predictability-validation-loss-and-nonstationary-queries}{%
\subsection{M3 / B3: Predictability, validation loss and nonstationary
queries}\label{m3-b3-predictability-validation-loss-and-nonstationary-queries}}

The retained predictable-evaluation and protected-query sections address
the separation between construction information and evaluation
observations. Work--loss admission is based on the declared validation
experiment and timing convention, while the sequential results retain
their filtration and predictability conditions. An original-optimum
verifier does not, by itself, establish a population speed advantage for
a trained proposal. The past warnings and unsuccessful admissions remain
in the compiled appendix.

The new stopping frontier addresses a different source of post-selection
risk. Once the finite attained-policy cost family is covered,
deterministic actionwise inequalities hold simultaneously for every
readout in every enclosed sublevel. The frontier may therefore be
inspected and a tolerance selected on that same event. No extra union
bound over an uncountable continuum is needed. This is proved from
containment, not from treating reused query or cost records as
independent.

Changing the observation law, frozen labels, terminal actor or cost
estimand is outside that event unless independently qualified. In
particular, the theorem is not offered as a substitute for prospective
validation of a new predictor under distribution shift.

\hypertarget{m4-b4-reuse-amortization-and-complete-work}{%
\subsection{M4 / B4: Reuse, amortization and complete
work}\label{m4-b4-reuse-amortization-and-complete-work}}

The R72 and R73 retained studies execute longer reuse catalogues with
protected fallback, query counts and complete service records. Earlier
failed reuse and adverse timing results are not deleted. Their full
phase records, not only the most favorable last block, remain available
in the Numerical and Development Appendix. The reuse claims retain their
declared query law, workload and charging conventions.

The R83 frontier is an additional precisely defined reuse object. After
the actionwise integrals have been verified, all tolerance strata are
formed by sorting the exact switch thresholds and updating cell extrema.
With \(P\) admitted alternatives and \(N\) cells this takes
\(O(P\log P+N)\) arithmetic operations and \(O(P+N)\) storage. Two
monotone tolerance lists then yield each sufficient two-dimensional
frontier with at most the sum of their stratum counts in predicate
evaluations. Integration work is excluded from this incremental count
only because it is reported separately and has already been paid, not
because it is assumed free. Rational bit complexity is also
distinguished from the arithmetic-operation count.

All four integration rungs are charged. The first completed R83 recovery
has its own phase, total-verification and memory records; subsequent
replay has separately named timings. The original failed R82 run retains
its printed phase clocks, but those partial clocks are not invented into
a missing complete-service wall clock. Neither the frontier operation
count nor the recovery runtime is a matched measurement of neural
fitting speed.

\hypertarget{m5-b5-cost-precision-and-the-economic-endpoint}{%
\subsection{M5 / B5: Cost precision and the economic
endpoint}\label{m5-b5-cost-precision-and-the-economic-endpoint}}

The higher-precision original-law experiment already deposited in R80
uses 524,288 common bin rows and the declared nine-controller family.
Exact conditional integration, continuous-bin uncertainty, arithmetic
enclosure and bounded-sample inference have separate roles. The
confidence event is the original preallocated event, not a new family
inferred from R83 postprocessing. Both conditional and ordinary
cash-cost estimands remain recorded, as do the control identities and
all ninety-four declared estimands.

R83 makes a previously conservative part of the cost transfer explicit.
The independent actionwise integration concerns every allowed first-date
switch conditional on its entire acquisition cell. It preserves the
original continuous initial and innovation laws and the fixed terminal
actor. Both arithmetic implementations reproduce all 18,270,720 paired
integration boxes per implementation, with 15,616 separately evaluated
rational midpoint checks. The checks supplement rather than replace the
whole-bin proof. This is a full re-integration, not simply a replay of
saved interval endpoints.

The all-tolerance calculation has 118 model strata and 18 comparison
targets. A separate exhaustive product-grid calculation verifies all
12,732 returned and excluded stratum-pair tests. For fine ReLU versus
fine Bernstein, the strict positive-ordering region on the
equal-tolerance diagonal extends to an exact rational supremum of
approximately \(0.0000674917368\), excluded at equality. Coarse
Bernstein versus fine ReLU is economically equivalent within \(1/256\)
throughout \([0,2^{-12}]\); its final band is
\texttt{{[}-0.001622,\ 0.003879{]}}. These are different economic
statements and both are reported.

Coarse ReLU pairwise class comparisons remain inconclusive even at zero
excess under these sufficient bounds. An inconclusive bound is not a
claim that the corresponding actual optimizer has failed. The complete
tables retain that case and every adverse or unresolved outcome. R83
adds zero independent cost rows and zero fitting attempts.

\hypertarget{m6-b6-vector-controls-and-horizon-dependence}{%
\subsection{M6 / B6: Vector controls and horizon
dependence}\label{m6-b6-vector-controls-and-horizon-dependence}}

The vector-control construction, simplex refinement, derivative bounds
and observed descendant growth remain in their original appendix
sections. The new actionwise theorem allows any finite horizon for which
the conditional contrasts with a fixed continuation are enclosed. This
is not evidence that the particular vector recursion scales favorably in
dimension or horizon. Its implementation costs remain explicit.

The new executed frontier concerns the original two-date, two-state
investment economy and its finite robust first-action menus. No newly
trained vector-control comparison or new sparse high-dimensional
experiment is claimed. Those questions are not answered by relabeling
nonuniform tensor coordinates as a non-tensor method, or by treating a
smaller sublevel radius as a faster vector solver. The paper retains the
vector results under their existing hypotheses while the new theorem
strengthens the optimizer-to-economic-decision connection that is
actually tested.

The original \nolinkurl{SOURCE_FREEZE82.json}, four model files, input

endpoint-stream digests, integer sums and refinement intersections. It
then reconstructs all 72 class-pair and 24 own-controller transfers
without altering the original statistical event. Eighteen new regression
tests cover ties, endpoint inclusion, reversed contrasts, missing

The publication build also reruns the inherited unskipped test suites,

The largest retained vector tree is not independently regenerated in

\hypertarget{m8-b8-numerical-fitting-reliability}{%
\subsection{M8 / B8: Numerical fitting
reliability}\label{m8-b8-numerical-fitting-reliability}}

We distinguish a verifier's ability to return a safe object, the
performance of a particular completed fitting service and a population
claim about optimizer success. The historical multiple-start and warning
records remain unchanged. A small seed catalogue is not turned into a
success probability, and a verified readout after numerical fitting does
not imply reliability of unrestricted hidden-layer training.

The new theorem instead provides a deterministic stopping implication.
If a feasible output has primal value \(F(\theta)\) and verified dual
value \(d\le\inf F\), with \(F(\theta)-d\le\epsilon\), then
\(F(\theta)\le\inf F+\epsilon\le U+\epsilon\). Its controller therefore
belongs to the covered class. A gap pair inside the economic region
certifies the intended decision regardless of which admissible output
the fitting algorithm returned. This is useful without an unobserved
error norm or a fitted success-frequency model.

The region is the largest one furnished by the supplied rectangular
action bounds. It is not asserted to be the exact jointly attainable
optimizer region, because different cellwise switches can require
incompatible common coefficients. Strict comparisons also require exact
boundary handling; an open supremum is not an admissible stopping
tolerance merely because its rounded decimal looks feasible.

\hypertarget{m9-b9-the-economic-decision-fees-and-deployment}{%
\subsection{M9 / B9: The economic decision, fees and
deployment}\label{m9-b9-the-economic-decision-fees-and-deployment}}

Actual implemented cost is the endpoint. For the fine-grid comparison at
allowance \(2^{-16}\), every additional Bernstein charge below
\(0.000496\) is covered by a strictly positive classwide saving in the
normalized investment units. Subtracting a supplied fee from both
cost-difference endpoints gives the full supported, unresolved and
opposite-choice regions. The paper does not substitute a wall-clock
second for a monetary charge or invent an estimated installation cost.

All four calibrated classes retain a positive saving relative to the
zero controller over the entire integrated tolerance domain. The lower
endpoints at its largest allowance are approximately \(0.153634\),
\(0.169562\), \(0.170436\) and \(0.171910\) for coarse ReLU, coarse
Bernstein, fine ReLU and fine Bernstein respectively; authoritative
interval endpoints are rounded outward in the tables. Coarse ReLU versus
its legacy controller remains unresolved. These comparisons concern
policies in the original model, not an unrelated benchmark inserted to
obtain a favorable result.

The inherited priced-selection and deployment-allocation theorems retain
separate loss scales, deployment counts, information prices and compute
charges. The frontier supplies an optimizer-accuracy input to those
decisions. It does not count Monte Carlo rows as deployments or claim
empirically calibrated welfare effects.

\hypertarget{m10-b10-manuscript-organization-and-original-scope}{%
\subsection{M10 / B10: Manuscript organization and original
scope}\label{m10-b10-manuscript-organization-and-original-scope}}

The main article follows the chain from constructive NBO continuation
through original-optimum accuracy, attained qualification, affine
calibration, optimizer-class transfer, economic stopping and actual
policy costs. The new transfer and stopping theorems include their
proofs in the main text. Numerical integration details, complete
tolerance and reference catalogues, and the linear frontier algorithm
are in the Numerical and Development Appendix. Core supplementary proofs
remain where they were.

All 35 inherited active sections remain compiled once, in the same
document, without text changes. The introduction and conclusion are
rewritten to connect the additional theorem to the original paper rather
than repeatedly append revision-status paragraphs. Their prior versions
are preserved. The unintegrated R82 editorial sources are also preserved
before corrections to their execution-status wording. The main title,
author information, original scientific records and old branches are
unchanged.

The broader developments involving diffusions, recursive or endogenous
preferences, temporal selves and games retain their own assumptions in
the preserved companion editions. The present expected-cost identities
are not silently extended to nonlinear recursive preferences. This
preserves the original scientific program without using its breadth to
claim an unproved extension of the new theorem.

\hypertarget{reproduction-and-submission-status}{%
\subsection{Reproduction and submission
status}\label{reproduction-and-submission-status}}

The R83 package provides ordinary LaTeX sources, a point-by-point
response, exact actionwise transfers, the complete rational tolerance
frontiers, source and output hash manifests, and the full build logs.
The final release requires all inherited and new unskipped tests, source
preservation, both new full-bin implementations, four-document
compilation and a clean archive rebuild. Visual inspection is recorded
separately against the actual PDF hashes; a successful compiler is not a
visual certificate or mathematical peer approval.

The submitted addition is a constructive
optimization-to-economic-decision theorem and its complete original-law
numerical realization. It strengthens the paper with positive classwide
gains, nonzero stopping tolerances and explicit recovery and
verification costs. Representation-specific universal superiority,
unqualified hidden-layer fitting reliability, empirically calibrated
fees and an independently regenerated largest vector tree are not
inferred from these results.
