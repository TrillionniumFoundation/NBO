                 1.2. M1 / B1: The contribution and the role of neural construction
20                                                                                            20

21       The original paper now distinguishes three contributions rather than assigning       21

22   all of them to the neural proposal. The constructive NBO produces an attained own- 22
23   future continuation and its feasible action witnesses. A common verifier supplies        23

24   original-optimum accuracy independently of whether a proposed warm start helps. 24
25   A representation-sensitive qualification rule and its optimizer-class transfer deter- 25
26   mine which constructed continuations can be used and what their implemented              26

27   policies cost. The nonlinear investment study compares the returned complete             27

28   controllers, not a neural label attached to an unchanged classical envelope.             28

29       We retain the original title because the constructive neural operator remains a      29

30   substantive object of the paper. Its primitive error account, feasible witnesses, affine– 30
31   ReLU construction and acquired implementation are not replaced with a different          31

32   model or a generic certification framework. At the same time, the actionwise and         32
                                      RESPONSE TO THE REFEREE                             3

1    stopping theorems are available to conventional representations. The numerical            1

2    evidence cannot be converted into a neural advantage by making those common               2

3    theorems representation-specific in the exposition.                                       3

4      The new positive conclusion is that verified approximate optimization can pre- 4
5    serve an identified economic decision even when it does not preserve every acquired       5

6    action. The fine-grid comparison below applies to an entire continuum of qualified        6

7    readout outputs, with an explicit sufficient stopping frontier and no further fitting     7

8    or sampling. Its meaning is stronger than reproducing one fitted coefficient vector       8

9    and different from claiming a universal neural ranking.                                   9

                 1.3. M2 / B2: Strong matched baselines and the qualification objective
12                                                                                             12

13     The retained qualification development includes fixed-feature ReLU and conven- 13
14   tional Bernstein readouts, exact convex primal–dual qualification, matched label- 14
15   loss ablations in their declared construction services and realized whole-policy          15

16   costs. The subsequent affine-calibrated rule isolates a representation-dependent          16

17   qualification charge instead of attributing an outcome solely to a basis name. Both       17

18   own-future dates, feasibility rules, action menus, acquisition resolutions and callable   18

19   identities are deposited.                                                                 19

20     The R80 factorial catalogue compares both bases, both qualification rules and           20

21   both resolutions, together with the zero controller. Calibrated qualification improves    21

22   both bases and reverses their earlier matched cost ordering. Both the earlier ReLU        22

23   advantage under the legacy rule and the Bernstein advantage under the calibrated          23

24   rule remain visible. The calibrated services did not repeat the complete label-loss       24

25   ablation; their metadata state that boundary explicitly. Their construction clocks        25

26   therefore are not used as a matched universal speed comparison with the legacy            26

27   services.                                                                                 27

30   class interval is [0.000496, 0.002503], rounded outward. The original bi- 30
31   nary uncertified-mass transfer gives [-0.421558, 0.424526] from the same                  31

32   attained-cost interval. Individually pricing the possible switches identifies the or- 32

               1.4. M3 / B3: Predictability, validation loss and nonstationary queries
9                                                                                              9

10       The retained predictable-evaluation and protected-query sections address the sep- 10
11   aration between construction information and evaluation observations. Work–loss           11

12   admission is based on the declared validation experiment and timing convention,           12

13   while the sequential results retain their filtration and predictability conditions. An    13

14   original-optimum verifier does not, by itself, establish a population speed advan- 14
15   tage for a trained proposal. The past warnings and unsuccessful admissions remain         15

16   in the compiled appendix.                                                                 16

17       The new stopping frontier addresses a different source of post-selection risk. 17
18   Once the finite attained-policy cost family is covered, deterministic actionwise          18

19   inequalities hold simultaneously for every readout in every enclosed sublevel. The        19

20   frontier may therefore be inspected and a tolerance selected on that same event. No       20

21   extra union bound over an uncountable continuum is needed. This is proved from            21

22   containment, not from treating reused query or cost records as independent.               22

23       Changing the observation law, frozen labels, terminal actor or cost estimand          23

24   is outside that event unless independently qualified. In particular, the theorem          24

25   is not offered as a substitute for prospective validation of a new predictor under        25

26   distribution shift.                                                                       26

                       1.5. M4 / B4: Reuse, amortization and complete work
29                                                                                             29

30       The R72 and R73 retained studies execute longer reuse catalogues with protected       30

31   fallback, query counts and complete service records. Earlier failed reuse and adverse     31

32   timing results are not deleted. Their full phase records, not only the most favorable     32
                                   RESPONSE TO THE REFEREE                                5

1    last block, remain available in the Numerical and Development Appendix. The               1

2    reuse claims retain their declared query law, workload and charging conventions.          2

3      The R83 frontier is an additional precisely defined reuse object. After the action- 3
4    wise integrals have been verified, all tolerance strata are formed by sorting the exact   4

5    switch thresholds and updating cell extrema. With P admitted alternatives and N           5

6    cells this takes O( P log P + N ) arithmetic operations and O( P + N ) storage. Two       6

7    monotone tolerance lists then yield each sufficient two-dimensional frontier with at      7

8    most the sum of their stratum counts in predicate evaluations. Integration work is        8

9    excluded from this incremental count only because it is reported separately and has       9

10   already been paid, not because it is assumed free. Rational bit complexity is also        10

11   distinguished from the arithmetic-operation count.                                        11

12     All four integration rungs are charged. The first completed R83 recovery has its        12

13   own phase, total-verification and memory records; subsequent replay has separately        13

14   named timings. The original failed R82 run retains its printed phase clocks, but those    14

15   partial clocks are not invented into a missing complete-service wall clock. Neither       15

16   the frontier operation count nor the recovery runtime is a matched measurement of         16

17   neural fitting speed.                                                                     17

20                                                                                             20

21     The higher-precision original-law experiment already deposited in R80 uses              21

22   524,288 common bin rows and the declared nine-controller family. Exact conditional        22

23   integration, continuous-bin uncertainty, arithmetic enclosure and bounded-sample          23

24   inference have separate roles. The confidence event is the original preallocated          24

25   event, not a new family inferred from R83 postprocessing. Both conditional and            25

26   ordinary cash-cost estimands remain recorded, as do the control identities and all        26

27   ninety-four declared estimands.                                                           27

28     R83 makes a previously conservative part of the cost transfer explicit. The inde- 28
29   pendent actionwise integration concerns every allowed first-date switch conditional       29

30   on its entire acquisition cell. It preserves the original continuous initial and innova- 30
31   tion laws and the fixed terminal actor. Both arithmetic implementations reproduce         31

32   all 18,270,720 paired integration boxes per implementation, with 15,616 separately        32
     6

1    evaluated rational midpoint checks. The checks supplement rather than replace the         1

2    whole-bin proof. This is a full re-integration, not simply a replay of saved interval     2

3    endpoints.                                                                                3

5    A separate exhaustive product-grid calculation verifies all 12,732 returned and           5

6    excluded stratum-pair tests. For fine ReLU versus fine Bernstein, the strict positive- 6
7    ordering region on the equal-tolerance diagonal extends to an exact rational supre- 7

                      1.7. M6 / B6: Vector controls and horizon dependence
19                                                                                             19

20       The vector-control construction, simplex refinement, derivative bounds and ob- 20
21   served descendant growth remain in their original appendix sections. The new              21

22   actionwise theorem allows any finite horizon for which the conditional contrasts          22

23   with a fixed continuation are enclosed. This is not evidence that the particular vector   23

24   recursion scales favorably in dimension or horizon. Its implementation costs remain       24

25   explicit.                                                                                 25

26       The new executed frontier concerns the original two-date, two-state investment        26

27   economy and its finite robust first-action menus. No newly trained vector-control         27

28   comparison or new sparse high-dimensional experiment is claimed. Those questions          28

29   are not answered by relabeling nonuniform tensor coordinates as a non-tensor              29

30   method, or by treating a smaller sublevel radius as a faster vector solver. The paper     30

31   retains the vector results under their existing hypotheses while the new theorem          31

32   strengthens the optimizer-to-economic-decision connection that is actually tested.        32

                            1.9. M8 / B8: Numerical fitting reliability
25                                                                                                25

26     We distinguish a verifier’s ability to return a safe object, the performance of a          26

27   particular completed fitting service and a population claim about optimizer success. 27
28   The historical multiple-start and warning records remain unchanged. A small seed             28

29   catalogue is not turned into a success probability, and a verified readout after             29

30   numerical fitting does not imply reliability of unrestricted hidden-layer training.          30

31     The new theorem instead provides a deterministic stopping implication. If a feasi- 31
32   ble output has primal value F (θ ) and verified dual value d ≤ inf F, with F (θ ) − d ≤ ϵ,   32
     8

1    then F (θ ) ≤ inf F + ϵ ≤ U + ϵ. Its controller therefore belongs to the covered class. 1
2    A gap pair inside the economic region certifies the intended decision regardless of         2

3    which admissible output the fitting algorithm returned. This is useful without an           3

4    unobserved error norm or a fitted success-frequency model.                                  4

5        The region is the largest one furnished by the supplied rectangular action bounds. 5
6    It is not asserted to be the exact jointly attainable optimizer region, because different   6

7    cellwise switches can require incompatible common coefficients. Strict comparisons          7

8    also require exact boundary handling; an open supremum is not an admissible                 8

9    stopping tolerance merely because its rounded decimal looks feasible.                       9

                     1.10. M9 / B9: The economic decision, fees and deployment                   14

15                                                                                               15

16       Actual implemented cost is the endpoint. For the fine-grid comparison at al- 16
17   lowance 2−16 , every additional Bernstein charge below 0.000496 is covered by a             17

18   strictly positive classwide saving in the normalized investment units. Subtracting          18

19   a supplied fee from both cost-difference endpoints gives the full supported, un- 19
20   resolved and opposite-choice regions. The paper does not substitute a wall-clock            20

21   second for a monetary charge or invent an estimated installation cost.                      21

22       All four calibrated classes retain a positive saving relative to the zero controller    22

23   over the entire integrated tolerance domain. The lower endpoints at its largest             23

24   allowance are approximately 0.153634, 0.169562, 0.170436 and 0.171910 for coarse            24

25   ReLU, coarse Bernstein, fine ReLU and fine Bernstein respectively; authoritative            25

26   interval endpoints are rounded outward in the tables. Coarse ReLU versus its legacy         26

27   controller remains unresolved. These comparisons concern policies in the original           27

28   model, not an unrelated benchmark inserted to obtain a favorable result.                    28

29       The inherited priced-selection and deployment-allocation theorems retain sepa- 29
30   rate loss scales, deployment counts, information prices and compute charges. The            30

31   frontier supplies an optimizer-accuracy input to those decisions. It does not count         31

32   Monte Carlo rows as deployments or claim empirically calibrated welfare effects.            32
