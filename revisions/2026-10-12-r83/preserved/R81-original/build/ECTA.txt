                                            1. INTRODUCTION
23                                                                                               23

24     A continuation approximation matters because it produces an economic decision. 24
25   Prediction error, original-optimum policy loss and the resources required to verify         25

26   that loss are different objects. This paper connects them through a constructive            26

27   Neural Bellman Operator (NBO), its attained own-future continuation and the                 27

28   complete feasible controller that is implemented under the original economic law.           28

29     The operator first fits each date against its own attained future and retains feasible    29

30   action witnesses. Its affine–ReLU realization and primitive error budget do not re- 30
31                                                                                               31

32     Qian QI: qiqian@pku.edu.cn                                                                32
     2

1    quire an unrestricted nonconvex optimizer to succeed. A complementary recursive          1

2    implementation queries the original Bellman problem at the states needed by action       2

3    search. Primitive curvature, verified endpoint bounds and protected conventional         3

4    refinement determine its validity; a trained proposal changes only optional query        4

5    locations. This distinction separates the constructive neural continuation from the      5

6    performance of a neural warm start.                                                      6

7        A qualified shallow continuation can be evaluated without the original-value         7

8    descendant tree. Its residual oscillation and implemented greedy gaps control            8

9    original-optimum policy loss, while exact conditional feature integration supplies       9

10   the evaluation primitives. Fixed-label qualification readouts have finite optimization   10

11   certificates. Purchased Bellman information can also be reused subject to verified       11

12   state displacement, capacity repair, retention and precision contracts. These con- 12
13   structions are retained together with their actual work accounts; the absence of a       13

14   tensor state table does not erase horizon dependence.                                    14

15       We address two additional questions about the attained continuation. First, does     15

16   a qualification bound charge a common represented function consistently across           16

17   bases? Coefficientwise bounds can differ even on identical affine functions. We          17

18   construct derivative-control qualification that is exact on the affine intersection of   18

19   the ReLU and Bernstein classes and retains the original residual and policy-loss         19

20   account. The construction is convex and admits exact primal–dual checks. It does         20

21   not assert that the nonlinear function classes or their numerical conditioning are       21

22   identical.                                                                               22

23       Second, can the economic conclusion survive changes in the numerical opti- 23
24   mizer’s output? For each robust alternative action, a rational dual certificate bounds   24

25   the qualification objective required to change the implemented choice. A strict          25

26   lower barrier certifies the action throughout an entire objective sublevel. With a       26

27   fixed terminal actor in the two-date construction, this yields a complete-policy and     27

28   original-law cost statement. Partially certified covers instead give an explicit cost    28

29   enlargement through their initial-law probability. This is an optimization-to-policy     29

30   transfer, not a fitting-seed success probability.                                        30

31       The prospective factorial experiment fixes both bases, both qualification rules      31

32   and both observation resolutions before evaluating nine complete controllers. Both       32
                                NEURAL BELLMAN OPERATORS                                3

1    own-future fits are constructed under the declared rule. Two independent whole-bin      1

2    implementations and exact rational checks qualify the continuous-law cost obser- 2
3    vations. The calibration improves the attained ReLU and Bernstein policies, but         3

4    their matched ranking reverses. The earlier ReLU advantage under coefficientwise        4

5    qualification and the new Bernstein advantage under calibrated qualification are        5

6    both retained. The comparison identifies an operational qualification mechanism         6

7    rather than attributing a ranking to a representation name.                             7

8      The attained-cost event also supports conditional economic choices. Joint             8

9    marginal and paired intervals have sharp difference-constraint and deployment- 9
10   allocation interpretations. Where action stability holds throughout a readout class,    10

11   the same cost intervals and conditional charge regions apply to that class without      11

12   a new union bound over optimizers. Monetary decisions still require a loss scale,       12

13   deployment count and resource charges; the experiment does not invent those             13

14   inputs or count simulation rows as economic deployments.                                14

16     Relation to existing methods. Lipschitz extensions and distance transforms under- 16
17   lie the constructive envelope (McShane, 1934, Felzenszwalb and Huttenlocher, 2012). 17
18   Approximate and safe policy iteration have established error analyses (Munos, 2003,     18

19   Pirotta et al., 2013, Scherrer et al., 2015), as does convex dynamic-programming        19

20   approximation (Yang, 2020). Learned warm starts and optimization-based control          20

21   supply related numerical methods (Sakaue and Oki, 2023, Sambharya, Hall, Amos,          21

22   and Stellato, 2023, Agrawal, Barratt, Boyd, and Stellato, 2020). We retain these con- 22
23   nections without claiming that a common verifier establishes the incremental value      23

24   of its neural proposal.                                                                 24

25     Value-function control variates and telescoping identities are established evalua- 25
26   tion tools (Munos, 2006, Jiang and Li, 2016). Time-uniform concentration and off- 26
27   policy confidence sequences have their own theory (Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe,           27

28   and Sekhon, 2020, Karampatziakis, Mineiro, and Ramdas, 2021). Our original-law          28

29   costs include the numerical qualification required to use such bounds for an im- 29
30   plemented controller. Minimax-regret decisions have an established economic role        30

31   (Manski, 2004); shortest-path difference constraints are classical (Floyd, 1962). The   31
