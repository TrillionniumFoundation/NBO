\section{Introduction}\label{sec:r24intro}
A numerical method for dynamic economics must specify its policy's information, the optimization mechanism that generates it, and the economic error certified for deployment. These are separate questions. A policy can use information stored at initialization while remaining admissible in the original economy. Parameterizations can represent identical policies but induce different optimization paths. A sharper upper comparison need not improve the policy being compared. We develop Neural Bellman Operators with these distinctions explicit.

The application is the stopped consumption--portfolio economy with costly preference adjustment in Section~\ref{sec:economy}. We retain its continuous actions, correlated shocks, settlement, complete state--time domain and uniform regret objective of $.01$. The current-state actor and financed expert construction are different implementations. The former maps $(t,u,x)$ to actions. The latter uses initial interpolation weights and an observable common pricing factor to reconstruct financed accounts. Its regional certificates concern an augmented-state feedback policy, not a feedback map on the original state alone.

Our first result supplies the dynamic-programming formulation of this implementation. The decoder has a finite-dimensional Markov realization with constant initial weights and one common factor. Conditional prices reconstruct expert wealth without independently simulated subaccounts. We give the generator, boundary condition and Bellman semigroup. Under the original full Brownian information structure, unrestricted augmentation preserves economic value: memory supplies no additional future information. This equality does not imply optimality of a restricted expert library or accuracy throughout the complete original domain.

Our second result identifies the optimization mechanism after finite-node class equality. The financed family has a $47$-dimensional quotient chart, while the neural generator has $355$ parameters. Its Jacobian induces a local output metric. With momentum, Adam transports historical gradients through historical Jacobians; its update is not generally the current output gradient multiplied by $JD^{-1}J'$. We prove the correct identity and Taylor remainder and state when a simpler metric interpretation is justified. Experiments include explicit diagonal, tangent-metric and whitening charts for direct optimization, alongside direct and neural Adam and L-BFGS-B.

The numerical study uses disjoint tuning and a prospectively fixed design. Each first-order method has equal tuning-call allocation but separately measured time. Evaluation crosses two new seeds, two proposal quadrature rules and five certification checkpoints. All configurations, failures and carry-forward decisions remain. A four-cell cover uses nine genuinely distinct experts per arm. A deterministic reference with an analytic optimum separates optimization error from approximation, temporal quadrature and arithmetic uncertainty.

A third analytical result propagates price-equation and derivative errors into the reduced payoff gradient. Root conditioning and derivative accuracy enter separately. A small price residual alone cannot provide the required derivative bounds. Executed two-rule differences at frozen policies are useful diagnostics, not rigorous errors for the gradient of the original stopped payoff.

The measured comparison in Section~\ref{sec:r24results} includes direct-method advantages. Tuned neural Adam reaches a central-state bound below $.001926$, while direct L-BFGS-B is more accurate and cheaper. Nine neural experts certify the expanded four-cell region below $.004281$. A transferred-rate reference case and neural L-BFGS-B failures prevent a universal neural-superiority interpretation. These results strengthen the method's mathematical definition and empirical identification without replacing the original objective by an easier one.

The current complete-domain current-state bound remains $7.241462443133396$, and no separate payoff improvement for that jointly changed actor is established. The retained residual-correction construction does not require exact replication; successful policy-sensitive execution of that construction remains a further numerical requirement. A local certificate or an analytic reference does not discharge it.

The background includes numerical economics and diffusion approximation in \citet{Judd1998,KushnerDupuis2001,BarlesSouganidis1991}, state coverage in \citet{BrummScheidegger2017}, unrestricted upper comparisons in \citet{BrownSmithSun2010,BrownSmith2011}, and neural control constructions in \citet{HanE2016,HanJentzenE2018}. Our contribution is the explicit relation between policy information, financed output geometry and certified economic performance. Earlier derivations, experiments and adverse outcomes are retained in historical appendices and supplements, rather than deleted or represented as observations from this new study.
