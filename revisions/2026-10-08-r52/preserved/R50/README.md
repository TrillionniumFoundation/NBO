## Substantive additions

The reference-policy improvement theorem converts signed evaluation bands into a centered advantage bound. A proposal is accepted only when the whole acquired-state cell satisfies the feasible nonincrease gate; otherwise the exact incumbent action is retained. Its quantitative expected-cost version uses the implemented-policy occupancy law. Native min-plus compilation and its affine–ReLU representation are stated as identical computations, not competing methods.

The executed specialization analytically integrates the original investment economy's terminal cost under continuous uniform uncertainty and improves only the final action. Both witness and FVI receive this same safety gate. The experiments separately charge construction, planning, every failed refinement, policy deployment and joint direct-cost evaluation. The new finite-catalogue net-cost theorem accounts for a specified information/resource technology without treating a conservative bound minimizer as the true ex post optimizer.

## Executed evidence

The common-accuracy amendment contains 18 new executions and 21 charged attempts, with all 18 reaching the common target five in dimensions two, three and four. Repetitions reproduce the same mathematical checkpoints; they are not independent optimizer draws.
