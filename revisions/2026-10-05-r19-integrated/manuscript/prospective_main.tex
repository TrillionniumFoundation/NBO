\section{Prospective Work to an Economic Tolerance}\label{sec:r19experiment}
\subsection{Temporary withdrawal charges in the capital economy}
The new design keeps the original nonlinear capital production, common and
idiosyncratic innovations, and terminal dispersion preference. It asks how
an economist should compute current withdrawals under temporary resource
charges, current utility weights, adjustment terms, and feasible caps. The
future reference withdrawal is fixed at $.55$. Current task changes are
therefore precisely changes for which the same continuation can be reused.
The transport theorem characterizes additional future changes, but those
perturbations are not silently added to the experimental population.

There are four periods of length $h=.25$. All integrated coefficients and
sixteen stored innovation paths are exact finite-model inputs, each path
having mass $1/16$. Let $\overline y$ denote the coordinate average and set
$f(y)=\lambda\tanh\{(1-\mu)y+\mu\overline y\bm1\}$, with
$\lambda=.08$ and $\mu=.20$. For uniform withdrawal $a\in[.05,\bar a]$,
\begin{align}
 x(y,a)&=y+h\{c_0\bm1+f(y)-a\bm1\},\notag\\
 Q_\theta(y,a)&=A_0\{w\log a-\eta a^2/2-\tau a\}-W_0a
                    +S_0(x(y,a)).\label{eq:r19capitaltask}
\end{align}
The stored $A_k,W_k$ correspond to $\rho=.04$, and $c_0$ includes the
original volatility correction. Subsequent transitions use the same
production map, the fixed withdrawal, and the remaining stored path
increments. Subsequent rewards and the terminal quadratic dispersion payoff
are all included in $S_0$; the current reward in
\eqref{eq:r19capitaltask} is not counted twice.

The ex ante task generator uses $w\in[.8,1.2]$, $\eta\in[.05,.15]$,
$\tau\in[0,.12]$, and $\bar a\in[.65,.95]$, independently uniform.
Initial coordinate means lie in $[-.5,.5]$ and dispersions in $[.05,.5]$.
The complete generator and the two innovation arrays are recorded in the
protocol. These ranges define a transparent counterfactual exercise rather
than an estimated distribution of tax requests. The six query sets are
fixed nested prefixes of 1,024 tasks in each dimension. A separate service
is actually executed for every prefix.

The economic effect of the charge is explicit. Where the true maximizing
action is interior, strict concavity implies
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial a^*(\tau)}{\partial\tau}
             =\frac{A_0}{Q_\theta''(y,a^*(\tau))}<0.
 \label{eq:r19charge}
\end{equation}
The constrained optimizer is nonincreasing in $\tau$ as well. The computation
therefore concerns the scale of a withdrawal response with an analytically
known direction, not a change of leaderboard alone. The reported zero-charge
counterfactual uses the same fitted object and changes only the current
charge. For a certified loss bound $U_i$, an external log-utility grant
$g_i=\exp\{U_i/(A_0w_i)\}-1$ supplies utility $U_i$ without changing
production or the future policy. This is the economic normalization of the
$10^{-4}$ tolerance; it is not an endogenous consumption-equivalent policy
computed under altered state dynamics. Complete withdrawal and compensation
records appear in Table~\ref{tab:r19economic}.

\subsection{Frozen procedures, stopping, and information rights}
The original implementation and finite economic inputs were frozen before
the registered study. This integration recovers that exact source and
executes the same fixed design. Its clock is a new machine observation,
not an additional independent training population. No seed, architecture,
budget, or tolerance is selected using the integrated outcomes. The readable
execution source, digest history, and complete records accompany the paper.

The seven procedures are a current-reward rule, quadratic continuation
regression, radial-basis continuation regression, NBO, a shared
state-and-task actor, cached sample-average optimization, and full-support
enumeration. The two fitting stages use respectively 64 and 192 training
states, 4 and 16 independently sampled cached paths, and 64 and 192 neural
optimization epochs. Current-only and enumerated methods require one stage.
Every stage is assessed on the true finite-law objective. The service stops
at its first mean certified loss at most $10^{-4}$; otherwise its final
outcome remains unresolved. No fallback substitutes a different method.

Quadratic, radial-basis, and NBO predictors share the sampled continuation
value and derivative labels. NBO fits one scalar potential using a
32--32 tanh network plus the known terminal quadratic component. The
conventional regressions use ridge-regularized value-and-derivative fitting;
the radial-basis regression uses 32 centers. The shared actor receives state,
weight, adjustment, charge, and cap and optimizes current plus cached future
payoff without a separate scalar critic. SAA reuses its valid innovation
cache and reevaluates at each queried postdecision state. The enumerated
procedure uses all sixteen atoms; this structure-exploiting comparator is
not withheld because it can outperform learned approximations.

The procedure's action search and the verifier are separate. A fitted
surrogate need not be concave, and its derivative-based action search is not
advertised as a proof of its own global maximum. Instead, every returned
feasible action is checked by \eqref{eq:r19residual} for the \emph{true}
objective. Section~\ref{app:r19curvature} derives the uniform curvature
bound and the outward derivative recursion, including nonlinear production
and terminal dispersion. The proof covers the whole scalar interval.

A process records initialization, fitting and cache creation, all action
queries, every accuracy check, and durable stage writes before reading its
stop clock. Failed earlier stages remain charged. Timing metadata is measured
separately and added to the service bill. Imports and other common startup
are charged once to the complete comparison, not assigned fictitious
per-method observations. Post-stop mechanism diagnostics do not influence
the stopping rule and have their own recorded cost. The final aggregate
serialization and publication build lie outside the scientific service clock.

\subsection{Symmetric prediction and decision diagnostics}
For every scalar predictor, we compute exact finite-law continuation means
at common exogenous lower, middle, and upper feasible actions. We report
absolute squared error, error centered across that common action set, and
absolute error at the predictor's own implemented action. We also report the
true payoff loss from maximizing the predicted objective over the common
three-action menu, and binary and menu misclassification frequencies.
The fitting and Raw caches are regenerated across the three complete
streams; stream-level records remain available. Current-only and direct
actor methods do not define scalar predictors and are not assigned invented
continuation risks.

These diagnostics repair the action-distribution asymmetry of the earlier
NBO/reference contrast assessment. They remain descriptive finite-design

cache or training draw. Prediction errors are evaluated against enumerated

Neither connection licenses substituting a smaller average prediction risk
