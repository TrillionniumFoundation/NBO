\section{Direct economic comparisons and constrained execution}\label{sec:evidence47}
The additional study tests three distinct objects: the actual values of the original scalar witness policies, a constrained multidimensional construction, and the cost of representing an identical envelope. Protocols and an implementation amendment were committed before catalogue execution. They are prospective relative to these executions, not an independently registered experiment. Previous scalar and coupled observations, including unfavorable results, retain their original identities and interpretation.

\subsection{Values of the original witness policies}
For every common successful first crossing in the R46 witness study, we compare all three unordered pairs of witness, same-critic nearest-node, and spline policies. There are ten economic-cell/target objects and thirty contrasts. No failing target is reclassified. Repetition-zero checkpoints are hash-bound to their original records; timing repetitions are not counted as independently trained policies. Initial capital is uniform on the entire state interval, rather than restricted to four diagnostic states.

The estimand is the expected difference of realized discounted costs. On 262,144 common paths per object, we enclose state propagation, innovation uncertainty and all compatible action indices. Each continuous uniform draw is represented by a uniformly selected 48-bit bin; the enclosure covers every position within it. Downward and upward $2^{-32}$ endpoint rounding permits exact integer reconstruction of the endpoint means and variances. Empirical-Bernstein bounds \citep{maurer2009} give simultaneous intervals for all thirty contrasts with familywise error $0.05$, conditional on the stated IID simulation model. The seed identifies the reproducible observation and is not a proof of independence.

Before execution we fixed a comparison margin of $0.005$ discounted cost units. It is a numerical decision tolerance in the original, uncalibrated normalization, not an estimated consumption-equivalent welfare threshold. All thirty intervals lie inside this margin; every interval also contains zero. Thus the study identifies neither a cheaper policy nor exact equality. It does establish, at the stated coverage and under the uniform initial-state distribution, that the differences are smaller than the prespecified decision tolerance. The largest absolute endpoint is approximately $0.0006491$. This is a direct economic comparison, not a comparison of regret upper bounds.

\subsection{Coupled investment with endogenous capacity}
Consider $d$ capital coordinates, a scalar investment action and cyclic indices. For $x\in[0,1]^d$, define
\begin{align}\label{eq:model47}
 0\le a\le b(x)&:=\tfrac14+\tfrac1{4d}\sum_{i=1}^d x_i,\\
 F_i(x,a,z)&:=\tfrac1{16}+\tfrac{x_i}{2}+\tfrac{x_{i+1}^2}{8}
                         +\tfrac a4+\tfrac z{16},\notag\\
 c_p(x,a)&:=\tfrac1{8d}\sum_{i=1}^d(x_i-\tfrac12)^2+\tfrac p2a^2,\notag\\
 g(x)&:=\left(1-\tfrac1d\sum_{i=1}^dx_i\right)^2
                     +\tfrac1{8d}\sum_{i=1}^d(x_i-x_{i+1})^2.\notag
\end{align}
Here $z$ is a common uniform innovation on $[-1,1]$, independent across dates, and $\beta=15/16$. The state domain is invariant: all feasible transitions lie in $[0,7/8]^d$. Nonlinearity enters capital propagation; neighboring capital affects each transition; and the admissible investment interval changes with aggregate capital. This is a numerical economic laboratory, not an estimated model.

The benchmark closes the formerly abstract primitive obligations. Feasible node actions are exact integer downward roundings of $jb(x)/N$, $j=0,\ldots,N$. Their cover radius is at most $1/(4N)+2^{-20}$. Midpoint quadrature uses $M=2N$ bins under the original continuous law, with remainder $\beta L_{t+1}d/(32M)$. The terminal quadratic is used directly. Primitive moduli and finite-precision allowances are derived in the supplement, rather than supplied by an optimization-success flag.

We execute $d\in\{2,3\}$, $T\in\{2,4\}$ and $p\in\{1/4,1\}$. The state ladders are $(4,8,16)$ in two dimensions and $(2,4,8)$ in three. The witness backend is compared with conventional multilinear fitted-value iteration; each method constructs its own future labels. The comparator receives an own-critic Lipschitz bound and a strengthened one-sided repaired-actor bound evaluated on all half-grid corners and tied nearest nodes. This is an executed fitted-value comparator, not an implementation of an adaptive sparse grid. Adaptive sparse-grid methods such as \citet{brumm2017} remain a distinct comparison class.

Three isolated process repetitions give 48 services and 144 rungs. Every rung starts from primitives; complete prefixes include earlier rungs, integration, compilation, actor construction, certification and durable checkpoints. Process startup is reported separately. Thread count and CPU affinity are fixed; CPU frequency is not controlled. Repetition checkpoints are identical, so repetitions measure clocks, not a distribution of optimizer outcomes.

All 24 services per method certify the target $1$; at $3/4$, witness attains 18/24 and fitted-value iteration 24/24; at $1/2$, each attains 12/24. Neither attains $1/4$ within this cap. Across the dense predeclared grid $j/32$, there are 363 common successful target-by-repetition comparisons. Fitted-value iteration reaches an earlier rung in 54, and the rungs coincide in 309; witness is never earlier. Witness is faster in 147 of these local clock comparisons. Because nearby targets often select the same checkpoint, these counts are descriptive, not independent trials. The conventional bound is tighter at all eight finest economic cells. These outcomes are part of the method comparison, not grounds for deleting the construction or the comparator.

The deployment executes nontrivial capacity repair. In the direct multidimensional simulations, 1,042,306 date-policy-path actions are reduced to satisfy the acquired-state capacity contract. The 12-bit and 20-bit policies carry their separate error allowances; finer acquisition does not eliminate action quantization. No simulated path has an unresolved acquisition ambiguity in this observation. Dedicated tests nevertheless force ambiguous cells and check the conservative action enclosure. Absence of an observed ambiguity is not the feasibility proof; integer repair and Theorem~\ref{thm:acquired47} provide that proof.

\subsection{Policy value, representation and the resource cap}
At the finest fixed rung, we compare witness and fitted-value policies on 65,536 common continuous-law paths per economic cell, starting uniformly on $[0,1]^d$. These are the implemented 12-bit policies, not ideal exact-state actors. All eight direct intervals lie inside the same prespecified $0.005$ margin and contain zero. The largest absolute endpoint is approximately $0.001001$. This eight-contrast family has its own $0.05$ error account; we do not assert joint 95 percent coverage for the union of the thirty and eight contrasts.

For the same frozen cone object, compiled native queries are approximately 35.3--68.8 times faster than flat native queries in the 24 date-level representation observations. The cone counts per query fall from 289 to four in two dimensions and from 729 to eight in three. The compiled native backend is faster than the ReLU minimum-gate backend in all 24 observations. Accordingly, the speedup is an exact compilation result shared across representations, not a neural-specific advantage or an end-to-end service speedup. The two backends have overlapping outward value enclosures on every test query; the exact equality is established by Proposition~\ref{prop:compiler47}, not by these samples.

A finite unsuccessful rung does not contradict deterministic termination with sufficient resources. In this benchmark, the primitive recurrence admits $L_t\le3/d$ and $D_t^Q\le77/64$. Let $S_T=\sum_{t<T}\beta^t$, let $b$ and $a$ be acquisition and action bits, and let $\epsilon_{\rm ar}$ bound numerical query error excluding quadrature. A sufficient uniform budget is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cap47}
 G\le S_T\left\{\frac{1735}{512N}
     +\frac{845}{256}2^{-b}+\frac{77}{32}2^{-a}
     +2\epsilon_{\rm ar}+\nu\right\}.
\end{equation}
Choosing each of the five terms below $\varepsilon/(5S_T)$ supplies an explicit cap as a function of tolerance, horizon, dimension and precision. In particular $N=O(S_T/\varepsilon)$ and $K=(N+1)^d$; the covering burden is not removed by ReLU representation. Arbitrarily small tolerances require precision to increase as well. Equation~\eqref{eq:cap47} is a sufficient-budget theorem, not an assertion that the published fixed ladder contains that budget.

The economic implication of the new direct comparisons is limited but identified: within the stated initial-state distributions and numerical margin, switching among these certified implementations does not materially change expected cost at the declared tolerance. This permits resource and feasibility considerations to enter the implementation choice without inferring equality from an insignificant difference. It does not show that neural computation is indispensable, calibrate a welfare policy, or transfer expectation-specific comparisons to nonlinear recursive utility. Those broader formulations retain the application-specific theory given earlier.
