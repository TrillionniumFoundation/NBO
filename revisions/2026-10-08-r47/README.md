## New results

The exact tensor compiler preserves original action witnesses off grid and reduces a continuation query to at most 2^d cones. A new theorem certifies policies with finite state acquisition, outward numerical selection and robust feasible action repair. Primitive moduli, continuous-law quadrature, cover sizes, arithmetic precision and storage yield an explicit sufficient resource cap. The shared distance-transform compiler is not claimed as neural-exclusive.

The completed constrained study has 48 services, 144 rungs, two and three coupled states, nonlinear dynamics, continuous uncertainty and endogenous action capacity. A strengthened conventional multilinear fitted-value comparator forms its own future labels. All finite-cap failures and complete prefix work remain visible. Three timing repetitions have identical policy/certificate hashes, not independent optimizer draws.

Thirty direct comparisons of original R46 policies and eight of the new deployed constrained policies all have intervals inside the fixed ±0.005 numerical expected-cost margin. Every interval contains zero: no cost ordering or exact equality is established. Each comparison family has its own 95% simultaneous coverage under the stipulated IID simulation model; no combined 38-comparison 95% claim is made. The margin is not a calibrated welfare threshold.

The native compiler is 35.3–68.8 times faster than flat native queries in the local same-object observations, while compiled native is faster than the ReLU minimum-gate backend in all 24 observations. Those are query-engine comparisons, not neural-specific or end-to-end service speedups. Conventional fitted-value bounds are tighter in all eight finest constrained cells. The original unfavorable comparisons and precision results are retained.
