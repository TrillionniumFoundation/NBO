\subsection{Absolute accuracy in the original control problem}

\label{sec:r15absolute}

Let $\nu$ be the declared uniform distribution over the nine initial profiles, and let $J_\nu^0$ denote the analytical schedule's payoff. The pointwise anchor account in \eqref{eq:r10anchorbound} implies $V_\nu^*-J_\nu^0\le B_{d,\nu}$ after averaging over this initial population. The repeated worker records concern this same bound. We verify their profiles and component weights and retain the maximum of the already averaged upper bounds if their final rounding differs.

For a method $m$, write $\overline J_{d,m}=16^{-1}\sum_s J_\nu(\pi_{m,s})$. On the simultaneous confirmation event, its existing gain lower endpoint satisfies $\overline J_{d,m}-J_\nu^0\ge L_{d,m}$. Consequently,

\begin{equation}\label{eq:r15methodregret}
0\le V_\nu^*-\overline J_{d,m}\le B_{d,\nu}-L_{d,m}.
\end{equation}

The optimum in this expression ranges over the original adapted action-box class. The returned methods use the declared common tube, which is a feasible subset. The inequality therefore accounts for absolute loss against the original economic problem. It does not identify the unobserved loss or require another probability allocation.

The reported full-class upper bounds range from 0.025971 to 0.038358 payoff units. Their ratios to the declared margin range from 259.8 to 383.6. Thus the current absolute account gives guarantees at those explicit tolerances. It does not certify proximity to the full optimum within the $10^{-4}$ margin, even when a relative method comparison is resolved.

Table~\ref{tab:r15absolute} puts this absolute account next to the economic margin used for direct method comparisons. A positive gain lower endpoint establishes improvement on the schedule; a direct comparison resolves a difference between two trained methods; and a small right-hand side of \eqref{eq:r15methodregret} would establish proximity to the full optimum. These implications concern different quantities. The neural HJB comparator supplies an independent candidate construction, while the scalar calculation supplies a separate finite-grid accuracy account. Neither supplies an exact high-dimensional reference value.
