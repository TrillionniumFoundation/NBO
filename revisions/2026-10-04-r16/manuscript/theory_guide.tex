\subsection{Evaluation, feasible improvement, and approximation}
\label{sec:r16theoryguide}
The algorithm produces a continuation approximation and a feasible policy. Its economic interpretation requires an error account for each. The general results in technical Section~\ref{sec:theory} bound the consequence of a fixed-policy evaluation error and an incomplete feasible maximization under the stated comparison and boundary conditions. The positive-weight Bellman formulation also provides a monotone approximation route when classical derivatives are unavailable. The complete statements and proofs remain current in the supplement.

The following capital economy permits a more operational account. Its payoff identity follows the implemented action through the original diffusion. Its finite Bellman identity measures continuation error along the candidate's actual action change. This specialization makes it possible to compare a learned continuation with a cached simulation continuation using the same economic decision and a common assessment design.
