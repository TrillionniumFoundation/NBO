\subsection*{B1. The learned Bellman critic still has no demonstrated incremental economic value}\label{resp:B1}

The earlier NBO--Raw and NBO--DPO intervals remain unresolved at the stated margin, and Raw's lower complete work at the fifty-dimensional online target remains visible in Section~\ref{sec:r16historicalevidence}. A gain over the schedule, a sharper deterministic transfer, or superiority to one HJB implementation is not presented as a substitute for incremental value of the learned continuation.

The completed menu provides the direct result. At the final prescribed stage, NBO's lower payoff advantage over the materialized Raw actor is $0.001802$ in Long and $0.000147$ in Intermediate, both in dimension fifty and rounded downward. The corresponding lower advantages over direct policy are $0.000327$ and $0.000130$. All four exceed the prospectively fixed margin $10^{-4}$. Neither of these economic cells had an outcome pilot. NBO also uses less complete construction and query work than direct policy in both cells. Table~\ref{tab:r16menucomparisons} reports every final-cell direct comparison, and the technical supplement retains all three budgets and all 216 protected endpoints.

The Raw alternatives remain distinct. Cached SAA attains economically equivalent final-budget payoffs within $\pm10^{-4}$ in six of eight cells, where NBO uses less construction and query work. SAA has materially higher payoffs in both Long cells. The vector and Raw actors are cheaper than NBO in every final cell. The contribution is therefore a demonstrated economic benefit of the declared fitted-continuation procedure in specific repeated decisions, together with explicit cost and accuracy comparisons. It is not an inferred reduction in independently measured critic risk or an assertion that NBO is the cheapest possible Raw implementation.
