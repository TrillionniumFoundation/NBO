\subsection{A signed Bellman account}\label{sec:r16signedreading}
Consider the finite held-action economy underlying the candidate-occupation bridge. At date $k$, let $\pi_k$ be the reference action, let $a_k$ be the selected candidate action, and write $x_b=z+h(c\bm1+f(z)-b)$. The true continuation is $S_k(x)=\E F_k(x;\xi)$, where $F_k$ excludes the current reward and follows the reference thereafter. Write $\widehat S_k$ for the frozen learned continuation. Draw a fresh candidate trajectory, a uniform date $K\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$, and independent antithetic continuation arrays. Use the same array at the two action endpoints. With $\overline F_K$ their average, define
\begin{align*}
 M&=N\{\ell_K(z,a_K)-\ell_K(z,\pi_K)
             +\widehat S_K(x_{a_K})-\widehat S_K(x_{\pi_K})\},\\
 C&=N\{\widehat S_K(x_{a_K})-\widehat S_K(x_{\pi_K})
             -\overline F_K(x_{a_K})+\overline F_K(x_{\pi_K})\},\\
 G&=M-C.
\end{align*}
Thus the learned continuation cancels \emph{before} clipping or statistical inference. The cancellation is an identity of the constructed observation, not an approximation requiring a small critic error.

\begin{proposition}[Signed occupation welfare]\label{prop:r16signedreading}
Conditional on the fitted candidate and reference, $\E G$ equals their finite Bellman payoff difference. If $L_G$ is a valid lower confidence endpoint for this expectation, $D_{\rm hold}$ bounds the reference holding deficit, and $E_{a,h}^{\rm all}$ is the established paired continuous-payoff transfer, then on that event
\[
 J(a)-J(m^0)\ge L_G-D_{\rm hold}-E_{a,h}^{\rm all}.
\]
This conclusion does not require the norm-based continuation-error lower account to be positive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Conditional expectation of the common-noise endpoint difference is the corresponding difference in the true continuation. Summing the one-step advantage over the candidate's occupation law telescopes to its finite payoff minus the reference finite payoff. Uniform selection of $K$ accounts for the factor $N$. The holding and paired-transfer bounds then give the displayed inequality. Proposition~\ref{prop:r16signedbridge} and the associated range argument in the supplement supply the complete finite-economy, antithetic, clipping, and arithmetic construction used in the experiment.
\end{proof}

The shared observation avoids the loss from estimating $M$ and $C$ separately and subtracting two marginal endpoints. It does not make the critic irrelevant to the \emph{construction} of the candidate: the fitted actor has already used it. Nor does it prove that an alternative constructor, such as cached Raw, would produce a worse candidate. Those comparisons require direct paired method evidence.
