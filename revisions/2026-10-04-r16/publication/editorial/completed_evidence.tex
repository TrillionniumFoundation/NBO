\subsection{The economic return to reusable continuation}
\label{sec:r16completedmenu}
We next compare repeated current decisions with a common continuation, the object identified in Section~\ref{sec:r16menu}. Temporary interventions vary current utility weights, adjustment costs, and action constraints; future primitives and the reference policy remain fixed. This is a finite-period capital economy with exactly specified stored coefficients, not a discretization result inferred without continuous-time transfer. Four designs comprise the original low-volatility anchor, the disclosed quarterly-reuse pilot, an unpiloted longer continuation, and untouched intermediate primitives. All are reported in both dimensions.

The five methods are scalar NBO, a fitted vector costate, a materialized Raw actor, a task-conditioned direct actor, and sample-average optimization with its complete innovation cache. All retain legitimate reusable data across three predetermined work stages. Each comparison averages every one of sixteen fixed streams and 384 fixed current queries. Each query has 512 independent antithetic future pairs, shared across methods; an opposite-sign pair is one observation. The 216-event family has its own probability budget $0.018$. No stage, seed, or economic design is removed after confirmation.

\input{revisions/2026-10-04-r16/results/continuation_menu/report/table_menu_comparisons.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:r16menucomparisons} displays the predetermined third stage; Appendix~\ref{app:r16menurecord} retains all three. The long fifty-dimensional case gives NBO-minus-vector, NBO-minus-Raw-actor, and NBO-minus-direct-actor intervals contained in $[16.38,18.13]$, $[18.02,19.72]$, and $[3.27,4.25]$, respectively, in units of $10^{-4}$. The untouched intermediate fifty-dimensional case also gives material advantages over the Raw and direct actors. These are direct economic comparisons, not an inference from a small critic residual. They concern the specified methods and complete finite menu; they do not assert that every Raw procedure must be worse.

Cached sample-average optimization is a particularly informative alternative. In six of the eight final-stage cases, the entire direct interval lies within $[-10^{-4},10^{-4}]$, establishing practical equivalence at the fixed margin. This is stronger than merely observing an interval that contains zero. In the two long-continuation cases the NBO-minus-sample-average intervals are instead contained in $[-1.99,-1.22]$ and $[-2.68,-1.89]$, in units of $10^{-4}$: the sample-average method has a material payoff advantage. The ten-dimensional long case also favors the direct actor in sign, without resolving the $10^{-4}$ noninferiority question.

The work comparison completes, rather than replaces, the payoff comparison. NBO uses less complete third-stage construction and query work than cached sample-average optimization in every case; this gives a work saving within the equivalence band in the six cases just identified. For example, the quarterly and untouched intermediate fifty-dimensional cases use 14.34 versus 62.90 seconds and 12.70 versus 79.46 seconds per stream. The Raw actor is cheaper than NBO, so the material payoff comparisons do not imply simultaneous dominance in both coordinates. The independent confirmation and scalar audit constitute a shared measured bundle, retained in full and charged conservatively in the complete account. No unobserved standalone verification cost is manufactured by dividing that bundle among methods. Earlier-stage work is a saved-prefix account with disclosed overhead, not an executed economic stopping time.

Absolute accuracy is separately assessed on the prespecified continuous scalar action segment at one fixed high-dimensional state. The independent $0.002$ family retains all 128 statements, each based on 65,536 antithetic pairs. Eighty candidates meet the $10^{-4}$ whole-segment margin: all streams in both original and quarterly dimensions and in the intermediate ten-dimensional case. Forty-eight do not. The upward-rounded largest bounds include $0.0000141$ for quarterly dimension fifty, $0.0002846$ for intermediate dimension fifty, and $0.1963168$ for the long fifty-dimensional case. Table~\ref{tab:r16menuscalar} retains every case. These are conditional accuracy guarantees for the declared scalar class, not full-vector or adapted-control near-optimality statements.

\subsection{A strengthened HJB comparison across economic designs}
\label{sec:r16completedrobust}
A separate experiment addresses the original HJB implementation and economic-variation concerns. The baseline uses exact covariance Hessian contraction, four prespecified collocation/learning-rate configurations, Adam followed by L-BFGS, and residual-only selection on independent banks. Both safeguarded-Newton greedy deployment and its distilled actor are retained. No final payoff selects between them. Appendix~\ref{app:r16registered} gives the full construction and charges all shared prerequisites.

The four designs are the historical high and low volatility cases, a longer horizon, and a joint economic stress. Each dimension uses eight new complete training streams, producing 64 trials and 320 policies. Independent confirmation uses 8,192 paths per method on 2,048 cells, with 648 simultaneous events and budget $0.01$. The NBO gain lower endpoint is positive in every economic--dimension case. Table~\ref{tab:r16readinghjb} shows, however, that strengthening the comparator changes the comparative conclusion. Only the low-volatility fifty-dimensional NBO-minus-HJB contrasts exceed the materiality margin at their lower endpoints. The other fourteen direct HJB intervals contain zero. The eight NBO-minus-Raw comparisons also remain unresolved, and Raw uses less measured work in each case. The complete method, target-attainment, residual, and cost records appear in Appendix~\ref{app:r16robustrecord}. These results establish feasible improvement over the analytical schedule under genuine economic variation while sharply separating it from a universal method ranking.
