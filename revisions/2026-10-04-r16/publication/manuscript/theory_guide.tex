\subsection{The theorem chain and economic scope}\label{sec:r16theoryguide}
The general evaluation and improvement results are collected in the supplement, with their complete statements and proofs retained. Fixed-policy evaluation, the selection of a Bellman solution, and the performance of the returned actor are separate claims. Their hypotheses specify admissibility, comparison, regularity or monotone approximation, and the relevant terminal or transversality condition. Recursive utility, endogenous preferences, temporal selves, and dynamic games retain their own hypotheses; the capital experiment does not provide numerical validation for those distinct applications.

The capital development below specializes this chain to explicit feasible actions, a tractable schedule, and a payoff statistic in economic units. It then separates two questions. The first is whether the selected policy produces a positive gain. The second is whether learning the continuation improves decisions or work relative to a credible procedure without that scalar critic. The signed occupation account addresses the first question. The common-continuation and decision-risk results describe a mechanism for the second, which still requires an appropriate empirical comparison for the implemented nonlinear methods.
