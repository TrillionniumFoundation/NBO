\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
An economic decision maker often changes a current choice while leaving its continuation problem unchanged. A tax or consumption constraint may change today, a portfolio manager may face a new current mandate, or an optimizer may revisit a nearby state. The continuation value is a common economic object across these decisions. Learning it can replace repeated continuation calculations by evaluations of one function. The relevant question is not whether the function interpolates its training observations, but whether its marginal values improve feasible decisions enough to justify learning and deploying it.

This paper develops Neural Bellman Operators (NBO) around that question. The operator separates policy evaluation from policy improvement. A continuation representation is fitted while the reference policy is held fixed. The actor then uses detached continuation channels to choose a feasible policy. The distinction matters both analytically and computationally: a joint derivative through the actor and its moving evaluation target is not the same map. A final independent economic comparison assesses the returned policy. The framework applies to continuous-time control and, under their additional assumptions, recursive utility, endogenous preference formation, temporal selves, and dynamic games. These applications and their original derivations remain in the current supplement; the quantitative method comparisons concern the capital economy developed in the article.

The first contribution is an error account from a learned continuation to a feasible policy and its continuous-economy payoff. The analysis distinguishes a fixed-policy numerical continuation from the limiting Bellman solution, and a held-action controller from a continuously updated feedback. The feasible analytical schedule supplies an explicit economic anchor. Candidate-occupation error, maximization error, holding loss, arithmetic, and diffusion transfer enter different terms. The general evaluation, viscosity, and improvement results are stated with the assumptions that support them, rather than inferred from a small training residual.

The second contribution is a signed occupation account. A norm-based sufficient condition replaces the continuation correction by a worst-case magnitude and can lose the cancellation that actually occurs along the policy's action changes. We instead evaluate common-noise continuation differences and form predicted improvement minus the signed correction before statistical inference. The expectation is exactly the finite Bellman gain. An antithetic range calculation then transfers a fresh lower endpoint to the continuous economy. In the executed assessment, the original negative norm-based bounds coexist with positive signed lower bounds of at least $0.000767$ and $0.000776$ in dimensions ten and fifty. This is evidence that the returned NBO policies realize positive occupation Bellman gains, not an identification of the critic's unique incremental contribution relative to Raw.

The third contribution concerns the economic value of the common continuation itself. We state precisely when a rollout, its innovations, and its derivative can be reused. For a scalar-gradient dictionary fixed independently of the fitting labels, the reduction in prediction risk is exactly removed noise variance less approximation cost. A quadratic-decision specialization translates the same decomposition into a payoff comparison. These results exhibit a positive economic mechanism for learning, including strict improvement when the dictionary is correctly specified and removes relevant noise. They also show why the mechanism cannot be assumed: a misspecified representation may cost more than the variance it removes, and a Raw competitor using the same projection receives the same benefit. The completed continuation-menu comparison gives every method its legitimate reuse rather than forcing Raw to regenerate observations unnecessarily. Across four finite-period designs, two dimensions, and three fixed work stages, it retains 128 trials and 640 method fits. At the last stage, the long fifty-dimensional case has material NBO payoff advantages over the fitted vector costate, Raw actor, and direct actor. In six of the eight final-stage economic--dimension cases, the entire NBO-minus-cached-sample-average interval lies inside the prespecified practical-equivalence band, while NBO uses less construction and query work. In both long-continuation cases, the cached sample-average optimizer has a material payoff advantage. Thus the experiment identifies an economic work--accuracy tradeoff, not universal dominance by a learned representation.

The numerical evidence makes three distinctions essential to the economic interpretation. First, independent confirmation of a fixed policy is different from retraining a method on new streams. A new prospective bank confirms the original NBO--HJB paired comparison in both dimensions. A separate completed experiment retrains the exact-trace HJB family over four economic designs and eight new streams per design and dimension. The strengthened comparator substantially narrows the original gap: the low-volatility fifty-dimensional contrasts remain positive against both deployments, whereas the other fourteen HJB contrasts are unresolved. Second, an interval containing zero is not evidence of equivalence or noninferiority. The original NBO--Raw and NBO--direct-policy comparisons remain unresolved after fresh confirmation; Raw remains cheaper in the original work account. Third, positive improvement over the analytical schedule is not a tight solution of the high-dimensional HJB problem. The broad adapted-control gap bounds, classical scalar comparisons, and state-specific ranking reversals are retained and interpreted at their stated scope.

The article presents the operator, the capital economy, the signed mechanism, and the economic evidence. The supplement contains the full theorem chain, proofs, original applications, complete adverse and favorable numerical accounts, and the prospectively fixed strengthened-HJB and continuation-menu designs together with every registered outcome. A separate response identifies the revision corresponding to each referee comment. Throughout, economic primitives, training-stream distributions, selected policies, and confirmation families remain distinct objects.
