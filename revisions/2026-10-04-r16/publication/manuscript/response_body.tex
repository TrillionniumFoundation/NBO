\section{Overview and the Revised Contribution}
We thank the referee for separating the progress in economic verification from the still-demanding comparison of continuation learning with credible alternatives. We have revised the original NBO article rather than substituting a different research topic. The new main text centers on the learned continuation object, feasible improvement, a signed Bellman mechanism, and the economic value of reuse. The original mathematical results and economic applications remain in the current supplement, with exact preceding sources preserved separately.

Three completed additions materially change the evidentiary position. First, a prospective signed occupation assessment supplies positive continuous-economy gain lower endpoints in both dimensions where the original norm-based sufficient account was negative. Second, an independent confirmation bank, evaluated only after the paired theorem and its constants were fixed, reproduces the specified NBO--HJB comparison. Third, an exact decision-value proposition translates fixed-dictionary gradient compression into expected economic payoff in a precisely stated quadratic specialization. Its proof identifies removed economically weighted variance and approximation cost, and records the feasibility condition under which the equality holds.

The two broader registered empirical families are also complete. The five-method continuation menu retains all 128 trials, 640 method fits, 216 primary events, and 128 scalar statements. The strengthened-HJB study retains four economic designs, 64 trials, 320 policies, and 648 events. Their inclusion changes this from a design-only response into an outcome-level comparison. NBO has material advantages over fitted alternatives in difficult reusable-continuation cases and practical equivalence with cached sample-average optimization at lower construction and query work in six of eight final-stage cases. The sample-average method wins materially in both long-continuation cases, and strengthening HJB leaves most direct contrasts unresolved. All outcomes, not only favorable signs, enter the revised article and complete supplement.

The report is a repository advisory review, not an editorial decision issued by Econometrica. The reading edition uses the retained journal class, author--year bibliography, numbered results, and separate main/supplement cross-references. Source identities, compilation receipts, and lossless-preservation checks are in the reproduction record rather than the economic exposition.

\section{M1 and B1: The Learned Critic's Incremental Economic Value}
\paragraph*{Referee concern.} Positive verified payoff for the NBO package does not establish that fitting the critic improves on cached Raw continuation targets. The reported Raw procedure has similar payoff, lower work, and unresolved direct intervals.

\paragraph*{Revision.} Section~\ref{sec:r16menu} states the precise common-continuation object and the valid forms of reuse. A payoff or derivative observation is reusable at its argument when future primitives and the reference policy remain fixed. Innovations can also be reused at another argument, but the state recursion and payoff then have to be reevaluated. This prevents a comparison that artificially charges Raw for unnecessary resimulation while treating NBO reuse as free.

Proposition~\ref{prop:r16menuprojection} gives an exact bias--variance identity for a scalar-gradient dictionary fixed independently of the fitting labels. Proposition~\ref{prop:r16decisionvalue} then gives its economic counterpart. In the stated quadratic Bellman tasks, the expected weighted payoff advantage equals one half of removed economically weighted noise variance minus approximation cost. Correct representation and nonzero removable economic noise give strict improvement. The statement also requires that the optimizers be feasible interior solutions; it does not replace a constrained nonlinear actor by an unconstrained first-order formula. The proof is complete, and independent finite-support manufactured tests check both favorable and adverse approximation regimes.

The completed nonlinear comparison is presented in Section~\ref{sec:r16completedmenu}, with its design and every registered outcome in Appendix~\ref{app:r16registered}. A vector costate fit, cached Raw actor, task-conditioned direct actor, and Raw sample-average optimizer receive legitimate reuse as well. All stages and all declared economic designs are retained. The fixed-dictionary proposition gives a proved economic mechanism; the nonlinear outcomes are independently evaluated rather than identified with that proposition.

\paragraph*{Completed evidence and scope.} The original and fresh-bank NBO--Raw intervals remain visible and unresolved. A Raw method applying the same projection has the same estimator and does not lose merely because its name is Raw. The complete menu now supplies a direct incremental comparison. In the long fifty-dimensional case, the NBO advantage over the vector costate, Raw actor, and direct actor has lower endpoints 16.38, 18.02, and 3.27 in units of $10^{-4}$. The untouched intermediate fifty-dimensional case also yields material Raw-actor and direct-actor advantages. Across all 24 registered stage--economy cases, the numbers of positive lower endpoints against vector, Raw actor, direct actor, and cached sample-average optimization are 22, 19, 16, and zero. Practical equivalence with the sample-average method holds in six of eight final-stage cases at the fixed margin, while the two long cases show material inferiority. These results identify a scoped economic tradeoff rather than necessity of a critic in every implementation.

\paragraph*{Binding-constraint extension.} Proposition~\ref{prop:r16activefacevalue} supplies a complete proof of the decision-value identity on stable active faces. It allows each task to have binding constraints when their tangent spaces jointly span the continuation coordinates. The active faces and scalar-gradient dictionary are fixed for the original centered observation law; no post-selection recentering is allowed. Ten additional finite-law tests compare the identity with exact constrained KKT solutions, including off-diagonal curvature, unequal economic weights, misspecification, and explicit rejection of face switching and singular metrics. These manufactured checks are distinct from the registered economic outcomes.

\section{M2 and B2: A Stronger Neural HJB Comparator}

Independent automatic differentiation checks the contraction and its weight gradients. The prospective fitting family crosses two collocation laws and two learning rates, allows up to 1,200 Adam updates and a subsequent L-BFGS stage, and selects only by exact residual RMS on independent selection banks. Separate untouched residual and probe-variance audits do not select a model. The code preserves failures, deterministic tie rules, and every retained candidate.

Two deployments are prespecified: a safeguarded Newton greedy solve, with charged fallback, and a distilled width-32 actor trained on cached greedy actions. Both receive final common-path evaluation; neither is selected after inspecting its economic payoff. The full shared HJB construction cost is charged to each reported deployment and disclosed as shared work. Appendix~\ref{app:r16registered} gives the full economic and computational specification.

\paragraph*{Completed evidence and scope.} The current original and replicated superiority statements are expressly limited to the original specified HJB implementation. They are not relabeled as evidence against the strengthened family. The strengthened family is now evaluated completely in Section~\ref{sec:r16completedrobust}. The low-volatility fifty-dimensional NBO-minus-greedy and NBO-minus-distilled intervals are contained in $[0.000334,0.000697]$ and $[0.000340,0.000704]$. The other fourteen HJB contrasts include zero. In particular, the enlarged family substantially narrows the original comparator gap; its point-estimate reversals are retained, not hidden. \section{M4, B1 and B6: Raw, Direct Policy, and Comparative Work}

\paragraph*{Revision.} The introduction, economic interpretation, fresh-bank section, and conclusion now state the direct-comparison object explicitly. The common-continuation section adds a transparent amortization calculation. A lower per-task cost repays additional shared fitting work only beyond its explicit break-even task count. The completed menu measures construction, deployment, optimization, and the full shared confirmation bundle for all five methods and every stage. NBO has lower observed complete third-stage construction and query work than cached sample-average optimization in all eight cases and satisfies the prespecified practical-equivalence criterion in six. For the quarterly and untouched intermediate fifty-dimensional cases, the construction/query clocks are 14.34 versus 62.90 seconds and 12.70 versus 79.46 seconds. Both long cases instead have material sample-average payoff advantages. Raw actors remain cheaper than NBO. The shared confirmation bundle is retained separately and can be charged in full to either method; it is not divided into fictional standalone costs. Consequently the claim is a measured work--accuracy tradeoff, not universal dominance or an invented work-to-certified-gain stopping time.
