\section{R19 execution, scope, and complete diagnostics}\label{sec:r19supp}
The R19 protocol was committed before the new actor training and the new finite-query study. The actor checkpoints are $0,50,200,800$ for seeds 19100--19104. All checkpoint networks, their exact compiled dyadic policies, payoff intervals, acceptance records, and resource logs are retained. The initial and final policies enter the coupled-payoff proof; its deterministic state-extension and wealth-compensation calculations are subsequent analytical consequences, not additional randomized experiments. The attribution study reuses historical frozen networks and is expressly not relabeled as new training.

The primary reproduction evaluates the independent payoff gate before the next Adam update. The optimizer state and network are restored on rejection. An acceptance requires a strictly positive lower endpoint for new payoff minus incumbent payoff; a smaller optimality bound alone is insufficient. All 15 noninitial proposals pass. A separate audit verifies the observable progress test at $\kappa=.02$ for every retained transition, $\kappa=.75$ for each reference-state initial-to-final pair, and the final-to-initial true-regret ratio below $.42$ on $K$. These are executed-trajectory statements, not an assumption of global optimizer convergence.

The publication workflow records immutable input, protocol, source, and publication objects separately.

Timing records identify generation, policy-value evaluation, query correction, final gradient checking, shared optimal-upper generation/verification, and peak process memory. Shared upper-comparator costs are retained from their historical execution and are not called zero-cost or newly timed. End-to-end neural first-attainment timing includes the earlier retained policy-value checks. Peak memory is process-wide and includes the runtime and loaded libraries; it is not falsely presented as network parameter memory.

All prior tracked files are checked for preservation against the R18 review commit. New arithmetic checks validate the MPFR primitive layer, directed policy ordering, work-count consistency, and stored-plan hashes. They do not constitute an independent formal proof of the model, quadrature, stopping, or comparison theorems. The deployment contract concerns the serialized dyadic controls and checked plans; it does not cover an undocumented online activation implementation or simulator.
