The coupled-payoff theorem certifies initial-to-final payoff improvement of at least $\NineteenGain$ throughout $K$, for every seed. The numerical check does not infer a continuum conclusion from sampled values.

A further use of Theorem~\ref{thm:r19transfer} compares each final neural policy directly with the fresh 16-slab SLSQP policy. Uniformly on $K$, the latter's payoff exceeds the neural payoff by at most $\NineteenClassicalLoss$. This bound does not suppose that the SLSQP policy is optimal. It supplies a trusted, policy-specific comparison on a nontrivial state set, rather than comparing descriptive action ranges or two loose regret bounds.

The six retained Markov-chain and semi-Lagrangian feedback candidates remain separate objects. Their bounds do not become sharp merely because a time transcription reaches the reference-state target. The supplement reports the positive and negative contributions for all six. At the finest grids the negative policy-side term becomes positive while the positive witness term changes comparatively little. This identifies the component responsible for the reported bound's deterioration; it does not by itself identify whether interpolation, witness fitting, or true policy quality is the ultimate cause.

\subsection{Welfare compensation for the neural policies}
The new neural certificate has its own budget-feasible economic interpretation. Increase initial wealth by $\delta=.0092$ and add $\delta/Q_r$ to every consumption slab of the neural policy. Preference adjustment and portfolio controls are unchanged. Directed checks over all five policies and all initial wealths in $K$ establish the original consumption cap, identical terminal wealth, and no wealth exit. Intermediate wealth is weakly higher, so the preference-exit time is common and the settlement contribution cannot fall.

\begin{proposition}[Sufficient neural-policy wealth compensation]\label{prop:r19wealth}
For every reported final neural policy and $(u,x)\in K$, the preceding feasible adjustment satisfies
\[
 J_k(0,u,x+\delta;\widetilde\pi)-J_k(0,u,x;\pi)
 \ \geq\ \frac\delta{Q_r}(.8)^{-1.2}C_\rho(1)(1-p_e)
 \ >\ .0119058,
\]
where $k=2$ and $p_e=2\exp\{- .58^2/(2(.05)^2)\}$. Hence
$J_k(0,u,x+\delta;\widetilde\pi)\geq V_k(0,u,x)$.
\end{proposition}
The increment is 0.736 percent of reference wealth $1.25$. It is sufficient compensation with an explicitly changed feasible consumption policy, not the exact minimum compensating variation of an unchanged policy. It applies to the new neural-generated policies on $K$, not to the old full-domain feedback certificate or solely to the non-neural price library.
