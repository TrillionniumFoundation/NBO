We study neural policy evaluation, improvement, and independent verification in a continuous economy with costly preference adjustment. Feasible learned policies and market-deflator
witnesses certify a positive value of access to adjustment under the original utility and first-exit
contract. At the central state, the certified gain is at least .04094994 when the adjustment-cost
coefficient is two. Constructive interval refinement encloses the no-adjustment optimum
within .00905. The flexible policy is evaluated much more precisely than its current .10634
regret bound. For coupled nonconvex controls, a scalar dual branch-and-bound procedure
certifies continuous actions through 128 dimensions, and a Cole–Hopf relaxation supplies
absolute value lower bounds. A frozen, symmetrically tuned, multi-budget comparison retains all holdout outcomes, including a stronger independently specified feedback baseline.
Complete proofs separate policy evaluation, optimality, computational cost, and the economic
inequalities each calculation establishes.
