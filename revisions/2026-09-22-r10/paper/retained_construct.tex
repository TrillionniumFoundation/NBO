\subsection{Fitting an upper witness without importing an optimal value}
Put $h=1-t$ and restrict the \emph{verification domain}, not the economic domain, to $u\in[1.5,2.6]$. Fit
\[
 F(h,u)=h\sum_{i=0}^{12}\sum_{j=0}^{14}\beta_{ij}
 T_i(2h-1)T_j\!\left(2\frac{u-1.5}{1.1}-1\right).
\]
The coefficients are obtained from a method-of-lines initializer and residual least squares. The optimizer's collocation residual is not the certificate. For $w=G+F$, the consumption and portfolio maximizers are $.8$ and $.8$. Maximizing the remaining wealth term over $x\in[.5,2]$ gives the scalar residual majorant
\begin{align}
 \mathcal R_kF={}&-F_h+.00125F_{uu}-.04F+
 H_k\{F_u-.04(u-2)\}+\frac{.8^{1-u}}{1-u}\\
 &+.0008(u-2)^2+.00195+.00352-.04-.004\log2,\label{r9:reduced}\\[-2mm]
 H_k(q)={}&\max_{|\theta|\leq.2}(q\theta-k\theta^2/2).\notag
\end{align}
The source is the original utility, not a payoff manufactured to make $F$ exact.

Power coefficients are enclosed at 70-digit interval precision and converted to Bernstein coefficients. The utility term is replaced by a polynomial with a proved uniform remainder below $2.117\times10^{-9}$. De Casteljau subdivision uses outward rounding at every elementary step. The positive-saturation, negative-saturation, and interior branches of $H_k$ are all included whenever their certified $q$ range permits them. Outward thresholds also cover a cell touching a switching surface. The same calculation proves $F/h\geq-8$.

\begin{proposition}[Localized fitted upper witness]\label{r9:upper}
Suppose $F(0,u)=0$, $F/h\geq-8$, and $\mathcal R_kF\leq\epsilon$ on the verification rectangle. For $k\geq.5$, let
\begin{align}
 Z(h,u)&=8h\{e^{-120(u-1.5)+42h}+e^{-160(2.6-u)+64h}\},\\
 U(t,u,x)&=G(u,x)+F(1-t,u)+Z(1-t,u)+\epsilon_+C_\rho(1-t).
\end{align}
Then $V_k(0,2,1.25)\leq U(0,2,1.25)$. No reflecting boundary or truncation of the original policy is introduced at $u=1.5$ or $2.6$.
\end{proposition}
The exponential terms bound continuation when an artificial verification face is reached. The globally valid upper witness $G$ is used beyond that face. At the central state the localization addition is less than $1.219\times10^{-7}$, rather than an eight-unit initial gap. Uniform residual correction and localization are different terms and are reported separately.

\subsection{A learned feasible policy and independent stopped evaluation}\label{r9:policy}
The actor has one time input, a twelve-unit tanh layer, and two outputs. At sixteen fixed time nodes, a sigmoid constrains adjustment to $[0,.2]$, and a differentiable budget projection constrains consumption to $[.05,.8]$. The portfolio is zero. Training uses the original expected payoff, Gaussian quadrature, and no optimal-value or greedy-action labels. The deployed controls are the resulting frozen binary64 constants on each time slab; there is no claim that this time-control class exhausts the state-feedback opportunities in \eqref{r9:objective}.

The budget projection enforces
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{j=0}^{15}c_j\int_{j/16}^{(j+1)/16}e^{-.02r}\,dr
 <1.25-.5e^{-.02}.\label{r9:budgetprojection}
\end{equation}
Thus $x_t=e^{.02t}\{1.25-\int_0^te^{-.02r}c_r\,dr\}$ is deterministic. Since consumption exceeds $.02(1.25)$, wealth decreases and its interval-enclosed terminal value remains strictly above $.5$. Wealth never exits. The remaining preference exit is controlled by a Brownian maximal inequality, not by a discretized boundary check.

An evaluator independent of the training quadrature approximates utility by a degree-90 polynomial in $u-2$, with a uniform analytic remainder on the original preference interval. Free Gaussian moments are evaluated by recurrence, integrated over outward time rectangles, and corrected for the stopped event using a maximal probability bound and Cauchy--Schwarz. The terminal wealth, adjustment expenditure, polynomial approximation, time integration, exact-exit correction, and arithmetic are all enclosed. There is no Euler, action-grid, or spatial-interpolation term in this route. Those omissions are consequences of the deployed policy and analytic evaluation, not uncomputed entries assigned zero.
