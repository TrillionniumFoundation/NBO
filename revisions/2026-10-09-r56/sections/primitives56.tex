\section{The nonlinear investment family}\label{sec:economy56}
The economic law is the original dimension-normalized investment family. For $x\in[0,1]^d$, scalar action $0\le a\le\kappa(x)$, and cyclic coordinate $v=j+1$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:investment56}
 \kappa(x)&=\frac18+\frac1{8d}\sum_jx_j,\\
 F_j(x,a,Z)&=\frac1{16}+\frac{x_j}{2}+\frac{x_v}{8}
              +\frac{x_j(1-x_v)}{16}+\gamma_ja+s_jZ,\notag
\end{align}
where $(\gamma_j,s_j)$ alternates between $(1/2,1)$ and $(1/4,-1)$ and $Z$ is uniform on $[-1/32,1/32]$. The initial state is uniform on the cube and $\beta=15/16$. The stage and terminal costs are
\begin{align}\label{eq:cost56}
 c(x,a)&=\frac2d\sum_j(x_j-5/8)^2+
       \frac1{4d}\sum_j(x_j-x_{j+1})^2+
       2\left(\frac12-\frac2d\sum_jx_j\right)_+^2+a^2+4a^4,\\
 g(x)&=\frac4d\sum_j(x_j-5/8)^2+
       \frac1{4d}\sum_j(x_j-x_{j+1})^2+
       2\left(\frac12-\frac2d\sum_jx_j\right)_+^2.\notag
\end{align}
The original coefficients and continuous laws are unchanged. The domain is invariant: the drift lies in $[1/16,11/16]$, $a\le1/4$, $\gamma_j\le1/2$, and the innovation magnitude is at most $1/32$. The results are for this theoretical economy, not calibrated installation fees or estimated welfare magnitudes.
