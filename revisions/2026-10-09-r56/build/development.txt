                                           1. INTRODUCTION
25                                                                                            25

26     A continuation value is useful to an economist because it changes a decision. Its      26

27   prediction error is not the loss of the policy actually implemented. Nor is a smaller    27

28   upper bound on that loss evidence that one policy has lower cost than another. When      28

29   observation, action selection and verification are costly, the numerical procedure       29

30   and the economic decision have to be evaluated together.                                 30

1        We develop Neural Bellman Operators as a policy-evaluation and feasible- 1
2    improvement method. The economic object remains a controlled economy with                 2

3    policy-specific continuations. The operator constructs each date’s continuation us- 3
4    ing its own fitted future, returns a feasible policy, and accounts for its loss against   4

5    the Bellman optimum. A general signed policy sandwich permits arbitrary fitted            5

6    candidates. A constructive affine–ReLU backend additionally produces the candi- 6
7    date from primitive moduli, without assuming that a nonconvex training algorithm          7

8    eventually succeeds. Exact compilation retains the original action witness through        8

9    off-grid evaluation and state-dependent feasibility repair.                               9

10       The principal question in this revision is how that construction produces a cer- 10
11   tified economic answer with finite resources. State coverage, action search and           11

12   integration have different error coefficients. Tying them to one grid size conceals       12

13   the allocation problem. We derive a separated primitive budget and an optimal             13

14   continuous allocation for its leading work proxy, including lower resource bounds         14

15   and dyadic implementation. The empirical comparison then records the complete             15

16   positive-tolerance frontier of a fixed common resource ladder, not only an isolated       16

17   favorable target. All failed earlier attempts belong to the work required to return a     17

18   policy.                                                                                   18

19       The next question is what the returned policy does. We evaluate the actual policies   19

20   at the declared first crossings, even when two generators cross at different reso- 20
21   lutions. Independent interval simulation preserves the original continuous initial        21

22   and innovation laws and encloses discontinuous actors and robust capacity repair. 22
23   Conditioning on construction information permits simultaneous cost inference after        23

24   this selection. The result does not infer a policy ranking from two certificates and      24

25   does not extrapolate three deterministic timing repetitions into an optimizer-success     25

26   probability.                                                                              26

27       Two economic decisions make this distinction operational. First, a controller         27

28   replacement is worthwhile only if its actual gain exceeds the replacement fee. We         28

29   identify the directional gain intervals and the full break-even fee account, retaining    29

30   but no longer relying on a single generous charge. Second, observation precision is       30

31   chosen on the same finite bit grid on which the implemented policies are evaluated. 31
32   A simultaneous upper-net-cost selector has a regret bound relative to the best            32

10     1.0.0.3. Policy iteration, invariance, and state metrics. Approximate policy iter- 10
11   ation already has a substantial error-propagation theory; Munos (2003) studies            11

12   approximation errors in policy iteration. Conservative policy iteration connects          12

13   improvement to an incumbent and a performance criterion (Kakade and Langford,             13

14   2002), and safe policy iteration develops related monotonic-improvement guaran- 14
15   tees (Pirotta et al., 2013). We do not claim the general idea of safe improvement. Our    15

16   finite-horizon statement requires an acquired-cell feasible action, a verified directed   16

17   advantage enclosure, and a lower cover of the entire continuous feasible action set. 17
18   Those conditions identify the error that enters each pass and make the economic           18

19   cost of verification explicit.                                                            19

20     Couplings also occur in bisimulation metrics: Ferns, Panangaden, and Precup             20

21   (2004) relate state distances to optimal values. Here the transported object is the       21

22   signed evaluation error of a fixed incumbent, and the pair computation may use            22

23   any verified coupling with the correct marginals. It is neither a proposed state- 23
24   aggregation metric nor an assumption that a transport optimum can be obtained             24

25   without cost. Potential-based reward shaping preserves policies by transforming the       25

26   reward specification (Ng, Harada, and Russell, 1999); the action-null construction        26

27   here leaves the economic reward and transition laws unchanged and concerns con- 27
28   tinuation components with identical feasible-action expectations. Value equivalence       28

29   instead identifies models through their Bellman updates on specified functions            29

30   and policies (Grimm, Barreto, Singh, and Silver, 2020). Our contrast equivalence          30

31   is a statement about continuation errors under the fixed actual kernels. These con- 31
32   nections explain the inherited principles and locate the additional contribution          32

                           15. ECONOMIC SCOPE AND CONCLUSION
8                                                                                           8

9      The economic contribution is an explicit connection between a policy’s construc- 9
10   tion resources and its implementable cost. A primitive error budget can determine      10

11   whether to refine the state cover, action search or integration. A complete finite     11

12   tolerance frontier states when one generator returns a certified policy earlier. In- 12
13   dependent comparisons of the returned policies then determine what can be said         13

14   about controller replacement and purchased information. These are different steps;     14

15   none can be replaced by the favorable outcome of another.                              15

16     The constructive backend and exact compiler make positive deterministic state- 16
17   ments under explicit moduli and effective-computability conditions. Their guaran- 17
18   tees are not success probabilities for unrelated nonconvex training algorithms. The    18

19   matched experiments and direct-cost intervals make finite, source-bound claims         19

20   about the declared economies, resource ladders and initial laws. Common-accuracy       20

21   dimension cells do not establish an asymptotic scaling law, and endogenous pre- 21
22   cision within a finite sensor catalogue is not an optimum over all information         22

23   technologies. The original broad theory, applications and adverse experimental         23

24   record remain part of the complete NBO development.                                    24

25     The cost-directed result adds a further positive statement: a feasible acquired      25

26   policy can be improved without sacrificing its incumbent cost guarantee, provided      26

27   its centered advantage is verified. In the executed final-stage specialization every   27

28   studied witness incumbent has a strictly positive identified reduction under the       28

29   declared initial law. Most witness–FVI differences remain unfavorable after both       29

30   methods are repaired. The improvement theorem and this comparative observation         30

31   are consistent: safe improvement relative to an incumbent is not dominance over        31

32   another method.                                                                        32

3    preserve the same safety and optimality targets. The exhaustive cell study checks       3
