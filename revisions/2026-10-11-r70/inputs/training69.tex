\section{An executed training and reuse contract}\label{sec:training69}
A training objective is useful only when its achieved value, its validation law and its deployment costs are identifiable. The previous fixed-feature readout proposition supplies an optimization certificate but does not assert that the earlier hidden-layer fits minimize that objective. We now execute the proposition as its own numerical arm. The original witness construction, query certificate and Bellman target remain unchanged.

\subsection{Matched information and finite optimization}
For a state $x\in[0,1]^d$, define two feature maps, indexed by $j=0,\ldots,4$ and $h=1,\ldots,d$:
\[
 \phi_{jh}^{\rm R}(x)=\frac1d(1-|4x_h-j|)_+,
 \qquad
 \phi_{jh}^{\rm B}(x)=\frac1d{4\choose j}x_h^j(1-x_h)^{4-j}.
\]
Each map is nonnegative and sums to one. Both therefore have $5d$ readout coefficients in the same unit box. The first admits an affine--ReLU realization by the identities $|z|=z_++(-z)_+$ and piecewise-linear interpolation; the second is an ordinary degree-four Bernstein construction. The hat basis and its ReLU circuit are one representation, not two independent methods.

Both arms purchase the same acquired states, boundary Bellman enclosures and approximate action labels. They use the same certificate loss in \eqref{eq:readout-loss67}, the same penalty, iteration cap and weight lattice. Approximate labels are retained for the label-loss control and are charged to every learned arm in this matched-information design. The ReLU label-loss control changes the objective while retaining its feature map and coefficient budget. The quadratic control uses the same states and labels, with its different coefficient count reported. Prediction-free adaptive and upper-chord controls do not incur unused training expenses.

The numerical optimizer is a finite proposal mechanism for a box readout. After quantizing the weights, the objective and its box dual gap are recomputed with exact rational arithmetic from the deposited endpoint contexts and feature values. The achieved dual gap certifies the returned iterate whether or not the numerical optimizer reports convergence. A nonzero optimization gap is retained, not relabeled as the attained global minimum. The recorded rounding allowance is also checked against the exact feature map and the actual rounded training proposals. Hidden-layer optimization and its population success probability are not premises of this result.

\subsection{Predictor-independent validation}
Validation draws a new uniform acquired-state context stream, independent of fitting and identical across the matched arms. Boundary values and the lower certificate are purchased anew. After the first screen, a failed context is resolved by a declared complete-action mesh, with its original-law descendant integrations. This supplies an explicit root-query cap $K(z)$. The experiment records the clipped loss, the actual number of additional root queries and all descendant work separately.

The validation procedure is deliberately distinct from recursive deployment. Its finite mesh makes the inequality $N_\theta(z)\le K(z)\ell_\tau(z,\theta)$ directly replayable under a specified context law. On-policy recursive states need not follow that law. Their observed transcripts receive the deterministic safety and work statements, not an unsupported independent-sample validation guarantee. Context generation, endpoint acquisition, exact optimization certification and validation are paid services, rather than deductions from action-label mean squared error.

\begin{proposition}[A finite catalogue of complete work]\label{prop:complete-work69}
Condition on a frozen finite family of predictors and a predictor-independent validation law. Suppose a failed screen invokes a deterministic recovery whose root-query count is at most $K(z)$, and every root query costs at most $W(z)$ under the declared descendant and arithmetic contract. If $0\le W(z)K(z)\le C_{\max}$, then with probability at least $1-\alpha$, simultaneously for $M$ fixed predictors,
\[
 \mathbb E[\text{recovery work}_\theta]
 \le\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n W(z_i)K(z_i)\ell_\tau(z_i,\theta)
       +C_{\max}\sqrt{\frac{\log(M/\alpha)}{2n}}.
\]
Training, context acquisition, prediction, loading, identity checks and output costs are additional terms in complete work. Replacing $W$ by one gives a root-query result, not a bound on elapsed process time.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A successful screen incurs no recovery queries. On a failed screen the clipped loss equals one, and recovery work is at most $WK$. The pointwise bound therefore holds for the bounded variable $WK\ell_\tau$. The bounded-sum inequality and a union bound give the claim. None of the separately named service costs is included in the definition of recovery work.
\end{proof}

The executed validation table uses the root-query version. It does not fit a runtime model and then treat that model as a verified $W$. Complete process clocks and machine-independent query counts are reported as different observations.

\subsection{Prospective reuse and identity failure}
Each reuse process fits and serializes one model before any of its sixty-four declared deployment blocks. A load operation checks the model hash, economic task, horizon, acquisition precision and source identity. The seventeenth block deliberately presents a stale acquisition identifier and must use the conventional verifier. The saved model is not corrupted. Every subsequent return remains a certificate-authorized action under the original economic law.

Let $C_{m,r}(b)$ be the measured cumulative cost through block $b$ for method $m$ and repetition $r$, inclusive of construction and all completed loads, checks, queries and durable records. The declared early persistent-crossing event is
\[
 \exists b\le48\quad\text{such that}\quad
 C_{m,r}(j)<C_{{\rm adaptive},r}(j)\quad
 \text{for every }j=b,\ldots,63.
\]
This is a finite observed event. It predicts neither an infinite-horizon crossing nor a new hardware's timing distribution. A crossing only at the last block fails this criterion. Four paired process repetitions and the full operation ledger are retained even when their timing ordering disagrees. Available CPU frequency information is recorded; clock frequency is not claimed experimentally fixed.

\subsection{Two-control proposals under a common certificate}
In the existing two-control economy, a trained two-output proposal can supply a further feasible upper witness without changing the triangular lower cover. The proposal is projected onto the acquired capacity simplex, rounded downward and evaluated under the original two-innovation kernel. All three original corner endpoints remain in the lower cover. A failed or unhelpful witness cannot raise that lower bound.

\begin{proposition}[Optional vector witnesses]\label{prop:vector-witness69}
Suppose a triangular action cover has a valid global lower bound $L$ and feasible evaluated witnesses with upper bound $U$. Adding any finite number of feasible, correctly evaluated witnesses and replacing $U$ by the minimum of all their upper bounds preserves containment of the original feasible infimum. Every returned witness with $U-L\le\delta$ has original Bellman regret at most $\delta$. The conclusion is independent of the training method and of whether an added witness is a vertex of the lower cover.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The lower cover still covers the entire feasible set, so its unchanged lower bound remains valid. Each additional upper endpoint bounds the true cost of its own feasible action, hence is an upper bound for the infimum. Their minimum preserves this property. The returned action's true cost is no greater than its own upper endpoint, proving the regret claim.
\end{proof}

The vector comparison includes adaptive critical-triangle refinement, uniform largest-edge refinement, a two-output hat--ReLU proposal and a quadratic proposal, all under the same lower certificate. Uniform refinement changes which triangle is split; it does not replace the global minimum lower bound by that triangle's bound. A finite precision or point-cap failure invokes the inherited, paid rational recovery. At four dates the new neural proposal transfers a three-date fitted readout; it is not described as a four-date supervised fit. The vector fitting objective is action-label loss, and is not relabeled as the scalar convex certificate-aware objective. The original horizon-four execution and its large descendant cost remain part of the evidence.
