                                           1. INTRODUCTION                                       22

23     A numerical continuation matters because it helps produce an economic decision. 23
24   The error of a fitted value, the loss of the implemented policy and the resources           24

25   required to verify that loss are different objects. A policy may improve without a          25

26   tighter numerical certificate; a tighter certificate may leave the policy unchanged. A      26

27   useful computational method must connect its approximation and resource choices             27

28   to a feasible decision under the original economic law.                                     28

29     This paper develops Neural Bellman Operators (NBOs) along that chain. The                 29

30   first implementation evaluates each continuation against its own fitted future and          30

31                                                                                               31

1    retains feasible action witnesses. Its exact affine–ReLU realization and primitive        1

2    error budget are constructive: they do not assume that an unrestricted nonconvex          2

3    optimizer succeeds. A second implementation queries the original Bellman problem          3

4    recursively at the states required by the current action search. A trained readout        4

5    proposes where to seek information. Verified lower and upper bounds, not fitted           5

6    values, determine when the complete policy meets its original-optimum contract.           6

7        In the nonlinear investment economy, state-dependent capacity and bilinear            7

8    transitions make those requirements substantive. Primitive curvature controls the         8

9    convexity defect of the transition and supplies regularity bounds for the true Bell- 9
10   man value. Continuous-action certificates therefore need no stored state lattice. A       10

11   strong-convexity upper chord can sometimes license an actual rounded action be- 11
12   fore another Bellman evaluation. When it cannot, protected conventional refinement        12

13   and a costed rational recovery remain available. Neither the learned continuation         13

14   nor the predictor is assumed convex.                                                      14

15       The operational training problem is to reduce unresolved verification work. The       15

16   certificate-based margin loss bounds additional root queries. With fixed nonnegative      16

17   ReLU features, its achieved convex readout objective and optimization gap can be          17

18   certified. We extend the work account to predictable selection among validated            18

19   readouts and dependent deployment contexts. The bound separates validation,               19

20   context-law drift, descendant work and deployment overhead. It does not replace           20

21   dependence by an independence assumption or treat a model-identity check as               21

22   evidence that the context law is unchanged.                                               22

23       The economic comparison also requires informative policy-cost inference. We           23

24   derive a Bellman-centered estimator using optimal advantages evaluated along              24

25   each actual policy trajectory. Telescoping identifies its expectation with the original   25

26   expected cost difference, while local accuracy bounds control its range at the scale      26

27   of policy regret. The conditional Bellman values are enclosed with a separately           27

28   purchased, tighter numerical calculation. This improves statistical precision without     28

29   assuming a policy-value oracle, changing the original law, or substituting two            29

30   loss certificates for the policy-cost estimand. The numerical and sampling width          30

31   allocations are specified before the new observations.                                    31

                                       14. CONCLUSION
19                                                                                           19

20     Neural Bellman Operators connect a continuation’s construction to feasible de- 20
21   cisions under the original economic law. The witness-producing implementation           21

22   retains an explicit own-future error account. The recursive implementation replaces     22

23   the stored state lattice by certified action queries. A trained proposal can reduce     23

24   verification work, but cannot alter the lower certificate’s authority over the policy   24
