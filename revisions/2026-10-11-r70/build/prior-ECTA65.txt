25                                           1. INTRODUCTION                                       25

26                                                                                                 26
       A continuation approximation is useful because it helps produce an economic
27                                                                                                 27
     decision. Small fitting error, a verified bound on the loss of the implemented policy,
28                                                                                                 28
     and inexpensive computation are different achievements. A method that improves
29                                                                                                 29
     one need not improve the others. This paper connects these objects within a con-

1    structive Neural Bellman Operator (NBO), and studies which role learning can play           1

2    when the decision must satisfy a stated accuracy contract.                                  2

3        The first construction forms each continuation against its own fitted future, retains   3

4    feasible action witnesses, and accounts for state coverage, action search, integration      4

5    and numerical implementation. Its exact affine–ReLU realization does not require            5

6    a successful nonconvex training run. A second, trained implementation uses a                6

7    network to propose an action query. It verifies that query through the original             7

8    Bellman problem rather than taking the network’s output as a value certificate. 8
9    The latter is an action-selector implementation, not a relabeling of an inaccurate          9

10   fitted-value policy.                                                                        10

11       Our original investment economy has nonlinear transitions, a state-dependent ca- 11
12   pacity constraint, and continuous innovations. Primitive state curvature dominates          12

13   the transition’s convexity defect. The resulting Lipschitz and semiconcavity bounds         13

14   permit conditional-mean quadrature and lower bounds on the full continuous ac- 14
15   tion interval. A recursive query obtains a continuation only at the descendants it          15

16   actually needs. It does not construct a conventional policy at every point of a state       16

17   lattice before allowing the neural proposal to act.                                         17

18       The key computational distinction is between the need for another query and             18

19   its proposed location. Both boundary actions are first evaluated. If their certificate      19

20   already meets the requested accuracy, no prediction is evaluated. Otherwise a               20

21   learned proposal can replace a required safeguarded split inside an unresolved              21

22   interval. Its numerical content has no authority over the lower bound, feasibility test,    22

23   or stopping tolerance. A conventional parabolic rule supplies the comparison, and           23

24   an explicitly costed rational recovery handles a failed floating certificate without        24

25   consulting a stored reference policy.                                                       25

26       Theorem 7 connects the resulting query to the complete policy’s own future un- 26
27   der the original continuous law. Theorem 8 gives its safeguarded termination and            27

28   prediction-free return properties. Proposition 9 further separates two requirements         28

29   for a useful learned query: it must supply a good witness and leave a sufficiently          29

30   localized certificate. These statements concern implementable policies and effec- 30
31   tive verification, not a presumption that training is successful or that a neural           31

32   representation is uniquely capable of certification.                                        32

12     This produces a positive state-storage result without concealing the comparative        12

22     Relation to existing methods. Lipschitz extension and distance transforms underlie      22

23   the constructive cone and its compiler (McShane, 1934, Felzenszwalb and Hut- 23
24   tenlocher, 2012). Approximate policy iteration and safe policy improvement have           24

25   established error and performance analyses (Munos, 2003, Pirotta et al., 2013, Scher- 25
26   rer et al., 2015). Convex Bellman approximation is studied by Yang (2020). Here           26

27   primitive curvature is established for the specified bilinear capacity-constrained        27

28   economy and is carried through actual acquired actions, continuous-action search          28

29   and the policy’s own future. The proof does not assume that an arbitrary fitted           29

30   neural continuation is convex.                                                            30

19   a continuation. An economist also needs to know how close the complete imple- 19

25   does not assume that a fitted neural continuation is convex.                             25

       The numerical question is whether the organization of a learned query changes
3                                                                                                    3
     the work of delivering an accurate policy. We use the original scalar investment
4                                                                                                    4
     primitives, discount factor 15/16, and continuous uniform innovations through-
5                                                                                                    5
     out. The returned object is the complete callable controller: at each subsequently
6                                                                                                    6

7                                                                                                    7

8                                                                                                    8

                            9.1. Design, controls and measured objects
11                                                                                                   11

12     Two protocols were frozen before their respective executions. The first com- 12
13   pared adaptive parabolic search, bisection, unconditional ReLU insertion and un- 13
14   conditional quadratic insertion. The second retained those controls and added                   14

15   certificate-gated routing for each fitted predictor. Its insertion and routing services         15

16   independently reconstructed exactly the same fitted model for a matched seed, task              16

17   and repetition. The initial and innovation workloads were also identical within each            17

18   task. This design separates query organization from a change in model parameters                18

19   or evaluated states.                                                                            19

20     The task catalogue is (d, T, N ) = (2, 2, 128), (8, 2, 128), (16, 2, 128), (8, 3, 32), with   20

21   original-optimum targets 1/32 and 1/128. Here N is the number of workload                       21

22   trajectories. Each learned method uses both specified fitting seeds, and every service          22

23   has two isolated-process repetitions. The first protocol therefore contains 96 services         23

24   and the second 160. The fitting seeds are 6301 and 6302 in the first catalogue and              24

25   6401 and 6402 in the second. No successful seed, task, target or repetition is selected         25

26   for publication.                                                                                26

27     Each fitted service pays for its own 96 training states, nested Bellman-generated             27

28   labels, feature construction and fit. The ReLU predictor has one hidden layer of                28

29   width 24 and uses a capped L-BFGS fit. The quadratic predictor uses the same                    29

30   features, their second-order products and regularization. The permitted training                30

31   iterations and function evaluations are fixed in the deposited protocols. At two dates          31

32   the training account records 6,912 Bellman action queries; at three dates it records            32

                            9.4. Policy cost and the purchase decision
14                                                                                            14

15     The workload starts from fixed twenty-bit dyadic states and uses fixed dyadic          15

16   shocks, with eight named boundary initial states. Every true state transition and        16

17   path cost is reconstructed with rational arithmetic. There are 26,624 path rows and      17

18   55,296 implemented decisions across the two catalogues. These are finite diagnostic      18

19   workloads, not independent draws furnishing new confidence intervals under the           19

20   continuous law. The rational workload mean is retained for exact reproduction and        20

21   is not substituted for the expectation in Section 6.2.                                   21

22     The preceding continuous-law policy comparisons consequently remain im- 22
23   portant and separate. All twenty pure fitted ReLU policies in the tensor study           23

24   have strictly higher expected cost than the common policy; the twenty common- 24
25   augmented comparisons are unresolved. The common policy has the smallest                 25

26   complete release work in all twenty attainable task–target cells. The query study        26

27   neither overwrites those observations nor asserts a new expected-cost reversal on        27

28   the basis of finite dyadic averages. A callable query controller, a pure fitted actor,   28

29   and a common-augmented nodal policy are distinct objects.                                29

                                       10. CONCLUSION                                         24

25     Neural Bellman Operators connect continuation construction to feasible economic        25

26   decisions. The original witness construction supplies an explicit own-future loss        26

27   account. In the nonlinear investment economy, primitive regularity also supports a       27

28   recursive, state-lattice-free implementation. The trained component proposes where       28

29   to obtain another piece of Bellman information; the certificate determines whether       29

30   that information is needed and whether the returned action is adequate.                  30

31     The resulting policy has a uniform original-optimum account even though only           31

32   a finite workload is executed. That distinction relies on the full continuous-action     32
                                          NEURAL BELLMAN OPERATORS                                            41

1    cover, original-law quadrature, acquisition allowance and finite recovery contract,                             1

2    not on extrapolating from successful sample states. The cost still grows with the                               2

3    depth of the continuation tree. Removing a tensor state table is not removing                                   3

4    horizon, innovation or arithmetic costs.                                                                        4

5      The matched evidence makes the role of learning assessable. Certificate gating                                5

6    improves the organization of learned queries, but its online savings do not pay                                 6

7    for training on the observed cold-start workloads. This does not invalidate the                                 7

8    construction or change its target; it determines which services the stated decision                             8

9    maker would purchase. Claims about repeated use require explicit saved work                                     9

10   and an amortization condition, and claims about expected policy gain require                                    10

11   independent economic inference. Keeping those obligations separate preserves the                                11

12   substantive NBO objective and makes the next comparison reproducible.                                           12
