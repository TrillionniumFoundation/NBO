23   ular predictor. The revised introduction presents the two constructive implementa- 23
24   tions of the original NBO program: the witness-preserving own-future continuation          24

25   and recursive original-Bellman queries. The learned readout remains an untrusted           25

26   proposal to the latter. It cannot change the lower certificate, the feasibility contract   26

27   or the original-optimum guarantee.                                                         27

32   readout uses the same hat features with a different objective. The quadratic arm           32

                1.4. B5 and M3: Independent validation and dependent deployment
27                                                                                             27

28     The new study includes sixteen independent-validation services, producing               28

29   twenty-four task–date–method–seed records of 256 contexts each. The contexts              29

30   are predictor-independent and disjoint from fitting. Each coordinate is drawn             30

31   uniformly as U/2ˆ16, with integer U from zero to 2ˆ16-1; this finite law differs          31

32   from both the full twenty-four-bit acquisition grid and the continuous law used           32
     4

1    for policy-cost inference. Shared contexts across methods are retained as shared       1

2    data, not multiplied into new independent observations. The recorded recovery          2

3    is a complete-action mesh with a declared root-query cap. Every failed screen          3

4    executes that recovery and its descendants. The replay verifies the saved action’s     4

5    exact strong-convexity chord, the actual recovery count, the pointwise work-loss       5

6    domination and the returned gap.                                                       6

7        The validation upper bound is recomputed from an exact sample sum and an           7

8    outward concentration radius. The main article and supplement distinguish root         8

9    work from descendant work and context acquisition. They also state that this           9

10   validation law and mesh fallback are not automatically the law and fallback of the     10

11   adaptive deployment service.                                                           11

12       The new Validated work under predictable deployment theorem makes the              12

13   missing bridge explicit. A readout may be selected using validation and the prior      13

14   deployment history. A total-variation allowance bounds the conditional context-law     14

15   change; a conditional bounded-difference argument controls realized work; and          15

16   deterministic endpoint-work caps charge recursive descendants and numerical            16

17   recovery. The proof requires neither independent deployment contexts nor a hidden      17

18   minimizing action. A model hash is not treated as a context-law certificate. The R69   18

19   deployment records do not purport to estimate an unverified drift allowance; they      19

20   supply the separate measured-work comparison.                                          20

             1.7. B4 and M5: Direct cost inference at a meaningful declared precision
30                                                                                             30

31     The new estimator addresses the width problem without increasing sample size            31

32   after inspecting results. For a frozen feasible policy, the discounted sum of optimal     32
     6

1    Bellman advantages along its actual trajectory has expectation equal to its policy       1

2    loss. Subtracting two such sums cancels the expected initial optimum under the           2

3    common initial law. Their expected difference is therefore the original expected         3

4    policy-cost difference. Local regret bounds control the observation range.               4

5        The Bellman-centered cost comparison theorem proves this identity, its range         5

6    and a simultaneous endpoint confidence interval. Its proof does not assert a path- 6
7    wise cash-flow identity, rely on an assumed-correct fitted critic, or compare two loss   7

8    upper bounds as though they were policy costs. The actual returned action is fixed       8

9    before a separately paid tighter optimal-value evaluation. Continuous-bin uncer- 9
10   tainty, all compatible acquired actions, quadrature, action coverage and outward         10

11   arithmetic remain in that calculation.                                                   11

12       The frozen design uses 1,024 new common-path rows for each scalar task, eval- 12
13   uation tolerance equal to the policy target divided by 32, and a loss margin of          13

14   1/256. Numerical and sampling half-width allocations are each 1/512. All twelve          14

15   paired contrasts and both allocations are reported. The four methods include the un- 15
16   changed original R67 ReLU controller loaded from its pinned model, not a retrained       16

17   replacement.                                                                             17

18       All twelve intervals lie within the declared equivalence margin, while all still     18

19   contain zero. This establishes two-sided equivalence at that normalized-loss margin,     19

20   not equality or strict superiority. For adaptive-minus-hat–ReLU, the centered inter- 20
21   vals are approximately [−0.00068263, 0.00070764] and [−0.00160291, 0.00160253],          21

22   rounded outward. Their widths are about 0.00139 and 0.00321, compared with               22

23   about 0.423 and 0.529 for ordinary cash-cost intervals on the same new rows. The         23

24   target has not changed.                                                                  24

25       The inference service is not free: the complete shared processes take approxi- 25
26   mately 9.03 and 408.63 seconds. Conditional Bellman integration dominates the nu- 26
27   merical width; bin propagation is much smaller. Raw pre-intersection components          27

28   are displayed as diagnostics and not misrepresented as an additive decomposition         28

29   of the final intersected width. The two new tasks contain 2,048 common-path rows,        29

30   not that number multiplied by methods, pairs or publication replays. Combining           30

31   centered and ordinary cash-cost statistical families requires the corresponding union    31

32   bound.                                                                                   32

22       Every one of the 188 new processes returned, but this is not a population training- 22
23   success probability. The verifier’s safety does not depend on a predictor converging. 23
24   Achieved objective and dual-gap certificates replace reliance on optimization flags      24

25   for the fixed-feature result. All original seeds, warnings, repetitions and failed       25

26   historical attempts remain distinguishable.                                              26

27       The publication also distinguishes parameter identity from numerical callable- 27
28   policy identity. Readout arrays are checked across the relevant inference and service    28

29   records. That check is necessary, but by itself does not prove all-state equality on     29

30   different arithmetic runtimes. The purchase proposition requires the same numerical      30

31   policy or an additional valid implementation-transfer account. A finite successful       31

32   replay or matching metadata is not silently substituted for that premise.                32

6      The new precision result makes a previously unresolved quality margin assess- 6
7    able for actual complete policies under the original law. The deployment proposition     7

8    then states the full price region for replacing a benchmark controller. For sixty-four   8

9    blocks of eight trajectories with one predetermined adaptive-fallback block, the         9

10   expected candidate-minus-benchmark policy cost is 504 times the normal-policy            10

11   cost difference. The proposition combines that simultaneous interval with complete       11

12   work, the value of a normalized loss unit and the installation fee.                      12

13     The supplementary table reports matched observed savings and zero-fee crit- 13
14   ical price ratios, including all cases with no positive saving. Its interpretation is    14

15   conditional on numerical policy identity and the declared work boundary. It does         15

16   not turn a favorable prefix clock into a completed service, or a timing range into a     16

17   future-latency confidence interval.                                                      17

18     The investment primitives and loss margin remain normalized theoretical quanti- 18
19   ties. No installation fee or monetary welfare magnitude is estimated. This preserves     19

20   the original economic purchase question while separating a proved eligibility con- 20
21   clusion, observed complete work and the purchaser’s price inputs. Equivalence            21

22   at a margin is a substantive precision result, but it is not strict neural economic      22

23   superiority.                                                                             23

24     The economic account now explicitly separates already qualified reuse from             24

25   a first purchase of qualification. The complete reuse receipts do not include the        25

26   separately paid policy-cost inference experiment. Any noncommon qualification            26

27   work must therefore be added to the candidate work before applying the purchase          27

28   proposition. Under the conservative boundary charging the entire observed shared         28

29   inference process, approximately 9.03 or 408.63 seconds, every displayed matched         29

30   work saving becomes negative. The tables and machine record distinguish these            30

31   two boundaries. Neither an unmeasured marginal inference allocation nor an               31

32   unexecuted longer amortization horizon is substituted for that cost.                     32
