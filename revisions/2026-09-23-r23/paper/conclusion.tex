\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:r23conclusion}
Neural Bellman Operators require an economic comparison, not merely a numerical stopping rule for training. The new results identify the financed policy coordinates, remove a redundant budget direction, and prove continuum payoff ordering without synchronized initialization. The held-out experiment shows that the neural--Adam payoff advantage survives both optimizer matching and removal of that redundant direct coordinate. Its interpretation is a parameterization effect within a fixed optimizer and work budget, not greater finite-node expressiveness or overall numerical dominance.

The stronger direct L-BFGS-B results and the adverse neural L-BFGS-B outcome remain visible. Active rejection, exact restoration, and supervisory recovery are supported by their executed records. The exact-price bracket, rank certificate, and nonsynchronized mixture theorem provide separate analytic and executable checks. All depend on stated mathematical hypotheses; agreement between arithmetic backends is not an independent economic proof.

The original full-domain $.01$ objective remains the target for the current-state neural operator. The complete-domain witness has improved, but the available bound does not yet establish that accuracy or a strict payoff improvement for the jointly refined actor. The general residual-correction cone identifies a route that does not rely on the selected replicable consumption family. We retain that objective, the original economy, and the earlier substantive development while reporting exactly which economic comparisons the present calculations establish.
