\section{Certified policy comparisons and current-state verification}\label{sec:r23results}
\subsection{A held-out crossed design with a budget-coordinate control}
The new experiment fixes ensembles 23000, 23100, and 23200, the four vertices of $K=[1.98,2.02]\times[1.24,1.26]$, sixteen time slabs, and a cap of 400 joint objective/gradient evaluations per configuration before execution. Corner $v$ uses seed $\mathrm{ensemble}+v$, so the four corners are independently initialized. The neural architecture is $1$--$16$--$16$--$3$ with tanh hidden layers and 355 trainable coordinates. The direct and direct-quotient versions use 48 and 47 coordinates. Adam uses step size $.005$; L-BFGS-B uses the same objective and a maximum of 40 line-search steps, with function and gradient tolerances $10^{-13}$ and $10^{-8}$. On budget exhaustion the last accepted L-BFGS-B iterate is restored.

All four primary neural/direct arms have exactly matched delivered initial policies. The quotient arm fixes the sixteenth raw intercept to zero and subtracts it from the other fifteen. This changes coordinates but not the financed family. Its delivered initial discrepancy is bounded and separately recorded; nonidentical binary64 incumbents are priced separately rather than assigned the neural incumbent's interval. No configuration is removed. A proposed final policy replaces the initial incumbent only when its independently certified payoff lower bound exceeds the incumbent's payoff upper bound. Unsuccessful proposals would leave the incumbent in place.

The primary computation uses positive quadrature only to generate proposals. Regret and acceptance use the inherited continuous-time stopped-policy checker, including its quadrature remainder, Gaussian tail, settlement, and unrestricted dual comparison. The nonsynchronized Jensen theorem then certifies every reported regional statement. This is not inference from four pointwise successes alone. These continuum properties are a posteriori checks of the frozen policies; the comparisons to be reported were specified prospectively.

All 72 final proposals are feasible and strictly improve their respective incumbent at the vertex where they were trained. The correct interpretation remains conditional on optimizer and budget. Direct L-BFGS-B obtains regional regret below $.002839$ for every ensemble, with fewer objective/gradient evaluations than the corresponding neural L-BFGS-B run. One neural L-BFGS-B ensemble has regret upper $.01050307$ despite a valid financing check and strict policy improvement. That observation is retained in Table~\ref{tab:r23crossed}. \subsection{Costs, numerical checks, and proof-critical arithmetic}
The held-out study uses 21,541 objective evaluations and the same number of gradient evaluations, 68.729 seconds of policy generation, and 188.034 seconds of final checking. Its complete elapsed time is 290.946 seconds, including initial-policy checks and overhead. The peak process resident set is 428,544 KiB. These are single-thread measurements in the recorded environment, not cross-hardware speed claims. The inherited non-neural unrestricted dual library cost of 415.789 seconds is additional and common to the methods. Both an unamortized cost and an explicitly shared-library interpretation are appropriate; neither sets that construction cost to zero. The supplement reports generation, checking, optimizer calls, parameter counts, environment, and the precise meaning of the extra-call proxy for every vertex.

The largest observed proposal-rule root residual is $4.45\times10^{-16}$ after outward display rounding. This is a numerical diagnostic, not a bound on exact economic prices. The unchanged directed checker certifies the delivered reserves and payoffs. In a separate proof-instantiation pass, all 72 frozen policies receive continuous Gaussian price brackets under Theorem~\ref{thm:r23budget}. The smallest verified endpoint margin exceeds $2.05\times10^{-10}$. The weakest bracket-wide derivative lower bound is only $5.45\times10^{-23}$; it is valid but conservative and is not presented as useful local conditioning. No policies are modified by this pass.

A separate MPFR rank computation encloses the determinant of the sixteen-node hidden-feature matrix in $[5.48546568232,5.48546568245]\times10^{-6}$, away from zero. This supports the explicit interpolation construction, not the behavior of either optimizer. The finite-difference, shift, and nonfinite-input regressions are code tests rather than substitutes for the proofs. The theorem-to-function-to-result map in the supplement distinguishes these roles.

\subsection{The original current-state operator and active acceptance}
The full-state numerical object remains distinct from the regional experiments. In the recorded current-state refinement, the actor and witness consume $(t,u,x)$, jointly have 2,500 parameters, and begin from the retained full-state checkpoint. Four hundred gradient evaluations produce 267 accepted L-BFGS-B iterations. A complete MPFR cover of 1,024 cells, with no skipped cells, and the discounted suffix construction tighten the all-start-time original-economy upper bound from $7.2783190635$ to \RXXIIGlobalBound{}. This is a genuine continuous-domain witness improvement. It is not a proof that the jointly changed actor has improved in payoff, and it is not the unchanged $.01$ accuracy target. The successful regional systems cannot be substituted into that sentence.

The residual-correction cone of Proposition~\ref{prop:r22cone} supplies a constructive comparison for general generators, including generators without exact replication. Its computed coefficients remain limited by the residuals they have to dominate. Thus the mathematical extension beyond the selected hedgeable consumption streams does not imply an executed sharp solution of the unspanned full-state problem. The inherited high-dimensional warm-start comparisons retain their separate models, finite-query scopes, training costs, and the stronger accelerated baseline at the strictest high-dimensional target.

An active-rejection study is retained because the new 72-configuration design has no rejected final proposals. In that study four of eight prescribed proposals are rejected, and exact model, optimizer, and random-state restoration is checked. Repeating an unchanged deterministic block reproduces rejection, exposing stagnation rather than hiding it. A separately identified supervisor changes the proposal schedule outside the restored state and yields six accepted and two rejected blocks. These are targeted robustness tests, not a convergence theorem for Adam or L-BFGS-B. They demonstrate a needed distinction between safe rollback and productive proposal generation.

\subsection{Deployment, scaling, and economic scope}
The regional implementation retains four financed subaccounts and fixed initial-state mixture weights. Networks are compiled to 192 binary64 coefficients, occupying 1,536 coefficient bytes per four-expert system; conditional prices and their derivatives still require integration. The retained four-expert timing diagnostic has at most 8,192 quadrature nodes per query and median latency about $.160$ milliseconds. Tests with 8, 16, and 32 duplicated experts measure implementation scaling only. They neither train a larger cover nor certify the accuracy of an online quadrature rule. Proposition~\ref{prop:r22cover} states the conditional domain/tolerance complexity, including its dimension dependence; it does not remove a high-dimensional covering cost by assertion.

The earlier fixed-policy price transport remains a sensitivity result on its stated $k$ interval, not current-state generalization. The $.002$ wealth increment is a \emph{sufficient re-budgeted wealth increment}: learned slopes and preference-drift shapes are retained while the financing offset is changed and checked again. Proposition~\ref{prop:r23wealth} explains the order of the corresponding exact-price family. Neither that proposition nor the numerical counterfactual estimates the minimum compensating variation of an unchanged policy map.
