10            1.2. B1, M1 and M10: The NBO contribution and the role of a predictor            10

11                                                                                             11

12       The referee is right that the validity of the safeguarded query procedure does        12

13   not identify a neural performance advantage. The revised introduction keeps the           13

14   original constructive NBO continuation central and distinguishes it from the op- 14
15   tional action-query proposal. A continuation is fitted against its own attained future,   15

16   qualifies residual oscillation and original-optimum policy loss, and constructs a         16

17   complete robust controller. Exact conditional integration of a shallow continuation       17

18   does not require an optimal-value descendant tree. These are constructive results         18

19   about the NBO itself, not conclusions inferred from the safe return of an optional        19

20   predictor.                                                                                20

21       The new question is whether qualification charges the represented function con- 21
22   sistently and whether a policy-cost result depends on a single numerically selected       22

23   readout. The affine-exact qualification proposition proves a common calibration           23

24   for the two fixed bases. The optimization-to-policy theorem and exact switch du- 24
25   als certify a class of readouts rather than only an optimizer seed. The observed          25

26   cost intervals can then extend to those classes without adding a statistical union        26

27   bound. This develops the original operator-to-policy-to-economic-cost chain while         27

28   retaining the method-neutral query theorem as a complementary result.                     28

29       The title remains Neural Bellman Operators. We do not claim that every optional       29

30   neural component improves work, or that the present expected-cost identities prove        30

31   results for all historical nonlinear recursive objectives. Those distinctions identify    31

32   the actual mathematical objects rather than substitute a new topic.                       32

                        1.9. B8: Fitting reliability versus return reliability
25                                                                                              25

26       The new theorem addresses a precise deterministic reliability question. Let U          26

27   be the attained feasible readout’s qualification objective. A verified switch lower        27

28   bound exceeding U + ϵ excludes every competing action throughout that objective            28

29   sublevel, including ties. Since the unknown optimum is at most U, every ϵ-optimal          29

30   readout belongs to that sublevel.                                                          30

31       Three of the four calibrated terminal-label problems have fully stable first-date      31

32   covers at zero extra allowance. Every exact minimizer of each such problem yields          32
                                  RESPONSE TO THE REFEREE                                7

1    the attained complete policy, and positive uniform dyadic allowances are also re- 1
2    ported. Larger allowances leave some cells unresolved; coarse ReLU is partially un- 2
3    resolved even at zero. For partial coverage the theorem gives an explicit original-law   3

4    cost enlargement based on the initial mass of uncertified cells. These enlargements      4

5    are not presumed small enough to preserve every empirical sign.                          5

                            1.10. B9 and M9: The economic decision
13                                                                                            13

14     The new actual-cost intervals give nonempty conditional charge regions. For            14

15   the fine calibrated basis comparison, a Bernstein per-deployment premium below           15

16   the lower ReLU-minus-Bernstein cost endpoint is covered by its identified policy- 16
17   cost saving; a premium above the upper endpoint favors ReLU after charges. The           17

18   intermediate region is unresolved. Both estimators’ marginal and pair intervals can      18

19   be combined on their original simultaneous event, and exact difference-constraint        19

20   closure sharpens these regions without additional sampling.                              20

21     The attaining cost potentials and the zero-charge pure-choice regret calculation       21

22   for the nine-controller catalogue are deposited. Four-term interaction intervals         22

23   are reported separately rather than silently treating the pair-constraint solution       23

24   as sharp for a smaller set including those extra restrictions. The preceding sharp       24

25   deployment-allocation and finite-cohort theorems remain available under their            25

26   original premises.                                                                       26

27     The amounts are normalized model units. We do not invent a monetary loss scale,        27

28   deployment count, processor price or installation charge, nor substitute a sample        28

29   count for an economic deployment horizon. The economic advance is an identified          29

30   constructed-policy cost and conditional choice region, including an optimizer-class      30

31   interpretation where stability is certified. It is not an empirical calibration or a     31
