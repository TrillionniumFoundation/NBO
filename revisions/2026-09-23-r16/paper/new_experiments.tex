\subsection{State-space candidate generators}
We implement a controlled Markov-chain approximation with nonnegative transition weights and a semi-Lagrangian weak Euler approximation with correlated shocks. Each uses the same utility, discount, action bounds, state domain, and first-exit settlement. Refinement uses 9, 17, and 25 nodes per state coordinate, 64, 128, and 256 time steps, and progressively richer bounded control-head grids. The Markov-chain implementation uses wide diffusion jumps and a checked nonnegative stay probability; the semi-Lagrangian method uses four symmetric shock directions. Approximate trajectories apply the liquidation contract at their first straight-segment exit. This discretization is a candidate-generation device, not an asserted exact solution of the stopped diffusion.

For verification, each numerical policy is extended by bilinear interpolation in state and piecewise-constant interpolation in time. Its portfolio head is multiplied by $(2-x)/1.5$, exactly as in the neural candidate class. These are feasible policies in the unchanged economy, although the candidate class is narrower than the original collection of all admissible controls. The upper comparison still uses every original control. We enclose the interpolated policies with the same complete verifier and the fixed seed 16100 final critic. Thus their discrete value outputs are never used as continuous-time upper or lower bounds.

\section{Arithmetic independence and fresh price-continuum construction}\label{sec:r16price}
We distinguish two complete calculations. The first re-evaluates all eighteen historical policy/dual nodes with a new arithmetic implementation. Every basic operation and every exponential, logarithm, square root, and tanh endpoint is evaluated using 128-bit MPFR directed rounding, followed by directed conversion to binary64 endpoints. Stored network weights and policy constants are interpreted as exact dyadic inputs, while model decimals are enclosed as exact rationals. Invalid domains and division through zero fail closed. The kernel passes 450 exact-rational regression checks, but these checks are diagnostics rather than a formal proof of the implementation.

The full-node MPFR calculation recomputes stopped policy payoffs, dual source integrals, expenditure intervals, and the final exact rational envelope. It does not read historical certified endpoints as inputs. It does share the R14 analytic decomposition, rational Gauss-node isolation, derivative remainder lemmas, and stopping inequalities. Thus it is an independent established-arithmetic cross-check of the decisive calculation, not an independent discovery of every mathematical premise. Its complete price-uniform bound is \RMPFRGap; full lower and upper intervals, not three pointwise comparisons, are retained at every node.

The second calculation starts from the economic model alone. It initializes the price set at $1/2$ and $8$, generates a 16-slab feasible time-control policy at each node by deterministic SLSQP initialization, and fits the corresponding dual from deterministic starts. It then selects the worst exact current envelope breakpoint, rounded to a spacing of $1/1024$; a repeated node triggers bisection of the widest price interval. The declared stopping rule is a proved uniform gap below $.01$ or thirty freshly generated nodes. No inherited policy, dual pilot, or historically selected intermediate node is read. Optimizer attempts, unsuccessful intermediate envelopes, and resource use are retained.

The fresh pipeline reaches \RFreshGap\ with \RFreshNodes\ generated nodes. This result belongs to the time-control/dual method, not to the neural feedback. The fresh calculation uses the retained rational--Taylor interval kernel; the independent MPFR label belongs only to the separately re-evaluated eighteen-node object. Fresh generation closes a reproducibility distinction without removing the dual relaxation's accuracy floor. The original $16/32/64$-slab experiment and its failure to attain $.005$ are retained below. Neither extra price nodes nor quadrature refinement alone supplies a convergence law to arbitrarily small error.

The canonical replication command executes all new neural trajectories and checks, both state-space solvers, the fresh price library, the historical-node MPFR audit, the structured inventory experiment, and the normalization calculations. Source identities, input hashes, complete cell records, failures, and hardware/software metadata accompany the results. Timings in different concurrent experiment processes are descriptive; they are not used to infer solver superiority from unequal accuracy or unequal processor contention.
