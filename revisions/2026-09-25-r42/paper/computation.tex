\section{New results and verification}
The twelve declared structural cases all have positive implementation cost. Their independent checks reconstruct the model contract, policy probabilities, every restart loss, initial implementation cost, and residual-corrected lower bound. The largest program has $64\cdot64=4096$ policy simplexes and 16,384 variables. These are exact certificates for the original continuous-state randomized objective through the analytic aggregation theorem. They are not a restriction of the original optimizer to an assumed finite policy sieve.

The density experiment has three declared perturbation sizes. Two permit both adjusted finite solves; one fails the sufficient tightening condition and is recorded as such. The finite check does not independently machine-prove the analytic density norm. The density calculation and finite proof checks are kept separate in the coverage map.

Forty-eight additional exact property tests exercise the slack-ladder repair on action-dependent three-state, three-action kernels with horizons 1, 2, 4, and 6 and discount factors $1$, $0.95$, and $0.8$. They check all-state feasibility, weak improvement of operating value, the policy-TV bound, and the implementation-cost bound. The tests complement the analytic proof; a finite test suite is not a proof of a general theorem.

\section{Verification boundary}
The independent structural checker does not import the constructor, NumPy, SciPy, or an optimizer. It does share the mathematical specification and the standard rational-arithmetic runtime. Thus its independence is algorithmic and implementation-level rather than independence from every possible common software fault. The structural identities and infinite-dimensional reduction are written proofs, not machine-checked formal developments. The models are designed known-model economies, not empirically identified institutions. All such boundaries are visible in the current article rather than being hidden in a manifest.
