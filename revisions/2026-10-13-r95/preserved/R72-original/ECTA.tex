\begin{abstract}
This paper develops Neural Bellman Operators for constructive policy evaluation and feasible improvement. A witness-preserving continuation connects approximation to the original economic optimum. Verified Bellman comparisons supply a trainable query loss and an acquired-policy guarantee. We prove that a frozen fitted continuation supports original-law cost inference with a residual-oscillation range; shallow ReLU continuations admit exact conditional integration in the investment model. A second theorem transports purchased Bellman witnesses across states after capacity repair, preserving accuracy under adaptive reuse. A finite-cover account separates fill cost from retrieval and verification. The retained training and cost-inference study is complemented by a predeclared scalar and two-control reuse experiment and independent rational checks. Policy-cost equivalence, computational savings and their qualification costs remain distinct economic objects. All preceding mathematical results and adverse evidence are preserved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction65}
A numerical continuation matters because it helps produce an economic decision. The error of a fitted value, the loss of the implemented policy and the resources required to verify that loss are different objects. A policy may improve without a tighter numerical certificate; a tighter certificate may leave the policy unchanged. A useful computational method must connect its approximation and resource choices to a feasible decision under the original economic law.

This paper develops Neural Bellman Operators (NBOs) along that chain. The constructive implementation evaluates a continuation against its own fitted future and retains feasible action witnesses. Its exact affine--ReLU realization and primitive error budget do not require a theorem that unrestricted nonconvex training succeeds. A complementary recursive implementation queries the original Bellman problem only where its action search requires information. A trained readout proposes actions; verified lower and upper bounds determine whether the actual policy meets its original-optimum contract.

The nonlinear investment economy has state-dependent capacity, bilinear transitions and one or two independent continuous innovations. Primitive curvature controls the convexity defect of the transition and supplies regularity of the true Bellman value. The action certificate can therefore dispense with a stored state lattice. A strong-convexity upper chord sometimes certifies a rounded action before another endpoint query. Protected conventional refinement remains available when a proposal fails. The certificate-aware fixed-feature readout and its optimization gap make the resulting training problem constructive. Neither a fitted neural continuation nor an arbitrary proposed action is assumed convex.

Removing a state lattice does not remove the cost of evaluating a future. A first original-value query can generate a large descendant tree. Independently evaluating optimal advantages for precise policy-cost inference can generate another such tree. We address these two costs separately, while retaining the original operator, economic law and optimum.

First, a frozen fitted NBO continuation can replace the optimal value in the cost observation. Its Bellman-residual oscillations and implemented greedy gaps control both the observation's range and the actual policy loss. A terminal correction makes the identity valid even when the fitted terminal value is inexact. State-independent continuation shifts cancel. For shallow affine--ReLU continuations, the original one- and two-innovation conditional expectations are finite positive-part moment calculations. An explicit state-and-action-cover construction qualifies their residuals without querying the optimal Bellman tree. This is an approximation, integration and certification result, not a claim that a fitted critic is already accurate because its training loss is small.

Second, a lower original-Bellman bound and its feasible upper witness can be transported to a nearby state. The rule pays for state displacement, capacity repair and action rounding before deciding whether the old certificate closes at the new tolerance. A failed check invokes the original recursive verifier. The policy guarantee holds after history-dependent selection among valid records, without assuming equality with the fresh-search policy. A finite-cover theorem states the tighter fill precision and retention conditions needed to bound the number of expensive fills. The executed bounded cache is evaluated by its actual counts, rather than by premises it does not satisfy.

These additions distinguish three uses of a neural object: a constructed continuation, an optional query proposal and a proposed record for retrieval. Their economic value is not identified by calling a common correctness argument neural. The neural continuation now enters a computable original-law evaluation identity; the proposal and retrieval mechanisms can change work but cannot manufacture a value bound. Exact ReLU encodings and conventional piecewise-linear interpretations remain related representations, not independent algorithms with an automatic ranking.

The retained frozen study executes certificate-aware ReLU and equally sized Bernstein readouts, independent-context validation, sixty-four-block reuse, direct continuous-law policy-cost inference and trained two-control comparisons. Its twelve scalar cost contrasts meet the prespecified equivalence margin, but none identifies strict superiority. Its complete cold-start evidence does not support a uniform neural work advantage. The new predeclared experiment compares fresh calculation, exact-key reuse and geometrically transported witnesses on the same localized query workloads, including a four-date, two-control stress case. It counts every original-law descendant on a miss and every rejected lookup, invalidation and durable record. The workload is not relabeled as independent policy-cost data.

\paragraph{Relation to existing methods.}
Lipschitz extension and distance transforms underlie the constructive envelope \citep{mcshane1934,felzenszwalb2012}. Approximate and safe policy iteration have established error and performance analyses \citep{munos2003,pirotta2013,scherrer2015}; \citet{yang2020convex} studies convex dynamic-programming approximation. Learned warm starts and optimization-based control have their own literature \citep{sakaue2023warm,sambharya2023,agrawal2020control}. Our contribution connects those numerical choices to an acquired feasible policy, a verified stopping decision and the complete cost of returning and qualifying the controller.

Value-function control variates and telescoping identities are established evaluation tools \citep{munos2006variance,jiang2016}. Here a verified residual-oscillation bound supplies both an original-optimum policy account and the concentration range of the actual original-law cost difference. Exact conditional ReLU integration and the explicit residual-cover account state what must be purchased to replace optimal-value centering by a fitted continuation. The witness-transfer theorem likewise supplements ordinary memoization with a capacity-feasible upper witness and a lower bound on the original continuous-action infimum.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion65}
Neural Bellman Operators connect the construction of a continuation to feasible decisions under the original economic law. The witness-producing implementation has an explicit own-future error account. Recursive action verification permits trained proposals without allowing fitting error to alter the policy certificate. The present additions place a frozen neural continuation directly in original-law policy-cost evaluation and permit purchased original-value witnesses to be reused after a verified capacity repair.

The resulting resource distinctions are substantive. Exact conditional moments remove optimal-value descendants from the evaluation of a qualified shallow continuation, but fitting and uniform residual qualification remain paid operations. Transported witnesses remove descendants on a certified cache hit, but the first fill and every later miss retain their original horizon cost. A history-dependent reuse controller retains a loss guarantee; it is not automatically the same policy used in an earlier independent cost experiment.

The retained cost-inference evidence identifies equivalence at a declared numerical margin, not strict superiority. The new query experiment tests localized reuse, not universal neural speed or calibrated welfare. Together with the constructive theorems, these results identify what can be certified, reused and economically compared, and what resources each step requires. They extend the original NBO policy-evaluation and improvement objective while preserving every historical application, assumption and unfavorable comparison for renewed scrutiny.
