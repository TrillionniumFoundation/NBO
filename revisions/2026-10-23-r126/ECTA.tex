\begin{abstract}
Neural Bellman Operators construct continuation critics and feasible actors
for recursive economic decisions. We separate action-aligned regret from
absolute-value precision, derive rationally verified finite calculations of
the information in complete Bellman supports, and prove continuous-action
regret resolution with a sharp grid bound. Exact geometry reuse preserves
all decisions and certificates while reducing repeated algebra. Two
prospectively frozen studies retain the original continuous-risk investment
model, 56 neural and conventional actors, and strong online and dynamic-programming
controls. Direct stopping meets the stronger regret target in all 522 service
cases, compared with 296 for the fused control. Independent reconstruction
certifies a lower mean original cost against that control, but leaves the
comparison with warm adaptation unresolved. In a fresh paired study,
geometry reuse reduces complete direct-service cost by 51.3 percent; direct
service remains 22.2 percent more expensive than equally optimized warm
adaptation. Complete-support computation sharpens 48 contrast bounds without
producing an additional qualified policy gain. Independent finite-design
refitting and exact arithmetic audits delimit each claim. Matched hinge
controls and complete-cost adoption comparisons preserve the remaining
neural-specific contribution question.
\end{abstract}
