\section{Using the action information in every paid Bellman support}
\label{sec:memory123}

An original Bellman calculation can purchase several valid supports before
returning a qualified action. A refusal is an economic decision about a
candidate; it need not invalidate the original information acquired at that
candidate. The single-payload bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:supportgradient122} uses one such support. We now retain
the complete same-state collection, intersect its implications along the
feasible action path, and integrate the result. This changes the supplied
information interface, so it does not contradict the fixed-payload lower
bound in Proposition~\ref{prop:informationminimax122}. It applies equally
to neural, polynomial, spline, matched-hinge and original-model proposals.

The economic objective is the same original optimal-future function
$q=Q_x$ on $[0,A]$, with $0<\mu\le L$, and the implemented cost remains
$J(b)=q(b)+\eta(b)$, $0\le\eta(b)\le\delta$. All anchors below concern
the same state, original innovation law, risk preference and future target.
Supports from another actor's separately charged service are not silently
pooled. If an experiment purchases a common pool, every comparator must
receive that pool and its full acquisition bill.

At each of $m\ge1$ paid anchors $a_i\in[0,A]$, suppose the valid
original information includes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:memorydata123}
 W_i\le q(a_i)\le U_i,\qquad
 \ell_i\le q'(a_i)\le u_i.
\end{equation}
The derivative interval can be obtained by the boundary-aware conversion
of an original quadratic support, intersected with any separately proved
original-gradient enclosure. A fitted marginal alone is not such an
interval. Repeated measurements at one anchor are retained and
intersected; an empty intersection is an error, not a favorable certificate.

\subsection{Intersect before integrating}

For $t\in[0,A]$ define
\begin{align}
 \lambda_i(t)
 &=\ell_i+\mu(t-a_i)+(L-\mu)\min\{t-a_i,0\},\nonumber\\
 \upsilon_i(t)
 &=u_i+\mu(t-a_i)+(L-\mu)\max\{t-a_i,0\},\label{eq:cones123}\\
 \lambda(t)&=\max_i\lambda_i(t),\qquad
 \upsilon(t)=\min_i\upsilon_i(t).\nonumber
\end{align}
These are bounds on the original derivative along the entire action
interval. With the usual signed-integral convention, put
\begin{align}
 C^+(a,b)&=
 \begin{cases}
  \int_a^b\upsilon(t)\,dt,&b\ge a,\\
  -\int_b^a\lambda(t)\,dt,&b<a,
 \end{cases}\label{eq:upperpath123}\\
 C^-(a,b)&=
 \begin{cases}
  \int_a^b\lambda(t)\,dt,&b\ge a,\\
  -\int_b^a\upsilon(t)\,dt,&b<a.
 \end{cases}\nonumber
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Action contrast from the complete paid derivative collection]
\label{thm:memorycontrast123}
Suppose $q$ is continuously differentiable, $\mu$-strongly convex and has
$L$-Lipschitz derivative, and \eqref{eq:memorydata123} holds. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:memoryvalid123}
 \lambda(t)\le q'(t)\le\upsilon(t),\qquad
 C^-(a,b)\le q(b)-q(a)\le C^+(a,b).
\end{equation}
For each individual anchor, define $C_i^+$ by using
$\lambda_i,\upsilon_i$ in \eqref{eq:upperpath123}, and similarly define
$C_i^-$. At every fixed pair of feasible actions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:memorydominance123}
 C^+(a,b)\le\min_i C_i^+(a,b),\qquad
 C^-(a,b)\ge\max_i C_i^-(a,b).
\end{equation}
Thus the comparison is with the best individual anchor in the entire
paid collection. The inequalities can be strict because the informative
anchor can change along the action path. Adding sound information can
only narrow these contrast intervals.

For fixed $a$, the upper contrast $b\mapsto C^+(a,b)$ is continuous and
$\mu$-strongly convex. Its unique minimizer on $[0,A]$ is obtained from
the following exact rule. If $\lambda(a)\le0\le\upsilon(a)$, retain $a$.
If $\lambda(a)>0$, minimize to the left: choose $0$ when
$\lambda(0)\ge0$, and otherwise the unique zero of $\lambda$ in
$(0,a)$. If $\upsilon(a)<0$, choose $A$ when
$\upsilon(A)\le0$, and otherwise the unique zero of $\upsilon$ in
$(a,A)$. This is an optimal guaranteed-contrast proposal for the
derived upper model, not a claim that the actual economic optimum has
been identified.
\end{theorem}

The global envelopes are continuous piecewise affine. With rational
inputs, their breakpoints, integrals and zeros can be computed by rational
arithmetic. Section~\ref{supp:memory123} gives an explicit finite partition.
The calculation pays for every retained record, intersection, integral,
proposal and rejected qualification; zero new original-risk queries does
not mean zero computation cost.

\subsection{Absolute qualification and the original implemented future}

Retain the raw quadratic supports as well. If the actual original support
slope at $a_i$ is known only to belong to $[p_i^-,p_i^+]$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rawsupport123}
 s_i(b)=W_i+\min\{p_i^-(b-a_i),p_i^+(b-a_i)\}
                   +\frac\mu2(b-a_i)^2.
\end{equation}
Here $W_i$ denotes the lower level of that very support. When a separate
value lower bound is available, it can also be used in
\eqref{eq:memorydata123}. Each $s_i$ is valid everywhere, but a nonzero
slope-interval width can give it a downward derivative jump at its
anchor. Its curvature term alone does not make this robust lower
function convex.

Define the pointwise value bounds and the original-optimum bound
\begin{align}
 \underline q(b)
  &=\max_i\{W_i+C^-(a_i,b),\ s_i(b)\},\nonumber\\
 \overline q(b)
  &=\min_i\{U_i+C^+(a_i,b)\},\label{eq:memoryabsolute123}\\
 B&=\max\left\{B_{\rm old},\
               \min_{t\in[0,A]}\max_i s_i(t)\right\}.
               \nonumber
\end{align}
The inherited $B_{\rm old}$ is any previously verified lower bound on
the optimum of this same $q$; it may be omitted. The minimum in the last
line is a global piecewise calculation, not the output of an unchecked
convex optimizer. Then $\underline q\le q\le\overline q$ and
$B\le\min q$.

\begin{theorem}[A qualified policy update from retained information]
\label{thm:memorypolicy123}
For distinct realized feasible actions $a,b$,
\begin{align}
 J(b)-J(a)&\in[C^-(a,b)-\delta,\ C^+(a,b)+\delta],\nonumber\\
 \underline q(b)&\le J(b)\le\overline q(b)+\delta,\label{eq:memorypolicy123}\\
 0\le J(b)-\min q&\le\overline q(b)+\delta-B.\nonumber
\end{align}
The signed contrast interval may be intersected with
$[\underline q(b)-\overline q(a)-\delta,
\overline q(b)+\delta-\underline q(a)]$.
For $b=a$, the same implemented policy has contrast exactly zero.

A candidate is adopted only if its checked contrast upper endpoint is
negative and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:memorygate123}
 \overline q(b)+\delta-B\le\tau,
 \qquad
 \overline q(b)+\delta-\underline q(b)\le\omega.
\end{equation}
These conditions preserve the original regret and objective-width
requirements. The bounds are recomputed at the actually rounded feasible
action. A finite sequence of accepted changes has actual total gain at
least the sum of its certified gains, by telescoping original costs.
No differentiability, continuity or convexity of $\eta$ is required.
\end{theorem}

The robust-model minimizer is a proposal. Qualification may reject it.
A fixed finite menu can additionally contain prescribed fractions,
feasible endpoints, envelope switchpoints and previously completed
qualified actions. The implemented rule and its ties must be frozen
before an independent experiment. Uniformly improved lower guarantees
do not imply that one selected candidate has lower actual cost than
another candidate: that comparison requires its own original-policy
contrast. All economic and complete-work comparisons therefore remain
part of the numerical study.

The declared implementation gives the individual-anchor and fused modes
the same candidate menu: the six fractions
$1,1/2,1/4,1/8,1/16,1/32$ of each individual robust-model displacement
and of the joint displacement, all realized on the same downward-rounded
$2^{-30}$ action lattice. Both modes use the same fused absolute lower
bound and raw-support optimum bound. The individual mode uses
$\max_i C_i^-$ and $\min_i C_i^+$ for each signed contrast and uses
the latter in every candidate upper value. It therefore chooses the
best available individual-anchor contrast separately for every queried
pair. The fused mode changes precisely the operation of intersecting
before integrating. Equal feasible candidates and common lower bounds
prevent attributing an advantage to a weaker comparator's missing
information or action menu.

\subsection{What can be certified from this information}

There is a sharp interpretation of the derived action model. Discard the
absolute levels and raw supports for this paragraph, and let the
information consist only of $[0,A]$, $\mu,L$, and the derivative
intervals in \eqref{eq:memorydata123}. Suppose this class is nonempty.
Both $\lambda$ and $\upsilon$ are themselves feasible derivatives:
their increments lie between $\mu(t-s)$ and $L(t-s)$ and their values
at every anchor satisfy the supplied intervals. Integrating either
one therefore constructs an admissible objective. It follows that
$C^+(a,b)$ is the exact worst original contrast in this reduced
information class at every fixed $b$.

Let $\Gamma=-\min_b C^+(a,b)\ge0$. With no additional query and only
an arbitrary Borel error $0\le\eta\le\delta$, the largest uniformly
guaranteed implemented-policy improvement is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:memoryminimax123}
                         \max\{0,\Gamma-\delta\}.
\end{equation}
Retention or the deterministic robust-model minimizer attains it;
randomization cannot improve the guarantee. The complete proof includes
random laws straddling the incumbent. This is an information bound for
the stated derivative interface, not an optimality claim after the
additional value levels, original investment formulas, qualification
width and finite-precision implementation are supplied.

The strict rational example in Section~\ref{supp:memorywitness123}
has two derivative anchors. Its joint guaranteed original gain is
$103/96$, whereas the better individual-anchor guarantee is $33/32$;
the difference is exactly $1/24$. It identifies the consequence of
intersecting action information before integrating. It is an abstract
smooth-convex witness, not a replacement investment application or an
observed neural advantage.

\subsection{Purchasing a new anchor only when the paid information is unresolved}
\label{sec:memoryprobe123}

If all free-information candidates fail, a new original calculation
should be directed at the unresolved action region. For a continuous
strictly increasing function $g$, define its constrained zero
\[
 z(g)=\begin{cases}
 0,&g(0)\ge0,\\
 A,&g(A)\le0,\\
 \text{the unique zero of }g,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The optimizer $a_q^*$ of the original optimal-future objective satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bracket123}
 a_q^*\in[\underline a,\overline a]
       :=[z(\upsilon),z(\lambda)].
\end{equation}
Every additional sound payload shrinks this interval. The same
information also gives the remaining original-gain ceiling
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bracketpotential123}
 q(a)-\min q
 \le K_q(a):=\max\{0,\max_{b\in[\underline a,\overline a]}
                          [-C^-(a,b)]\}.
\end{equation}
The ceiling is a global piecewise calculation over the bracket.
It cannot be evaluated by assuming that $C^-$ is convex.

\begin{proposition}[A charged midpoint probe with an explicit precision condition]
\label{prop:midpoint123}
Let the current bracket have width $w=\overline a-\underline a>0$.
Purchase a valid original derivative interval of full width at most
$\epsilon$ at a feasible action $c$ with
$|c-(\underline a+\overline a)/2|\le r$.
After fusing this new interval, the new optimizer bracket has width
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bracketcontraction123}
 w_{\rm new}\le
 \min\left\{w,\max\left\{\frac w2+r,\frac\epsilon\mu\right\}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, if a declared sequence of original-risk calculations
actually achieves $r_k\le\xi w_k$ and
$\epsilon_k\le\theta\mu w_k$, with
$\max\{1/2+\xi,\theta\}=\gamma<1$, then
$w_k\le\gamma^k w_0$. Every queried support, failed accuracy target,
refused policy and arithmetic operation remains charged.
\end{proposition}

The proposition is a precision-qualified bracketing argument, not a
new general bisection theorem or a free derivative oracle. A fixed
128-bin original-risk query may return a derivative interval too wide
to reduce the bracket; that is an unresolved result. At an interior
probe with room $s=\min\{c,A-c\}$ and support gap
$e\le (L-\mu)s^2/2$, the exact R122 conversion supplies the sufficient
full-width bound
\[
 (p^+-p^-)+2\sqrt{2(L-\mu)e}.
\]
Thus the desired action resolution determines a real required support
precision and risk-query bill. A finite-radius implementation must use
its actual wider bound. At a capacity boundary the appropriate
one-sided formula replaces this interior display.

The bracket concerns $a_q^*$, not the minimizer of an arbitrary
$q+\eta$ with discontinuous $\eta$. A bracketed action is still
subject to the original-policy gain, regret and width predicates.
For a single decision without reuse, a qualified incumbent with
regret upper bound $K$ can also be retained whenever the compulsory
price of any new probe exceeds $K$, as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:pricedstop122}. A tighter valid ceiling for
the possible gain in $J$ is $K_q(a)+\delta$, intersected with $K$.
This provides a stated economic stopping condition; it does not
assert optimal sampling from shared-host timings.
