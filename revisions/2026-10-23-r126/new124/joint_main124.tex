\section{Joint action information from paid continuation values}
\label{sec:joint124}

A continuation calculation returns both a value enclosure and action
information. Keeping the value enclosures only as separate endpoint bounds
can discard restrictions on the derivative between endpoints. We use those
restrictions to bound the original action contrast. The economic object
remains the original optimal-future objective $q=Q_x$ on $[0,A]$;
the implemented controller has cost $J=q+\eta$ with
$0\le\eta\le\delta$. All data below concern this one state, preference,
innovation law and future target. A different representation receives the
same data and the same feasible actions, and is charged for the same
acquisition. The new calculation does not identify a neural-specific
approximation capability.

Smooth convex interpolation and its use in performance estimation are
established ideas \citep{THG2017Interpolation122}. The interpolation
lemma below is a scalar constructive specialization. Its role here is to
use the joint information in an original Bellman service and to separate
an optimizer's numerical proposal from an independently checkable economic
certificate. Neither a generic interpolation principle nor numerical
conic optimization is claimed as a new mathematical discovery.

Assume $A>0$, $0<\mu\le L$, and that $q$ is continuously differentiable,
$\mu$-strongly convex, and has $L$-Lipschitz derivative. At finitely many
paid anchors $a_i$ retain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jointdata124}
 W_i\le q(a_i)\le U_i,\qquad
 \ell_i\le q'(a_i)\le u_i.
\end{equation}
Repeated anchors impose all their constraints at the same node. At least
one finite value interval and one finite derivative interval are available.
Every derivative interval must be justified for the original $q$, rather
than inferred from a fitted derivative. A verified global derivative cap
$|q'|\le M$ can be added. If no sharper cap is known, a finite one follows
from any derivative anchor and the bound $L$: one may take
$M=\max\{|\ell_i|,|u_i|\}+LA$.

Let $\mathcal Q_I$ denote exactly this class of functions satisfying the
supplied levels, derivative intervals and declared derivative cap. Assume
it is nonempty; a sound original calculation provides one member. Raw
quadratic supports, additional formulas of the investment model, or an
actor's representation can restrict the class further. Unless explicitly
encoded, those restrictions are not part of $\mathcal Q_I$ and no
sharpness statement below includes them.

\subsection{A finite exact interpolation problem}

For a fixed pair of realized feasible actions $a,b$, form the sorted
distinct nodes $0=x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_n=A$ from both endpoints, every
anchor, and $a,b$. Let $f_i,g_i$ denote unknown value and derivative
at node $x_i$. Put $K=L-\mu$ and, for adjacent nodes, set
\begin{align}
 d_i&=x_{i+1}-x_i,\qquad
 s_i=g_{i+1}-g_i-\mu d_i,\nonumber\\
 w_i&=f_{i+1}-f_i-g_i d_i-\frac\mu2d_i^2.
 \label{eq:interpolationcoordinates124}
\end{align}
For $K>0$ impose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjacentinterpolation124}
 0\le s_i\le Kd_i,
 \qquad
 s_i^2\le2K w_i,
 \qquad
 s_i^2\le2K(s_i d_i-w_i).
\end{equation}
Add the paid bounds \eqref{eq:jointdata124} at their nodes and
$-M\le g_i\le M$. Finite value boxes can be supplied without
additional oracle calls:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:valueboxes124}
 \max_j\{W_j-M|x_i-a_j|\}\le f_i\le
 \min_j\{U_j+M|x_i-a_j|\}.
\end{equation}
When $K=0$, replace \eqref{eq:adjacentinterpolation124} by the linear
equalities $s_i=w_i=0$. Let $\mathcal P_I(a,b)$ be the resulting
finite feasible set. Every inequality has at most quadratic degree;
the two quadratic inequalities are convex and have a rotated-cone
representation. Only a linear number of adjacent interpolation
constraints is required after sorting the nodes.

\begin{theorem}[Exact scalar interpolation with joint levels and marginals]
\label{thm:jointinterpolation124}
A list $(f_i,g_i)$ belongs to $\mathcal P_I(a,b)$ if and only if
it is the restriction of a function in $\mathcal Q_I$ to these nodes.
Such a function can be constructed with a continuous piecewise-affine
derivative. Consequently
\begin{align}
 U_I(a,b)&:=\max_{\mathcal P_I(a,b)}(f_b-f_a)
          =\max_{q\in\mathcal Q_I}\{q(b)-q(a)\},\nonumber\\
 L_I(a,b)&:=\min_{\mathcal P_I(a,b)}(f_b-f_a)
          =-U_I(b,a).
 \label{eq:exactcontrast124}
\end{align}
Here subscripts $a,b$ refer to their nodes. These extrema are attained.
The result includes boundary actions and repeated measurements. At
$A=0$ the only action contrast is zero and no interpolation problem is
needed.
\end{theorem}

For clarity, consider the derivative-envelope bound made from the same
derivative intervals. At $t\in[0,A]$ define
\begin{align*}
 \lambda(t)&=\max_i\{\ell_i+\mu(t-a_i)
                        +(L-\mu)\min(t-a_i,0)\},\\
 \upsilon(t)&=\min_i\{u_i+\mu(t-a_i)
                        +(L-\mu)\max(t-a_i,0)\}.
\end{align*}
One may intersect these with the global derivative cap. Integrating
$\upsilon$ for rightward moves and $-\lambda$ for leftward moves gives
the upper contrast $C_D^+$; reversing the endpoints gives the lower
contrast $C_D^-$. Put
\[
 \overline q_D(t)=\min_i\{U_i+C_D^+(a_i,t)\},\qquad
 \underline q_D(t)=\max_i\{W_i+C_D^-(a_i,t)\}.
\]
Every member of $\mathcal Q_I$ satisfies these inequalities. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jointdominance124}
 U_I(a,b)\le
 \min\{C_D^+(a,b),\overline q_D(b)-\underline q_D(a)\}.
\end{equation}
The inequality can be strict. It does not assert dominance over a
different bound using raw supports absent from $\mathcal Q_I$.
An implementation can always intersect the new valid bound with every
previously verified same-objective bound. Thus adding the new calculation
cannot weaken the returned certificate, although its computational cost
can make the resulting service less attractive.

\subsection{A rational certificate that does not trust solver convergence}

Computing a floating point optimum of
\eqref{eq:exactcontrast124} does not give a verified upper contrast.
Write its minimization form as
\[
 \min_{z\in[\underline z,\overline z]}
       H(z),\qquad c_j(z)\le0\quad(j=1,\ldots,m),
\]
where $z=(f,g)$, $H=f_a-f_b$, the $c_j$ are differentiable convex
linear or quadratic constraints, and the finite rational box contains
the values and derivatives of every original function under consideration.
The box may be obtained from \eqref{eq:valueboxes124}, derivative
transport, and their outward intersections. Equality constraints may be
represented by a pair of affine inequalities. Empty intersections or an
inconsistent original payload must be reported, rather than repaired by
discarding an inconvenient observation.

\begin{theorem}[Exact residual correction for an action certificate]
\label{thm:dualcertificate124}
Choose any rational point $z_0$ and any nonnegative rational multipliers
$y_j$. No primal feasibility or stationarity of $z_0$ is required.
Define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal L(z)&=H(z)+\sum_j y_jc_j(z),\nonumber\\
 r&=\nabla\mathcal L(z_0),\nonumber\\
 B&=\mathcal L(z_0)+
   \sum_k\min\{r_k(\underline z_k-z_{0k}),
                    r_k(\overline z_k-z_{0k})\}.
 \label{eq:rationaldual124}
\end{align}
Then every original compatible function satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:verifiedcontrast124}
                    q(b)-q(a)\le -B.
\end{equation}
All quantities in \eqref{eq:rationaldual124} are rational when the
input positions, bounds and curvature constants are rational. The same
construction with $H=f_b-f_a$, $H=f_b$, or $H=-f_b$ gives respectively
a lower contrast, a lower absolute value, or an upper absolute value.
A solver may propose $z_0,y$, but an independent implementation must
verify their finite values, nonnegative multipliers, exact constraint
identity, enclosing box and the final rational expression.
\end{theorem}

This certificate charges any lack of numerical stationarity through
the box residual. It remains sound when the solver fails to converge,
although it may then be too wide to permit a policy change. Arbitrary
nonnegative multipliers, including zero, are valid; a success flag is
neither necessary nor sufficient for a useful certificate. Floating
primal residuals are diagnostics, not replacements for
\eqref{eq:rationaldual124}.

For the original investment model, the derivative cap used here is
independently justified by the retained value modulus. Since
$\|\nabla V_1\|_\infty\le15/d$ and
$\sum_j b_j=3d/8$, the original continuation term is action-Lipschitz
with constant $45\beta/8$. Also $0\le h'(a)\le3/4$ on
$[0,1/4]$. Thus
\[
 |q_x'(a)|\le \frac34+\frac{45\beta}{8}
      =\frac{771}{128}<7
 \qquad(\beta=15/16).
\]
This supplies the conservative cap $M=7$ without fitting it to outcomes.
It bounds the derivative of the original optimal-future objective, not
the derivative of the possibly discontinuous deployed-policy error.

\subsection{The same original-policy gain, regret and value requirements}

Let independently verified bounds satisfy
$\widehat L(a,b)\le q(b)-q(a)\le\widehat U(a,b)$, and
$\underline v_b\le q(b)\le\overline v_b$. These can intersect the
new joint certificates with retained original-value and support bounds.
Let $B_*\le\min_{[0,A]}q$ be any verified original-optimum lower bound.
For distinct realized feasible actions,
\begin{align}
 J(b)-J(a)&\in
 [\widehat L(a,b)-\delta,\widehat U(a,b)+\delta],\nonumber\\
 \underline v_b&\le J(b)\le\overline v_b+\delta,
 \qquad
 J(b)-\min q\le\overline v_b+\delta-B_*.
 \label{eq:jointpolicy124}
\end{align}
For $b=a$ the actual contrast is exactly zero. In particular, any
strictly negative contrast upper endpoint certifies improvement of the
same implemented economic objective. Applying the declared regret
tolerance and objective-width tolerance to the same absolute enclosures
preserves those requirements. All inequalities are evaluated at the
actually rounded feasible action, and every rejected candidate remains
in the work account.

There is also a useful consequence when an incumbent already has
original regret at most $K_a$. If $u$ is a valid upper bound on
$J(b)-J(a)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regrettransport124}
                   J(b)-\min q\le K_a+u.
\end{equation}
Strict improvement therefore preserves an incumbent's regret
qualification without a fresh global lower-bound solve. The absolute
objective-width requirement is a separate checked condition. One must
not infer that width from a gain inequality alone.

Applied at a finite set of candidates, this is a certified candidate
selection rule. The exact continuous-action optimization in
\eqref{eq:exactcontrast124}, a finite implemented menu, and a numerical
search for a useful dual witness are different operations and are
reported separately. Repeated accepted updates telescope their verified
original-policy gains. Their extension through several Bellman dates
uses the retained centered lower-gain recursion with the same incumbent
future, rather than replacing a nonlinear risk evaluation by a physical
expectation.

\subsection{A matching information bound and an economic stopping ceiling}

Let
\[
 \Gamma_I=-\min_{b\in[0,A]}U_I(a,b)\ge0.
\]
The function minimized here is continuous and $\mu$-strongly convex.
Its minimizer is an ideal information benchmark; the finite numerical
menu is not assumed to attain it.

\begin{proposition}[The exact gain at the joint information interface]
\label{prop:jointminimax124}
An admissible method receives only $I$ and chooses a probability law
$\nu$ on the same feasible interval without further original queries.
The adverse $q$ is chosen knowing this law before the action is drawn.
Then
\begin{align}
 \inf_\nu\sup_{q\in\mathcal Q_I}
       \mathbb E_\nu[q(B)-q(a)]&=-\Gamma_I,\nonumber\\
 \inf_\nu\sup_{\substack{q\in\mathcal Q_I\\0\le\eta\le\delta}}
       \mathbb E_\nu[J(B)-J(a)]
       &=\min\{0,-\Gamma_I+\delta\},
 \label{eq:jointminimax124}
\end{align}
where $\eta$ ranges over Borel functions. The robust deterministic
minimizer, or retention when $\Gamma_I\le\delta$, attains the
corresponding bound. It applies equally to all representation labels
at this same interface. It is not a lower bound for free access to the
full investment formulas, additional raw supports or new acquisitions.
\end{proposition}

To turn possible remaining gain into a priced stopping test, let a finite
action set $\mathcal B$ cover $[0,A]$ with radius $h$ and suppose
$k_b$ is a verified upper bound on $q(a)-q(b)$ at each $b\in\mathcal B$.
The cap $|q'|\le M$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jointceiling124}
 q(a)-\min q\le
 K_I:=\max\{0,\max_{b\in\mathcal B}k_b+Mh\}.
\end{equation}
The complete incumbent's regret bound $K_a$ and the future allowance
therefore give the possible implemented-gain ceiling
$\min\{K_a,K_I+\delta\}$. If every allowed continuation of a
single-decision solver must first pay at least $c_*>0$ additional work
units at economic price $\lambda$, and information reuse is excluded,
then
\[
 \lambda c_*\ge\min\{K_a,K_I+\delta\}
\]
certifies that stopping weakly dominates every such continuation.
The work to build the action cover and its certificates has already
been charged. This conditional stopping region does not presume a
forecast of future solver gains or convert shared-host elapsed times
into a universal economic price.

The rational residual formula also provides exact refinement credits.
Hold $\mathcal L,z_0,r$ fixed and replace its enclosing box by a
valid nested box $[\underline z',\overline z']$. The bound improves by
exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:refinementcredit124}
 B'-B=\sum_{r_k\ge0}r_k(\underline z'_k-\underline z_k)
        +\sum_{r_k<0}(-r_k)(\overline z_k-\overline z'_k)\ge0.
\end{equation}
Likewise a paid inequality $f_i-U_i\le0$ with multiplier $y_i^+$
and $W_i-f_i\le0$ with multiplier $y_i^-$ contributes the additional
credit
$y_i^+(U_i-U_i')+y_i^-(W_i'-W_i)$ when only those endpoints tighten.
These are action-certificate sensitivities in original objective units.
They can prioritize a declared refinement menu and quantify the margin
actually purchased. They are not a prior guarantee that a noisy future
query will achieve the proposed endpoint changes; each realized query
and its cost must still be recorded and verified.
