\section{Matching guarantees at a fixed paid-information interface}
\label{supp:informationminimax122}

The sharpness witnesses in Theorem~\ref{thm:supportgradient122} imply a
conditional information bound for the gain component of a decision. We state
the deduction explicitly because comparing representations requires the same
information and the same feasible actions. The conclusion concerns a
particular scalar support interface. It does not assert a general new
minimax optimization principle, a lower bound for access to the full
investment formulas, or a neural-specific advantage.

Fix numbers
\[
 \mathcal I=(A,a,\mu,L,W,U,p),\qquad
 A>0,\quad 0<a<A,\quad 0<\mu\le L,\quad U\ge W.
\]
Here the support slope $p$ is supplied exactly. Let
$\mathcal D=[-a,A-a]$, $e=U-W$, $c=(L-\mu)/2$, and
\[
 I=[g_-,g_+]
   =[p-\Psi(e,c,A-a),\,p+\Psi(e,c,a)].
\]
Both endpoints are finite. This is the exact, untruncated interval:
there is no finite-probe enlargement and no additional gradient bound.
Let $\mathcal Q(\mathcal I)$ consist of all continuously differentiable
functions $q:[0,A]\to\mathbb R$ that are $\mu$-strongly convex, have
$L$-Lipschitz derivative, and satisfy
\[
 q(a)\le U,\qquad
 q(a+d)\ge W+pd+\frac{\mu}{2}d^2
 \quad(d\in\mathcal D).
\]
No other restrictions on $q$ are imposed. In particular, membership does
not assert that $q$ is generated by the investment model. Write
\[
 \sigma_I(d)=\max_{g\in I}gd,\qquad
 \overline m(d)=\sigma_I(d)+\frac L2d^2,\qquad
 \Gamma=-\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\overline m(d)\ge0.
\]
The robust proposal $d^\star$ in
Section~\ref{sec:supportaction122} attains this minimum.

An admissible method receives only the fixed information $\mathcal I$ and
chooses a Borel probability law $\nu$ on $\mathcal D$, without another
objective, derivative, or supporting-value query. A deterministic method
is a point mass. Write $Z\sim\nu$. The adverse function is selected knowing
the announced law, before the displacement is drawn. Thus the adversary
does not observe or respond to a realized random draw. Arbitrary internal
calculations and representation labels do not enlarge the supplied
information.

\begin{proposition}[A matching bound for the fixed support interface]
\label{prop:informationminimax122}
For every admissible law $\nu$,
\begin{align}
 \sup_{q\in\mathcal Q(\mathcal I)}
     \mathbb E_\nu[q(a+Z)-q(a)]
 &=\sigma_I(\mathbb E_\nu Z)+\frac L2\mathbb E_\nu[Z^2]
       \label{eq:qrandomsharp122}\\
 &=\overline m(\mathbb E_\nu Z)
       +\frac L2\operatorname{Var}_\nu(Z).
       \nonumber
\end{align}
The supremum is attained. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qminimax122}
 \inf_{\nu}\sup_{q\in\mathcal Q(\mathcal I)}
     \mathbb E_\nu[q(a+Z)-q(a)]
   =\min_{d\in\mathcal D}\overline m(d)=-\Gamma.
\end{equation}
The deterministic robust proposal attains the value. Randomization cannot
increase the guaranteed gain.

For the implemented-policy interface, let $\mathcal E_\delta$ be the set
of all Borel measurable functions $\eta:[0,A]\to[0,\delta]$, where
$\delta\ge0$, and let $J=q+\eta$. Impose no continuity, convexity, or other
information on $\eta$. Then for every admissible $\nu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jrandomsharp122}
 \sup_{\substack{q\in\mathcal Q(\mathcal I)\\
                  \eta\in\mathcal E_\delta}}
     \mathbb E_\nu[J(a+Z)-J(a)]
 =\sigma_I(\mathbb E_\nu Z)+\frac L2\mathbb E_\nu[Z^2]
        +\delta\,\mathbb P_\nu\{Z\ne0\}.
\end{equation}
This supremum also is attained, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jminimax122}
 \inf_{\nu}\sup_{\substack{q\in\mathcal Q(\mathcal I)\\
                           \eta\in\mathcal E_\delta}}
     \mathbb E_\nu[J(a+Z)-J(a)]
   =\min\{0,-\Gamma+\delta\}.
\end{equation}
The largest uniformly guaranteed implemented-policy gain over this
interface is therefore $\max\{0,\Gamma-\delta\}$. It is attained by
retaining the incumbent when $\Gamma\le\delta$ and choosing $d^\star$
when $\Gamma>\delta$. At equality either action is minimax, although
retention incurs no policy change.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:supportgradient122} gives $q'(a)\in I$ for every
$q\in\mathcal Q(\mathcal I)$. Conversely, for each $g\in I$ define
\[
 q_g(a+d)=U+gd+\frac L2d^2,\qquad d\in\mathcal D.
\]
This function has constant curvature $L$, is $\mu$-strongly convex, and
has the required derivative Lipschitz constant. The support difference is
\[
 q_g(a+d)-\left(W+pd+\frac\mu2d^2\right)
       =e+(g-p)d+cd^2.
\]
For $d>0$, its nonnegativity is equivalent to
$g\ge p-e/d-cd$; requiring this for all positive feasible $d$ is exactly
$g\ge g_-$. For $d<0$, the equivalent condition for all negative
feasible $d$ is $g\le g_+$. At $d=0$ the difference is $e\ge0$.
Thus every $q_g$ belongs to the information class and has $q_g(a)=U$.
This verifies the witnesses against all the supplied information,
including the support away from the anchor.

For an arbitrary $q$ in the class, the smooth upper Taylor inequality
with its single derivative $g=q'(a)$ holds simultaneously at every
feasible displacement:
\[
 q(a+d)-q(a)\le gd+\frac L2d^2.
\]
The compact displacement interval makes these random differences
integrable. Taking expectations and then the supremum gives
\[
 \sup_q\mathbb E_\nu[q(a+Z)-q(a)]
 \le\max_{g\in I}\left\{g\mathbb E_\nu Z
                    +\frac L2\mathbb E_\nu[Z^2]\right\}.
\]
Choose an endpoint $g$ maximizing $g\mathbb E_\nu Z$, or any $g\in I$
when the mean is zero. The single function $q_g$ constructed above gives
equality for every displacement, hence for the announced law. This
proves the first line of \eqref{eq:qrandomsharp122}; the variance
identity proves its second line. Since $\mathbb E_\nu Z\in\mathcal D$,
the right side is at least $\min_{\mathcal D}\overline m$. The point
mass at $d^\star$ attains that lower bound. This proves
\eqref{eq:qminimax122}. In particular the argument does not exchange a
supremum and an expectation of pointwise adversarial choices.

For the implemented future, the difference
$\eta(a+d)-\eta(a)$ is at most $\delta$ if $d\ne0$, and is exactly zero
if $d=0$. Consequently its expectation is bounded above by
$\delta\mathbb P_\nu\{Z\ne0\}$. The single Borel function
\[
 \eta_*(b)=
 \begin{cases}
  0,& b=a,\\
  \delta,& b\ne a
 \end{cases}
\]
attains this bound for every displacement at once. It can be combined
with the same $q_g$ that attained the preceding expectation. This proves
the exact formula \eqref{eq:jrandomsharp122}. In particular a penalty
$\delta$ cannot be charged to retaining the identical action.

It remains to show that mixing retention and replacement cannot improve
the worst-case guarantee. Put $r=\mathbb P_\nu\{Z\ne0\}$. If $r=0$,
\eqref{eq:jrandomsharp122} equals zero. If $r>0$, write
$v=\mathbb E_\nu[Z\mid Z\ne0]\in\mathcal D$ and
$s^2=\operatorname{Var}_\nu(Z\mid Z\ne0)\ge0$.
Positive homogeneity of the interval support function gives
\[
 \sigma_I(\mathbb E_\nu Z)
       +\frac L2\mathbb E_\nu[Z^2]+\delta r
 =r\left\{\overline m(v)+\frac L2s^2+\delta\right\}
 \ge r(-\Gamma+\delta)
 \ge \min\{0,-\Gamma+\delta\}.
\]
For the last inequality, if $-\Gamma+\delta$ is nonnegative the left
bound is nonnegative; if it is negative, multiplication by
$r\le1$ increases it. Retention attains zero. When $\Gamma>\delta$,
$\Gamma>0$ implies $d^\star\ne0$, and the point mass at $d^\star$
attains $-\Gamma+\delta$. If $\Gamma=\delta>0$, that same nonzero
point mass and retention both attain zero. If $\Gamma=\delta=0$,
the robust minimizer is retention, also attaining zero.
These cases establish \eqref{eq:jminimax122} and the stated guarantee.
\end{proof}

The upper and lower guarantees match for every deterministic or randomized
method at this same interface, whether its proposal is represented by a
neural network, a hinge basis, a spline, a polynomial, or no fitted
representation. The conclusion concerns achievable certified improvement
after one fixed payload; it is not a lower bound on the number of original
risk evaluations needed to acquire that payload, on global learning, or on
multistage Bellman error propagation. Nor is it a dominance result after
charging distinct methods' computational costs.

The investment problem supplies additional structure, including its exact
stage cost, risk formulas, derivative cap, and fixed terminal controller.
Its implemented finite-radius, outward-rounded intervals, six-candidate
menu, candidate precision requirement, and complete-policy qualification
checks also differ from this exact interface. Accordingly
Proposition~\ref{prop:informationminimax122} does not assert minimax
optimality of the executed service or impossibility of improvement using
those additional data. The functions $q_g$ and $\eta_*$ prove a lower bound
only over the stated abstract information class; they are not asserted to
be attainable investment-model instances or error functions of the fixed
terminal controller. The original-policy acceptance requirements remain
those of Theorem~\ref{thm:payloadsafeguard122}.
