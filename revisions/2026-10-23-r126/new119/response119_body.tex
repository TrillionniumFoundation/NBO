\hypertarget{the-substantive-addition}{%
\section{The substantive addition}\label{the-substantive-addition}}

The new information algorithm resolves the adverse part of the original innovation distribution before investing in fine quadrature. For a convex K-Lipschitz continuation on the original uniform interval {[}-R,R{]}, write \(t=1-\alpha\) and \(L=2R\alpha\). The complement of a central interval of length L is an admissible AVaR tail with total length 2Rt. The central integral H(u) has monotone, 2K-Lipschitz derivative

\[
D(u)=f(u+L)-f(u).
\]

After m paid sign bisections, a feasible selected tail has risk deficit at most \(KRt\,4^{-m}/2\). Midpoint and secant information on at most n+1 cells within those selected tails encloses their risk average to width \(KRt/n^2\). Thus the complete exact-point information width is at most

\[
KR(1-\alpha)\left(n^{-2}+\tfrac12 4^{-m}\right),
\qquad
N\le 2n+2\lceil\log_2 n\rceil+8
\]

when \(m=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\). Together with the retained lower width \(KR(1-\alpha)/[6(N+1)^2]\), this establishes an error-versus-information order \(KR(1-\alpha)N^{-2}\), with constants uniform in \(\alpha\in[0,1)\), for \(N\ge32\). The method pays for localization. It uses exact point values of a univariate convex Lipschitz continuation and does not assume access to its formula, derivatives, or integrals. The \(\alpha=0\) case uses ordinary whole-interval quadrature; \(\alpha=1\) is excluded from the displayed normalized-tail formula.

The original investment implementation supplies the missing connection between information and a realized decision. Its primitive curvature gives the innovation continuation the strong-convexity modulus 181/64. An outward interval for D(u), including an unresolved sign, produces a constrained strong-convexity defect \ensuremath{\kappa}(u). A feasible fixed tail and global original-continuation supports then enclose the cost of the actual proposed action and give a lower bound on the original optimum over the entire feasible action interval. The deployed terminal actor remains in the policy cost through its established allowance \(\varepsilon_1=2^{-20}\). A new quadrature certificate never silently replaces that actor.

The supplement also develops common-tail action contrasts, an atom-safe remainder for AVaR, and signed recursive policy gains. These statements compare the original actions under the same conditional risk operator. They preserve the reference dual penalty and can discard reference densities whose slack exceeds the possible oscillation of the continuation gain. A paid precision allocation result balances the quadrature and tail-localization allowances under an explicitly stated work model.

These are information and decision-service contributions shared by every representation with the same information and feasible actions. Their value for learned NBO proposals must be assessed in the matched complete-work comparison. They do not constitute a neural-exclusive representation theorem.

\hypertarget{a-bounded-original-policy-preliminary-service}{%
\subsection{A bounded, original-policy preliminary service}\label{a-bounded-original-policy-preliminary-service}}

Proposition \NBOresponseResult{prop:budgetedtail119}{bounded original-policy preliminary certificate} makes the new precision choice executable. A floating endpoint comparison and at most six interior bisections propose a feasible central-window cut. They do not certify its sign or optimality. The two outward original-continuation endpoint values are purchased once, shared between attempted resolutions, and give the constrained strong-convexity defect used in Theorem \NBOresponseResult{thm:investmenttail119}{original-policy qualification from a feasible active tail}. Thus a wrong floating proposal may be inefficient but cannot authorize an invalid decision.

Only tails of exactly positive dyadic length receive midpoint and endpoint payloads. With \(p\in\{1,2\}\) positive tails, the two preliminary resolutions \(m=2,4\) purchase at most

\[
2+p\{(2\cdot2+1)+(2\cdot4+1)\}=2+14p\le30
\]

outward original-continuation values. A boundary tail needs at most 16. The separate floating proposal count is at most \(4+2\cdot6=16\). These are additional outer payload caps per proposed action, including the two defect endpoints. Internal compiler work, duplicate requests, model fitting, rejected attempts, original 128-bin fallback, further conventional repair and any eventual exact recovery remain charged separately. The positive-tail masked ablation instead retains the full six-level ladder, whose corresponding outward preliminary cap is \(2+258p\le518\).

The packed implementation retains that same two-level policy. It concatenates the positive-tail midpoint and endpoint coordinates with their original state and action owners, evaluates them in one original compiler call per attempted resolution, and scatters the resulting interval values and slopes back to the same columns. An exact zero tail contributes zero mass; outward arithmetic still encloses every zero product and sum. The mathematical statement is reindexing and sound enclosure, not bitwise identity: batch-dependent compiler fallback can return different valid bounds. Complete returned-policy identity must therefore be checked in the actual records. The source and full proof are \nolinkurl{new119/budgeted_tail_main119.tex}, \nolinkurl{new119/budgeted_tail_proofs119.tex} and the packing statement in the supplement. Neither payload cap nor packing proves a wall-clock saving.

\hypertarget{bellman-foundations-constrained-gradients-and-stopping-boundaries}{%
\subsection{Bellman foundations, constrained gradients and stopping boundaries}\label{bellman-foundations-constrained-gradients-and-stopping-boundaries}}

The new foundations supplement states sufficient assumptions instead of inferring well-posedness from a residual loss. On compact state spaces with continuous compact feasible-action correspondences, continuous bounded primitives and terminal values, the conditional risk operator must preserve parameter measurability and continuity under essential-uniform changes of continuation. Fixed-law expectation and AVaR satisfy the stated requirements; the AVaR measurability proof takes a countable infimum over rational thresholds. Backward induction then gives continuous finite-horizon values and Borel minimizing selectors, and a separate policy argument compares them with all admissible history policies under the same nested criterion. Stability on this Bellman space is proved for bounded continuous terminal values. A fixed Borel policy has its own bounded-Borel comparison under the kernel property. These different continuation classes are not silently interchanged.

For the original investment model the proof derives the invariant state cube, joint state/action curvature and unique feasible minimizers from the nonlinear primitives. It establishes the original value and action-objective differentiability needed by the certificate, including the constrained envelope

\[
\nabla V(x)=Q_x(x,a^*(x))-\lambda(x)\nabla A(x).
\]

The capacity term cannot be discarded at an active upper action boundary. The actor itself need not be globally differentiable across changes in the active constraint. Downward 40-bit state observation has a separate feasibility and policy-loss transfer; it does not claim that an arbitrary discontinuous rounded policy can be differentiated as the exact optimizer.

For the actor/critic graph the stated objective is \(L(\theta)=\tfrac12\mathbb E[r_\theta^2]\). Under the declared differentiability and domination conditions its derivative includes the current critic path, the explicit future-parameter path and the actor path. Detaching a target differentiates a different surrogate. The AVaR derivative needs its stated unique, atomless quantile condition; neither graph identity establishes optimizer convergence. The original strong convexity makes that risk regularity check possible here, rather than assuming every neural continuation has it.

The stopped Brownian BSDE result uses bounded \(C^{1,2}\) test functions, stated coefficient and driver regularity, the actual stopping payoff and a well-posed closed-loop feedback. Boundary discrepancy and both signed residual errors enter the policy-gap bound. A zero interior residual alone can leave positive stopping-policy regret. A ReLU function's zero Hessian almost everywhere is therefore not a classical Ito verification argument without a justified weak formulation or smoothing error. General NDU, fully coupled FBSDEs, temporal-self equilibria and games retain their distinct mathematical obligations. These corrections are proved in \nolinkurl{new119/foundations_supplement.tex}, with the main statements in \nolinkurl{new119/foundations_main.tex} and the learning graph in \nolinkurl{new119/policy_main119.tex}.
