\paragraph{Packing the same original point information.}
\label{rem:fusedtail119}
At a fixed attempted resolution, index every positive-weight midpoint
request and every endpoint request of a positive-length tail by its
original row, column, and request type.  Concatenating these indices
defines a bijection to a packed list.  A packed entry retains the
original state $x_i$, action $a_i$, and innovation $z_i$ and requests
the same original payload at $F(x_i,a_i,z_i)$.  Distinct requests at
an identical location remain separate paid entries.  The inverse
indices return the lower values, endpoint upper values, and supporting
slopes to precisely their original summands.

Conditional on a given valid collection of original payloads, this
operation is only a reindexing of the selected-tail lower sum, secant
upper sum, and all-action supporting slope.  A zero-length tail has
zero weights, so its omitted terms can be represented by zeros without
asserting that the unobserved continuation equals zero.  Termwise
outward operations may enclose an exact zero by a nondegenerate
interval; that adds width and does not invalidate the bound.
The state/action transition is formed with a singleton innovation
axis for each packed entry, preventing cross-products between one
entry's state and another entry's shock.  Thus the inverse scatter
preserves the original probability mass and objective.

The compiler's batch-level fallback may supply different valid
outward payloads after a change of batching.  In that case the
qualification proof applies to the actual returned collection; no
bitwise equality of certificates or returned policies is assumed.
Proposition~\ref{prop:budgetedtail119}'s $30/16$ preliminary payload
bounds are unchanged.  The packed implementation makes one original
compiler invocation per attempted resolution for its positive-weight
midpoint and endpoint requests, in addition to the separately counted
cached cut-defect values.  Compiler-internal fallback, packing,
scattering, zero-term interval arithmetic and the complete decision
fallback remain charged operations.  Their effect on elapsed time
requires the separately frozen current comparison.
