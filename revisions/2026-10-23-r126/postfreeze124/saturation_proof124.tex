\section{Saturation, computation selection and inactive candidate menus}
\label{supp:saturation124}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:saturation124}]
For every feasible displacement $d$, strong convexity and derivative
Lipschitzness give
\[
 q(a)+q'(a)d+\frac\mu2d^2
 \le q(a+d)\le q(a)+q'(a)d+\frac L2d^2.
\]
The inequalities hold on either side of the anchor by integrating the
one-sided derivative increments. Extremizing the linear term over
$[\ell,u]$ gives $m_-$ and $m_+$. The anchor levels then give the
corresponding absolute lower and upper bounds.

For $d>0$, the upper contrast is attained by
$q(t)=f_0+u(t-a)+L(t-a)^2/2$; for $d<0$ replace $u$ by $\ell$.
Every $f_0\in[W,U]$ is allowed. This quadratic is continuously
differentiable, $L$-smooth and $\mu$-strongly convex. Its anchor
derivative belongs to the supplied interval. Choosing $f_0=U$ also
attains the absolute upper value. For the lower bounds use curvature
$\mu$, derivative $\ell$ for $d>0$ or $u$ for $d<0$, and $f_0=W$
for the absolute lower value. These quadratics belong to the same
class when $\mu=L$. At $d=0$ every contrast is zero, while the two
anchor levels are attained by the same choices of $f_0$.

Every derived derivative-cone or transported-level box is valid for
every function in this class. The quadratics therefore satisfy the
boxes at all inserted nodes. Their values and derivatives satisfy all
adjacent interpolation constraints by Theorem~\ref{thm:jointinterpolation124}.
Conversely those constraints only restrict the same information class.
Their feasible points cannot reduce a maximum already attained by an
admissible function or increase a similarly attained minimum. Repeating
an anchor adds only intersections of its level and derivative intervals;
after those intersections the same argument applies.

For the optional cap let $l=\min(a,b)$ and $r=\max(a,b)$.
The clipped affine expression in
\eqref{eq:localcurvaturewitness124} is continuous. Its almost-everywhere
slope is $L$ on $(l,r)$ and $\mu$ outside. Integrating it from $a$
therefore defines a $C^1$, $\mu$-strongly convex, $L$-smooth function.
On the entire segment from $a$ to $b$ its derivative is
$g_*+L(t-a)$, so its contrast equals $m_+(b-a)$.
It is monotone as a derivative, with its smallest and largest values
at $0$ and $A$. The two stated endpoint inequalities therefore impose
$|q_+'|\le M$ everywhere. This supplies an admissible cap-respecting
witness for the upper equalities. An arbitrary stronger global cap
need not satisfy these conditions; no unconditional capped equality
has been claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:saturationbypass124}]
A sound dual upper bound must majorize the contrast of every feasible
primal vector in its interpolation program. The preceding witness
has contrast exactly $m_+(b-a)$; hence $D_b\ge m_+(b-a)$, regardless
of the optimizer's status or residual. The intersection consequently
returns $m_+(b-a)$. With one paid incumbent anchor the transported
absolute upper is $U+m_+(b-a)$, so the additional absolute intersection
also leaves it unchanged. Adding the same future-policy allowance,
retaining the same lower and optimum bounds, and applying the same
outward conversions gives identical numerical predicates. Identical
candidate values and tie rules give the same selected action.

No optimization is needed to establish this identity. After checking
the anchor and constraint identities, a scalar formula suffices for
each candidate; the optional cap requires the two endpoint checks.
If there are $m$ candidates to which the gate applies, the switch
omits $m$ optimizer calls and $m$ corresponding dual evaluations.
This is an operation count, not a claim about measured elapsed time,
and the identity does not delete any oracle work already incurred.

The exact contrast expression contains neither $W$ nor $U$, proving
the level-only precision claim. For $b>a$ its slope endpoint is $u$;
for $b<a$ it is $\ell$. Direct subtraction proves the stated linear
credits. Keeping the information feasible ensures the extrema are
still those of a nonempty class. The potential improvement in an
absolute upper or width is a different quantity and has not been
discarded. Since redundant optimizer work cannot change this
certificate or the completed service's action, any positive incremental
cost for that work has no offsetting benefit in the specified comparison.
This does not compare additional oracle acquisitions, alternative
model restrictions, or different candidate-generation algorithms.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{A general primal saturation test.}
The same reasoning gives an implementable extension beyond the one-anchor
formula. Suppose $C_b$ is an already valid contrast upper for a finite
interpolation program. If a supplied exact vector satisfies every box
and interpolation inequality and has $f_b-f_a=C_b$, then $C_b$ equals
that program's maximum. No further dual optimization can lower it.
The check uses linear many adjacent constraints after node construction.
The one-anchor formula provides such a vector without solving a primal
optimization problem. Additional constraints, including raw supports,
must be checked when they are part of the claimed program; a witness
for a relaxation does not establish saturation of a stronger program.

\paragraph{Proof of the inactive-menu counterexample.}
At a point $t\in[0,A]$, transport of the anchor-zero derivative interval
gives lower $-\mu A+\mu t$ and upper $\mu A+Lt$.
Transport of the anchor-$A$ interval gives lower $L(t-A)$ and upper
$2\mu A+\mu(t-A)$. Since $L\ge\mu$, their maximum lower and minimum
upper are respectively $\mu(t-A)$ and $\mu(t+A)$.
At the incumbent zero the first individual interval is
$[-\mu A,\mu A]$, the second is $[-LA,\mu A]$, and their
intersection also contains zero. Each derivative-cone proposal
therefore retains the incumbent. Fixed fractions and feasible downward
rounding of zero leave every menu entry at zero.

Strong convexity at the midpoint gives
\[
 q(A/2)\le\tfrac12q(0)+\tfrac12q(A)-\mu A^2/8
                =-\mu A^2/8.
\]
The function $q(t)=\mu(t^2-At)/2$ has curvature $\mu$, endpoint
values zero, and endpoint derivatives $-\mu A/2,\mu A/2$, which
belong to both declared intervals. It is admissible for every
$L\ge\mu$ and attains the bound. Thus the inactivity of this menu
is not the absence of a strictly beneficial action in the supplied
joint information. If only $0\le\eta\le\delta$ is known about the
unchanged future policy, the same information guarantees a complete-policy
gain of at least $\mu A^2/8-\delta$ whenever that quantity is positive.

\paragraph{Exact saved-record verification and custody.}
The separate standard-library checker \texttt{verify\_saturation124.py}
reads the complete frozen primary file and binds its hash to the passed
independent A audit. It imports no economic primitive, actor, production
envelope, optimizer, NumPy or SciPy. For every submitted candidate it
reconstructs the one-anchor derivative and value boxes, verifies the
nodes and curvature, constructs the three exact primal vectors used
above, and checks every adjacent inequality and box coordinate.
It then checks exact equality of the matched candidate and returned
certificate fields. The cap-compatible witness is checked at all saved
nodes, including both endpoints, which suffices by its monotone derivative.

The actual saved audit passed 56,157 exact checks over all 404 candidates.
All 928 paired final actions agree; the complete joint-row census is
830 one-anchor rows and 98 two-anchor rows. All 98 two-anchor incumbents
are interior, every individual transported derivative interval contains
zero there, and every recorded candidate entry equals that incumbent.
All rows and all 404 primal witnesses are retained. These are complete
post-execution facts about that saved file, not a new population experiment.
The recorded 404 calls include 3,461 SLSQP iterations, 7,167 objective
evaluations and 3,313 objective-Jacobian evaluations. They remain part
of the original campaign's bill; no counterfactual elapsed-time saving
is inserted into its economic account.
