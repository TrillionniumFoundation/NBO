\section{A prospective original-economy comparison}\label{sec:experiment126}

\subsection{Economic object and common information}

The experiment uses exactly the two-dimensional primitives in
Section~\ref{sec:continuous95}, with $\alpha=1/2,3/4$ and the same
implemented continuation controller. Write $q_x$ for the optimal-future
Bellman action objective and $J_x$ for the complete implemented policy value.
The proved relation is $q_x\le J_x\le q_x+\delta$, with
$\delta=15/2^{24}$. An ordinary service must have original-policy regret
at most $1/256$ and an absolute original-value interval no wider than
$1/8192$. The separately reported stronger regret target is $1/65536$.
Neither passing the ordinary gate nor narrowing a residual constitutes
success at that stronger target.

All nine states in $\{3/16,9/16,15/16\}^2$ enter in lexicographic order.
Every coordinate is absent from both acquired training meshes. These are
fixed test locations, not random draws from an economic state distribution.
The complete executable closure of 538 scientific files and the three
protocols were remotely frozen at
\nolinkurl{769e3eb3ac08beaa7bb6778c1676f15e1f8d6d2b}.
All 543 published file objects were read back before the first economic
calculation. The source manifest and execution receipts identify this study
separately from the preceding four-state experiment and protected independent
studies. The states and service parameters were chosen after examining the
published prior evidence, and before observing any present outcomes.

The 56 actors retain acquisition commit
\nolinkurl{796c5f2badf68b051ac6c144884c0e61b87faf5e}.
For each risk, both five- and nine-node coordinate meshes are used, with
quadratic, ReLU, matched native hinge, spline, degree-two polynomial,
degree-three polynomial and grid representations, each fitted by value or
by original marginal information. Their 25 and 81 training states receive
13 original-objective contexts each. Every representation at a given risk
and mesh has access to the same acquired labels. The hinge copy pays its
required ReLU acquisition parent; its matched features and coefficients
are checked exactly. Historical acquisition allocations remain historical
measurements and are not replaced with new clocks.

Each actor and each risk-specific original online controller is served by
three independently executed complete routes. The fused control is the
retained strong service including qualification, rejected finite-menu
proposals and fallback. The warm adaptive route first pays that whole
service and then buys additional action information. The direct terminal
route pays only its own actor proposal and initial original support before
attempting qualification; it receives no completed prefix for free. A
33-action original-law dynamic-programming service is included at each risk.
The catalogue therefore has 176 cohorts and 1,584 primary decisions.

\subsection{Paid refinement and unchanged-policy improvements}

The direct route starts at 32 risk bins. Subsequent per-center precisions
are $32,64,128,256,512,1024,2048$. A query at $n$ bins requests
$2n+1$ terminal midpoint or endpoint contexts, with all actual terminal
iterations and risk calculations recorded separately. The limits are eight
centers and 32,768 requested contexts, checked before acquisition. Rejected
levels and unsuccessful candidates remain charged. Subsequent centers use
the midpoint of a certified optimizer bracket, rounded down on the inherited
30-bit feasible action lattice; contraction tests include the actual rounding
displacement. Fixed caps need not attain an asymptotic theoretical allocation.

At every saved candidate, the service intersects the valid level-gap,
projected-gradient and terminal-bracket regret accounts. The first ordinarily
qualified candidate is chosen by direct regret, then upper value and action.
After that point, changing the action requires an independently valid upper
original contrast plus $\delta$ to be strictly negative, as well as both
ordinary qualification gates. An unchanged action may receive a sharper
certificate without being counted as a gain. The service stops when its
returned action meets the stronger regret target and the separate value-width
gate. If the cap expires, it keeps a qualified incumbent; if none exists, it
pays the full fused fallback, retaining all failed direct work. Infrastructure
or source-identity errors abort and preserve their partial outputs.

The support-certificate subexperiment is separately fixed to 74 paid pools:
all 58 first-state fused services and the 16 remaining state--risk online
services. Its candidate actions are the realized incumbent and the capacity
boundary. Five equally spaced action nodes, augmented by the incumbent,
control continuous-action regret. Each pairwise problem permits at most eight
linear-programming calls, with at most 2,000 iterations per call; the initial
all-constraint tangents are followed by at most 32 exact most-violated cuts
per round. Points and dual proposals are rationalized on a $2^{-36}$ lattice.
Every proposed cut and weight is retained. The cheap comparator uses the same
paid supports, clipped derivative cap and original-optimum lower bound.
This experiment purchases no additional economic evaluations. Its original
prefix cost and added numerical and rational-algebra work are both charged.
It does not rewrite any primary service policy after seeing the results.

\subsection{Independent evidence and fitting audit}

The saved-arithmetic audit replays feasibility, budgets, every incumbent
change, exact regret algebra, the matched ReLU/hinge identities, and all timed
scientific repetitions. A separate rational implementation reconstructs the
original continuous-risk economics without importing the production compiler,
actor, quadrature or certificate code. Every distinct final state--risk--action
key is evaluated at eight bins, followed in fixed hash order by 32 and 128 bins
while unresolved and within 32,768 requested contexts. Terminal root bisection
uses 32 iterations. An audit that cannot cover all keys at its initial level
fails; precision is not extended after observing outcomes. Complete-policy
identities imply zero contrasts exactly. Otherwise independently bounded
improvements, losses and unresolved comparisons are all reported.

Value-interval containment, confirmation of the claimed regret, ordinary and
stronger qualification, and contradictions are separate outcomes. A looser
independent regret bound is unresolved. A disjoint value enclosure or a proved
regret lower bound above the production upper claim is a contradiction. The
independent evaluator encloses the optimal-future objective and uses the proved
same-controller allowance; it does not replay every terminal implementation
branch throughout the continuum. The full witnesses and bisection ledgers are
retained. Independent standard-library rational code also reconstructs each
support-program cut, residual, weight and final bound without importing its
production solver or scientific libraries.

The separate acquisition audit uses the fixed 181-file historical input
closure. It independently rebuilds feature matrices, including ordinary
hinges, spline recursions and original-action derivatives, then solves 48
finite-design fits. The eight matched hinge models are accounted for as copies,
not additional independent fits. Value fitting uses an independently assembled
bounded-variable least-squares problem configured for BVLS instead of TRF.
Four actual solves execute the different active-set routine; 20 return the
feasible unconstrained solution through the shared initial numerical path.
Four VALUE hinge readouts copy their paid ReLU solution;
marginal fitting uses independently assembled shifted robust linear programs
with the shared HiGHS library. Exact rational lower bounds and coefficient
rounding allowances audit the finite-design objectives
(Supplement, Section~\ref{sec:independentfit126}). No refitted actor replaces
a frozen production actor. The audit does not acquire fresh labels or identify
a population neural advantage.

\subsection{Complete costs and economic adoption}

Three serial processes each repeat the entire 176-cohort catalogue at
$(3/16,3/16)$, for 528 further decisions. Their order rotates by $13r$ in
repetition $r=0,1,2$. Models, actions, certificates, operation counts and stop
reasons must reproduce the corresponding primary records. Each clock includes
its service and output, together with an equal allocation of the measured
parent residual. Thread counts are one; host load and clock frequency are
uncontrolled. The observed range is descriptive, not a sampling confidence
interval or a portable operation-time conversion. Source verification,
independent economics, fitting, solver certificates and reporting have separate
research-work receipts. Unknown interrupted historical parent costs remain
unknown.

For each ReLU service, risk and $N\in\{1,32,1000,10^6\}$, adoption is
assessed against every conventional service through
$J+\lambda\{t+K/N\}$, where $t$ is complete first-state decision time,
$K$ is its retained acquisition allocation and $\lambda\ge0$ prices a second
in objective units. Sufficient regions use own upper versus rival lower
bounds; optimistic outer regions reverse them. Exact identical-policy blockers
are also reported without replacing the interval comparisons. Economic values
from the other eight states are not paired with first-state timing. These
prices and amortization counts are explicit sensitivity scenarios, not an
estimated value of computation or a calibrated adoption forecast.
