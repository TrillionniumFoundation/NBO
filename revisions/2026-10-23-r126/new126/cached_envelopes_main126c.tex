\subsection{Exact reuse of certificate geometry within a paid decision}
\label{sec:cachedgeometry126c}

An original-risk support can be expensive to acquire, but repeated manipulation
of its certificate can also dominate a complete service.  The transported
lower and upper derivatives are piecewise-affine functions.  Reconstructing
their partitions separately for every support anchor, candidate and contrast
repeats a calculation determined entirely by the same paid information.  We
therefore examine a separately frozen implementation which retains this
geometry within one paid decision.  The first implementation and its measured
costs remain distinct evidence.

For a fixed immutable record, let $e_-$ and $e_+$ be the two derivative
envelopes, and store their exact full-domain partitions together with
\[
 F_\pm(t)=\int_0^t e_\pm(u)\,du.
\]
For $a<b$, the original contrast interval is
$[F_-(b)-F_-(a),F_+(b)-F_+(a)]$; for $a>b$ the two endpoints reverse.
A previously unseen endpoint requires a binary search and one rational
quadratic evaluation.  Previously evaluated endpoints, gradients and
constrained envelope roots are retained.  Requested subpartitions are obtained
by clipping the full partition, with every original crossing retained.
The raw-support minimum has a separate key containing its complete support
levels and slope intervals.  Candidate policy certificates are recalculated
using the current incumbent, original-optimum bound and future allowance;
none of those quantities is silently omitted from a cached policy result.

Proposition~\ref{prop:cacheidentity126c} proves equality to every substituted
original geometry calculation, including its returned subpartition list and
boundary cases.  Under the unchanged deterministic original evaluations and
stopping rules, this yields the same economic queries, rejected candidates,
policy decisions and certificates.  The implementation also requires exact
saved-record equality in its separately declared experiment; mathematical
equivalence is not substituted for that execution check.

Proposition~\ref{prop:cachework126c} gives the precise work scope.  With $s$
retained derivative observations, each inherited partition has at most
$1+s+\binom{s}{2}$ pieces.  Its original all-crossing construction takes
$O(s^3)$ rational arithmetic and comparison operations.  Once constructed,
a new primitive or gradient argument takes $O(\log P)$ breakpoint
comparisons for $P$ stored pieces; a requested subpartition additionally
pays for each piece returned.  Each distinct immutable key incurs its own
construction.  Exact key formation, support-minimum requests, storage and
cache management remain charged.  These are operation bounds for the stated
certificate calculation, not a bit-complexity bound, an uncached lower bound,
or a theorem about elapsed time.

Every neural and conventional service receives the same implementation.
The cache is destroyed after each individual decision, including exception
paths.  It reuses no economic observation, terminal solve, actor output or
completed service from another paid decision.  Thus any measured improvement
belongs to the common decision service under the same information, and its
economic value still depends on complete costs and the original decision
accuracy.  The new paired study retains slower outcomes as well as faster
ones and does not reinterpret unchanged policies as new policy gains.
