\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction126}

A firm choosing investment needs the continuation values of feasible actions,
the risk of their future consequences, and a decision about how much computation
to buy. These three objects are linked but are not interchangeable. A small
critic fitting error can conceal the action error that matters for investment.
An uncertain common value level can coexist with an exactly identified optimal
action. A more accurate certificate can leave the implemented policy unchanged.
Neural Bellman Operators (NBO) address this recursive economic decision problem
by constructing a continuation critic, a feasible actor, and an account of the
original policy's error and complete computational cost.

Our running application has nonlinear investment technology, a state-dependent
capacity, continuous innovations and a nested tail-risk preference. Optimality
is defined by the original Bellman recursion. The innovation bins in a numerical
calculation bound its continuous law; they do not define a replacement economy.
The same implemented future controller is charged in every method. The central
question is whether learned continuation information can improve the original
decision, or reduce the complete work required to make it to a stated economic
accuracy, relative to strong conventional methods with the same information.

We develop a decision service that separates action accuracy from absolute value
accuracy. Previously computed global Bellman supports imply both a constrained
derivative enclosure and an interval containing the original optimizer. These
quantities can certify small original-policy regret even when a level-gap
certificate is unnecessarily wide. The service buys a proposed action and its
first original-risk support directly, uses separate action and value gates to determine whether further refinement
is needed,
and pays for a full conventional fallback when qualification is unsuccessful.
A later policy change requires a strictly negative bound on the actual original
contrast. A smaller regret certificate for an unchanged action is recorded as
better information, not as a new policy gain. The supplement proves the
projected-gradient, rounded-bracket, terminal-width and improvement statements
used by this implemented rule.

A second contribution makes the complete Bellman support characterization
computable with finite verification. Each paid support constrains the objective
on every feasible action, with one slope shared across that domain. The retained
finite interpolation theorem describes exactly the original functions compatible
with these records. We construct outer linear cuts at possibly different
rational points and combine their numerical dual proposals with an exact
residual correction. Any accepted combination yields a valid action contrast,
even when the optimization routine fails or reaches its resource cap. A separate
finite-grid theorem turns these contrasts into a continuous-action regret bound,
with a sharp $Lh^2/8$ resolution term and an exact cellwise improvement. A verified
primal gap, when available, identifies proximity to the information bound; a
solver status does not. Under an explicitly compulsory further computation cost,
the remaining-regret bound also gives a sufficient economic stopping rule.

These calculations build on classical cutting planes, smooth convex interpolation
and information-based optimization \citep{Kelley1960Cuts126,THG2017Interpolation122,FloreaNesterov2024Oracle125}.
Their contribution is the specific, fully verified passage from the original
recursive Bellman payload to a feasible action, its economic regret, and a paid
decision to continue computing. No general invention of cutting planes is
claimed. The full-support solver is compared with a conventional certificate
that uses the same derivative cap, supports, curvature, and already acquired
original-optimum lower bound. Neural, hinge, spline, polynomial and online
representations have the same certification rights under this information.

The original NBO construction remains central. Its critic is constructed
backwards using its own acquired continuation; its actor carries a feasible
witness. The operator foundations establish existence and uniqueness of the
finite-horizon value, measurable selection, stability, boundary conditions and
implementation allowances. One-sided and centered recursive-risk accounts
propagate local errors to the original objective. The supplement retains the
complete actor/critic computational-graph proofs and the conditional link to
stochastic control, nonlinear recursive utility and forward--backward systems.
Those links require their stated well-posedness, filtration, regularity and
boundary assumptions. Evidence under expectation, nested AVaR or a different
recursive driver retains that particular economic objective.

The new prospective computation includes all 56 previously acquired actors,
covering ReLU and exactly matched ordinary hinges, quadratic, spline, polynomial
and grid representations. It also includes the original-model online controller
and uniform-action dynamic programming. All nine off-training states, both risk
levels, complete method catalogue, precision ladders, budget caps and verification
rules were committed remotely before the first evaluation. The three complete
services pay their own work. The support-solver study uses a separately declared
subset of those newly executed records, and charges its additional algebra.
A further independent finite-design audit rebuilds the acquired feature matrices
and fitting problems with separately written code. It audits the historical
training inputs without changing the actors deployed by the experiment.

This division of work is substantive. The nine-state study is a deterministic
census with no population confidence interval. Independent reconstruction can
confirm, contradict or leave unresolved an original-value or regret claim; mere
interval overlap is not confirmation. The finite-design fitting audit does not
supply fresh economic labels, whole-domain training validation, or an independent
implementation of the shared linear-programming library. The capped support
solver does not inherit the limiting accuracy of an ideal exact cutting-plane
algorithm. Each restriction is tied to a concrete theorem or execution record.

The strongest conventional wins and the unproved neural adoption regions remain
part of the evidence. ReLU and its exactly matched native hinge encode the same
acquired function and feasible policy. An advantage they share cannot identify a
neural-specific effect. Acquisition, fitting, rejected candidates, certification,
inference, output and complete decision clocks enter the comparison. The goal
remains a competitive neural method for recursive economic decisions; the new
algorithms and information bounds sharpen the test that a learned representation
must pass. Complete proofs are in the technical supplement. The development
companion retains earlier economic models, all prior source statements and
negative findings, distinct experimental identities and the preservation map.
