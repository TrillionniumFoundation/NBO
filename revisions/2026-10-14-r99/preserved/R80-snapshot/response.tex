\hypertarget{r79-addition-attained-deployment-allocation-and-explicit-setup-costs}{%
\subsection{R79 addition: attained deployment allocation and explicit
setup
costs}\label{r79-addition-attained-deployment-allocation-and-explicit-setup-costs}}

R79 continues the original R78 manuscript at
\nolinkurl{6b88c6b3a61193c98c822bd62af1203df09c992d} and the same
controlling R67 advisory report. The earlier publication attempt did not
complete, so its inherited R77 logs are not used as R79 verification.
The present build must execute the entire retained verification chain
and compile the actual new ordinary sources.

The new sharp deployment-allocation theorem extends the original
controller-choice problem. Nonnegative controller shares and a common
attaining cost potential reduce worst-case expected catalogue regret to
a finite matrix game. Exact primal and dual feasible probabilities
certify the solution and its optimization gap. The new proof
distinguishes a pre-observation mixture of entire policies from
averaging actions, fitted coefficients or information sets. Installation
costs are not presumed linear: an installed-subset formulation charges
every supported controller and retains outside controllers as oracle
comparators. A finite-cohort construction realizes the allocation in
expectation without pretending that the statistical sample size is the
deployment horizon.

For the two attained ReLU precision levels, the complete cost intervals
imply an exact fee-dependent allocation and the additional cost that
would erase its advantage. At the ambiguity midpoint, the worst-case
expected-regret certificate is half the best pure certificate before
extra setup charges. This is a sharp economic decision calculation from
existing qualified costs, not a new simulation, neural speed claim, fee
calibration or unconditional statistical-risk result. The corresponding
theorem, full proof, rational primal-dual witnesses and finite-cohort
exhaustive checks are deposited. The new suite has twenty-two unskipped
exact tests; the retained cost catalogue supplies seven full-catalogue
linear-program checks, thirty-five finite-cohort checks and four
explicit setup-charge regimes. These are decision calculations, not new
economic observations. Manski (2004) is cited for the established
minimax-regret decision perspective.

All R78 active section files remain byte-identical and are compiled
once. The pure-choice exposition is placed next to its full proof in the
Numerical and Development Appendix; no theorem, proof or unfavorable
experiment is deleted. The introduction and conclusion have been
rewritten to remove accumulated repetition and distinguish attained
continuations from optional query proposals. Their original wrappers and
response are preserved. The subsequent ten point-by-point replies remain
applicable; the new result further addresses M4/M5/M8/M9 and B3/B4/B7/B9
through explicitly costed deployment rather than unsupported neural
timing dominance. The high-dimensional, unknown-law and
population-ranking distinctions in those replies remain unchanged.

\hypertarget{m1-b1.-identify-what-the-neural-continuation-contributes}{%
\subsection{M1 / B1. Identify what the neural continuation
contributes}\label{m1-b1.-identify-what-the-neural-continuation-contributes}}

We retain the referee's distinction between an optional action-query
predictor and an actual fitted continuation. Correctness of the
safeguarded query procedure is method-neutral. It does not imply a
neural computational advantage, and the adverse query-selector results
remain in the appendix.

The original NBO continuation now has a separate, executed chain.
Qualification-optimal fixed-feature fitting minimizes a verified
residual-oscillation account conditional on its actually fitted future.
An exact primal--dual calculation certifies the achieved optimization
gap. Backward construction returns feasible acquired actors. The
original-law experiment evaluates those same actors, with their
identities frozen before the evaluation rows. Matched
ReLU-versus-Bernstein cost differences are strictly negative at both
acquisition resolutions. The manuscript reports this finite,
construction-specific result without inferring dominance over all
conventional methods or confusing an exact ReLU encoding with a separate
algorithm.

R78 adds the next decision in that chain. Its qualified cost set retains
all jointly covered observations. Sharp worst-case catalogue regret is
computed for each controller and every declared resource-charge vector.
An explicitly feasible cost potential attains each upper difference, so
the resulting choice guarantee is not merely a loose union of marginal
intervals. The classical difference-constraint algorithm is attributed
to existing graph methods; our contribution is the executable,
price-uniform decision account for the actual constructed NBO
controllers.

The retained readout validation uses predictor-independent contexts

For the latter, the frozen design uses 524,288 rows, forty-bit

marginal costs and ten paired differences for each estimator enter one
thirty-estimand family. Every paired interval meets the declared

equivalence criterion, because other narrow intervals identify material
differences. The width account retains numerical enclosure, endpoint
dispersion and finite-support terms.

R78 uses the already jointly covered intervals to identify the full cost
order: fine ReLU, coarse ReLU, fine Bernstein, coarse Bernstein, then

Bernstein-minus-ReLU gains are enclosed by {[}0.0758058,0.0775680{]} and
{[}0.0753723,0.0771589{]}, respectively, with outward printed endpoints.
The exact record is authoritative. These are actual complete-policy
comparisons, not differences between qualification certificates.

Both estimators refer to the same costs and share the same coverage
event. Intersecting their linear implications adds no sample size.
Transitive closure does not further tighten the paired endpoints in this
particular dataset; the revision does not claim a new precision
improvement where none occurs. Its new contribution is an attainable

rectangles, importing none of the production economic or actor routines.

and boundary tests complement the full-bin replay.

The R79 release procedure repeats that full dual-arithmetic calculation
and recomputes all statistical intervals. The new decision calculation
is independently implemented using all-pairs Floyd closure and repeated
single-source Bellman--Ford relaxation. Exact cost potentials are
checked against every original constraint and attain every claimed upper
difference. Negative cycles cause failure; they are not repaired by
widening selected intervals. Exact fee-boundary and minimax-tie tests

This fully covers the critical new constructed-controller cost and
choice calculations. It does not independently regenerate every
descendant of the historical eight-million-query vector computation.
That distinction and the earlier aliasing counterexample remain

\hypertarget{m9-b9.-make-the-economic-decision-explicit}{%
\subsection{M9 / B9. Make the economic decision
explicit}\label{m9-b9.-make-the-economic-decision-explicit}}

The installed zero-investment reference supplies a replacement decision
within the original economy. It is not substituted for stronger
conventional query baselines. Matched attained ReLU-versus-Bernstein
comparisons and gains over the installed reference are separate results.

The new theorem gives the exact maximum catalogue regret implied by the
retained cost intervals. Minimizing this quantity uses precise paired
information instead of only marginal upper costs. It yields a controller
choice for every resource-charge vector on one coverage event. The
attaining-potential proof states exactly the sense in which the bound is
sharp: relative to the retained interval inequalities, not relative to
all information in the raw observations or all possible statistical
procedures.

For acquisition refinement, the identified gain from coarse to fine ReLU
is approximately {[}0.0002277949,0.0011900893{]}. When only the finer
controller bears an incremental per-deployment charge f, its worst-case
regret is (f-l)+ and that of the coarse controller is (u-f)+. The fine
controller is universally optimal below l, the coarse one above u, and
the regret-minimizing choice changes at (l+u)/2, approximately
0.0007089421. All thresholds and boundary choices are calculated with
exact fractions. The other three constructed controllers remain
dominated in this stated charge experiment.

Normalized loss units do not identify a monetary scale, an installation
fee or a deployment horizon. The manuscript makes these inputs explicit
rather than selecting favorable prices to infer a universal purchase
recommendation. It now supplies an identified complete-controller
comparison and a full conditional choice region within the original
economic model.
