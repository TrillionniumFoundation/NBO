\section{Response to the Referee: Neural Bellman Operators,
R92}\label{response-to-the-referee-neural-bellman-operators-r92}}

We thank the referee for distinguishing a valid certificate from
evidence of computational or economic advantage. We respond to the
latest deposited report,
\nolinkurl{b030d87d7e96440eb25a3178c94ee6a87940cdb0}, which reviews the
R84 delivery. The intervening completed R91 manuscript at
\nolinkurl{368a9e754f5de04eb1957e152f22d6ef2c34f2fe} is the baseline for
this revision. Its original title, author, economic primitives,
full-policy objective, broader applications, active sections and
unfavorable evidence are retained. We have not adopted the
recommendation to replace the original paper with a narrower
stopping-method article.

The new analytical contribution is reference-centered qualification
under the original recursive preference. A state-dependent error is
propagated through the original conditional risk operator rather than
charged by an independent worst state at every date. Centering the risk
dual at the actual stored NBO continuation supplies a nonnegative
penalty. Theorem \texttt{thm:centeredrisk92} compares the complete
implemented policy with the original Bellman optimum. Proposition
\texttt{prop:riskflow92} gives an exact penalized risk-occupation
representation for a finite affine risk primitive and independently
checkable primal and dual witnesses. A strict support-function
inequality excludes risk pieces only on a previously verified error set.
Full proofs, sharp threshold examples and objective-preservation
counterexamples are in the Technical Supplement.

The verification manifest, not this response, determines which execution
and publication checks have passed. New exact diagnostics are not
counted as fitting attempts or independent economic observations.

\hypertarget{m2-b2.-keeping-the-original-problem-and-scope}{%
\subsection{M2 / B2. Keeping the original problem and
scope}\label{m2-b2.-keeping-the-original-problem-and-scope}}

The article remains Neural Bellman Operators. Its main construction,
feasible witnesses, complete-policy objective, joint state-context
extension, diffusions, endogenous preferences, temporal selves and games
are retained under their original assumptions. The added theorem is
formulated on the original augmented state space, not only a first-date
finite partition. It permits actions and endogenous occupations to
change at every date.

A dual representation is explicitly required for the centered-risk
result. When a recursive aggregator lacks that representation, the
existing order and continuation-modulus results remain active. This
separates the conditions of different results without removing the
corresponding original economic problems. We do not reinterpret an
expected-cost experiment as evidence for every preference primitive.

\hypertarget{m3.-complete-policy-qualification-under-nonlinear-preferences}{%
\subsection{M3. Complete-policy qualification under nonlinear
preferences}\label{m3.-complete-policy-qualification-under-nonlinear-preferences}}

At the actual stored continuation, the centered operator is exactly the
original risk of the continuation plus its error, minus the original
risk of the stored continuation. Its dual penalty retains the cost of
moving away from a risk-maximizing reference kernel. The policy upper
certificate and optimum lower certificate propagate state-dependent
errors at every date. The lower certificate covers all original feasible
actions; a sampled-action minimum cannot establish it.

The exact state-dependent recursion is no looser than the earlier signed
scalar account. A two-state, two-action absorbing example makes the
distinction strict at every declared horizon: the earlier scalar charge
equals the horizon while the new certificate is zero. The
state-dependent bias is propagated, not cancelled without justification.
A risk kernel inactive at the reference can become active after
perturbation; an exact two-outcome example shows that freezing the
reference maximizer would give an invalid upper bound. Equalities at the
exclusion threshold are retained.

\hypertarget{m4-b4.-shared-continuations-and-population-claims}{%
\subsection{M4 / B4. Shared continuations and population
claims}\label{m4-b4.-shared-continuations-and-population-claims}}

The joint-domain construction and adaptive acquisition results in
\texttt{population86} remain active. Their verified primitive
regularity, feasible action transport and whole-domain coverage
conditions are unchanged. The held-out shared-proposal study retains its
fixed parameters, calibration/evaluation split and unseen dimension.

The new theorem fixes one common continuation sequence and one complete
feasible policy for each qualified parameter before propagating errors.
A class conclusion requires the stated inequalities for each member; it
cannot be obtained by combining a favorable continuation from one
parameter with an action from another. This pointwise analytical
extension neither assumes independent endogenous contexts nor turns a
finite diagnostic catalogue into a distribution-free learning result.

\hypertarget{m5-b3-b6.-work-runtime-and-the-neural-contribution}{%
\subsection{M5 / B3 / B6. Work, runtime and the neural
contribution}\label{m5-b3-b6.-work-runtime-and-the-neural-contribution}}

The new support exclusions depend on the continuation through its
centered penalty. They therefore identify a concrete channel through
which a constructed continuation can change verification work.
Equivalent neural and non-neural representations have the same
guarantee. A representation-specific speed advantage still requires a
matched experiment, and the earlier negative incremental ReLU result is
preserved.

The finite recurrence stores successor layers rather than re-expanding
histories. Its arithmetic bound includes every retained risk-piece
support, while construction, original-law integration, model evaluation,
support certificates and rational bit complexity are separately charged.
The diagnostics report a defined support-term count, retained rational
bit lengths and measured process durations without calling a partial
operation count an end-to-end resource model. Existing complete-time and
cross-machine evidence remains unchanged. Energy and
application-specific monetary calibration are not measured by the new
diagnostic calculation.

\hypertarget{m7-b8.-prices-and-occupation-sensitive-precision}{%
\subsection{M7 / B8. Prices and occupation-sensitive
precision}\label{m7-b8.-prices-and-occupation-sensitive-precision}}

All prior uniform price-fee regions, unfavorable fee ceilings and
capacity constraints remain. The new occupation budget bounds how local
residual allowances can affect a fixed-policy risk value. It improves on
a separate worst-state charge at each date while keeping the penalty and
original preference fixed. This is a sufficient numerical-accuracy
budget, not an estimate of the value of computation.

Changing the stored continuation changes the penalty and requires new
qualification. Choosing a risk parameter or a price after seeing results
is not permitted as evidence of a favorable economic ranking. The
original conditional purchase interpretation and the absence of an
application-specific monetary calibration remain explicit.

\hypertarget{m9-b10.-horizon-growth-and-a-qualified-stored-recursion}{%
\subsection{M9 / B10. Horizon growth and a qualified stored
recursion}\label{m9-b10.-horizon-growth-and-a-qualified-stored-recursion}}

The new finite risk-occupation formulation and backward recurrence
eliminate repeated descendant evaluation for the stated finite risk
representation. They do not erase state-dimension, action-dimension or
risk-envelope complexity. In particular, enumerating risk vertices can
itself be expensive, and a continuous-state aggregation requires
original-law enclosure before this finite implementation supplies a
valid certificate for that model.

All declared diagnostic horizons, from two through 64 dates, are
retained. Their state count is three, and their original preference is
fixed before execution. The two shortest horizons also permit exhaustive
complete-policy and descendant-tree comparison. The longer cases are not
described as the original high-dimensional vector problem. The inherited
adverse vector query counts remain visible, and the independent
largest-vector reimplementation and full matched vector comparator
tournament remain uncompleted empirical obligations rather than being
replaced by these fixtures.

\hypertarget{m11-b11.-independent-critical-kernel-checking}{%
\subsection{M11 / B11. Independent critical-kernel
checking}\label{m11-b11.-independent-critical-kernel-checking}}

The production calculation enumerates capped-simplex vertices using
bound/free-coordinate constraints and then evaluates centered risk
recursions. The independent code uses the scalar-threshold variational
formula for original risk values, a separate
original-policy/optimal-value recursion and a different greedy-ordering
construction to check completeness of the risk vertices. It reads
serialized primitive inputs and does not import the production
centered-risk module.

The independent audit checks every reported potential inequality against
original values, every strict risk-piece exclusion, all fixed-policy
risk-flow equalities and every risk dual inequality. It also enumerates
all complete Markov policies at horizons two and three and compares an
un-memoized descendant evaluator with its backward evaluator. A clean
rerun must reproduce all deterministic case-file hashes. This is new,
explicitly scoped independent kernel evidence, not a claim to have
completed the still-missing largest-vector solver.
