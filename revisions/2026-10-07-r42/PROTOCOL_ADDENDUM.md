# Protocol addendum: comparator strength and development disclosure

The targets, seeds, economic primitives, and reporting rules in STUDY_PROTOCOL.md remain unchanged. Before the publication catalogue is executed, add a second coupled-economy conventional comparator: convex nonnegative least-squares fitted value iteration on a fixed 96-feature squared-hinge ridge dictionary. Its hidden directions and cutpoints are fixed, not trained; the output coefficients and affine term are fitted on its own future continuation targets. It receives the same analytic continuous-innovation integration, continuous-action primal-dual verification, state-cover ladder, stopping targets, and complete-work boundary as the learned-hidden-feature network. Retain the simpler convex quadratic projection as well. The richer comparator prevents degree-two approximation failure from being interpreted as neural superiority over flexible conventional methods. Neither comparator is claimed to exhaust sparse-grid, high-order projection, or approximate-policy-iteration methods.

Two coupled development designs have already been observed. The initial positive-coefficient random-feature initialization did not reach either target. A primitive-directed initialization followed by hidden-weight optimization passed both targets in the first price-one development service, with a final all-state bound approximately 0.0835. Its earlier loose dual multiplier was valid but conservative; the revised multiplier uses active controls. These development outcomes are retained with weights, certificate components, clocks, and source identities. They are not publication-catalogue observations, blinded replications, or evidence of population success probability. The scalar development service also passed its targets. All fresh publication seeds and failures must still be reported.

Each publication service is isolated in a fresh subprocess. Each refinement writes and fsyncs its candidate and certificate before a target crossing is timestamped. A final service record and its clock are retained even when the target cap fails. Precision repetitions retain every precision attempt and distinguish kernel/update counts from hardware instruction counts. Process initialization and outer-process wall time are reported separately from in-service target-crossing time.
