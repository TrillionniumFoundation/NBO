## Development trials, not selected scientific outcomes

The first transition experiment used a count-informed upper bound. Its all-parameter gaps were about 0.0128, 0.0123 and 0.00929 at the three contracts, so it did not meet 0.001. These results are retained and rerun as a comparator. The corrected endpoint construction was then implemented and checked independently; the manuscript does not conceal the looser valid method.

An initial corrected-interval implementation repeatedly rebuilt unchanged cells and used the inherited dense indexed-array multiplication. Two exploratory runs were stopped to cache unchanged intervals and replace that multiplication with a CSR representation of precisely the same positive rows. This changed computation, not the model or acceptance threshold. Agreement with the original arithmetic is tested; fixed-policy evaluation remains separate. Final tables are generated only from the complete committed implementation's run.

Initial exact-oracle reruns differed from deposited neural-action counts by a small number of floating-point policy ties. R6 therefore stores the deposited count, rerun count, index disagreement count, and separately evaluated value discrepancy. Selection frequency is not used as a proxy for economic importance.

The matched preference experiment was chosen to test deliberate adjustment against otherwise identical stochastic preferences. The cost-coefficient values and displayed contract points are explicit in source; no operating boundary or settlement was changed to obtain a desired result. Bisection outputs are reported as opposite-sign brackets, not upgraded to a uniqueness theorem.
