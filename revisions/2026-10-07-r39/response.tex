We thank the referee for recognizing the earlier revision's
policy-comparison chain and for distinguishing mathematical correctness
from a persuasive numerical contribution. We agree that an exact
quadratic construction and a confounded timing contrast cannot alone
substantiate the broader numerical claims. We respond by extending the
original paper, not by replacing its topic with matrix factorization or
deleting its economic applications.

The revised article adds a constructive nonlinear full-policy theorem,
an all-state verifier for genuinely trained ReLU continuations, a
residual-based native floating-point implementation theorem,
complete-work accounting, identified factorial comparisons, and a
nonlinear investment counterfactual with independently certified
reoptimization gains. It retains the controlled-diffusion framework,
recursive preferences, temporal selves, games, existing full-policy
theory, historical article and supplement, and unfavorable conventional
comparisons. Every active main-text and technical-supplement component
is materialized into the current source tree. Historical sources remain
unchanged.

There are three distinct types of evidence. The 315 economic services
were already deposited in R38 and are re-audited here; they are not
described as new R39 executions. The R39 breakpoint checks are new
offline verification calculations, not new training runs or timing
observations. The mathematical proofs state their assumptions separately
from the finite regression checks. This response is to an
owner-commissioned advisory report, not to an editorial decision by the
Econometric Society.

\hypertarget{b1-and-m6-nonquadratic-continuation-and-a-meaningful-neural-construction}{%
\subsection{B1 and M6: Nonquadratic continuation and a meaningful neural
construction}\label{b1-and-m6-nonquadratic-continuation-and-a-meaningful-neural-construction}}

The new section ``Constructive Neural Bellman Operators beyond quadratic
continuations'' proves a residual-oscillation full-policy comparison for
nonlinear finite-horizon recursive economies on compact state and action
spaces. Its comparator is all feasible adapted policies, not the neural
class or an action catalogue. The loss bound uses globally enclosing
residuals, an actual selected-action allowance, and terminal error. The
proof uses monotonicity and cash invariance and is independent of a
Gaussian quadratic identity.

A constructive proposition verifies the assumptions for interval Bellman
evaluation, continuous feasible actions, and off-grid states. Refining
state and action meshes and arithmetic precision yields a finite
procedure at any positive target for a fixed finite horizon. Its
continuation has an explicit ReLU representation. We do not regard that
representation alone as a neural computational advantage over the
equivalent spline routine.

The deposited second implementation trains every affine hidden weight of
a 31-unit ReLU network. Its own continuation selects actions; an
independent interval bracket certifies those actual actions. Data
generation, neural fitting, verification and policy evaluation all enter
the cost. No convergence of unrestricted Adam or absence of
representation bias is assumed. A certified constructed fallback
separates termination of the overall procedure from acceptance of every
learned candidate.

The new breakpoint proposition further certifies the uniform difference
between the trained continuation and its reference over every state,
including nondyadic hidden-unit roots. All 60 stored date records map to
17 distinct networks. The new bounds are below 0.001, compared with
roughly 0.027--0.031 under the prior mesh-Lipschitz check. Independent
exact rational endpoint evaluation verifies all 17, with two additional
edge-case fixtures. This sharpening is not relabeled as a new
policy-loss or service-time result.

\hypertarget{b2-and-m1-precision-caching-and-update-rule-identification}{%
\subsection{B2 and M1: Precision, caching and update-rule
identification}\label{b2-and-m1-precision-caching-and-update-rule-identification}}

The active evidence now uses fixed64/adaptive precision crossed with
uncached/cached target inverses. All four cells share the same update
rule, candidate-policy check boundary and economic stopping rule. An
additional cached fixed method searches the ordered native set binary32,
binary64; unsuccessful tuning trials are charged. The structural policy
remains in the comparison.

The factorial isolates the precision and caching contrasts within that
fixed update rule. It does not estimate an interaction with every
historical update rule. Those older methods and their results are
retained as separate historical comparisons, not pooled into this
factorial. The introductory interpretation and historical-evidence
preface explicitly withdraw a causal reading of the old joint
adaptive-cached clock contrast.

The identified results are not uniformly favorable to adaptation. At
target 1e-4, fixed64-cached and adaptive-cached have approximately 0.415
and 0.433 second median complete-service times. Each has eleven factor
updates and three inverse builds. Their uncached counterparts build
eleven inverses. The structural median is approximately 0.170 seconds.
We preserve this result and locate the contribution in verified
construction and policy accuracy rather than a timing victory
unsupported by the design.

\hypertarget{b3-and-m2-common-economic-targets-and-first-pass-stopping}{%
\subsection{B3 and M2: Common economic targets and first-pass
stopping}\label{b3-and-m2-common-economic-targets-and-first-pass-stopping}}

Five targets, from 1e-2 to 1e-10, are evaluated at the first successful
economic certificate. Initial and intervening failed checks remain in
the records and in total work. Tables report achieved policy error and
time together; the supplement gives every cell and its counters.

First-pass stopping does not force a discrete quadratic update to meet
the constraint at equality. At target 1e-6, the adaptive procedure still
oversolves substantially. We report that slack instead of calling all
five comparisons binding. The nonlinear construction supplies a separate
nearly binding mesh frontier: first-pass bounds 0.9352, 0.4704, 0.2359
and 0.1181 for targets 1, 0.5, 0.25 and 0.125. Cumulative construction
times include preceding coarser failures and are not confused with the
full process clock or counted as independent observations within one
repetition.

\hypertarget{b4-m4-and-m5-realistic-arithmetic-and-total-work}{%
\subsection{B4, M4 and M5: Realistic arithmetic and total
work}\label{b4-m4-and-m5-realistic-arithmetic-and-total-work}}

The new residual-verification proposition treats arbitrary stored
proposals Z and V. Enclosing MZ minus the original factor Gram and V
minus the proposed matrix product bounds the complete deviation from the
ideal factor step. Original stored inputs are used, so conversion
errors, inverse approximation, caching, multiplication and final storage
error are included. Proposals use binary32 or binary64 and verification
uses outward binary64 balls. No exact rational matrix solve is used by
these production services.

The proof covers every certified initial radius below one and explicitly
distinguishes that range from the earlier half-radius budget. A direct
terminal Gram certificate is distinguished from a squared-radius update.
Failing all available native types is an explicit implementation
failure, not evidence of impossibility at higher precision.

The complete arithmetic-operation statement includes target-inverse
builds, every update attempt, residual verification, complete own-policy
evaluations, full policy checks, actor solves and output work. It states
storage, the dependence of contraction count on initial radius and
tolerance, conditioning in the residual enclosure, and the entry of
moment margins and actor/evaluation allowances into the economic
allocation. Proposal multiply-add counters are not presented as complete
certification-operation counters. Native factors remain stored in
binary64; binary32 proposal work is not claimed to reduce persistent
factor memory.

This is a native arithmetic bound, not an arbitrary-precision
bit-complexity theorem. The policy certificate concerns the exact-real
interpretation of stored feedback coefficients; floating-point actuator
execution needs the separate execution allowance already in the
manuscript. These distinctions are explicit in the theorem and
implementation discussion.

\hypertarget{b5-m3-and-m7-scale-repeated-timing-and-transported-failures}{%
\subsection{B5, M3 and M7: Scale, repeated timing and transported
failures}\label{b5-m3-and-m7-scale-repeated-timing-and-transported-failures}}

The inherited, source-frozen protocol has 315 isolated services, with
three clocks per deterministic object and one separately recorded
warm-up. The policy design includes dimensions 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32;
horizons 4, 8 and 12; different condition parameters; an additional
moment-margin case; the target sweep; and changes of scale 1.005, 1/4
and 4 under cold and transported starts. All 288 policy services certify
their requested target.

The study has controlled thread counts and a fixed shuffled order, but
no CPU affinity or frequency control. Medians and observed min--max
ranges are reported, with startup-inclusive clocks retained separately.
Three repetitions do not support population inference or a universal
timing ranking. The finite grid does not establish asymptotic scaling or
exhaustive near-singularity performance.

Transported starts are not restricted to successful gates. The small
change takes three new adaptive factor updates rather than twelve from
cold construction. Each large change fails all three transported factor
gates and falls back. Warm setup is charged jointly, so the full warm
service can be slower than a cold solve. Complete records retain these
failures. Native precision fallbacks not observed in a particular
economy are not invented to strengthen the narrative.

\hypertarget{b6-and-m8-original-scope-title-and-theorem-boundaries}{%
\subsection{B6 and M8: Original scope, title and theorem
boundaries}\label{b6-and-m8-original-scope-title-and-theorem-boundaries}}

We retain ``Neural Bellman Operators,'' the original topic, and the
complete economic framework. We respectfully do not follow the proposed
replacement by a narrower linear-quadratic factorization paper. Instead,
the new theorem and nonquadratic capital realization expand the
established constructive chain while preserving the prior
unbounded-state recursive-risk construction.

The abstract now states the actual nonlinear comparison, verified
floating-point implementation and economic counterfactual. It does not
suggest that the nonlinear compact-state theorem, or the earlier
quadratic theorem, automatically establishes all assumptions in the
diffusion, endogenous-preference, temporal-self and game applications.
Those applications retain their own assumptions, statements and proofs.
This is an extension of the existing paper, not deletion of its central
program or an unsupported universal neural approximation theorem.

\hypertarget{b7-economic-interpretation-and-a-substantive-counterfactual}{%
\subsection{B7: Economic interpretation and a substantive
counterfactual}\label{b7-economic-interpretation-and-a-substantive-counterfactual}}

The new realization includes irreversible bounded investment, nonlinear
accumulation, clipping, quartic adjustment costs and a capital shortfall
penalty. The cost of investment changes by a factor of four, rather than
a small perturbation of a quadratic state matrix. Both risk-neutral and
risk-sensitive specifications are evaluated.

A fixed-rule exposure proposition derives the marginal repricing burden
as discounted investment exposure under risk-tilted transition weights.
An exact accounting identity separates mechanical repricing from the
gain due to reoptimization. Independent shock-tree intervals certify a
positive gain from the new neural-selected rule over retaining the old
conventional rule at each of four reported initial states, for both risk
specifications. The new-price spline policy remains a stronger work
comparator; the economic gain is not a neural-versus-spline speed or
welfare superiority claim. The experiment is theoretical, not
empirically calibrated, and the global policy bounds are displayed in
actual cost units.

\hypertarget{m9-literature-and-novelty}{%
\subsection{M9: Literature and
novelty}\label{m9-literature-and-novelty}}

The article retains its polar-iteration, matrix-function,
risk-sensitive-control and approximate-dynamic-programming references.
It adds a direct discussion of Carson and Higham's three-precision
iterative-refinement analysis and Rump's verified-numerical-computation
survey. The distinction between computed residuals and certified
enclosures is central to the new implementation proposition. The novelty
is located in the numerical-error-to-policy-loss connection and the
implemented nonlinear construction, not in classical matrix or spline
identities.
