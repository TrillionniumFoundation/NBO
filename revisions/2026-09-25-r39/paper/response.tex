\section{Overview of the revision}
The report distinguishes a reliable computational record from a complete numerical method. We address that distinction directly. The revised article defines one central object: the minimum integrated cost of a common randomized Markov revision satisfying operating constraints after every state and date. The new theory connects approximate feasibility to that object's optimum, rather than proving only validity of a necessary-action relaxation.

The repair theorem constructs a feasible common continuation and bounds its extra cost by $D_C\delta/[(1-\beta)\varepsilon+\delta]$. It yields a complete finite-state policy-lattice bound and a stopping theorem for the executed interval Bellman search. A separate reset-kernel theorem transfers finite-state intervals to the original measurable-policy problem under explicit total-variation approximation assumptions. These assumptions are not attributed automatically to the atomic maintenance benchmarks.

The computational additions keep the original models and incumbents. Whole-domain randomized policies now serve all restarts and the original uniform initial law. They certify strict cost improvement over every feasible deterministic policy in \PrimaryStrict{} of the 42 primary configurations. The connected local-support recursion improves \LocalImproveCount{} primary lower endpoints. In the nonlinear model, the finest horizon-32 interval changes from a zero lower bound to $[\NonlinearFineLower,\NonlinearFineUpper]$. Eight new finite-state environments and both solvers were frozen before generation; every budget stop is retained. The direct McCormick comparison targets exactly the same policy class, initial law, and operating constraints.

The work does not assert that all intervals are now tight. All \PrimaryPositive{} positive-cost randomized primary intervals retain nonzero gaps. The nonlinear relative gap remains \NonlinearFineRelative\% at the finest horizon-32 mesh. The stopped diffusion's 0.01 target is not certified, and no synthetic primitive is called calibrated. These statements identify the remaining tasks without replacing the original optimization target or removing the scientific program. All prior article and supplement pages are preserved verbatim; the older historical annex and failed runs remain unchanged.

\section{Major comments}
\subsection{R38-F1: Convergence to the original constrained objective}
The revised Section 3 supplies the missing bridge. A policy satisfying the all-restart constraints at $\varepsilon+\delta$ is repaired backward to the original tolerance $\varepsilon$, using one state/date-dependent mixture and one already fixed next-date continuation. The proof establishes measurability, all-restart feasibility, operating-value improvement, and a vanishing implementation-cost modulus. It does not assume strong duality or that outer selectors are feasible.

For finite states, rational policy lattices then give explicit global lower and feasible upper sequences whose gap converges to zero at the actual $K_R(\nu,\varepsilon)$. For the binary-action implementation, the new stopping proposition shows that a surviving box of maximal width $w$ has repaired-midpoint cost within $D_C[w+\rho_\varepsilon(D_Jw)]$ of its rectangular Bellman lower bound. Weighted longest-side bisection therefore reaches every positive accuracy without a node or time cap. We report the exponential worst-case depth and do not confuse completeness with practical speed.

The continuous-state transfer in Section 4 additionally identifies sufficient primitive approximation conditions under which finite computation brackets the unrestricted measurable-policy optimum. Thus the paper now has an end-to-end result rather than a convergence statement for an adjacent relaxation. Its assumptions and the atomic-model exception are explicit.

\subsection{R38-F2: The uniform-initial randomized Markov problem}
The revised definition uses one common randomized Markov policy. The primary construction fixes its next-date operating function, compares the installed action with the best action against that continuation, and mixes only enough to restore the current operating inequality. The resulting value is $\max\{q_0,V-\varepsilon\}$. This determines a single measurable policy at every state and date, with fresh conditional randomization rather than a persistent seed. The exact operating verifier checks every knot and open piece.

Costs of these affine-ratio lotteries are enclosed over the whole domain and integrated under the original uniform law. They are paired with valid randomized lower functions for the same object, including the newly connected support recursion. The 42 complete intervals and two nonuniform-law sensitivity tables appear in the supplement. This resolves the policy-class mismatch and establishes \PrimaryStrict{} strict randomized-versus-deterministic separations. It does not falsely identify the remaining positive-width intervals with exact randomized optima. The finite-state study separately demonstrates complete search on the same common-Markov formulation.

\subsection{R38-F3: Short-horizon concentration and unresolved deterministic cases}
The deterministic results remain intact and are decomposed into 12 zero-cost and 12 positive-cost equalities. Ten positive-cost equalities occur at horizon 4, two at horizon 8, and none at horizon 12. The supplement reports every deterministic relative gap and outer-selector operating violation. We do not improve a headline by counting unchanged zero-cost policies as nontrivial searches.

The new economic result is not a restatement of those deterministic equalities: in \PrimaryStrict{} cases, a verified randomized upper cost is below the deterministic lower bound. Longer horizons are included in that comparison. The finite stopping theorem also specifies how global search addresses residual constraints rather than assuming tighter operating witnesses will remove a structural outer-relaxation gap.

\subsection{R38-F4: Zero nonlinear lower bounds}
The new nonlinear construction propagates 11 nonnegative global support prices through the entire horizon and combines their positive floors with five local multiplier bounds. Every support is a supersolution for operating reward minus revision cost; its conversion uses the operating lower witness, while local necessary feasibility uses the upper witness. The resulting lower bound is valid for randomized policies and is paired with exactly the retained feasible upper policy at each mesh.

This changes the finest horizon-32 interval to $[\NonlinearFineLower,\NonlinearFineUpper]$, rather than merely certifying feasibility. Complete scalar independent checks cover $(T,N)=(8,64)$ and $(32,64)$, including the candidate's upper cost and operating inequalities. The finer meshes are not assigned the same independent label. The remaining relative width and memory burden are reported. Four additional $\varepsilon=0.05$ lower-only diagnostics do not borrow upper policies certified only at $0.5$. This is a substantive lower-bound improvement, not a claim that a stringent high-dimensional target has already been solved.

\subsection{R38-F5: One end-to-end error budget}
The reset-approximation theorem gives a single bracket for $K_R(\nu,\varepsilon)$. Certified primitive errors yield operating error $a_h$ and cost error $b_h$. A relaxed finite problem at $\varepsilon+2a_h$ gives the lower side; a tightened problem at $\varepsilon-2a_h$ gives a lifted feasible upper side. The width is bounded by the two finite optimization widths, $2b_h$, and the repair modulus applied to the $4a_h$ tolerance difference. The measured bracket is a stopping rule for the original objective.

The proof averages arbitrary measurable policies only because the verified reset kernel regenerates a stated within-cell law. It does not assume that a convenient policy sieve is dense in the original feasible set. Atomic shock laws in the primary and nonlinear experiments are explicitly excluded from an automatic vanishing-total-variation claim. Their direct certificates remain valid but their mesh widths are not relabeled as end-to-end approximation errors.

\subsection{R38-F6: Strong same-object baselines}
The direct comparator is a global mathematical-programming formulation in common variables $p,J,C$, with all Bellman equalities, all restart constraints, and the same initial cost objective. Each policy box uses classical McCormick envelopes. Any proposed LP multiplier is projected to a nonnegative rational vector and accepted only through an exact finite-box residual bound. A floating infeasibility status cannot prune a policy box.

Both methods use the same contraction, midpoint repair, best-bound ordering, split rule, known primitives, node allowance, and wall-clock allowance. The LP method additionally pays for its stronger relaxation and LP-proposed candidate. All eight pairs are reported, including long-horizon failures to close. The comparison often favors the direct relaxation on lower-bound strength; the paper does not assert superiority of interval Bellman search. An uncoupled family of restart occupation measures would relax the common policy constraint, so it is not used as a supposedly identical benchmark.

The article now states that exact operating values are inexpensive in the affine one-dimensional development model.
