\subsection{Economic interpretation and conclusion}
Let $C^{\mathrm{op}}$ be the exact implementation cost of the operating-best policy in a reset model. The quantity $C^{\mathrm{op}}-U$ is a certified implementation saving achievable without breaking any restart's operating promise. For a fixed administration fee $f$, a certified cost advantage follows when $C^{\mathrm{op}}-U>f$; no such advantage is possible when $C^{\mathrm{op}}-L<f$. The interval width bounds the additional implementation expense of the deployed policy relative to the least feasible expense. The supplement reports $(U-L)/U$, $(U-L)/C^{\mathrm{op}}$, and $C^{\mathrm{op}}-U$ for every primary reset model. These comparisons are invariant to a common positive scaling of costs, fees, budgets, and absolute accuracy. They are conditional economic decisions, not estimates of welfare or market prices.

The paper's target remains certified minimum-cost revision under one common policy. Section~\ref{sec:controlled51} now executes genuinely controlled densities through a common two-moment continuation. The new comparison proves state-contingency savings while preserving global operating protection; it does not resolve the historical finite failures by relabeling a new cohort. The new results give an explicit price-optimization loss bound, a smaller continuation-budget cover with a repair modulus, and paired approximation for density kernels. The executed reset problem supplies diffuse initial laws, continuous kernels, global feasibility, and Borel-valid lower endpoints within a single solver. General finite positive-cost scalability, long-horizon action-dependent diffuse-kernel performance, expensive learned operating witnesses, statistical model uncertainty, and a calibrated economic application are not inferred from that structured success. These distinctions preserve the research object while locating exactly which parts are theorem, executed certificate, and further empirical validation.
