\begin{abstract}
We study neural policy evaluation and improvement as inputs to constructive verification in stopped continuous-time economies. A whole-domain certificate combines neural derivatives, continuous-action optimization, stopping traces, and feasible-output allowances; a coefficient-transport bound extends the calculation to a class of perturbed models. We execute this certificate on a stored state-feedback actor and critic for an economy with costly preference adjustment. The neural execution is complete, but its current regret bound is not small: the boundary trace exposes an economically important source of error. A separately identified affine-preference dual and feasible policy library provide sharp comparison objects. A second numerical implementation certifies regret below \pricebound\ uniformly over adjustment prices in $[.5,8]$, and below \statebound\ jointly over a nondegenerate initial-state rectangle and those prices. Exact breakpoint calculations distinguish policy accuracy from resolved welfare differences. Full-library perturbation tests, an alternative high-precision decomposition, and newly generated policy-richness experiments make the numerical claims reproducible without treating optimizer convergence or solver superiority as consequences of verification.
\end{abstract}
