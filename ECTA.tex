\title{Neural Bellman Operators}
\runtitle{Neural Bellman Operators}
\begin{aug}
\author[id=au1,addressref={add1}]{\fnms{Qian}~\snm{QI}\ead[label=e1]{qiqian@pku.edu.cn}}
\address[id=add1]{Peking University}
\end{aug}
\begin{abstract}
Neural Bellman Operators learn and reuse a policy's continuation value to improve economic decisions. We characterize the common continuation and the approximation--variance tradeoff of fixed gradient dictionaries in a nonlinear capital economy. In two previously unpiloted fifty-dimensional finite decision menus, NBO yields material payoff gains over materialized simulation and direct-policy actors, using less observed construction and query work than direct policy. At the final prescribed budget, NBO is economically equivalent to cached sample-average optimization in six of eight design cells with lower construction and query work; the latter yields higher payoffs in both longest-horizon cells. Independent confirmation supports the original-policy paired comparison and positive signed Bellman gains. An exact-trace neural-HJB study extends the comparison across four diffusion calibrations, while separate diffusion and scalar accounts retain their respective accuracy targets. Complete economic applications preserve the distinct continuation objects of recursive utility, endogenous preferences, temporal selves, and strategic interaction.
\end{abstract}
