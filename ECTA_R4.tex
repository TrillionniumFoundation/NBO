\begin{abstract}
We study the computation of economic policies when preferences, financial choices, and aggregate resource constraints enter the same dynamic problem. Neural Bellman Operators separate the approximation of continuation values from feasible policy improvement. We establish a finite-horizon value-loss bound that distinguishes evaluation error, improvement error, boundary error, and approximation of the transition operator. The bound supports two implementable safeguards: comparison with a feasible candidate set and convex improvement certified by a linear minimization gap. A stopped portfolio economy makes preference adjustment operational without introducing artificial wealth transfers. Its value is decreasing and convex in the adjustment-cost coefficient, and its optimal discounted adjustment expenditure is decreasing in that coefficient, even though individual policy coordinates need not be monotone. We implement neural policies and critics, evaluate their induced policies independently, and solve a coupled stochastic resource-allocation problem with binding shared constraints. A convex scenario-tree calculation bounds the latter policies' cost losses at held-out initial states. Recursive utility, sophisticated temporal selves, and dynamic capacity competition supply additional analytical and computational tests. The results identify which approximation errors matter economically and which conclusions require stronger information than a small training residual.
\end{abstract}
