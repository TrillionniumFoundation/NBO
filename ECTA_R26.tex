\begin{abstract}
We study certified computation in a stopped consumption--portfolio economy with costly preference adjustment. In the executed financed policy class, direct L-BFGS-B remains more accurate and cheaper than the neural alternatives. We develop a synchronous policy-improvement operator with complete optimizer-state rollback, a finite-work quotient-polling theorem under explicit oracle and smoothness conditions, and second-order interval verification for current-state neural policies. Expanded, disjoint calibration brackets all six first-order configurations; a moving-transport control retains historical Jacobians and Adam moments. Eighteen new online decisions include five actual rejections. In a separate stopped economy with unspanned risk and an independently known value, unchanged neural policies obtain whole-state--time regret certificates below $.001$ on 32,768 cells, rather than 262,144. Exact rational polynomial verification provides a stronger classical reference. The original economy and its uniform $.01$ objective are retained: its present bound is $7.181835$, and neither neural efficiency dominance nor a continuous stopped-gradient certificate is asserted.
\end{abstract}
