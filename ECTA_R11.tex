\begin{abstract}
We study neural policy evaluation, improvement, and independent verification in a continuous economy with costly preference adjustment. An affine-preference market-deflator dual and validated policy evaluation certify regret below .01 for three adjustment-cost coefficients under the original utility, adaptive controls, and first-exit contract. Access to adjustment and the welfare cost of more expensive adjustment are resolved by intervals narrower than their respective effects. A quantitative output-error allowance, interval neural differentiation, and a structured global action oracle connect implemented policies to verified quantities. We add prospectively frozen method-specific tuning and independent holdout evidence without replacing the earlier comparison. Reference-solvable tracking, mean-reverting, and ill-conditioned control problems provide absolute-accuracy frontiers for stored held-action actors. Their exact error decomposition separates deployment error from implemented optimization error. Proofs, retained unsuccessful outcomes, and independent recomputations distinguish policy evaluation, optimality, sampling uncertainty, and the economic inequalities established.
\end{abstract}
