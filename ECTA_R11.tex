\title{Neural Bellman Operators}
\runtitle{Neural Bellman Operators}
\begin{aug}
\author[id=au1,addressref={add1}]{\fnms{Qian}~\snm{QI}\ead[label=e1]{qiqian@pku.edu.cn}}
\address[id=add1]{\orgdiv{Peking University},\orgname{No.5 Yiheyuan Road, Haidian District, Beijing, P.R.China 100871}}
\end{aug}
\support{This paper develops the numerical framework accompanying \citet{qi2024}. The author thanks Darrell Duffie for discussions.}
\begin{abstract}
We study how preference adjustment changes financial choice and the price of operating commitments. A dynamic transport test identifies when downward adjustment is dominated on a complete feasible-support tube, without imposing global monotonicity of continuation value. In a stochastic settlement economy, we certify a joint region of transition laws, benefits, surrender charges, and investment permissions in which adjustment reverses the initial position ranking while a counterfactual surrender option remains strictly valuable. The direction of adjustment is explained by continuation gains and their class-specific exposure, rather than inferred from a two-stage example. An explicit technology pricing both guarantee capacity and compulsory terms characterizes the institution that implements a selected operating allocation. Neural Bellman Operators separate policy proposal, evaluation, improvement, and certification. For transition laws that change at every date, a signed endpoint correction compresses a sharper count-information upper value; feasible-policy Bernstein representations supply continuum welfare and decision bounds. Matched comparisons charge common construction, endpoint, and validation costs. Reward-transfer correspondences, cardinal perturbations, adverse permission and surrender cases, structural computational controls, recursive utility, and dynamic competition remain part of the analysis. The numerical claims refer to identified finite-array targets with explicit evaluation and target-transfer accounts.
\end{abstract}
