\begin{abstract}
We construct Bellman solution procedures that separate neural approximation, economically meaningful policy improvement, and independent error certification. For a finite-horizon stopped control problem, a backward completion operator converts an arbitrary candidate into a uniformly $\varepsilon$-optimal policy using local Bellman deficits, without an optimal-value reference. An exact-rational implementation certifies all \InventoryCases\ prespecified neural and polynomial candidates in an unknown-solution inventory economy at tolerance $.01$. For continuous control, an interval-oracle polling theorem supplies projected-gradient stationarity at active box constraints and includes the precision-dependent cost of verification. We instantiate its assumptions in an unchanged slice of a stopped consumption--portfolio economy with costly preference adjustment, certifying both a boundary optimum and an interior solution. An exact recentered historical-transport identity, implemented independently, removes the linearization and carrier-drift confounds in the neural-geometry comparison. A fixed production-schedule experiment evaluates complete-state rollback against matched ungated and classical optimizers. These results provide constructive accuracy guarantees for the stated finite-state problem and constrained slice; they do not imply a neural speed advantage or solve the original all-state, all-restart continuous objective. That objective remains $.01$, with retained certified bound $7.181835$. The full scientific record, including unsuccessful candidates, classical comparators, and earlier derivations, is preserved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion28}
A Bellman computation is complete only relative to a specified policy class, domain, restart set, and error criterion. We obtain an end-to-end, reference-free accuracy result for a genuinely unknown finite-state stochastic problem, an instantiated constrained certificate in the original stopped economy, and a faithful finite-path explanation of neural optimizer transport. The completed inventory policies meet the same declared tolerance across all retained architectures and initializations. In the original slice, the preference-adjustment cost determines whether the optimal adjustment binds its upper constraint, and the certificate distinguishes that boundary case from an interior optimum.

The price of these conclusions is explicit. Exact Bellman completion has the enumeration order of classical backward induction; the recentered control retains the neural carrier and its optimizer history; a deployment gate need not improve the accuracy--work frontier. The general original current-state objective is not replaced by these applications. Its uniform bound remains $7.181834580823298$ against $.01$, and the financed 47-dimensional stopped-gradient bridge and a high-dimensional continuous verifier cost bound are not established by this revision. Closing those particular statements requires a stronger original-domain construction, not reinterpretation of a finite-state certificate. The numerical operators, proofs, executed counter-controls, and complete retained record below specify what has been established and make the remaining scientific statements testable.
