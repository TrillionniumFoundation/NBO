\begin{abstract}
We study neural policy evaluation and improvement in stochastic economic models with endogenous preferences, recursive utility, and strategic interaction. Independent verification separates the implemented policy from its critic and from any corrective action search. For stopped diffusions, one-sided smooth witnesses bound continuous policy regret without requiring a smooth optimal value at a controlled exit boundary. We establish error-calibrated comparative statics for costly preference adjustment and a complete constrained Hamiltonian selection rule for the economic application. Executed interval certificates cover retained policies in the original continuous economy, although the resulting enclosures remain too wide for precise welfare comparisons. A learned-actor experiment on the same state, control, covariance, and exit geometry attains continuous regret bounds below .00113 for an explicitly modified payoff. Thresholded finite correction preserves substantially more of a retained long-horizon neural policy while accounting for the full action-search cost. In eight- and sixteen-dimensional coupled nonconvex control problems, paired experiments favor NBO over a pinned author implementation of SOC-MartNet at the declared short training budget. The theoretical scope, numerical precision, and computational comparisons are stated separately and tied to complete reproducible records.
\end{abstract}
