\begin{abstract}
We study neural policy evaluation, improvement, and independent verification in a continuous economy with costly preference adjustment. An affine-preference market-deflator dual, combined with validated policy evaluation, certifies regret below .01 at the central state for three adjustment-cost coefficients under the original utility, continuous controls, and first-exit contract. The dual retains adapted adjustment through an explicit conditional-variance allowance. At cost coefficient two, access to adjustment raises optimal welfare by at least .04096859; increasing that coefficient to eight lowers optimal welfare by at least .02318257. Each effect exceeds the width of its corresponding certified interval. We provide an executed absolute-accuracy verification frontier and a quantitative allowance for implemented control errors. A structured action oracle and an absolute lower reference accompany the complete frozen external comparison. Proofs and computations distinguish policy evaluation, optimality, sampling uncertainty, and the economic inequalities actually established.
\end{abstract}
