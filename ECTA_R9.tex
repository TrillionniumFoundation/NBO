\title{Neural Bellman Operators}
\runtitle{Neural Bellman Operators}
\begin{aug}
\author[id=au1,addressref={add1}]{\fnms{Qian}~\snm{QI}\ead[label=e1]{qiqian@pku.edu.cn}}
\address[id=add1]{\orgdiv{Peking University},\orgname{No.5 Yiheyuan Road, Haidian District, Beijing, P.R.China 100871}}
\end{aug}
\support{This paper develops the numerical framework accompanying \citet{qi2024}. The author thanks Darrell Duffie for discussions.}
\begin{abstract}
We study reusable policies for changes in economic contracts and transition laws. Neural Bellman Operators separate proposal, evaluation, improvement, and certification. For affine finite-horizon transition mixtures, a signed endpoint correction compresses a sharper count-information upper value; feasible-policy Bernstein representations deliver regional welfare and decision certificates with an explicit policy-bank error account. In a portfolio economy with adjustable risk aversion, the certified decision concerns a positive-duration initial position. We embed the operating economy in a participation contract and characterize its signing grant, service-procurement condition, and statewise enforcement frontier. Adjustment weakly reduces the compensation and enforcement needed to operate, but its relative value across position classes can reverse under voluntary surrender. A finite-charge region implements the original opposite-position comparison. An exact first-date risky-share reduction and permission shadow values explain how long and short limits move both indifference boundaries. Common-cardinal-tilt robustness, dynamic feature counterfactuals, and wealth-settlement perturbations retain distinct economic roles. Streaming chord and count are compared on the same decision, including common construction and validation costs; mesh-only and exact structural countercomparisons remain visible. Recursive utility, temporal selves, and persistent capacity competition extend the framework. Computational precision is linked to the price and scope of an economic contract, rather than equated with a training residual.
\end{abstract}
