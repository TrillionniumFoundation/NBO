\begin{abstract}
We study when a computed policy can be reused after an economic contract changes. Neural Bellman Operators separate policy proposal, evaluation, improvement, and certification. For a stopped portfolio economy with adjustable risk aversion, operating benefits and liquidation levels affect behavior through a single contract contrast. Value envelopes certify welfare losses uniformly across a continuum of contracts; action-separation bounds distinguish portfolio reversals from numerical ambiguity. The implementation repairs improvable neural proposals and evaluates all controls, stopping times, and adjustment effort. A coupled resource-allocation experiment isolates the contributions of neural continuation values and actor initialization, with cost-matched comparisons at increasing query volumes. Persistent capacity competition, recursive utility, and temporal selves provide further tests. The analysis connects operator errors to economic decisions without equating a small training residual with an optimal policy.
\end{abstract}
