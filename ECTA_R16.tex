\begin{abstract}
We develop neural Bellman operators with stopping-accessibility-aware verification and explicit policy-improvement error budgets. In a stopped preference-adjustment economy, we prove that continuation value has a strict jump at an inward wealth boundary, and construct a neural policy and witness that eliminate the trace budget without changing the original control problem. A quantitative certified policy-iteration recurrence separates optimization defects from discretization error. Twenty full-horizon neural checkpoints and six classical state-space candidates are verified in the original economy; their residual bounds remain above the declared accuracy target. In a coupled inventory economy with up to 128 states, a quadratic performance-difference identity gives state-global certificates for all retained neural gain functions, with every final checkpoint below \RInventoryFinalMax\ per coordinate. Separate original-economy comparisons include a full eighteen-node MPFR arithmetic audit and a freshly generated price continuum with regret below \RFreshGap. A precisely defined consumption-top-up bound translates the latter into a scale-normalized compensation measure. The complete numerical record preserves the distinction between a valid certificate, a convergent approximation criterion, and demonstrated policy accuracy.
\end{abstract}
