\begin{abstract}
We develop Neural Bellman Operators with separate policy-value acceptance and optimal comparison. A coupled-payoff theorem turns independently enclosed value differences into uniform policy-improvement certificates on an initial-state rectangle. In the original stopped consumption--portfolio economy, five newly trained neural control generators use no solution labels. Every retained update improves the reference-state policy value; initial-to-final true regret decreases by at least 58 percent throughout the rectangle. Final reference-state optimality bounds are below $.01$, and the rectangular bound is $\NineteenK$, with explicit budget-feasible wealth compensation. Independent policy evaluation supplies constructive one-sided continuation targets. A new matched-tolerance study identifies regimes in which learned initialization reduces nonlinear-control gradient work, while retaining strong classical comparators. The full-domain feedback target is unchanged and remains distinct from these localized certificates. All previous mathematical developments, complete-domain experiments, and adverse outcomes are preserved. The analysis distinguishes actual policy improvement, witness refinement, optimality, and computational work.
\end{abstract}
