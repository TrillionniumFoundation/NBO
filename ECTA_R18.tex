\begin{abstract}
We develop certificate-aligned neural Bellman operators for dynamic economic decisions. A complete-cover differentiable objective guides neural proposals, while independent directed arithmetic determines acceptance. A quantitative envelope theorem links that implemented objective to policy loss, and stopping-accessibility conditions correct an inconsistent hard-settlement approximation space. On the original nonlinear economy, all ten prescribed raw certificate trajectories improve with work, although none reaches the unchanged $.01$ target; matched ablations and a computable witness floor identify the remaining obstruction. In a heterogeneous nonlinear inventory problem, full-state neural initialization and a provably contracting correction yield a state-uniform total-cost bound below $1.037\times10^{-6}$ at 128 coordinates. Classical methods are compared under the same certificates and remain competitive. A separate sharp original-economy policy library admits explicit budget-financed initial-wealth compensation. The analysis distinguishes a valid certificate, improvement of a witness, actual policy accuracy, and numerical work.
\end{abstract}
