\begin{abstract}
We study neural policy evaluation, improvement, and independent verification in a continuous economy with costly preference adjustment. An affine-preference market-deflator dual and validated policy evaluation retain the original utility, adaptive controls, and first-exit contract. A constructive cost-continuation theorem turns eighteen certified policies into a policy rule with regret below .01 for every cost coefficient in [.5,8] at the central initial state. Convex value chords, sign-aware expenditure bounds, and exact envelope breakpoints provide a finite acceptance test for this continuum guarantee. Certified access and cost-comparison intervals identify economic effects separately from policy expenditures. Quantitative output-error allowances, interval neural differentiation, and a structured global action oracle connect implemented policies to verified quantities. Frozen method-specific tuning, complete holdout evidence, and reference-solvable control problems retain separate roles: relative performance, robustness, and absolute-accuracy frontiers. Full proofs and preservation of prior manuscripts and unsuccessful calculations accompany the new construction.
\end{abstract}
